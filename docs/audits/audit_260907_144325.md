**Scope:** the police system in Redline Interstate, and nothing else
**Auditor:** session, at the owner's request — "let's make sure the full police system is

## Executive Summary

**The police system is built, and most of it works.** Heat is earned rather than counted,
traps arm and catch, patrols engage, the chase drives, cruisers take damage and go down,
the bust fires, and roadblocks go up. Six of the seven stages were driven live and
behaved.

**Three findings, and only one of them is a defect.** The defect is small and old: the
function that dispatches an ordinary chasing cruiser, `spawnCop`, is **never called from
anywhere**. It is dead code, and its absence leaves a hole in the escalation that traps
and patrols happen to cover.

The second finding is not a defect but a **contradiction the owner has to settle**: a
chasing cruiser reaches 169mph, measured, while the CRUISER a player can drive tops out at
142. The pursuit AI is clamped to a global `AI_TOP` of 180mph and never asks the car what
it can do. That is an NPC in a better version of the same car, which the standing
one-physics ruling forbids with the rubber band as its only named exception.

The third is coverage: **four of the seven stages have no automated check at all.**

**And this audit found one of its own claims wrong before writing it down.** The bust
appeared broken — six seconds stopped with a cruiser alongside and the game state never
changed. It was the probe that was wrong: a wreck in Interstate is a two-second penalty
and not a state change, by the owner's own 2026-09-06 ruling that the world must not
freeze. Reading `wreckWait` instead showed the bust firing at 3.3 seconds, exactly as
written. It is recorded here because "the check was wrong" is the most common outcome in
this file's history and the near-miss is worth more than the corrected result.

## Finding 1 — `spawnCop` is dead code

**Severity: Medium.** A whole rank of the escalation ladder does not exist.

`spawnCop()` is defined at `road.js:8570`. Searching the file for a call finds none: its
only two other mentions are inside comments. `nextCopT`, the timer whose name says it once
drove this, now gates trap spawning instead.

So what can chase you is: a trap you trip, a patrol you speed past, and a super cruiser at
heat three with a recorded 170mph pass. **There is no ordinary "they have called it in"
cruiser** — nothing arrives because your wanted level is high, only because you personally
tripped something. Heat four and heat two summon the same cars.

This is not visible in play as a bug, which is why it has survived: traps are frequent and
patrols are new, so something is usually behind you. It shows up as the escalation feeling
flat between heat 2 and heat 3.

**Recommendation:** either wire it to heat, or delete it. Leaving a dispatcher that is
never called is the state that let this go unnoticed. The owner should choose — wiring it
changes difficulty, and that is not an auditor's call.

## Finding 2 — a chasing cruiser is faster than the same car in your hands

**Severity: Medium. This one needs an owner ruling, not a fix.**

Measured, on a fresh road with one cruiser and the player at 0.98 of top speed:

| | speed |
|---|---|
| fastest a chasing cruiser reached | **169 mph** |
| the CRUISER in the garage, `vmax` 0.71 | **142 mph** |
| `AI_TOP`, the global clamp on every AI car | 180 mph |

The chase reads `AI_TOP` and never asks the cruiser what its own body can do. The comment
on `AI_TOP` states the intent plainly — "they are quick, but the car you are driving is the
fastest thing out here" — and against a 200mph supercar that is true. Against the CRUISER
itself it is not: the same car is 27mph faster with a policeman in it.

The standing rule is the owner's, 2026-08-28: **one physics for every car, and the rubber
band is the only named exception.** A rival is competitive because it drives well. This is
the one place in the pursuit where that does not hold.

Both readings are defensible and they lead to different games:

- **Cap a cruiser at its own 142.** Consistent with the fleet rule. It also means a
  supercar simply outruns the police in a straight line, and the pursuit becomes about
  traffic, roadblocks and corners rather than top speed.
- **Keep `AI_TOP` and say so.** Then the CRUISER's `vmax` is a garage number and the
  pursuit is deliberately exempt — which is a second named exception alongside the rubber
  band, and should be written down as one rather than left implicit in a constant.

**Recommendation:** the owner picks. Whichever way, the answer belongs in a ruling, because
the next person to read `AI_TOP` beside `BODY.CRUISER.vmax` will ask this again.

## Finding 3 — four stages have no automated check

**Severity: Low, but it is why Finding 1 survived.**

| Stage | Harness |
|---|---|
| heat earned, cooled, supers dispatched | `heat-test` |
| patrols | `patrol-test` |
| the chase, damage, the takedown | partly `stat-test`, via `API.hurtCop` |
| **the bust** | none |
| **roadblocks** | none |
| **the PIT manoeuvre** | none |
| **the escalation ladder as a ladder** | none |

The last is the one that matters. Nothing checks that heat *does anything* — that heat 4
puts more on the road than heat 2. A check of that shape would have failed the day
`spawnCop` stopped being called, and there has never been one.

**Recommendation:** one harness covering the ladder, asserting that what is on the road
grows with heat. The bust and the roadblock are each a handful of lines once something can
observe them, and today nothing can: **there is no API exposing the roadblock array**, which
is why this audit had to infer roadblocks from `blockedAhead` rather than count them.

## Notes carried, not findings

- **The survey could not reproduce a super cruiser** in its own run, though `heat-test`
  dispatches four reliably. The likely cause is the survey's own driving: it pins speed
  with `setSpd` every 400ms and the car decays between calls, so `fastFor`, which needs
  four unbroken seconds above 150mph, may never accumulate. Recorded rather than chased,
  and **not claimed as a defect** — `heat-test` is the better evidence and it is green.
- Heat floors at 1 and never returns to 0. That reads as deliberate (one star is the
  baseline) and nothing here treats it as a fault.
- `drive-test`'s nitrous check and `traffic-test`'s corridor and amber checks are
  unreliable, and `collide-test` has two checks red on `main`. All are already written up
  as separate work and none is a police finding.
