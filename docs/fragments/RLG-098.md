### RLG-098 — The tyre screech takes its pitch from the engine
- **RULED 2026-08-31T08:35:31-04:00** — Owner, 2026-08-31: "It sounds like the tire screeching pitch is affected by the current RPM because
if I slam on my brakes in a high gear each time the car downshifts the pitch of the tire squealing
changes, but the pitch of the tire squealing is a different physical component. It's just the tires
rubbing against the ground."

THE ARGUMENT IS THE RULING AND IT IS EXACTLY RIGHT. A tyre squeal is rubber against tarmac. It has no
knowledge of what gear the gearbox is in, and the gearbox is on the other side of the car from the
contact patch. A downshift changing the pitch of the squeal is two systems sharing a number that
belongs to one of them.

AND THE SYMPTOM NAMES THE TEST, WHICH IS UNUSUAL AND WORTH USING. A downshift is a STEP in engine
revs with NO step in road speed - that is what a gearchange is. So the check writes itself: brake
hard in a high gear, record the squeal's pitch across the downshift, and assert it does not step
where the revs do. Nothing else in the car changes at that instant, which makes it a clean
experiment rather than a correlation.

WHAT THE PITCH SHOULD FOLLOW INSTEAD, and this is the part to settle before building. A squeal is
driven by how fast the rubber is sliding over the road, which is the difference between the wheel's
surface speed and the car's speed - the slip. Under braking that is a function of ROAD SPEED and how
hard the brake is applied; in a corner it is a function of how far the car is sliding sideways, which
is RLG-099. Both are speed and slip, and neither is engine revs.

BE CAREFUL WHAT ELSE READS THAT NUMBER. `road.js` serves both driving games, so anything changed here
ships to Interstate and Motorsport at once. And the project has already been caught by a sound whose
gate was the wrong quantity: the brake screech is gated on MEASURED deceleration rather than on the
pedal, which was a deliberate fix recorded in DESIGN.md - so the screech's TRIGGER is already
correct and it is only its PITCH that is wrong. Do not re-fix the trigger.

A SOUND CHECK CANNOT LISTEN, BUT IT CAN READ THE GRAPH. `tools/audio-test.py` exists for exactly this
and RLG-065's lesson applies: counting calls read zero on a fixed build and a gain read healthy on a
broken one. What works is asking the node itself what it is set to, at the moment the fault would
show.
