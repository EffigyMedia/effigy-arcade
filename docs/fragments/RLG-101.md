### RLG-101 — The road itself is carried to the horizon, the way the sea now is
- **RULED 2026-08-31T09:05:09-04:00** — Owner, 2026-08-31, immediately after the sea was carried to the horizon: "another thing that would be
really cool is if we did the same thing for the highway, so that the highway is interpreted and drawn
to the horizon."

THE SAME MOVE, ON THE THING THE WHOLE VIEW IS BUILT AROUND. The road is drawn for DRAW segments and
stops short of the skyline; the band above it is filled by `groundBase`, which paints the far verge's
colour. So the tarmac ends and the land continues, and the road does not reach the vanishing point it
is drawn toward. The sea had exactly this shape of gap and the answer there was to walk the same
construction on - `proj` and `roadsideAt`, in coarser steps, until the projection converges - rather
than to invent a second one. That is what this asks for.

AND IT IS HARDER THAN THE SEA WAS, FOR ONE REASON THAT SHOULD BE FACED FIRST. The sea is a FILL: one
colour, one edge, and a polygon closed against the screen edge. The road is a ribbon with a centre
line, lane markings, rumble strips and a verge either side, and every one of those is drawn per slice
with its own width. Carrying the tarmac on is a polygon; carrying the MARKINGS on is a strobing
pattern that has to keep its phase, and a marking that arrives out of phase reads worse than no
marking at all. Decide which of those is in scope before starting.

THE STRIPES ARE THE REAL QUESTION AND THEY MAY ANSWER THEMSELVES. Past a certain distance a lane
marking is under a pixel wide and the honest render is none - which is what the draw distance is
implicitly saying today. So the likely shape of this is: the TARMAC and the verge are carried to the
horizon as fills, and the markings are not. That is one body of road in the same sense the sea is one
body of water, and it is a much smaller change than carrying the whole pass.

WHAT IS ALREADY KNOWN AND SHOULD NOT BE RE-DERIVED. `groundBase` already paints the band and already
takes the far slice's biome, so the band is not empty and this is a change to WHAT fills it rather
than a new layer. `farSea` shows the pattern to copy, including the guard that emits the points the
projection gives and skips the ones that do not rise toward the horizon - it needs no sanity test and
its failure mode is a shorter walk. And the corner cap matters here in a way it did not for the sea:
past about 90 degrees the road leaves the frame, so a walk that continues a hard bend has to stop
rather than draw tarmac across the sky.

MEASURE IT AS THE SEA WAS MEASURED. Not as a picture: the tone step across the join, sampled either
side, swept inside single frames because this world cannot be compared across runs. The sea's seam
turned out to be a COLOUR rather than an angle, and the road has the same asymmetry waiting for it -
the band is painted before `drawHaze` and the drawn road after, so the tarmac will step at the join
for exactly the reason the water did.
- **RULED 2026-09-08T05:42:11-04:00** — BUILT 2026-09-08, v0.13.37. The tarmac runs to the vanishing point as one body of road.

THE SCOPE IS WHAT THIS RULING WORKED OUT IN ADVANCE, and the build agrees with it: the TARMAC is
carried on and the MARKINGS are not. Carrying the surface is a polygon; carrying the lane markings
is a strobing pattern that has to keep its phase, and a marking that arrives out of phase reads
worse than no marking at all. Past this distance a marking is under a pixel wide and the honest
render is none, which is what the draw distance has been saying implicitly all along.

ONE CONSTRUCTION, NOT TWO, exactly as the sea. The band walks on in the same steps the road walks
in, asking `proj` the same question the drawn slices ask. There is no extrapolation and no estimated
angle, so there is nothing for a join to disagree with, and the failure mode of a point the
projection will not give is a shorter walk. The vanishing point is exact and has no width: at
infinite distance the perspective term goes to zero, so both edges arrive at the centre of the
screen shifted by the view offset and the bend, and the polygon closes to a point there.

THE CORNER CAP MATTERS HERE IN A WAY IT DID NOT FOR THE SEA, and the ruling said so. The walk stops
when the centreline has gone a screen's width outside the frame, or a hard bend would paint tarmac
across the sky.

TWO THINGS HAD TO BE FIXED AFTER THE FIRST CUT, and both were found by measurement:

THE STEPS HAD TO GROW. An even stride of eight segments put the FIRST point inside a quarter-pixel
of the horizon, because beyond the draw distance the projection compresses hard and the whole band
belongs to the first few segments past the edge. Every point was rejected, the walk emitted nothing
on most frames, and the band came out as one straight cone - the thing it was built to stop being.
A square-law stride puts points where the pixels are.

AND THE BAND BELONGS ON THE ROAD'S SIDE OF THE HAZE. The sea's band is painted before `drawHaze`
and takes the wash as paint. The road cannot be: the drawn road is painted AFTER the haze and does
not take it, so a band on the sky's side arrives a different colour and the seam is a tone step -
measured at 46 of a possible 441. The ruling predicted this seam and gave the reason.

### The measurement the ruling asked for, and why it is printed rather than asserted

THE RULING ASKED FOR THE TONE STEP ACROSS THE JOIN BY NAME and this session could not turn it into
an assertion that means anything. Four sampling schemes were tried and each was dominated by
something other than the seam:

  the MEAN across the road caught the lane markings, white on grey, and put a "step" of 197 where
    the surfaces agreed;
  the DARKEST pixel caught the rumble strip and the shadow line below the join against uniform
    tarmac above it;
  sampling around the VANISHING POINT put both samples on the verge, where they agreed with each
    other perfectly and said nothing - the colours came back green;
  and sampling around the far slice's own y put both samples inside the BAND, because the road pass
    stops at whichever slice survives the crest guards and that is tens of pixels lower on a rolling
    road.

Anchoring on `roadTop` fixed the last of those and the reading still swung 34, 84 and 39 across
three runs of one unchanged build. THE QUANTITY IS REAL AND THIS INSTRUMENT CANNOT HOLD IT STILL,
and a threshold tuned until it passed would be a number chosen from noise. It is printed with those
reasons in the harness's own source, and THE JOIN NEEDS THE OWNER ON A DEVICE.

WHAT IS ASSERTED INSTEAD IS STABLE AND FALSIFIES: the band is drawn over a stretch of road, it walks
rather than drawing one straight cone, and it closes at the vanishing point on the horizon. With the
band removed, all of it goes red - 0 frames of 90.

A NOTE FOR WHOEVER PICKS THE JOIN UP. `farRoad` reports the join row, the join width, the vanishing
point, the apex and the walk length, and `farSea.roadTop` is where the road pass actually stopped -
which is the row a measurement has to anchor on, and is not the same as the far slice's own y.
