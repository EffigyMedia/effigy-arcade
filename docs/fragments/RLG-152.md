### RLG-152 — A deer sprints across the road in a forest, very rarely
- **RULED 2026-09-01T20:16:30-04:00** — Owner, 2026-09-01: "This might be silly but in the forest biome I'd like a very very small
chance for deer to sprint across the road from one tree line to the other."

IT IS NOT SILLY AND IT IS NOT SCENERY, WHICH IS THE WHOLE OF WHY IT IS WORTH A FRAGMENT. Every
roadside thing this engine draws is placed at a fixed offset from the tarmac and is passed by;
a deer crossing is the first thing in the game that MOVES ACROSS the road. That is the traffic
system's shape, not the scenery system's, and building it as scenery with a moving offset would
be a second, weaker traffic system living in the painter.

SO IT IS A TRAFFIC ENTITY WITH NO LANE DISCIPLINE. The traffic array already carries a lateral
position, a lateral drift and a collider; a deer is an entry that starts outside the verge on
one side, crosses at a stated rate, and is culled when it reaches the other. Nothing about the
spawner, the culling, the mirror or the crest occlusion has to learn what a deer is.

THREE THINGS NEED THE OWNER BEFORE IT IS BUILT, and the third is the real one:

    HOW RARE IS "VERY VERY SMALL"? The owner said it twice, so it is deliberately at the edge
    of never. Rare enough and a player never sees it, which is a feature nobody gets; too
    common and a forest becomes an obstacle course. A number, or a target like "about once in
    ten forests".

    HOW FAST DOES IT CROSS? This decides whether it is an event you react to or one you watch
    happen in front of you. A deer that clears the road in half a second at 150mph is a flash;
    one that takes two seconds is a hazard.

    AND WHAT HAPPENS IF YOU HIT IT. This is the question that decides what the feature IS.
    Nothing at all makes it decoration. Damage makes a forest a place you slow down for, which
    is a real change to how one biome plays and touches nothing else in the game. And there is
    a third answer the owner may want and may not: that the deer is hit and killed, which is
    a tone decision rather than a mechanical one and is not mine to make.

WHAT IT MUST NOT DO. It must not be a branch that names a vehicle class - the standing ruling
is that the car is capable and the driver decides, and a deer is a driver with a very simple
mind rather than a special case in the physics. [[RLG-054]]'s three personalities are the
mechanism a fourth kind of mover should be expressed through if it can be.

AND IT WANTS THE FOREST'S OWN TREE LINE. The owner said "from one tree line to the other",
which is a forest with scenery on both sides - so it should not fire in a place that has water,
a wall or a bore down one side. `sideRoll` and the place's own spec already say which sides
carry what.
- **RULED 2026-09-08T04:46:47-04:00** — BUILT 2026-09-08, v0.13.34. THE THREE QUESTIONS THIS RULING PUT TO THE OWNER ARE ANSWERED HERE
AND THE ANSWERS ARE DECIDED-FOR-NOW, NOT SETTLED. The owner said on 2026-09-08 to run the rest of
the queue without checking back, so a session that stopped for them would have delivered nothing.
Each is a single tunable and each is stated plainly so it can be overruled from the device.

HOW RARE: about one forest in ten. It is rolled ONCE when a forest opens rather than tested per
second, and that is what makes the odds mean what they say - a per-frame chance would put two in
one wood and none in the next four. Measured over 400 openings: 10.8%, 10.5%, 9.5%.

HOW FAST: 2.5 lane units a second, which clears the two-unit tarmac in about eight tenths of a
second. The ruling framed the choice as a flash against a hazard and this is the fast end of a
hazard - long enough to react to, short enough to be a sprint rather than an amble.

AND A HIT COSTS WHAT ANY COLLISION COSTS, which is the answer the ruling says makes it a feature
rather than decoration: a forest becomes a place you slow down for. It needed no code, because a
deer is an entry in `traffic` and the player's collision with traffic already exists. THE ANIMAL IS
NOT KILLED and nothing depicts one being killed - the ruling calls that a tone decision and not a
session's to make, so it is simply gone from the road.

IT IS A MIND, NOT A VEHICLE CLASS, exactly as the ruling asked. `CROSSING` sits beside CIVILIAN,
SPEEDER and RACER, and the one branch that exists is about what that mind DOES rather than what
body it wears. Everything that reads `traffic` - the spawner, the culler, the mirror, the crest
occlusion, the collision - goes on working without knowing what a deer is.

AND IT IS KEPT OUT OF THE LANE SWEEPS, DELIBERATELY. `laneClear`, `wouldBlock`, `laneSpeed`, the
follower and `keepLaneOpen` all skip a crossing. It is on the road for under a second and it is
nobody's lane; treating it as an obstacle would set every car near it braking and swerving for a
thing that has already gone, and the corridor guarantee [[RLG-037]] protects is measured over
exactly those sweeps and already fails about one run in four. The hazard is the PLAYER'S, which is
the point of it.

IT WANTS TWO TREE LINES. A forest with water down one side plans none - measured at 0 of 400 - for
the reason the owner gave: "from one tree line to the other".

### One mistake, and it is the kind that would have shipped looking finished

THE SKIP WAS WRITTEN AS A `continue` A DOZEN LINES TOO EARLY, in the loop that advances every car.
It stepped over the culling and over THE PLAYER'S COLLISION, so the animal could be driven straight
through and cost nothing - which is precisely the "nothing at all makes it decoration" outcome this
ruling names as the failure. It looked completely correct: the crossing ran, the sprite was there,
the odds were right. Only the damage check caught it, and only because the check drives INTO one
rather than watching one go past.

`tools/deer-test.py` is the check. It samples the PATH rather than the endpoints - a thing that
vanished on one side and reappeared on the other would satisfy a start-and-finish test - and it
falsifies: freeze the lateral rate and four assertions go red while the odds and the collision stay
green.
- **RULED 2026-09-08T07:47:13-04:00** — THE TONE QUESTION IS ANSWERED, 2026-09-08, v0.13.38. Owner: "the deer should explode in a bloody
mess and it should do appropriate damage to your car." That is the third answer this ruling left
open and called a tone decision rather than a mechanical one, and it was not a session's to make.

IT DIES. A car takes damage and drives on; an animal does not. The branch is on the MIND, which is
how this ruling chose to express a deer in the first place, so nothing in the collision names a
species.

THE MESS IS NOT `burst` IN A DIFFERENT COLOUR. Debris off a car is fourteen light flecks that fly up
and away; an animal at a hundred miles an hour is a spray that goes forward and over, plus heavier
pieces that arrive and drop. So it is forty particles in three reds on two velocity budgets - a fast
bright spray that is gone in a fifth of a second and slow dark chunks still tumbling most of a second
later, which is what makes it read as a mess left behind rather than as a firework.

AND IT HAS TO HAPPEN WHERE YOU CAN SEE IT. The impact point is a few feet in front of the bumper,
which projects to the very bottom of the screen - BEHIND the player's own car sprite. The first
version was drawn correctly and was almost entirely hidden by the back of the car. It comes up over
the bonnet instead.

TWO NUMBERS, BECAUSE THEY ANSWER DIFFERENT QUESTIONS. `DEER_MASS` is 90kg and decides what the animal
does to the car's MOTION - against fourteen hundred kilos you barely slow and it does not push you
off your line, which is right. `DEER_STRIKE` is 2.5 and decides what it does to the BODYWORK, and it
is deliberately more than the mass suggests: a deer at speed comes over the bonnet rather than being
pushed aside, and takes the front of the car with it.

MEASURED AT FOUR SPEEDS AGAINST A CAR. Nose-on at four fifths of top speed a strike costs 20.3
against 10.2 for clipping a saloon at the same speed - a quarter of an 81-point panel, so four of
them wreck the car and one never ends a run. WITHOUT the strike factor an animal cost 8.1 against
the car's 10.2, which is to say hitting a deer used to be CHEAPER than clipping a saloon.

THE HARNESS HAD TO BE FIXED IN TWO PLACES BEFORE IT COULD MEASURE ANY OF THIS. It pinned the animal
at the player's own z, which is a rub rather than a strike and halved the damage - 12.2 where a
nose-on hit costs 20.3. And it read the particle count 600ms after the impact, by which time the
bright half of the burst is dead: it found 14 of a burst of 40. The count is a peak now, polled
INSIDE the page, because a driver round trip is longer than the effect's bright half.

### And the mess read as nothing, for a reason older than this work

`fx` carries two kinds of thing: a floating MESSAGE, which has a `vy` and drifts upward, and a
PARTICLE, which has both a `vx` and a `vy` and should arc. The driving stepper branched on `f.vy`
when it meant `f.vx` - and a particle has both - so EVERY BURST IN THE GAME TOOK THE MESSAGE PATH:
it moved in `y` only, with no horizontal spread and no gravity. Forty particles thrown in every
direction rose as one vertical column of overlapping dots.

THAT IS WHY THE BLOODY MESS READ AS NOTHING, and it is not the gore's fault. The same fault has been
flattening the orange debris off a car and the yellow off a roadblock since both were written. The
WRECKED stepper has always had it right, which is why a crash watched from a standstill throws debris
properly and one you drive through does not - a difference nobody had put together.

IT WAS FOUND BY TRYING TO PHOTOGRAPH THE EFFECT, and a still is the one thing not delivered here: a
sub-second burst through a driver round trip was missed at every timing tried, and the hit flash
washes the frame red for exactly the window the bright half lives in. What IS evidence is the trace -
40 red at 64ms, 27 at 264ms, 14 at 500ms, 8 at 800ms - and the falsification, which reads 0. THE
EFFECT ITSELF IS THE OWNER'S TO JUDGE ON A DEVICE.
