### RLG-184 — Every vehicle's front and rear agree in both dimensions, with tyres showing

Owner, 2026-09-09, on the STALLION card after [[RLG-182]] matched the two heights: *"Why is the
width cut off in those pictures? You've made them the same height, but now the width is cut off.
And this needs to be across the fleet not just what's visible in the garage so we need to fix the
entire fleet to have equal height and width between the front and the rear. We might as well add
the tires in the same pass."*

**THE CLIPPING WAS CAUSED BY RLG-182 AND IS ALREADY FIXED.** Matching the heights scaled the
shorter end UP — a STALLION's tail by a quarter — and its width grew with it, past the half of the
card it has to fit in. Each end's scale is capped by its own width as well now, so nothing is cut
off. **That fix re-opens the height difference for six cars**, and it should: no arithmetic on the
card can make two drawings of different shapes into the same shape. The fault is in the sprites.

## The fleet, measured

`tools/car-ends-test.py` reports the ink box of both ends of every garage body. **All seventeen
differ**, and they fall into two clean groups with two different causes.

**GROUP A - the heights already agree and the front is NARROWER** (eleven cars):

| car | back | front | dW | dH |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| PICKUP | 202x159 | 190x159 | **-12** | 0 |
| COUPE, MUSCLE, ROADSTER, TUNER | 202-206 wide | 192-196 | **-10** | 0 |
| CAB, CRUISER, SALOON | 196x… | 188x… | **-8** | 0 |
| AMBULANCE, VAN | 196x… | 192x… | **-4** | 0 |

**GROUP B - the front is WIDER and TALLER** (six cars):

| car | back | front | dW | dH |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| STALLION | 220x126 | 230x157 | +10 | **+31** |
| MATADOR | 220x113 | 230x139 | +10 | **+26** |
| APEX, COMET, VECTOR | 220x113 | 230x130 | +10 | **+17** |
| CREST | 220x145 | 230x153 | +10 | +8 |

LORRY sits on its own: 226x241 against 226x246, so the width agrees and the height is out by 5.

## The two causes, and they are not the same

**GROUP B IS THE CANVAS.** The tail is built as `sprite(220, 168, paintCar(...))` and the face as
`sprite(230, 215, paintFront(...))` — **two different boxes**. Group A is built from `rz`, one box
used for both ends, under a comment that says exactly why: *"the SAME box as its tail. A different
one is a different car."* The supercars never got that treatment.

**MEASURED, NOT ASSUMED:** building the supercar face on the tail's 220x168 box makes the **width
agree exactly — 0 for all four** — and moves the height difference to -12, -25, -4 and -4. So
`paintFront` does scale with its box, and the box is the whole of the width story. It is a
one-line change and it is **not enough on its own**, because the heights then disagree the other
way.

**GROUP A IS THE PAINTER.** Both ends already share one box and the heights already agree exactly,
so the 4-to-12 pixel width difference is `paintRigFront` drawing the body narrower than `paintRig`
does inside the same canvas. Nothing about the box will fix it.

## So the work is per painter, and it is not small

Seventeen bodies, two painters each, and the invariant to establish is that a car is the same width
and the same height whichever end you look at — which is true of real cars and is what makes the
garage read as one vehicle rather than two pictures.

**THE ORDER THAT FOLLOWS FROM THE MEASUREMENT:**

    1. Put the supercar and formula faces on the tail's box. One line, and it settles the width
       for six of the seventeen. Re-measure before anything else.

    2. Bring `paintRigFront`'s body width into line with `paintRig`'s. Eleven cars, one painter
       pair, and the heights are already right so nothing else should move.

    3. Then the heights, which after step 1 is the only thing left. Expect this to be the real
       work: it is the drawings disagreeing about how tall a car is, not a number.

    4. And the tyres last, deliberately - see [[RLG-183]]. **The wheels do not exist in the
       sprite at all**, which the silhouette proves: the ink's bottom edge is flat across the
       full width on both ends of every car. Adding them changes the ink height of every sprite
       and therefore every number in the table above, so doing it before steps 1 to 3 means
       measuring twice.

**AND THE OWNER'S QUESTION FROM [[RLG-183]] IS STILL OPEN and gates step 4:** these are billboards
seen straight on, so "a little bit of the tires" is either two slivers at the outer corners or the
body raised to show the lower half of each wheel. They look very different and only one matches
the sprites' own perspective.

**DO NOT START THIS WITHOUT RE-RUNNING THE TABLE.** `tools/car-ends-test.py` prints all seventeen
whether it passes or fails, which is what it is for.

**AND STEP 1 IS NOT A CLEAN WIN ON ITS OWN, which the same experiment shows.** Moving the face
onto the tail's box takes STALLION from 31 pixels out to 4, MATADOR from 26 to 4 and the three
formula cars from 17 to 12 — but it takes **CREST from 8 to 25**, because its face happens to fit
the taller box better than the others do. Four improve, one gets materially worse.

So step 1 must not be taken and left. It settles the WIDTH question for the whole group and it
moves the height question around; the two have to land in the same unit or the garage is worse for
one car than it is today. That is the argument for doing this as one pass across the fleet, which
is what the owner asked for.
- **RULED 2026-09-09T14:46:51-04:00** — ### 2026-09-09 — the invariant is the SILHOUETTE, not the bounding box

**Owner:** *"It's not just the height and the width that need to match, but their silhouettes need
to match too, because thinking about it it's looking at the same vehicle straight on from the front
and straight on from the back."*

**THAT IS A BETTER RULE THAN THE ONE THIS RECORD WAS WRITTEN AGAINST, and it makes the earlier
plan wrong rather than incomplete.** Matching width and height is matching a BOX. Two drawings can
agree on both and still be different shapes, and the measurement below shows that is exactly what
the fleet does. The reason the owner gives is the reason to believe it: a face and a tail are one
car at one distance, so the outline is the same object and only the DETAILS inside it differ —
lamps, glass, grille against plate, a wing if the car has one at the back.

`carProfile` divides each end's ink box into 24 columns and reports the top and bottom of the ink
in each, as a fraction of the box, with the front **mirrored** — a face and a tail are the same car
seen from opposite ends, so the left of one answers to the right of the other. Sizes cancel, so
this compares shape and nothing else.

**THE BIG THREE ARE THE BIG VEHICLES**, and that is a lead rather than a coincidence: LORRY,
AMBULANCE and VAN are the rig-built bodies, where a face and a tail are assembled from parts rather
than drawn as one outline. **CREST passing is the other lead** — whatever that car does, the rest
should do.

## What this changes about the work

**THE FIX IS ONE OUTLINE PER VEHICLE, CONSUMED BY BOTH PAINTERS.** Not two drawings brought into
agreement by adjusting numbers until a checker goes quiet — that is the failure mode this codebase
has already paid for three times in two days, where a second copy of a shape was drawn from guessed
coordinates over one that already knew its own geometry ([[RLG-053]], [[RLG-177]], [[RLG-179]]).
The body outline is declared once; `paintRig`/`paintCar` fill it and add a tail; `paintRigFront`/
`paintFront` fill the same one and add a face.

**THAT ALSO DISSOLVES THE EARLIER PLAN'S STEPS 1 TO 3.** The canvas difference and the width
difference stop being two separate jobs, because one outline drawn into one box cannot disagree
with itself about either. The measured experiment above — that moving the supercar face onto the
tail's box fixes the width and breaks CREST's height — becomes irrelevant rather than wrong.

**AND THE TYRES BELONG TO THE SAME DEFINITION.** Owner, approving the recommendation: two slivers
at the outer corners, not a raised body showing whole wheels. From straight on you see a contact
patch and a sliver of sidewall, not a circle; raising the body reads as a lifted truck and fights
the perspective the sprites are drawn in, and at the size these render on a phone the sliver is the
only version that stays legible. **They go into the shared outline**, so both ends gain them from
one definition and the profiles still agree afterwards.

## The instrument exists now

`tools/car-ends-test.py` prints the box table and the profile table for all seventeen bodies, pass
or fail. **Re-run it rather than re-deriving any of this**, and use CREST as the reference: it is
the one car whose two ends already agree.
