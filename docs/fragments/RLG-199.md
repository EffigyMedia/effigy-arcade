### RLG-199 — A speed pinned four times a second is a range, not a speed, and the interceptor gate sat inside it

Owner report, 2026-09-09: `tools/heat-test.py`'s check *"and one is dispatched once it has been"*
reads 0 supers and fails. Both halves of the mechanism were demonstrably fine — a direct probe saw
interceptors dispatch normally, and the point decay passes its own check in
`tools/heatpoints-test.py` — so the fault was in the harness, and **a green here has to mean the
harness can see an interceptor**, not that the assertion was relaxed until it stopped complaining.

## What was wrong

**The check did not have a speed. It had a range, and the rule's threshold was inside it.**

`hold()` called `API.setSpd` once per 250ms tick and let go. Between two of those pins the car is
left to the world with nothing on the throttle — a harness has no thumb on the pedal — so it sags
on the flat, and a slope or a shove can put it back over. Three runs of the proof read **144-157,
151-158 and 140-167mph from the same 160mph request**, the width and the position of the range
depending on how much wall-clock passed between round-trips.

A super cruiser is gated on `spd > 150mph` held for **four unbroken seconds**, and `fastFor` resets
to zero every time the car crosses under. So the four seconds were banked or not banked by chance:
measured at **3.7 to 11.9 seconds** across runs of the same arm. That is the whole of why the check
read 4 supers on one run and 0 on the next.

**AND IT EXPLAINS WHY LENGTHENING THE HOLD DID NOTHING.** The hold was taken from 9,000ms to
16,000ms and the result did not change. More time buys nothing when the counter keeps going back to
zero; the check was not short of seconds, it was short of control.

## Two corrections this fragment records against itself

**The first account said the car simply ran slow, at 144-157.** That was the first measurement
mistaken for the rule. The next run overshot to 167, and the honest statement is loss of control,
not a shortfall. The proof asserted the wrong thing twice and went red twice — recorded as EVD-613
and EVD-614, kept rather than deleted, because a proof catching its author is the only evidence
that it is doing anything.

The assertions it may **not** make are written into the proof so they are not restored: that the
car always dips under the gate (it does not, on a quiet machine — that reading failed *because the
box was fast*), and that the car never reaches the speed asked for (it overshoots). What is true on
every machine is that the spread is tens of mph wide and the bottom of it eats the headroom.

## And two things still not explained

**The reported zero was never reproduced here.** Five runs before any change all passed, at 4, 1, 3
and 4 supers. What is proved below is a mechanism that produces a zero and accounts for the one
reported. Nobody watched the reported failure happen.

**The frozen point total is still unexplained.** The report was `{heat: 5, pts: 500, cool: 60.01,
chasing: 0, supers: 0}`, and 500 points after sixteen clear seconds should have decayed. The first
answer written down was that accumulated cruisers pinned the cooling clock — the super sections do
run with `clear=False`. **That answer is wrong and is recorded as wrong**, because the same report
says `chasing: 0`, and with nothing on the player `coolT` increments and the decay runs. The report
is from v0.13.27, forty-three versions back. If it is seen again it wants its own unit.

## What was built

**`API.holdSpd(v)`** — the throttle a harness does not have. It pins `spd` every frame, in the one
place `countIn` already pins it, and `null` gives the road back to the physics. It joins `holdNos`,
`holdCurve`, `holdSeaClock` and `holdBlink`, which exist for the same reason. A count-in still wins
over it: a car on the line is not going anywhere, whatever a check asked for.

**The gate is named rather than copied.** `SUPER_MPH` and `SUPER_HOLD` replace the `150/200` and the
`4` written inline in `superWatch`, and `API.pursuit` now reports them alongside `mph` and
`fastFor`. This is the lesson [[RLG-030]]'s cooling check already learnt when `HEAT_COOL` moved from
twelve to thirty and a working engine went red: **a check that copies a tunable measures the number
it was written against, not the rule.**

**And the zero is now the rule's, not the driving's.** The check asserts the precondition before the
result — that `fastFor` really did exceed `SUPER_HOLD` — so *"no super cruiser without the 170 past
a trap"* can no longer pass because the car was too slow to earn one either way. **That check had
been green for months while the car it drove never reliably reached the speed the rule watches**,
and that is worth more than the failing one: a guard that cannot go red is not a guard.

## The proof

`tools/super-gate-proof.py`, recorded green as EVD-615. Three arms:

| arm | how the speed is set | what the car did | banked | supers |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| OLD | `setSpd` every 250ms | 144–169mph, a 25mph range with the gate inside it | 11.12s | 4 |
| NEW | `holdSpd`, every frame | a flat 160mph, spread of zero | 10.93s | 4 |
| SLOW | `holdSpd` at 140mph | a flat 140mph, under the gate on 64 of 64 | 0.00s | **0** |

**SLOW is why the green in NEW is worth anything.** Without it the middle row proves only that an
interceptor turned up; with it, the arrangement is shown still able to report zero when zero is the
truth. The first draft had SLOW reporting **1 super at 140mph** — the arms ran back to back and
`fastFor` is engine state that no arm resets, so SLOW inherited what the fast arm had banked. That
is the falsification arm falsely reporting the rule broken, caught only because it was there. Every
arm now starts below the gate and asserts an empty bank.

`heat-test` banks **17.2 seconds** where it banked 3.7 to 4.8, and reports an interceptor.
Recorded as EVD-616.

**WHAT THIS DOES NOT PROVE.** Nothing here drives past a real speed trap at 170 or wrecks a real
cruiser; both still raise heat by one line that was read rather than driven, which the harness has
said since it was written. And `holdSpd` is a test affordance — it is not how the game reaches
160mph, so no measurement taken through it says anything about whether a car can actually get
there. [[RLG-125]] made the same statement about `setTimed`.

## The store carries a scar, and it will happen again

This work was written **three times**. First as RLG-195 / UNT-241 / EVD-610-611, then as RLG-197 /
UNT-243 / EVD-612 after a session running at the same time in the main checkout took the first set
for the OUTLAW/RACER rename, then as the numbers here after **the same thing happened a second
time** and that session took RLG-197, UNT-243, EVD-612 and v0.13.69 for the LORRY-to-SEMI rename.
Both sessions read "next free" off the same store, so both allocated the same identifiers, and
neither branch could merge without putting two different rulings under one id.

Each rebuild was re-proved against the merged engine rather than trusting the earlier evidence, and
the superseded numberings are kept as the branches `superseded/UNT-241-first-numbering` and
`superseded/UNT-243-second-numbering`. **Two worktrees running at once will do this again.** The
only thing that stopped it here was the owner holding one session still until the other finished.
