### RLG-283 — A place is half as long, so a run crosses twice as many
- **RULED 2026-09-16T22:20:41-04:00** — OWNER, 2026-09-16: "Can we cut the biome distance in half so we see more biomes in a race?" Built, v0.14.69. `placeSpan` rolled 6.5 to 12 miles; it rolls 3.25 to 6 now, and the bounds are `PLACE_MILES` rather than two numbers inside the function.

MEASURED RATHER THAN REASONED, and the measurement is the point. Four sixty-second runs at three-quarters throttle crossed ONE place each on the old figure and TWO each on the new one - every run, both builds. A mile is about 128,700 units, so sixty seconds covers 690,000: under one old place and about one and a half new ones, which is why the count is small and why halving it moves the number at all.

AND IT CORRECTED A FIGURE IN THE ENGINE'S OWN NOTES. The comment beside `TEMP_STEP` claimed a sixty-second run saw "one to three places". The measurement says one, every time. Whatever speed that was taken at, it is not the speed the interstate is driven at, and the note is corrected in place rather than left to be quoted again.

THE 10F STEP BETWEEN NEIGHBOURS IS UNCHANGED, and that is the owner's choice from three offered. The board is a WALK - almost no two places are within 0.077 of each other, so a run travels from cold to hot through the places between - and halving the span does not break the walk, it carries the run further along it. Temperature travelled in a sixty-second run went from 0F, a single place, to between 1F and 9F. Widening the step would have kept the old reach at the cost of the geography; narrowing it would have kept the reach and made neighbours nearly identical.

A PLACE THAT STATES ITS OWN SPAN IS UNTOUCHED. A bridge is 1.7 miles because that is how long the crossing is, not because it is a share of some typical place - `B.span` is an authored length, and halving it would have halved a structure rather than a stretch of country.

THE CHECK ASSERTS THE SPAN, NOT THE COUNT. How many places a run crosses depends on how fast it is driven, so a check that drove for sixty seconds and counted would be measuring the throttle - which is exactly how the figure it replaced went wrong. What is checkable is that the generator's bounds are what the ruling set.

AND THE OWNER ASKED, SEPARATELY, WHETHER THE TEMPERATURE AND PRECIPITATION SYSTEM IS ACTUALLY IN PLACE. IT IS, and it is load-bearing rather than declared. `climateAt` derives, per INSTANCE: the temperature on a -10F to 120F scale; the precipitation; the share of that falling as SNOW rather than rain, which is all snow at freezing and all rain at 0.50 and above; and a SNOW FLOOR from the same temperature, which is why a tundra lies under snow before anything falls and why a cold-rolled city does too. `pickNext` will not let a place be followed by one more than 10F away, and that is a constraint on the INSTANCE ROLL rather than a filter on the pick - filtering would bias the board toward whatever is near and deadlock at the ends of the scale. `API.climateNow` reports it so a check can read it rather than infer it, and biome-test now asserts that a run stands at a real temperature, that its snow and rain are shares of its own precipitation, and that the step is ten degrees.

biome-test and smoke-test pass.

WHAT NO HARNESS CAN JUDGE: whether twice as many places reads as variety or as churn at speed, and whether a place that now lasts about three and a half miles is long enough to feel like somewhere rather than a stripe. `API.placeMiles` reports the bounds and the constant is one line.
