### RLG-141 — drive-test's nitrous check fails at random, and it is the harness rather than the game
- **RULED 2026-09-01T09:25:15-04:00** — FOUND WHILE SHIPPING v0.11.7, and it is NOT a regression from that work - nothing in the
biome or profile changes goes anywhere near the nitrous button.

THE SYMPTOM. `drive-test.py` reports 38 of 39 about one run in three, and the check that
misses is always the same one: "the bottle can be held open for the check", with the detail
"the nitrous button did not take, so nothing below is about the bottle". The harness presses
the button, the game does not see it, and five further checks about the limiter then measure
a car with a shut bottle while claiming to measure an open one.

IT IS THE HARNESS, AND THE HARNESS ALREADY KNOWS. The detail line is written by the check
itself: it was built to say when its own premise failed, which is the right instinct. What it
does with that knowledge is the fault - on some runs it FAILS and on others it passes and
prints the same sentence as a note. Two behaviours for one condition means the gate's exit
code depends on which branch ran, and a green run cannot be distinguished from a run where
the bottle was never opened.

WHY IT MATTERS MORE THAN A FLAKE USUALLY WOULD. This project has already recorded, twice,
that a check which fails at random gets switched off - it is the reason `occlusion-test.py`
prints its cull count instead of asserting on it, and the reason the drive-test speed
threshold was deliberately NOT lowered when it began failing one run in four (RLG-056). A
gate at 38/39 teaches a reader that 38/39 is normal, and the next real defect arrives inside
that noise.

AND THE FIVE CHECKS BELOW IT ARE WORSE THAN THE ONE THAT FAILS. "the bottle is the one thing
that lifts it", "it lifts it by a tenth and no more", "the car settles a tenth above its own
top end while the bottle is open" - all of them report ok on a run where no sample had the
bottle open. They are asserting on a measurement that was never taken.

TWO ANSWERS AND THE OWNER MAY PREFER EITHER. Make the press RELIABLE - find why the input is
dropped, which is a fact about how the harness drives a touch control and may be a real
finding about the control itself. Or make the premise a HARD GATE: if the bottle did not
open, fail once, loudly, and SKIP the five that depend on it rather than reporting them
green. The second is cheap and honest and does not need the first to be understood; the first
is the one that makes the gate mean what it says.

NOT DIAGNOSED. Whether the button is being missed because of timing, because of where the
harness presses, or because the control genuinely ignores a press in some state is unknown,
and guessing at it is what RLG-041 cost eleven versions.
- **RULED 2026-09-10T10:01:26-04:00** — **IT RECURRED ON 2026-09-10, on the CIRCUIT arm and on the settle reading rather than the button.** Three runs of `drive-test` against one unchanged build read 110.0 per cent, 110.0 per cent and 98.0 per cent for "the car settles a tenth above its own top end" - the same check v0.13.73 reworked after it read 58.8 per cent on a circuit. A run of the previous commit passed, which is ONE sample and is not evidence that anything regressed; the variance is in the check. Recorded here rather than acted on, because tuning a threshold is what this ruling exists to warn against.
