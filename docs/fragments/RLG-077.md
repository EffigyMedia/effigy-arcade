### RLG-077 — The title menu shows the wrong tail lights
- **RULED 2026-08-29T10:33:25-04:00** — Owner, 2026-08-29: the title menu shows the wrong tail lights on the car, and it may be that the
title is not using the right renderer. Recorded, not started, at the owner's request.

The owner's guess is worth keeping because it names the likeliest mechanism. The title's attract card
is drawn by the LAUNCHER's `draw` map, which is a small hand-drawn illustration of each cabinet -
not the engine's sprite painters. So a car on the title is a second drawing of a car, and it was
made before RLG-053 turned every lamp into a declaration that the sprite bakes unlit and re-runs lit.
A second drawing cannot drift back into line on its own: it says whatever it was drawn to say.

That gives two candidate fixes, and they are quite different sizes:

  REDRAW THE CARD to match what the fleet now looks like. Cheap, and it goes stale again the next
  time a lamp moves, which is the fault that produced this report.

  DRAW THE CARD FROM THE ENGINE'S OWN SPRITES, the way the garage and the fleet sheet do. Then a lamp
  is described once and every screen agrees for ever. The launcher is deliberately kept ignorant of
  any machine's internals (see the architecture note in CLAUDE.md), so this needs a seam rather than
  a reach-in: the machine hands the launcher a picture, rather than the launcher knowing how to paint
  a car.

The second is the one that matches every other decision in RLG-053, and it is the reason this
fragment says which renderer rather than which pixels.

To confirm before building: check whether the wrong lamps are on the LAUNCHER's attract card, on the
game's own title screen, or on both - they are different code paths, and the report says "title
menu", which could be either.
- **RULED 2026-08-30T10:36:32-04:00** — IT WAS USING THE RIGHT RENDERER AND THEN PAINTING OVER IT. The owner's guess named the launcher's
attract card as the likely culprit, and the fragment's first job was to settle which surface the
report meant. A capture of both answered it in one look: the launcher's card is a 90-pixel thumbnail
where a car is a trapezoid three pixels tall, and the machine's own title screen is a full-height
picture with the ENGINE'S OWN SPRITE in the middle of it. The report is about the title screen.

AND THE CAR THERE HAS ALWAYS BEEN THE RIGHT ONE. `drawTitleArt` draws `SP.player` - the same sprite
the road draws. What was wrong is what came next: two red rectangles at hand-written coordinates
(`cw*0.135` and `cw*0.60`, `ch*0.10` tall) and a halo at two more, painted over a sprite that already
knew where its lamps were, what shape they are and what colour. In the capture the rectangles overhang
the real housings at both ends and the sprite's own amber sections show past them.

THAT IS EXACTLY THE SHAPE RLG-053 EXISTS TO REMOVE, and it survived because of a detail in the
plumbing rather than an oversight: `lampsHere` painted into the module's own `ctx`, which is the road.
The title draws onto a different canvas and there was no way to say so. One argument fixes it -
`lampsInto` takes the context - and now every surface that draws a car can light it from the one
declaration.

THE PULSE STAYS. A car idling on a title card with a dead tail reads as parked, so the lamps still
breathe - but it is the lamp's own brightness pulsing rather than a smudge fading in and out behind
it.

THE CHECK RUNS THE EXPERIMENT BACKWARDS, and that is what makes it worth having. Strip the sprite's
lamp DECLARATION at runtime and look at the title again: if the lamps came from the declaration they
go out, and if the title is painting its own rectangles nothing changes at all. Measured 550 red
pixels lit and 0 stripped. Falsified by putting the rectangles back: 1,253 stripped against 1,331
lit, which is the old build's answer and a clear failure.

AND IT COUNTS RATHER THAN DIFFS. The title car sways, the sun's bands creep and the stars twinkle, so
a frame-to-frame diff is mostly churn - the trap RLG-053 already paid for twice. A count of
strongly-red pixels across the car's own band does not care where in its sway the car is. The band
comes from `API.titleCar`, which reports where the car was actually drawn, because a check cannot
find a moving car by arithmetic.

STILL THERE AND NOT FIXED: the launcher's attract card draws its cars as trapezoids with ORANGE tail
lights. At 90 pixels it is an illustration rather than a picture of a car, and fixing it properly
means the seam the fragment describes - the machine handing the launcher a picture, rather than the
launcher learning to paint a car. That is a real piece of architecture and it is the owner's call
whether a thumbnail is worth it.
