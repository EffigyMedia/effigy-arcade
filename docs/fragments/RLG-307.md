### RLG-307 — A canyon's boundary face does not connect to the walls beside it
- **RULED 2026-09-21T00:16:00-04:00** — OWNER, 2026-09-21, from the device: 'The approaching (and leaving faces) of a canyons look odd because they dont connect with the side faces.'

THIS IS THE DISCONNECTION AGAIN, ON THE PLACE THAT KEPT ITS FACE. [[RLG-303]] recorded the same reading of a MOUNTAIN: 'the wall running alongside and the face at the boundary are separate drawings at different angles and the eye reads them as two things'. The mountain's face was withdrawn; the canyon's was kept because the owner accepted it. The fault was never in which place had one.

WHY THEY CANNOT MEET AS BUILT. `drawEndWall` paints columns across the frame at ONE z - the boundary segment - each a rectangle whose top comes from ridge noise sampled along the LATERAL axis. The wall beside the road is a plane at a height above the road, tiled per slice, whose top edge runs toward the vanishing point. One is a flat card facing the camera and the other is a surface running away from it, so their top edges meet at an angle and their surfaces meet at a corner that nothing draws.

THE SLOT IS ALREADY SHARED AND THE REST IS NOT. RLG-301 made the face's gap `wallFootX` either side, 'so the face and the walls cannot disagree about how wide the way through is'. That is the only quantity the two drawings share. Their HEIGHT at the join, and the corner between them, are computed independently.

FIRST READING, NOT A PLAN: the face's outermost column and the wall's band at the boundary slice are the two edges that have to agree, in height and in shade. RLG-304's method is the one that works on this class of fault - write the expression once and have both read it.

AND IT IS THE LEAVING FACE TOO, which is the mirror's copy of the same drawing. See [[RLG-306]] and [[RLG-312]]: three reports about the same seam.

Not started.
