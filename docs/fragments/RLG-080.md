### RLG-080 — The skylines are one flat silhouette at one depth, and want more detail
- **RULED 2026-08-29T23:40:20-04:00** — Owner, 2026-08-29, while the roadside scenery was being built: "After the scenery session is done, I
think we should make the skylines more detailed."

RECORDED, NOT STARTED, and the order is the owner's own - it follows the scenery.

WHAT THE SKYLINES ARE TODAY, so the next session does not have to find out. `buildSkyline()` generates
one silhouette strip 1024 by 220 per biome and tiles it across the horizon. Every biome shares one
plan structure and differs only in the SHAPE generator:

  CITY      towers, 18 to 54 wide and 30 to 180 tall, with lit windows on a second sheet
  DESERT    mesas, wide and flat-topped, 70 to 190 by 24 to 74, spaced far apart
  MOUNTAIN  peaks, overlapping triangles, 90 to 240 by 70 to 200
  TUNDRA    the same peaks as MOUNTAIN, and NOT yet white - see the tundra ruling above
  FOREST    a treeline of narrow conifers, 12 to 30 by 38 to 96

SO EVERY SILHOUETTE IS ONE FLAT COLOUR - `#150c22` - AT ONE DEPTH. That is where the detail is
missing, and it is worth saying precisely rather than as "more detail":

  ONE LAYER. Real distance has several: ridgelines behind ridgelines, each paler than the one in
  front. The engine now has two skylines drawn at once for the biome crossfade ([[RLG-022]]), so the
  machinery for drawing more than one already exists.

  NO INTERNAL FORM. A tower is a rectangle, a peak is a triangle. No setbacks, no roofline variety,
  no snow line on a mountain, no gaps in a treeline.

  NO LIGHT ON THEM. The silhouette does not take the sky's colour at dusk or the moon at night. The
  note in `drawSky` records that a tint pass was tried and removed because `source-atop` washed the
  sky as well as the buildings - so any second attempt must tint the SPRITE rather than the frame.

  WINDOWS ARE A CITY IDEA ONLY, and the roadside city buildings now being built have to agree with
  them. The owner made that explicit for the scenery: buildings "will need to have their illuminated
  windows for nighttime for consistency with the skyline." Whatever the skyline does, the roadside
  must do the same, or a city reads as two different cities.

AND THE SCENERY WORK CHANGES WHAT THIS COSTS. Once each biome has roadside objects, the skyline is
the same subject at a greater distance. Sharing the generators - a distant conifer being the roadside
conifer drawn smaller and paler - would make the two agree by construction rather than by matching
them by eye. That is a design decision for whoever picks this up, and it is the one that decides
whether this is a small job or a large one.
- **RULED 2026-08-30T01:07:12-04:00** — THE SKYLINES HAVE DEPTH, FORM AND THE HOUR'S LIGHT. All three of the things this fragment named as
missing are built, and the third is the one that had been tried and abandoned once already.

DEPTH IS THREE BANDS IN ONE SPRITE, painted back to front, each mixed toward the sky AT THE HORIZON
by its own amount - 0.70, 0.42, 0.12. The mix is what makes distance read. Three ridges in one colour
is a wall; the same three with a haze gradient between them is a range. The far band is also shorter
and more spread out, because a distant ridge subtends less.

FORM IS THE PART THAT SAYS WHAT A THING IS. A tower was a rectangle and a peak was a triangle. Towers
now carry a setback and, on the wider ones, a mast; peaks carry a snow line and a sunward shoulder;
mesas carry the talus skirt a mesa always has; a treeline has holes punched in it so it is not a comb.
None of that is detail for its own sake - each one is the feature that identifies the object at the
size it is actually drawn.

THE HOUR'S LIGHT WAS TRIED BEFORE AND REMOVED, and the note in `drawSky` records exactly why: a tint
pass with `source-atop` paints over every opaque pixel, and the sky is opaque, so it washed a band
across the sky as well as the buildings. The fix is not a better tint pass. It is to tint the SPRITE
instead of the frame: the colours are mixed in at BUILD time from the same `skyStops()` the windscreen
uses, so the silhouette is already the right colour when it is drawn and nothing passes over the frame
at all.

WHICH MOVED THE PROBLEM INTO THE CACHE, and this is the part that would have been a silent defect. A
sprite whose colour is baked is a sprite of one HOUR as well as of one biome. Keyed by biome alone it
would have taken the colour of whatever moment it was first built at and held it through dusk, sunset
and midnight - and that fails as "the tint does not work", not as "the cache is stale", which is a
much harder thing to find. It is keyed by an hour bucket now, one fortieth of the cycle.

THE CACHE HOLDS ONE BUCKET PER BIOME, NOT ALL OF THEM. Forty buckets across five biomes at 1024x220
is about 180 megabytes of canvas for a horizon. One entry per biome, replaced when the bucket moves.

MEASURED: the rebuild costs nothing visible. About ten rebuilds a minute per visible biome, and all
five biomes hold 60 fps driving at 0.8 of top speed - the same figures as before the change.

SEEN, NOT ASSUMED: city and mountain at midday, city and forest at midnight. The city reads as towers
receding into haze with masts on the near ones; the mountain as a range with snow on the near peaks
and pale ridges behind. At night the silhouette goes to the night sky's own colour rather than staying
a fixed near-black, which is the whole of what the third item asked for.

WHAT THIS FRAGMENT SUGGESTED AND THIS DID NOT DO: sharing the generators with the roadside scenery, so
a distant conifer is the roadside conifer drawn smaller and paler. The two still have separate shape
code. It was not needed to answer the owner's request and it would have been a much larger change; the
suggestion stands for whoever wants the two to agree by construction rather than by eye.
- **RULED 2026-08-30T01:28:55-04:00** — THE ROADSIDE TAKES THE HOUR TOO. Found in the headlight captures: at midnight the mountain's rock
faces were pale while the skyline behind them, which had just learned to follow the light, had gone
dark. The scenery sprites bake fixed colours, so a tree was a daylight tree at every hour.

THE ASYMMETRY IS OLDER THAN THE SKYLINE WORK. It was invisible while both were fixed. Fixing one made
the other obvious - the ordinary way a fix surfaces the next fault, rather than a fault in the fix.

SAME TREATMENT, SAME REASON. The tint is mixed in at BUILD time and the cache is keyed by the hour
bucket, because tinting at draw time means a pass over the frame and four of those have been thrown
out of this engine in two days. On an OFFSCREEN sprite `source-atop` is exactly the right operation -
it paints only where the sprite already has pixels - which is the same operation that was wrong for
the skyline when it was applied to the frame, where the sky is opaque.

AND THEN THE CHECK CAUGHT WHAT THE EYE AND THE PICTURE BOTH MISSED.

First it caught ME. The night capture showed pale rock and I read it as the tint not working. Probing
said otherwise: the sprite's mean was (63,71,91) and the canvas pixels where the "pale" masses sat
were (64,72,93) and (69,78,99) - dark blue-greys. They only LOOKED pale because everything around
them was darker still. A round of investigation went into a fault that did not exist, and the numbers
were what ended it.

Then it caught a real one that the picture could not have. `source-atop` is a MIX toward the colour
given, so the colour decides the DIRECTION: anything darker than the target gets BRIGHTER. The first
tint used a mid blue-grey at luminance 68, and the new check measured a forest tree going from 45.6
by day to 63.5 at night. The night tint was lighting the trees up. No screenshot would have shown it:
against a near-black sky a slightly brighter tree still reads as dark, which is exactly the trap the
first half of this note describes.

THE TARGET IS NOW DARKER THAN ANYTHING IT IS APPLIED TO, so every surface moves the same way. Blue
rather than black, because night is blue and mixing toward black would drain the colour out of a
place instead of putting it under a moon. Measured after: rock 74.3 to 44.1, forest 45.6 to 33.0,
skyline 68.9 to 25.8.

`API.setPhase` EXISTS NOW because the day is 240 seconds long and a check that waited for night would
take two minutes to ask one question. It is the difference between this being tested at all and being
asserted about.

MEASURED: no frame cost. All five biomes hold 58 to 60 fps at 0.8 of top speed, the same band as
before the rebuilds were added.
