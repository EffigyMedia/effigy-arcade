### RLG-116 — Built means implemented and signed off working
- **RULED 2026-08-31T12:15:57-04:00** — Owner, 2026-08-31, answering the question [[AUD-001]]'s first recommendation put: "Built means
implemented and signed off working."

TWO CONDITIONS, AND THE SECOND ONE IS THE OWNER'S ALONE. Implemented is a thing a session can know.
Signed off working is a thing only the person with the device can say. So no session can mark its own
work built, ever - which is the whole force of this ruling and the reason it was worth asking for.

THE RULING STORE ALREADY HAS THE THREE STATES THIS NEEDS, and none had to be invented:

    requested   asked for, and no work has begun
    in-flight   work has begun, and the ruling is not BOTH fully implemented AND signed off
    built       implemented, and the owner has confirmed it works

`in-flight` covers two situations that turn out to be one situation. A ruling whose code shipped this
morning and has not been on a device is in flight. So is a ruling half of which shipped and half of
which has not - [[RLG-059]] has shipped nine times and still has sparse boats in it. Both are "started
and not finished", and the distinction between them is in the prose rather than in the status.

AND THAT ANSWERS THE MULTI-PART QUESTION THE AUDIT ACTUALLY ASKED. A ruling with four parts is built
when all four are implemented and signed off, and in flight before that. There is no partial credit in
the status, because a status that could mean "mostly" would have to be read alongside the prose to be
understood, and then it is the prose doing the work.

WHAT THIS COSTS IMMEDIATELY, AND IT IS BETTER TO SAY IT THAN TO QUIETLY NOT APPLY IT. Everything built
in this session was marked `built` by me under the old, looser reading - implemented and proved by
harness. Under this ruling none of it qualifies: not one has been on the device. Eight fragments move
back to `in-flight` here, and that is the correct record rather than a demotion.

WHAT THIS RULING DOES NOT DO, and the limit is the important part of the record. It cannot reconstruct
the past. There are 115 rulings in the store: 47 marked built, 67 requested, and one in flight. For
anything before this session, whether the owner ever signed it off is a fact that lives in
conversations that are not in the store - so a sweep would be guessing, and a guessed status is worse
than an inconsistent one because it looks settled.

TWO WEAK SIGNALS WERE TRIED AND BOTH WERE REJECTED, which is worth recording so nobody tries them
again. Cross-referencing the changelog finds 36 rulings that are `requested` and mentioned in a
shipped entry - but a changelog entry CITES the ruling it serves, and RLG-037, RLG-029, RLG-030 and
RLG-033 are all cited while the thread lists them as open. And scanning each fragment's own prose for
"BUILT IN 0.x" against "NOT STARTED" resolves 15 of 67 and leaves 52 unclear, because six months of
records do not share one phrasing. Neither signal is strong enough to move a status on.

SO THE RECONCILIATION IS THE OWNER'S, AND IT IS SMALL IF IT IS DONE AS IT GOES. From here every unit
closes its ruling at `in-flight`, the owner moves it to `built` when they have driven it, and the
question never accumulates again. The backlog of 47 already-built rulings is left exactly as it is:
they may well all be right, and nothing in the store can say.

AND THE THREAD IS WHERE THE UNSEEN PILE LIVES, which is what makes this workable rather than
bureaucratic. It already carries a line naming every version the device has not judged. That line and
the `in-flight` status answer the same question from two directions, and the status is the durable
half.
