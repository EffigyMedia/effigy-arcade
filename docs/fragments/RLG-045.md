### RLG-045 — Traffic personality: the limit is 70-80, and some drivers do not keep it
- **RULED 2026-08-28T17:33:46-04:00** — **Owner, 2026-08-28:** "Traffic vehicles should have a bit of personality but for the most part,
let's say 90% of the time they follow the speed limit of 70-80 miles an hour. The occasional traffic
personality will want to speed and that percentage goes up if that traffic personality is driving a
sports car."

**The ruling in numbers:** about 90% of traffic drivers keep a 70-80mph limit. The rest speed. A
driver in a sports car is MORE likely to be one of the speeders - so the tendency belongs to the
driver, and the car raises the odds.

**This is the other half of [[RLG-042]]'s second ruling:** traffic cars have real stats, and the
DRIVER is the limitation. A traffic car is not slow because its `vmax` is small - it is slow because
whoever is driving it is keeping to the limit. That distinction is what makes the speeder
interesting: it is the same car, driven differently.

**What is there now, and it is not this.** Traffic speed is drawn at spawn from a range keyed off
the type string - `rnd(0.26, 0.42) * MAX_SPD`, which is 52-84mph, and `rnd(0.22, 0.28)` for a lorry.
There is already a "rogue" at one in five tuners or muscle cars, cruising 100-124mph (road.js:4060).
**The rogue is the seed of what the owner is asking for and it is in the wrong place:** it is a
property of the SPAWN keyed to the car type, not a driver with a disposition. The owner's version
inverts that - the driver has the disposition, and the car adjusts the odds.

Note the range moves as well: 70-80 is a narrower and higher band than the 52-84 that ships.

**Design it with [[RLG-043]].** If speed comes from a driver keeping a limit, lowering a traffic
car's `vmax` changes nothing you can see until the driver decides to exceed it. The two rulings
have to be built as one design or the stat reduction is invisible.
- **RULED 2026-08-28T20:21:34-04:00** — **CORRECTION from the owner, 2026-08-28.** This fragment recorded the limit as 70-80mph. That is
wrong on both counts and is withdrawn.

> "although the speed limit is 80 miles an hour (the speed in which exceeding draws police
> engagement), most civilians drive between 60 and 80."

| | |
|---|---|
| **the limit** | **80mph** - a single number, not a band |
| **what exceeding it does** | draws police engagement ([[RLG-046]]) |
| **what a Civilian drives** | **60-80** - at or under the limit, never over |

**The difference matters and is not cosmetic.** Under the old reading a car doing 80 was at the top
of a 70-80 band; under this one it is at the limit exactly, and one mile an hour more is an offence.
That gives the police system in [[RLG-046]] a hard threshold to test against instead of a range to
interpret, and it makes a Civilian a driver who is definitionally lawful - so **any car the police
engage is a Speeder or a Racer, by construction** ([[RLG-054]]). That is a much cleaner world model
than 'mostly obeys a band'.

**The 60-80 spread is what gives Civilians their variety**, and it is wide - twenty miles an hour
between the slowest and fastest lawful driver. That is where overtaking between traffic cars comes
from, and it means a Civilian can still be an obstacle without breaking any rule.

**Consequence for the traffic rewrite:** a Civilian's speed is now a property of the DRIVER within
60-80, not of the car. A lorry that cannot reach 80 is limited by its engine; a coupe doing 65 is
limited by the person in it. Both are correct and they are different mechanisms, which is exactly
[[RLG-042]]'s 'the car is capable, the driver decides' - and the first case is why traffic must read
`BODY` before any of this is believable.
