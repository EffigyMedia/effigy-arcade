### RLG-105 — A tunnel, where it is dark by day
- **RULED 2026-08-31T10:50:57-04:00** — Owner, 2026-08-31, answering [[RLG-102]]: canyon and tunnel are the two to build.

NOTHING ON THE BOARD IS DARK BY DAY, AND THAT IS THE WHOLE POINT. Weather and the hour drive the
light, and every place is lit the same way at the same hour. A tunnel breaks that: the sky is gone,
the daylight is gone, and the only light is what the road and the cars bring with them.

IT MAKES WORK THAT IS ALREADY BUILT EARN ITS KEEP. The headlight beams, the tail lamps, every front
and rear sprite's lamp declaration from [[RLG-053]], and the street lighting all exist and currently
only matter after dark. `lampsOn()` is the one clock they follow, and it reads the hour. A tunnel is
the first thing that would make it read the PLACE as well - which is a small change to one function
and a large change to what the game looks like at midday.

SO `lampsOn` IS THE SEAM, AND IT SHOULD STAY ONE FUNCTION. Every lamp in the game asks it. A tunnel
that lit its cars by a second route would be two answers to one question, and this file has the
receipts on what that costs. The place should contribute a darkness the way it contributes rain.

THE HORIZON IS THE HARD PART. A tunnel has no sky, no skyline and no far ground - the vanishing point
is a dark mouth rather than a horizon line, and `drawSky`, `groundBase` and `buildSkyline` all assume
there is something up there. This is the opposite problem to the canyon's and it needs the same
decision made first: what does the far field draw when the place has no distance in it.

AND THE ENTRANCE AND EXIT ARE THE FEATURE. Driving into darkness and back out into daylight is the
moment worth building; a tunnel that is simply a dark corridor for two minutes is a place with one
idea and no event in it. The transition machinery already exists - a biome arrives at the horizon and
drives toward you ([[RLG-022]]) - so the mouth can be the boundary rather than a special case.

A COMFORT NOTE, BECAUSE IT IS THE SAME HAZARD AS THE LIGHTNING. [[RLG-060]] treats the storm flash as a
photosensitivity risk and gives it an option. A tunnel's exit is a large sudden brightening at speed,
repeated every time the place ends. It should be a ramp rather than a cut, and it should be judged on
the device.
- **RULED 2026-09-01T00:37:40-04:00** — BUILT, 0.11.2. A tunnel is on the board and the seam held.

`lampsOn` IS STILL ONE FUNCTION, which was the thing this ruling cared about most. The place is a
third term in the same `Math.max` that already took the hour and the weather - not a second route, not
an override. Measured: at MIDDAY inside a tunnel the lamps read 1.00 while the day clock itself still
reads 0.00, so the sun did not move; at the same hour in farmland they read 0.00; and heavy rain at
midday still lights them, which is what says the place was added as a term rather than as a switch.
The jungle's canopy asks this same question next and now has one answer to inherit.

A ROOF MEANS NO WEATHER AND IT FALLS OUT RATHER THAN BEING RULED. `precip` is 0, so `canRain` and
`canSnow` are both false from the model's own arithmetic. 600 rolls of the engine's own weather
produced nothing at all.

THE FAR FIELD DECISION, which the fragment said had to be made before any art: the sky, the skyline
and the haze are drawn EXACTLY as they always are, and the bore is laid over them. That is why the
mouth costs nothing - the crossing machinery RLG-022 already built is what fades one into the other,
and a tunnel that switched the sky off would have needed its own transition.

AND IT IS A TUBE RATHER THAN A BACKDROP, on the owner's correction. Owner, 2026-09-01: "The tunnel
needs 3D walls and a ceiling above the camera." The first build filled the sky rectangle with a dark
gradient, which is a hole cut in the picture: there was nothing over your head and the eye reads that
at once. The bore is now sampled off the road's OWN projection at sixteen distances, so the walls
converge and bend with the road, and the near samples project above the top of the frame - the vault
closes over the camera. The lamps sit where the wall meets the ceiling and bend with it, because they
are placed off the same points.

AND THE MOUTH IS AN OBJECT. Owner: "Entering it also requires a forward wall or cliff face or mountain
side something." Without one the entrance was a dimmer rather than a place - the whole picture darkened
and there was nothing to drive INTO, which wastes the moment this ruling calls the feature. It is a
hillside standing at the boundary with the bore cut through it, projected like anything else, so it
grows as you close on it and stops existing when you pass. ONE PIECE OF GEOMETRY SERVES BOTH ENDS:
entering, the arch is dark and the rock is in daylight; leaving, the same arch is bright and the rock
is in the bore's light, which is the light at the end of a tunnel.

THREE THINGS WERE WRONG IN THE FIRST BUILD AND EACH WAS FOUND BY LOOKING RATHER THAN BY A CHECK.

  THE PORTAL WAS A CURTAIN. It filled the whole frame above the horizon and cut the arch from that,
  so it painted over the farmland the car was still driving through and showed daylight through the
  hole. A portal is an object at a distance and subtends what it subtends.

  THE BORE WASHED IN BEFORE THE MOUTH. A straight blend across the weather band starts 36 segments
  BEFORE the boundary, so the world dimmed while the car was still in the open. Entering and leaving
  are NOT symmetric and should not be: going in runs over 18 segments once the mouth is reached,
  because a portal is sudden; coming out eases over the full 36 that remain, because the brightening
  is the photosensitivity hazard and the eye adapts to light more slowly than to dark.

  AND THE OPENING WAS A LETTERBOX. 2.05 wide against 0.62 high gave a slot four road-widths across and
  barely one and a quarter tall. It is 1.45 and 1.05 now, measured off the captures.

A LATENT FAULT IN EARLIER WORK CAME OUT OF THIS AND IS FIXED. The darkness faded 1.00, 0.94, 0.85,
0.75, 0.67, 0.57, 0.47 and then CUT to 0.00 in one frame. `biomeFrom` and `biomeTo` collapse when the
GROUND crossing finishes - 18 segments - while everything scoped to the WEATHER band is 72 wide, so
the blend was thrown away three quarters of the way through it. THE SNOW FLOOR HAD THE SAME
DISCONTINUITY and nobody had looked. There is now a separate pair, `wxFrom` and `wxTo`, that collapses
on its own crossing. The fade is smooth end to end: 1.00, 0.94, 0.85, 0.76, 0.68, 0.60, 0.49, 0.39,
0.26, 0.15, 0.04, 0.00.

AND THE HORIZON FORM STOPPED BEING A LIST OF NAMES, which was found by photographing farmland and
seeing a city on it. `skylinePlanFor` branched on `B.name` and fell through to TOWERS for anything it
did not recognise, so both new places were given skyscrapers. A place now STATES its form -
`skyForm` - the way it states its rain: tunnel `none`, farmland and coastal `open`, and a new place
gets what it asked for rather than what the fall-through happens to be.

NOT JUDGED ON A DEVICE. Whether the bore reads as concrete rather than as a dark box, whether the
lamps flick past at a comfortable rate at speed, and above all WHETHER THE EXIT IS COMFORTABLE - the
ramp is about a third of a second at nine tenths of top speed, and a screen is not a harness.
- **RULED 2026-09-01T07:49:48-04:00** — CORRECTED ACROSS SIX ROUNDS OF OWNER FEEDBACK, AND I GOT THE DIAGNOSIS WRONG FOUR TIMES BEFORE
MEASURING. The record of that is worth more than the fix.

THE WALLS WERE NEVER TOO WIDE. I moved `BORE.wide` from 2.05 to 1.45 to 1.12 to 1.03 and sent a
capture each time saying it was tight. The owner said, correctly, that the pictures looked no
different - a 9% change to a number is not a change anybody can see. Then they named the answer:
"tight to the road edge would be where the corn is in the farmland biome."

TWO FAULTS CAME OUT OF MEASURING RATHER THAN LOOKING.

  A UNIT ERROR. The cornfield's `out` is 0.12 in ROADSIDE units, applied through `roadsideAt`, whose
  unit is `SCENE_UNIT`. Mine multiplied `p.w`, the road's HALF-WIDTH, which is an order of magnitude
  larger - so 1.12 stood the wall a twelfth of a carriageway out, the "100 or 200 feet" the owner
  described. The bore uses `roadsideAt` now, so its number is directly comparable with the corn's.

  AND THE DRAW ORDER, WHICH WAS THE REAL ONE. Measured after the unit fix: the wall sat 25.5 pixels
  past a 308.8-pixel half-width at 1,500 units - TIGHTER than the corn's 30.6 - and it still looked
  a hundred feet away. `drawRoad` paints the verge out to the frame edge and it ran AFTER the bore,
  so whatever width the wall had, its foot was painted over and what remained was a band of ground
  between the road and a wall starting somewhere out of sight. THE BORE NOW DRAWS AFTER THE ROAD.
  A wall is nearer than the ground behind it.

AND THEN THE WALL HAD TO COME BACK OUT AGAIN, which is the honest end of the story. Owner: "the car
is clipping through the wall when I pull over to the side. Will there be enough room to put the pick
up crates? Will there be enough room to pass someone on the shoulder?" `playerX` clamps at 1.18 and
the tarmac edge is 1.00, so there is 0.18 of shoulder that is DRIVEN ON. At `out` 0.10 the wall stood
at 1.083 and the car went through it. It is 0.30 now - about 1.25 - clear of the clamp, with crates
untouched at 0.86 to 1.02. "Tight to the road edge" and "do not clip the car" cannot both be taken to
the limit, and the shoulder is what decides it.

THE CEILING WAS A SEPARATE ERROR OF THE SAME KIND. It was placed as an offset from the ROAD's
projected y, which is how a roadside object is placed and not how a roof works - so the whole near
section fell below the frame and nothing converged anywhere the player was looking. A ceiling is a
PLANE ABOVE THE CAMERA and enters the projection as one; it now converges down onto the horizon
exactly as the road converges up onto it.

THE REST OF THE OWNER'S CORRECTIONS, each taken as given:

  A ROAD TUNNEL IS BRIGHTLY LIT. Two reference photographs settled it: pale tiled walls, a lit vault,
  a bright road. I had built a cave. The darkness of a tunnel is the SKY being gone, not the scene
  going dark - the lighting exists precisely so it is not dark in there.

  TWO ROWS OF INTERMITTENT LAMPS, not the continuous runs the photographs also show. The owner has
  seen both and chose the discrete pair, which is also what makes speed read: a strip gives the eye
  nothing to count.

  THE FAR END GOES BLACK. A hard cap at the last sample read as a black rectangle beside the tunnel
  on any climb or bend, so it is a run of quads with a rising alpha - the bore simply darkens until
  there is nothing, and the exit portal draws over it when a mouth is in range.

  AND THE OUTWARD FILL IS A SURFACE, NOT A RECTANGLE. A blanket rect to the frame edge is fine on a
  straight and catastrophic on a bend: the near point swings with the curve and the fill covered half
  the frame as a flat slab. The capture showed it at once.

  BOTH AXES CAPPED AT 0.10. "A bore that climbs and swings reads wrong" - it is cut, not grown.

THE SOUND, WHICH IS THE SAME SHAPE OF PROBLEM. Owner: the engines, brakings, tyre squeals, sirens and
horns want a wash of reverb in a tunnel. EVERY ONE OF THOSE IS A HELD VOICE, built once at the start
of a run - so a per-voice send could not touch them, because driving into a tunnel has to change a
sound that is already playing. There is one send per bus now, starting at zero, and moving that
single parameter moves the whole mix into a space. It is OPT-IN: "not on every sound effect", so a
crate chime and a menu blip stay dry. `placeDark` drives it, so the ears ramp on the same curve the
eye does.

AND ONE THING THAT IS NOT ABOUT TUNNELS AT ALL. Owner: "I want the fall off for each individual car's
engine to be a lot closer to the car so I don't really hear a car engine unless I'm pulled up on it."
It was 36,000 units - over a quarter of a mile - so every car in a stretch longer than the drawn
world was in the mix. 6,000 now, about sixteen car lengths.

NOT JUDGED ON A DEVICE, and after six corrections that is the only thing worth saying about what
remains: whether the bore reads right at speed, whether the lamps flick past comfortably, whether the
reverb is a wash or a swamp, and whether an engine at sixteen car lengths is the right distance.
