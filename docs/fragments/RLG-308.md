### RLG-308 — The coastal biome puts its sea on the other side at random
- **RULED 2026-09-21T00:16:16-04:00** — OWNER, 2026-09-21, from the device: 'For some reason I've seen the coastal biome flip sides randomly.'

FIRST SUSPECT, AND IT IS STRUCTURAL RATHER THAN A GLITCH. `sideRoll` is ONE GLOBAL, and `rollSide()` reassigns it with a fresh coin. The roll is made when a boundary is PLACED, which is at the far end of the drawn road - three hundred segments ahead of the car, and seconds or minutes before the player reaches it. The coast the player is currently driving through reads the same global. So planning the NEXT place can move the sea of the place you are IN, from one frame to the next, with nothing else changing.

THAT IS A PREDICTION FROM A CODE READ AND IT IS NOT MEASURED. It is testable without driving: place a boundary while a coast is on screen and watch `API.sideRoll` against the shoreline the frame actually paints. `API.setSeaSide` and `API.rollSide` exist for exactly this kind of question, and `rollSide`'s own note says a harness must ask the GAME's coin rather than imitate it.

THE SAME SHAPE HAS BEEN FOUND HERE BEFORE. [[RLG-150]] is the ruling that the place the picture is SHOWING and the place the generator is BUILDING FOR are different questions that were once one variable - 'asking biomeEdge at generation time got the answer for the place BEHIND it'. A side that belongs to a place, held in a variable that belongs to the generator, is that fault wearing different clothes.

AND THE HAZARD COIN IS THE SAME SHAPE. `hazardRoll` is a second global rolled on the same occasion, so a MOUNTAIN's cliff could move sides by the same mechanism. Nobody has reported it, which is not evidence that it does not happen - a sea is simply easier to see move than a drop is.

Not started.
- **RULED 2026-09-21T08:34:04-04:00** — BUILT in v0.14.117, and UNSIGNED (RLG-116). The first suspect in this record was right, and it is now measured from the pixels.

IT WAS ONE GLOBAL, AND THE COIN WAS THROWN FOR THE WRONG PLACE. `rollSide()` runs when the next place is PLANNED - at `here + DRAW >= planEdge`, three hundred segments ahead of the car - and its own comment says 'a fresh coin for every new place'. The place the player was still driving through read the same variable. MEASURED FROM THE PAINT: a coast pinned with its water on the left, then the next place planned twenty times over while the car sat in the coast. On v0.14.116 the painted water CHANGED SIDES 13 TIMES IN 20. On v0.14.117, 0 in 20.

EACH END OF THE PAIR KEEPS ITS OWN SIDE NOW. `sideFrom` is the place you are in and `sideTo` the place ahead, with `hazardFrom` and `hazardTo` the same way. A new place rolls only the one it owns; `openBiome`, which has no place behind to protect, rolls both; and the crossing hands `sideTo` over at the same moment `biomeFrom` becomes `biomeTo`. Every reader asks `sideFor(B)` with the place it is drawing - the road pass's shoreline, the far sea band, the horizon's clip, the scenery, the rail through `hazardSide`, and all four of the mirror's. That is how `bioGrow` already tells the two ends apart, and it is fifteen call sites, every one of which already had the place's record in hand.

`hazardRoll` WAS THE SAME FAULT AND NOBODY HAD REPORTED IT. A mountain's cliff was thrown on the same occasion into the same kind of global, so the drop could move to the other side of the road in exactly the same way. It is simply harder to see a drop move than a sea.

THE COIN STILL DOES ITS JOB. Over twenty plans the place ahead was dealt both sides, so holding the current side still was not done by never rolling. And across the line the place you are now in keeps the side it was drawn with on the way in: dealt -1, read -1 after crossing.

AND IT FOUND A SECOND, OLDER FAULT, ON THE SAME VARIABLE. `planDeer` read `if(biome !== 'FOREST' || sideRoll)`, with a note saying the coin 'is zero when there is none'. It was never zero - only ever thrown to -1 or 1 and started at 1 - so every forest was rejected as a forest by the sea, and no deer was planned in a real run from the day [[RLG-152]] shipped. Counted across 3,000 real run openings each: v0.14.116 planned 0 deer in 310 forest openings; v0.14.117 planned 23 in 282, 8.2 per cent against stated odds of 10, and none anywhere else. `deer-test` had passed the whole time because its probe set the coin to zero by hand - a value the game never produces. Whether a place has a sea is a property of the place, so it reads `B.sea` now, and the probe passes a sea as an argument rather than faking a coin.

PROVED BY `tools/side-hold-proof.py`, WHICH IS NEW. It reads which side the water is PAINTED on with `coast-test`'s own classifier, word for word, and it counts the deer through `API.restart` - the path a run actually takes - rather than through the probe that hid the fault. Run against v0.14.116 it fails with the water moving 13 times in 20.

WHAT IS STILL THE OWNER'S: whether a coast that holds its side is noticed at all, which is the point; and now that deer actually run, whether one in ten forests is 'very rarely'. `DEER_ODDS` is the constant.
- **RULED 2026-09-21T09:10:21-04:00** — THE PROOF WAS FRAGILE ONCE AND IT IS RECORDED IN IT. Under `step.py` the first build failed on a correct engine (EVD-1174): the road is generated fresh per load, `holdCurve(0)` only shapes road not yet made, and that load was curved - so the near road's centre sat well off the far vanishing point the water is split at, the coast read as water on BOTH sides, and every clear reading after an unclear baseline was then scored as a move. Two fixes: the road is FLATTENED for the measurement with `flattenRoad`, which replaces the whole table, and a move is only scored away from a side that could be read. Then three passes out of three on this build, and against v0.14.116 the water moves 8 and 11 times in 20 with 0 deer in 296 and 308 forests.

THREE HARNESSES LEANED ON THE DEFECT AND WERE CORRECTED, which is worth stating because it is the reason this fault survived. `hazard-test`, `farmland-test` and `biome-test` each turned the coin by placing a new place and then read the side of the place the player was IN. They passed only because the shared coin overwrote the current place with the new one's side. Each now reads `API.sides().sideTo`, the side the placed place was actually dealt, and each still asks exactly the question it asked: is the side rolled per placement, and are the sea and the cliff two independent coins. `hazard-test` now reads 110 agreements in 200 rolls - two coins - and is back to the four rock-on-a-walled-side failures it has at HEAD.
