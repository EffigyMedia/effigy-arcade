### RLG-279 — A car's collider must be exact enough to meet the edge every time
- **RULED 2026-09-16T11:47:37-04:00** — OWNER, 2026-09-16: "For the cars to consistently hit the edge physics object objects we need their collide to be perfectly correct."

WHAT IS KNOWN, AND ONE PIECE OF IT IS A NUMBER I MOVED. [[RLG-058]] gave every car its own collider derived from the painter, so a roadster and a muscle car are different widths in your hands. `collide-test` proves that by driving two cars together and finding the offset at which the hit starts, then deriving the player's own half-width from the pair.

THAT DERIVED FIGURE NOW READS 0.2774 AGAINST A DRAWN 0.2300, where it read 0.2298 before [[RLG-277]]. The separation resolves overlap at a RATE now rather than instantly, so where two cars come to rest after a staged collision is different and the arithmetic that reads the width off that resting position moved with it. The harness prints that figure rather than asserting it, by its own note - its arithmetic multiplies a shift in one edge by about seven and it is bimodal between runs - and the check that IS asserted, the edge against the half-width sum the engine declares, still passes. So this is not proof of a defect, but it IS the one number in the suite that says the collider and the drawing disagree, and it got worse.

AND collide-test HAS FIVE STANDING FAILURES, pre-existing and confirmed at HEAD: "no hit at zero offset in a roadster" and the same for a muscle car, which take the two per-body measurements with them and then the comparison between them. So the check that exists for exactly this question is HALF DEAD, and has been for some time. THAT IS THE FIRST THING TO FIX - not the collider, the instrument. A collider tuned against a broken measurement is worse than one left alone.

THE EDGE IS A SEPARATE COLLIDER FROM A CAR'S. The car meets the road edge through `edgeX()` and the barrier block, which clamp the CENTRE and know nothing about the painter; the rail is drawn at `railX()`, which is computed from the WIDEST body rather than the one being driven ([[RLG-265]]). So a narrow car stops with daylight between it and the rail while the widest one just touches it. Whether that is the inconsistency the owner is reporting has NOT been measured and must not be assumed - the owner said "edge physics object objects", which may mean the rail, the verge, or both.

WHAT TO DO FIRST, IN ORDER: repair collide-test's five failures so the instrument answers again; then measure, per body, where the car comes to rest against the edge and where its painted flank actually is; then decide whether the edge reads the driven car's width rather than the widest. NOT STARTED.
- **RULED 2026-09-16T12:18:23-04:00** — THE INSTRUMENT IS DIAGNOSED AND NOT YET REPAIRED, v0.14.61. What landed is the measurement that finds it: `API.contactStats()`, which counts the three outcomes of the contact path separately - `seen`, `hushed` and `fired`.

WHY THAT WAS NEEDED. `collide-test` decides whether two cars collided by reading whether the DAMAGE TOTAL rose, so every question it asks about the COLLIDER is answered through the severity model. A staged collision that produced no damage and a staged collision that never happened are the same answer to it, and its five standing failures all read "no hit at zero offset in a <car>" - which names the car, and the car turns out to be innocent.

WHAT IS ACTUALLY HAPPENING, MEASURED. With a car parked exactly on the player - `parkTraffic(0, 0, 'truck')` - `contactState` reports the two bodies overlapping: `within` true, gap 0.0 against a width of 0.2285. And `contactStats` reports `seen: 0`. `seen` increments at the TOP of `contactFires`, before any gate, whenever the bodies touch - so the contact path is NEVER REACHED for that car. It is not the mercy window, which was ruled out by clearing `iframe` every 4 ms and getting the same zeroes; it is not the count-in, which `startLine` shows finished at `left 0, go -0.008` with the clock still running down; and it is not the body, because the DEFAULT car fails it exactly as a roadster does. All four bodies tried read `fired 0, hushed 0, seen 0`.

AND THE DRIVEN PATH IS FINE, which is what localises it. `contact-test` drives into a car parked 3,000 units ahead and gets its hit every run - 132 frames nose-to-tail, speed 6,893 to 0. So what is broken is the STAGING at dz = 0, not the collider and not the collision code. The next session should look at what the traffic loop does with a car whose `z` is the player's own `z` on the frame it is inserted, between the loop head and the contact block - there are four `continue`s in that stretch and one of them is the suspect.

THE SEVERITY MODEL WAS SUSPECTED AND CLEARED. A dead-flank contact at zero closing speed looked like it might be worth nothing, which would have explained a silent hit - but `IMPACT.side` is 0.55 and `force` floors at 0.30, so such a hit is worth about two points of damage. It would have shown.

NOTHING ABOUT THE COLLIDER ITSELF IS ANSWERED YET, and that is the honest state. The owner's report - that cars must hit the edge consistently - still needs the per-body measurement that `collide-test` cannot currently take.

ONE THING WAS FIXED IN PASSING. `contact-test`'s lean check required more than 20 frames of contact and failed a run at exactly 20. A lean that fails to make contact at all reads 0 to 5 frames and one that holds reads 20 to 46, so the bar is 12 now - in the gap rather than on the edge of the working band. That is the same mistake as a threshold drawn on the noise, made from the other side.
