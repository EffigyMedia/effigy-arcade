### RLG-167 — Clouds read as clouds: fluffy and white when clear, low and grey in snow, dark in a storm

Owner, 2026-09-07, adding to the aurora item: "stars are already in, but clouds don't read
as clouds - fluffy white in clear skies, light snow storms, and dark thunder storms."

THE STARS ARE DONE AND THE CLOUDS ARE NOT, which is why this is its own ruling rather
than a line on RLG-151. Cloud cover exists as a NUMBER - `cloud` and `storm` are rolled per
place and read by the sky, the light and the weather - and what is missing is a FORM. A
sky that is more overcast currently reads as a flatter colour rather than as more cloud.

THREE STATES THE OWNER NAMED, and they are three appearances of one thing rather than
three effects:

    clear        fluffy and white, the shapes you look up at on a good day
    light snow   low and soft, the flat pale lid a snow sky has
    thunder      dark and heavy

Pairs with RLG-151, which is the aurora and the night sky, and with RLG-060, which holds
the lightning the dark version implies. It is the LOOK of the sky; the grip and the
visibility that go with the weather are already built and are not in scope here.
- **RULED 2026-09-08T05:04:02-04:00** — BUILT 2026-09-08, v0.13.35. The cover had a NUMBER and no FORM, which is the owner's sentence
turned into a diagnosis: a sky with more cloud in it read as a flatter colour rather than as more
cloud. What stood in `drawSky` was a full-sky wash plus five soft ellipses.

THE THREE THE OWNER NAMED ARE ONE THING SEEN THREE WAYS, not three effects, so they are one
painter driven by two values that already existed - `storm`, which is how black it is, and `snowy`,
which says the precipitation is snow. Between them a cloud is placed on two axes, how HIGH it piles
and how DARK it is: clear is piled and white, snow is a low flat pale lid, thunder is dark and
heavy sitting on top of you.

A CUMULUS IS A CLUSTER, NOT AN ELLIPSE. One ellipse is a smudge at any size; four overlapping
circles along a flat base is a cloud at any size. The base is drawn darker than the top because a
cloud is lit from above, and the flat grey underside is the part the eye actually names.

THE WASH DID NOT GO AWAY, it stopped being the whole story - it is squared against the cover now,
so a light day is shapes on open sky rather than a grey film with shapes on it. The old version was
linear, so the lightest sky a place ever rolls still had a film over it and there was no such thing
as a clear day.

AND THEY WERE TOO BIG AT FIRST. A fifth of the screen a cloud, a handful of them, and one bank
filled a third of the sky - which reads as something near the car rather than as weather miles
away. Distance is what makes a cloud a cloud, so they are smaller and there are more of them.

### Three statistics, and the first two measured something else

A visual claim needs a number that a repaint cannot satisfy, and this took three attempts:

CONTRAST ACROSS A ROW - brightest minus darkest - scored an EMPTY sky at 85, because the horizon
glow is a RADIAL gradient and is therefore bright in the middle of every row and dark at both ends.

NEIGHBOUR TO NEIGHBOUR scored an empty sky at 1.39 and an overcast one at 1.43, because the SUN is
a small bright disc with hard edges and dominates that number at midday.

WHAT WORKS IS COMPARING TWO SKIES. The sun, the bloom and the day gradient are identical in both
and subtract away exactly. A wash leaves a difference that is the same everywhere ALONG A ROW;
cloud leaves one that is large where a cloud is and nothing where there is sky. The statistic is
the spread of that difference within each row against that row's own average - and the row is the
whole point, because the wash is a VERTICAL gradient and so is patchy over the picture while being
flat across any line of it. Measured over the picture instead, a build with the shapes deleted
still scored 0.82 to 1.10 and passed.

FALSIFIED: with the shapes removed and only the wash left, the three patchiness lines read 0.30 to
0.39 and go red, against 0.61 to 0.95 with them.

AND THE SKY HAD TO BE PINNED. The engine re-rolls the cover on its own clock every twenty-five to
seventy seconds, so a set of captures taken over a minute had the sky change underneath it - the
overcast reading came back 1.25 on one run and 0.44 on the next, which reads as a broken painter
and was a sky that had moved on.

NOT JUDGED ON A DEVICE. Whether a fair sky has too many clouds or too few, whether the snow lid
reads as a lid or as a row of bubbles, and whether the thunder sky is too dark to see the road are
all the owner's. tools/cloud-test.py --shots writes the five skies.
