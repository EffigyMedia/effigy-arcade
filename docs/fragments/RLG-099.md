### RLG-099 — The tyres screech when the car slides in a turn
- **RULED 2026-08-31T08:35:31-04:00** — Owner, 2026-08-31: "I also feel like the tire should screech if the car is slid across the ground
because of the centripetal forces of a turn."

IT IS THE SAME SOUND WITH A SECOND CAUSE, WHICH IS WHY IT SITS BESIDE RLG-098 AND NOT INSIDE IT. A
tyre squeals when rubber slides over tarmac. Braking makes it slide along the car's length; cornering
makes it slide across. The engine already knows how hard the car is cornering and already knows how
much grip it has, so the quantity exists - what is missing is that only one of the two ways of
exceeding it makes a noise.

DO THEM IN ORDER, AND THE ORDER IS NOT NEGOTIABLE. RLG-098 fixes what drives the squeal's PITCH.
Adding a second trigger first would mean tuning a cornering squeal against a pitch that is about to
change, and the tuning would have to be thrown away.

WHAT IT SHOULD BE DRIVEN BY. The lateral slip - how far the car is travelling sideways relative to
where it is pointing - against the grip available, which `wetGrip()` already reduces in rain and
snow. That gives the behaviour a driver expects for nothing: the same corner squeals earlier on a wet
road, and a car with more grip stays quiet longer. A branch that names a vehicle class to decide
whether it squeals would be exactly the thing three separate owner rulings have already forbidden -
the car is capable, the driver decides.

AND IT MUST NOT SQUEAL AT EVERY CORNER. The sound means "you are at the limit". If it plays whenever
the wheel is turned it stops carrying information and becomes noise, which is the same failure the
indicator work was warned about. There has to be a threshold, and it should be the grip limit rather
than a steering angle.

THE HARNESS CANNOT STEER TO THE LIMIT WITHOUT LYING ABOUT IT. `drive-test`'s autopilot saws at the
wheel - this project has already been told once that Raceway's tyres died in twenty seconds and the
autopilot was the cause. A check for this needs a steady, deliberate cornering input, not the
autopilot, and it should read the slip the engine computes rather than infer it from the sound.
- **RULED 2026-09-19T08:21:12-04:00** — HALF BUILT, checked 2026-09-19 (the owner thought it might be done). The squeal is fed max(braking decel, scrubOf x 0.9), and scrubOf measures how fast the car moves ACROSS the road - so a hard snatch at the wheel at speed squeals and lays rubber. But the corner's own load never reaches the sound: the curve pushes the car outward (pushK x v^2 x cornerG), and a driver holding the line through a fast corner is fighting that load while barely moving sideways, so the tyres stay silent. What is missing is the ruling's own quantity - the lateral load against the grip available (wetGrip) - driving the squeal.
- **RULED 2026-09-19T09:08:04-04:00** — BUILT, v0.14.84. A loaded corner now squeals and lays light rubber, not only a hard steer.

THE QUANTITY IS THE RULING'S OWN: the corner's pull against the grip. `cornerLoad` is the corner's push on the car (|pushK| x v^2 x cornerG, and `cornerG` already divides by the car's grip and by the wet) against how fast THIS car can steer. Near one, the corner pulls about as hard as the car can answer: the tyres are at their limit. Measured on a HATCH: under 0.06 for a gentle bend at cruise, about 0.5 for the hardest bends at a production car's top speed. `CORNER_SQUEAL` starts the squeal at 0.25 and makes it full at 0.60, so ordinary bends stay quiet. It feeds the same squeal voices as braking and a snatch at the wheel, and lays rubber at 0.6 of its strength.

PROVEN by `tools/squeal-test.py`, which reads the squeal voice's own gain rather than the load. The car is HELD ON ITS LINE, as a driver countering the corner would be - left free, the corner carries it sideways and the older squeal fires on its own, which made the first version of the check vacuous (with the new path cut, the hardest bend still squealed at 0.0293). Held: a straight road at speed and a gentle bend are silent; the hardest bend at speed squeals (0.0595); a wet road loads the tyres harder than a dry one (0.287 against 0.202). With the corner load cut from the sound, the hardest bend reads 0.0000 and the check fails.

FOR THE DEVICE: whether it sounds right, and whether 0.25 brings it in at the right corner. Both thresholds are tunables.
