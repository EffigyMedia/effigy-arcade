### RLG-270 — Each police car sounds its own siren, out of step with the others
- **RULED 2026-09-16T01:00:55-04:00** — OWNER, 2026-09-16: "I don't think I've heard multiple sirens moving out of sync realistically when there's more than one cop in your proximity."

CONFIRMED BY READING, NOT BY LISTENING - a sound test cannot hear, but it can read the graph (this project's own rule). THERE IS ONE SIREN VOICE IN THE ENGINE. `snd.siren` is a single held oscillator built once in `snd.arm`, and the frame loop reduces every police car to ONE number: `near` is the loudest - that is, the nearest - and that number drives the one voice. So two cruisers alongside each other cannot be out of step, because only one siren is ever sounding. The same is true of the lights in the other direction: `sirenPhase` is one clock, and each car adds its own `k.phase` to it, so the BARS are already out of step while the sound is not.

WHAT IT WOULD TAKE. A voice per car, held on the car (the traffic engine loop already does this - `snd.traffic` takes a list), each with its own phase, panned by the car's lateral position and gained by its distance. The ambulance keeps its own scheme (RLG-065 gave it a different voice), and the mix has to stay under control when four cars are on the player: the existing loop takes a maximum precisely so that three cruisers are not three times as loud.

NOT ASKED YET: how many voices are worth spending - one per chasing car, or a small pool of two or three with the rest folded into the nearest - and whether a siren should pan with the car across the screen.
- **RULED 2026-09-16T23:01:02-04:00** — BUILT, v0.14.71. Each police car and ambulance near you now sounds its own siren, with its own phase, its own slight rate and its own place across the stereo.

THERE WAS ONE SIREN IN THE ENGINE, which is exactly what the ruling said after reading the code. The frame loop reduced every police car to ONE number - the loudest, which is the nearest - and that number drove one held oscillator. Two cruisers alongside each other could not be out of step because only one siren was ever sounding. The LIGHTS already were, and that is the shape the sound borrowed: `sirenPhase` is one clock and each car adds its own `k.phase` to it.

THE POOL IS THREE, and the ruling left the count open between "one per chasing car, or a small pool of two or three with the rest folded into the nearest". Three is the smaller answer for the reason the ruling itself gives: the mix has to stay under control when four cars are on the player, and the loop this replaces took a MAXIMUM precisely so that three cruisers were not three times as loud. Three is enough for the ear to hear that they disagree; a fourth adds loudness rather than information. And [[RLG-002]] makes this a phone game, so a held oscillator per police car is a cost that grows with the one thing the player does not control - how many turned up. Anything past the third is folded into the nearest, exactly as the ruling offered.

THE MIX IS HELD DOWN DELIBERATELY: the nearest voice keeps the old level and the rest are quieter shares of it rather than additions to it.

`snd.siren` STAYS AND IS NOW THE PLAYER'S OWN BAR. It is driven by `barOn` and belongs to the car you are in, so it has no position to be panned to - and sounding the world's sirens through it as well would have doubled every one of them.

THE PHASE BELONGS TO THE CAR, NOT TO THE VOICE, AND THE FIRST BUILD GOT THAT WRONG. Three voices were built and each kept its own phase counter - and two cruisers read 4.497 and 4.497, because two counters started together and advanced at the same rate never diverge. That is the defect the owner reported, rebuilt with more machinery behind it. The phase is seeded once on the car the first time it is heard, and written back each frame, so it survives a car moving between voice slots.

AND EACH CAR RUNS AT ITS OWN SLIGHT RATE. A fixed offset is still lock step - two sirens a beat apart for ever. `sirenWob` is a small permanent multiplier per car, so a pair wanders in and out of phase the way two real units do.

MEASURED BY READING THE GRAPH, WHICH IS THIS PROJECT'S OWN RULE - a sound test cannot listen. Two cruisers staged either side of the player read phases 3.520 and 2.988, 0.531 apart, panned to -0.39 and 0.49. NOT VACUOUS, watched failing with one clock restored: both read 0.760.

siren-test, audio-test and smoke-test pass.

WHAT NO HARNESS CAN JUDGE: whether three sirens together are a wall of noise or a pursuit, whether the drift rate reads as two units or as one unit wobbling, and whether panning a siren across the ears helps you find the car or just moves the noise about. `SIREN_VOICES` is one number and the wobble range is one line.

STILL NOT ASKED: the ruling's second open question, whether a siren should pan with the car, was answered in the build rather than by the owner - it does. It is the half of "out of sync" that the ear uses to tell two cars apart, and it is trivially removed if the owner does not want it.
