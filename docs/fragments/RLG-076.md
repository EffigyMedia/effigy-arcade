### RLG-076 — Damage should be the magnitude of the impact
- **RULED 2026-08-29T10:33:07-04:00** — Owner, 2026-08-29: the damage taken from hitting a vehicle should be based on the total magnitude of
the impact.

It is not today. A collision costs a fixed amount with only coarse variation, so brushing a van at a
few miles an hour of closing speed and spearing a lorry at 200 are close to the same event. That is
the wrong shape for a game where speed is the whole currency: the risk of a pass should scale with
how fast you commit to it.

MAGNITUDE MEANS RELATIVE VELOCITY AND MASS, not the player's speedometer. Two cars travelling at 190
alongside each other and touching is a small impact; a stationary car struck at 60 is a large one.
`BODY` already carries `mass` for every vehicle - 690kg for a formula car to 14,000 for a lorry - and
the traffic and the racers both carry a speed, so both terms exist and neither has to be invented.

Three things to decide when this is built, and they are the whole of the design:

  THE CURVE. Kinetic energy goes with velocity squared, which is realistic and probably brutal: a
  180mph shunt would end the run outright. The house rule is "mostly realistic, biased toward fun",
  so the proposal should name what it picks and why.

  WHOSE MASS. A lorry weighs twenty times a formula car. Straight mass ratio would make a lorry
  unhittable at any speed and a formula car made of glass, which may be exactly right or may be the
  end of the game - it needs a number, not a principle.

  THE FLOOR AND THE CEILING. A touch at walking pace should cost something visible or players learn
  that contact is free; a maximum-magnitude hit should probably not be survivable, which makes the
  wreck a consequence of a decision rather than of bad luck.

Recorded, not started. It should be measured the way RLG-055's braking was: a harness that stages
impacts at known closing speeds and reads the damage, watched failing on a build where damage is
flat.
