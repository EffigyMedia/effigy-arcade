### RLG-169 — Incidents are worth less, and cooling is slower by the same measure

Owner, 2026-09-08: incidents should be worth FEWER points, so the stars climb more slowly
and a hot pursuit lasts longer. At the rates this shipped with, two sightings made a star
and the fifth star was inside one bad minute.

**The rates fall to roughly two fifths.** Being seen by a new cop goes from 50 to **20**,
running a roadblock from 70 to **30**, and putting a cruiser out from 80 to **35**. A star
is still 100 points, so a star is now **five sightings** instead of two, and the three
events keep their order: being seen is the common one, taking a cruiser out is the
deliberate one, and a roadblock sits between them.

**THE DECAY HAD TO MOVE WITH THEM, and the owner said so before the work started.** Cutting
the earners alone would have been worse than leaving them: at the old decay of 8.33 points
a second, a 20-point sighting is undone in under three seconds of clean road, and the level
would never climb at all.

**THE LEVER THAT MOVED IS `HEAT_COOL`, NOT `PTS_PER_STAR`.** A star is the unit the meter is
drawn in and the unit the API reports, so rescaling it would move every rate against
nothing and change no ratio in the game. `HEAT_COOL` is stated in SECONDS TO SHED A STAR,
which is the exact quantity the owner asked to lengthen. It goes from **12 to 30**, so the
decay falls from 8.33 to 3.33 points a second and five stars take two and a half minutes of
clean road to shed instead of one.

**WHAT THAT PRESERVES IS WHAT AN INCIDENT IS WORTH IN SECONDS OF RUNNING.** A sighting cost
6.0 seconds of cooling before and costs 6.0 now; a roadblock 8.4 against 9.0; a takedown 9.6
against 10.5. The economy keeps its shape and only its grain changed, which is the test of a
retune rather than a re-balance. What did change is the count: two incidents to a star
became five.

**Nothing cools during a pursuit**, and that is worth stating because it decides how the
numbers are read. The cooling clock is reset on any frame a live cruiser is on the player
and inside `LOST_AT`, so the decay is the price of a CLEAN road rather than a clock running
against the player while the chase is on.

### The harnesses were sized against the old numbers, and two of them broke

**`heatpoints-test.py` DID NOT NEED ITS RATES EDITED, and that was true.** It reads them off
the engine through `heatPoints()`. What it did hardcode was the WAIT: 210 quarter-seconds,
sized against a decay of 8.33 a second in its own comment. That is a second dependency on
the same tunable and nobody had noticed it. The budget is now computed - points on the clock
divided by the rate they leave at, plus the grace, plus half again for slack.

**AND BOTH HARNESSES WERE KILLED BY THE RUN CLOCK, which is the finding worth carrying
forward.** A run starts with sixty seconds, and a car parked for a measurement reaches no
checkpoint and buys no more. When the clock expires the update stops, and the point total
and the cooling clock FREEZE exactly where they stood. Traced in `heatpoints-test`: 350
points fell to 162 and then sat unchanged for a further two minutes while the harness kept
polling. It read exactly like a tunable that had stopped working, and the same run reported
an identical 162 twice, which is what said the cause was deterministic rather than a race.

`API.setTimed(false)` is the affordance for it and already existed for [[RLG-125]]. With the
clock off, `heatpoints-test` sheds 350 points in 107.6 seconds against a predicted 105 plus
the 3-second grace - which is the tunable measured doing exactly what it says.

**`heat-test.py` HAD THE SAME TWO FAULTS.** Its cooling waits were flat numbers written
against a twelve-second star, so the retune turned a working engine into two red checks;
they now read `coolNeeds` off `pursuit()` and derive the wait. And its super-cruiser check
was ALREADY FAILING before this work - measured on the pre-retune engine to be sure - for
the same run-clock reason. Turning the clock off fixed a defect this unit did not set out to
fix and did not cause.

**A LEAKY CLEAN ROAD WAS SUSPECTED FIRST AND WAS NOT THE CAUSE.** Clearing cops from the
driver every 250 ms leaves each newly dispatched cruiser alive for a frame, and each one
costs the whole grace - so `heatpoints-test` now sweeps from inside the page every 4 ms,
faster than the frame it has to beat. That change alone moved nothing, which is how the run
clock was found: the second run returned the identical 162.
