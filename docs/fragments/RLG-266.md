### RLG-266 — The reward splits into three pickups: repair, NOS and fuel
- **RULED 2026-09-15T23:47:46-04:00** — OWNER, 2026-09-15: "I'd also like to split the rewards into their three pick ups, which means we need to design the look of the three pick ups. Remember if a pick up is not needed for a mode, we just omit it from being spawned."

WHAT ONE CRATE PAYS TODAY (road.js near 19855): 25 of damage repaired, 25 of NOS, and CRATE_SECS on the clock when `clockRuns()`. One box pays all three, and the line that announces it reads "+n HEALTH +n NOS +n SECONDS" (RLG-126). RLG-107 already ruled that a reward paid in a currency the mode does not use is an invisible reward, which is why the seconds are gated on the clock; splitting the box carries that rule into the SPAWNER instead - a pickup a mode has no use for is never laid.

THE THREE LOOKS, the owner's own after a proposal: a RED TOOLBOX with a silver handle and a silver latch for the repair; a BLUE NOS BOTTLE lying on its side with a metal collar and a valve, as proposed; and a RED JERRY CAN for the third, because "time is represented by fuel".

TWO SILHOUETTES ARE RED, which is deliberate and is the owner's choice - the toolbox and the can are different shapes and that is what has to carry at distance on a phone.

NOT SETTLED YET: which pickup each mode spawns; whether the amounts stay at 25, 25 and CRATE_SECS; and whether fuel pays the clock, the fuel gauge, or both.
- **RULED 2026-09-16T20:31:28-04:00** — BUILT, v0.14.67. Three pickups, one currency each, and the road lays only the ones whose currency is live.

THE THREE LOOKS ARE THE OWNER'S OWN and the silhouettes carry before the colours do. A RED TOOLBOX: squat, wider than tall, with a silver handle standing clear above it and a silver latch on the front - the handle is what makes it a toolbox rather than a crate, so it is painted FIRST and the box over its feet. A BLUE NOS BOTTLE on its side: a cylinder with a lighter top, a darker underside so it reads as round, a metal collar and a valve off the end. A RED JERRY CAN: taller, flat-sided, with the spout at one shoulder, a handle across the top and the pressed X on its face in shadow rather than in a second colour. TWO ARE RED BY THE OWNER'S CHOICE, so the outlines do the work - a squat box with a handle over it against a tall slab with a spout, which is the same rule the fleet's own bodies follow.

THE SPAWN RULE IS DERIVED, NOT TABULATED. Owner, 2026-09-15: "if a pick up is not needed for a mode, we just omit it from being spawned", and 2026-09-16: omit the ones irrelevant to the settings currently engaged. Each kind states when its currency is worth something - repair always, nos only if the car HAS a bottle, fuel only when `clockRuns()` - and the spawner asks. That is [[RLG-107]]'s rule moved from the AWARD, where it could only stop a pickup paying nothing, to the SPAWNER, where it stops the pickup being laid. A mode added later inherits it instead of needing a row, and `live` is asked PER SPAWN because a player changes car between runs and the clock is switched from the garage.

NOS WAS ALREADY WRONG BEFORE THE SPLIT. Half the fleet carries no bottle - a production car has none by ruling - and the single crate offered them a top-up they could not take, which is the hole `awardNos` returning 0 was papering over.

EACH KIND KEEPS ITS OWN TIMER at the interval one crate ran at, which is the owner's choice over a sparser road: three pickups on their own clocks pay about what one crate paid to a driver who takes all three, and the CHOICE of which to swerve for is the new play. One shared timer would have cut the reward to a third. The first of each is staggered at 16, 22 and 28 seconds so they do not all arrive together, and a kind that is not live still RE-ARMS its clock rather than holding it - a bottle won mid-session should not be met by a pickup that was due twenty seconds ago.

THE AMOUNTS ARE UNCHANGED at 25, 25 and `CRATE_SECS`.

MEASURED, over three combinations: a car with a bottle and the clock on lays all three; a car with NO bottle lays repair and fuel and never nos; a car with a bottle and the clock OFF lays repair and nos and never fuel. NOT VACUOUS, watched failing with the live gate removed - nos laid twice for a car that cannot use it, fuel laid with no clock to pay.

AND THE CHECK ASKS WHETHER A KIND WAS REFUSED OR NEVER ASKED, which is the distinction that makes it worth having: a pickup correctly omitted and a pickup whose timer is stuck look identical from outside, so a kind that IS live must actually appear.

WHAT PICKUP-TEST DOES NOT ASSERT, and why it is written up rather than left blank. Staging a pickup on the car to read what it PAYS defeated six configurations: a rival takes pickups too and the crate vanishes looking unpaid; clearing the field every frame is a race-over condition that stops the update; pinning the lane made every attempt miss; `setClock` and `restart` each broke runs that had been working. The one configuration that worked reached one kind in three. What it would have proved is a three-branch `if`, and it was measured by hand once - a nos pickup paid +27 nos, +0 health and no clock. The branch is read from the code more reliably than a moving car is steered over a box.

CRATE-TEST ENCODED THE DESIGN THIS REPLACES and was brought forward rather than left red. It asserted that a car with nothing to gain is PAID IN SECONDS INSTEAD, and that a repair pays seconds AS WELL "because every award is its own" - both descriptions of one box paying three currencies with a fallback. Those are deleted, because the situation they describe no longer arises: the spawner does not lay a pickup whose currency is dead. Its remaining checks now name the KIND they need, and the two gauge checks are taken in turn, because one pickup cannot light both the clock and the bottle any more.

pickup-test, crate-test and smoke-test pass; drive-test is 36/37 on the known environmental settle line.

WHAT NO HARNESS CAN JUDGE: whether a toolbox reads as a toolbox at speed on a phone, whether two red silhouettes are distinguishable at distance, and whether three pickups on their own timers read as more road furniture than one crate did.
