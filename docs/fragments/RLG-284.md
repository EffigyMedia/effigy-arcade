### RLG-284 — The mountain cliff edge is jagged, not a straight line
- **RULED 2026-09-16T23:56:33-04:00** — TRACKED, NOT STARTED. Owner, 2026-09-16: "The cliff edge in the mountain biome should be jagged a non-uniform."

WHAT IT IS ABOUT. The mountain biome draws a cliff, and its edge is a single clean line. A cliff is rock that has broken away, so a straight edge reads as a wall rather than as stone - the eye takes uniformity as manufactured.

WHAT TO LOOK AT FIRST: the mountain entry in `SCENERY` in `road.js` and whatever paints the cliff face. The shape of the fix is the same one the coast already uses for its shoreline - a per-segment offset that is deterministic from the segment index rather than random per frame, because a cliff that reshuffles as you drive is worse than a straight one. `coast-test.py` measured the shoreline's largest step against its mean and is the model for measuring this.

AND IT IS A LOOK, SO THE OWNER JUDGES IT ON THE DEVICE. A harness can say the edge is not a straight line and cannot say whether it reads as rock.
- **RULED 2026-09-17T07:57:02-04:00** — BUILT, v0.14.77. The cliff rim no longer runs at exactly `railX()`. `rimX(p, seg, side)` puts it at `railX() + rimJag(seg)`, and every place that draws the rim uses that one function: the windscreen's ground cut, the face, the lit lip, and the mirror.

`rimJag` is `sceneRand` of the ABSOLUTE segment, so the rim is fixed to the road and does not shimmer. A slice uses its own segment's value at its near end and the next segment's at its far end, so the slices meet in one unbroken zig-zag. It is non-uniform in two ways: every segment has a small jag up to `amp` (0.16 road widths), and stretches of `run` (6) segments roll once, with `odds` 0.30, for a deeper bite of up to `bite` (0.42) on each segment. The jag only goes outward, so the drop never comes under the rail.

PROVEN by `tools/cliff-test.py`. The numbers: over 600 segments the rim is never inside the rail, has a spread of 0.118, and has both deeply bitten and only rough stretches (42 and 56 of 100), and asking twice returns the same values. The picture: the same mountain rendered straight and jagged differs by 358-660 pixels on the drop side for each side of the road, and by 33-56 pixels in the mirror. Two straight renders differ by 0. Falsified by making `rimX` ignore the jag: all six render checks failed. The first version of the mirror check asked for more than zero pixels and passed under that defect, because the small pane moves 4-6 pixels on its own. Its bar is now 18, set from those measurements. hazard-test still passes.

FOR THE DEVICE: whether the edge reads as rock at speed, and whether the bites are deep enough. The captures show a broken, lit lip beside the rail, but a subtle one. `RIM_JAG.bite` is the dial.
