### RLG-044 — Colour says what a car IS: racers saturated, player iridescent, traffic muted
- **RULED 2026-08-28T17:33:46-04:00** — **Owner, 2026-08-28:** "Only racers get the nice saturated colors, and in addition the player only
has the iridescent colors that are rewarded on the sports tournament. All traffic cars get more
muted color palettes. Sports cars that are part of traffic get these muted colors so they do not
read as racers."

**Colour is not decoration here. It is the readout that tells the player what a thing on the road
IS**, at a distance, in a fraction of a second, before any other cue arrives. Three classes:

| class | palette |
|---|---|
| racers | saturated |
| the player | iridescent, and those are a TOURNAMENT REWARD rather than a starting choice |
| all traffic | muted |

**The load-bearing case is the last sentence.** A tuner or a muscle car in traffic wears the muted
palette, because the fault being fixed is that a sports car in traffic reads as a rival. The engine
already has a note about this from the other direction - road.js:4055 records that after the
acceleration retune "a striped muscle car going past at 94 looks exactly like a rival" - so this is
the same confusion, and colour is the fix the owner has chosen for it.

**Consequence to settle before building:** the player's palette becomes a reward, so a new save has
no iridescent paint. What the player starts with is therefore an open question - saturated like a
racer, which they are, or something else. **Ask the owner.**

Touches `PAINT_KEYS`, `paintChoices()`, the garage, the tournament reward table, and the traffic
painters. It is a rule about which palette a painter draws from, not a change to any painter.
- **RULED 2026-08-28T17:41:46-04:00** — **Owner's answer, 2026-08-28, to the question this fragment parked.**

**A new save starts with the SATURATED palette - the same class the racers wear.** The player is a
racer and reads as one from the first race. Iridescent then becomes an upgrade WITHIN that class
rather than the player's admission to it.

This also settles a risk the question was really about: at no point in the game is anything on the
road wearing the wrong class of colour. Had the player started muted, the first hour would have
taught them that muted means traffic while they were driving a muted car - which is the exact
confusion RLG-044 exists to remove, introduced at the one moment the player is least able to spot
it.

So the three classes hold from the first frame of a new save:

| class | palette | how it is obtained |
|---|---|---|
| racers | saturated | always |
| the player | saturated, then iridescent | iridescent is won in the sports tournament |
| all traffic, sports cars included | muted | always |
- **RULED 2026-08-28T20:22:01-04:00** — **Owner, 2026-08-28, closing the question [[RLG-054]] raised:** "only true racers get the saturated
color paints."

**So the palette follows what a car IS, not how its driver behaves.** A traffic car with a Racer
personality wears MUTED paint like every other traffic car. Saturated means a true racer - the
tournament field - and nothing else.

| | palette |
|---|---|
| the tournament field | saturated |
| the player | saturated, and iridescent once won |
| all traffic, including a traffic Racer | muted |

**This is the right way round and it is worth saying why, because the other way is tempting.**
Colour is a promise about what a thing is. If a fast traffic car wore racer colours, the signal
would mean 'drives quickly' - which the player can already see from the car's behaviour and does not
need telling. Meaning 'this is a rival' is information the player cannot get any other way, and it
is the thing they must not be wrong about, because it decides whether a car matters to their
placing.

**It also makes the traffic Racer better rather than weaker.** A fast car in muted paint is a
genuine surprise: it reads as traffic, and then it does not behave like traffic. That is a lesson
worth teaching once, and it survives being learned - the player learns to watch behaviour rather
than colour, and the colour still tells them the truth about placing.

**One thing to check on a device rather than assume:** whether a muted car moving at racing speed
reads as a rendering fault. [[RLG-041]] has vehicles disappearing and reappearing, unexplained, and
'that car does not look like it should be doing that' is the kind of report the two could be
confused for.
