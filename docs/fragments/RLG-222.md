### RLG-222 — Depth is a detail: what sits at one end of a car is darker from the other

**RULED 2026-09-12.** Owner: *"Across the fleet, make sure that the rearview mirrors from the rear
perspective are shaded darker than the ones in the front perspective. Remember if we are tinting
stuff based on depth."* And, widening it: *"It's not just mirrors, anything that is exposed but
further forward has a slightly darker shade from the rear perspective. The perfect example of this
are spoilers."*

---

## WE ARE NOT TINTING BY DEPTH AT RUNTIME, WHICH IS WHY THIS IS BAKED IN

The owner's "remember if" was worth checking and the answer is no. Vehicle sprites are built once and
drawn with nothing but the draw-edge alpha ramp ([[RLG-218]]); the atmospheric haze belongs to the
skyline and never touches a car. **There is no runtime tint that could tell a car's two ends apart**,
so the difference has to be in the drawing — which means the shared code has to know which end it is
painting.

## THE RULE ALREADY EXISTED AND THIS IS ITS FOURTH AND FIFTH LANDING

[[RLG-196]] states it once: *"Any part that lives at one END of the car is near in one view and far in
the other, and should say so."* It had reached the formula wings, the pickup's cab, and the supercars'
rear wings. **The owner's two sentences are that same rule, from both sides:**

| part | lives at | far in the | |
|---|---|---|---|
| door mirrors | the front | **rear** view | new here |
| the tuner's boot spoiler | the back | **front** view | new here |
| the muscle car's bonnet scoop | the front | **rear** view | new here |

**THE SPOILER IS THE OWNER'S OWN EXAMPLE AND IT WAS THE ONE THAT NEVER TOOK THE RULE.** The front
painter draws the tuner's wing under a comment that reads *"the spoiler is BEHIND the car from here,
so it goes first"* — the draw ORDER was right and the colour was the near one. The same shape of miss
as the muscle car's scoop, whose comment says *"seen from behind"* above a line drawing it in the
near tone.

## THE FACTOR IS NAMED ONCE AT LAST

`0.74` was written inline in `carWing`, in the formula wings and in the pickup's cab — **four copies
of one number describing one distance**, which is how a rule quietly becomes four fixes again.
`FAR_K` is that number now, `FAR_HI` is what a highlight drops to, and `farTone(hex, far)` is the one
call. `mirrorTone(P, back)` is a thin wrapper for the bodies that have mirrors.

**The highlight dims rather than disappearing.** The white edge on a van's mirror head is a glint off
the glass, and the glass faces forward, so a case could be made for removing it outright from behind.
[[RLG-196]]'s own answer for a highlight on a far surface is to dim it, and **taking the other answer
here would leave the fleet holding two rules for one question.**

`rigFurniture` and `carMirrors` take a `back` flag and the two painters pass opposite values — the
same shape `carWing` and `formulaWing` already use.

## WHAT IS PROVED, AND WHAT IS NOT

`car-ends-test` passes: nineteen cars, every silhouette matching, which is what says this changed
**colour and not shape**. That is the invariant this work could actually have broken.

**`lamp-test` FAILS, and it fails identically on the unchanged engine.** *"The cars behind you have
their headlights on after dark"* read 0 lit, and the fleet-wide *"pixels lit up at the bulb"* fell
from about 705 earlier in the day to 184 — **on the committed build with this change stashed out**.
It is the machine, not the code, and it is a load effect rather than a lottery: it reproduces.

## AND I CAUSED THAT LOAD MYSELF, WHICH IS THE LESSON WORTH KEEPING

For roughly an hour every harness in this project appeared to be wedged — `car-ends-test` and
`fleet-sheet` both stopped after fetching `road.js` and never finished. I read that as a defect in the
engine and went looking for an infinite loop that was never there.

**IT WAS TWENTY-ONE ORPHANED CHROME AND PYTHON PROCESSES, AND I HAD STARTED ALL OF THEM.** Every
harness run that overran the shell's timeout was moved to the background and left its browser behind;
each new run then had less machine than the last, until pages stopped finishing at all. Killing them
made `fleet-sheet` complete on the first attempt.

**Two things follow, for whoever is next:**

- **Run these harnesses one at a time and wait.** They each drive a real browser. Overlapping them
  does not make them faster; past about three concurrent runs it makes all of them fail, and it fails
  in a way that looks exactly like a bug in the product.
- **A harness that stops after `road.js` with no traceback is starved, not broken.** The giveaway is
  that stdout is missing entirely while the server's stderr GET lines are present — buffered output
  lost on a killed process. Check the machine before reading the source.

## WHAT THIS DOES NOT COVER

**Whether the difference is visible at the size a car is drawn on the road is the owner's verdict on
the device.** It is clear on the fleet sheet, which is a desktop render at roughly three times that.

**Only the parts named above were changed.** A sweep for every remaining one-ended detail across the
fleet has not been done; the truck's underrun bar and mud flaps were checked and are at both ends, so
they correctly take nothing.
