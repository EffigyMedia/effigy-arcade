### RLG-022 — Interstate: biome changes should sweep down the road toward the player
- **RULED 2026-08-28T13:51:38-04:00** — - **Owner report:** when the biome switches, it should happen in road slices coming toward the
  player rather than all at once, so it is more immersive.

- **What stands in the way.** `biome` is a single global that `stepBiome` flips, and every ground,
  skyline and weather read goes through `bio()`. There is no per-segment notion of place at all, so
  this is not a tuning change - it needs the biome to become a property of the road AHEAD, with the
  boundary travelling toward the camera as `pos` advances.

- **Watch the skyline.** `buildSkyline()` is called on the switch and is global too. A sweep that
  changes the ground under you while the horizon changes in one frame is worse than the instant
  switch, because it draws attention to the seam.
- **RULED 2026-08-29T15:15:31-04:00** — THE TRANSITION, STATED IN FULL BY THE OWNER, 2026-08-29. Restated deliberately - "we should have a
record for what I've asked for before, but just in case I will state it all again" - and it adds
detail this fragment did not carry.

  "The painted ground under the horizon in the background needs to match the same color but slightly
   darker as the current foreground. As a new biome is entered we paint the road slices, the correct
   color from the furthest point forward and they get generated towards the camera. This will create
   an immersive transition between the biome.

   We could do an addition to that have a blur color transition for several to a dozen or a couple
   dozen slices between the old biome to the new biome and while that's happening, we can swap out
   the skyline biome scenery. Maybe we could either do an alpha fade so take the old scenery faded
   out and then fade in the new scenery or we can move the old scenery as its own layer behind the
   horizon and the new scenery from behind the horizon."

THREE THINGS, AND THEY ARE SEPARABLE.

FIRST, THE FAR FIELD MUST MATCH THE FOREGROUND. `groundBase()` already fills the band from the
horizon down to the furthest drawn slice, and it already derives from `grassLo`. What the owner is
asking for is that it track the biome it is showing and sit slightly DARKER than the near ground, so
the gap reads as more of the same place seen further off. Today it washes 30% toward the haze, which
is a different move: it goes GREY rather than dark, and under a biome change it shows the new biome's
colour instantly while the drawn slices still show the old one.

SECOND, THE SWEEP. The road slices take the new biome's colour from the furthest point and the change
travels toward the camera as the road is generated. This is the part this fragment already said was
not a tuning change: `biome` is one global and there is no per-segment notion of place, so a segment
has to carry its own biome before any of this is possible.

THIRD, THE BLEND BAND. Between the old biome and the new there are "several to a dozen or a couple
dozen slices" whose colour is interpolated between the two, so the join is a gradient rather than a
line. That number is a tunable and belongs in configuration with a committed default.

AND THE SKYLINE SWAPS WHILE THE BAND PASSES. The owner offered two mechanisms and did not choose
between them - an alpha crossfade, or moving the old scenery out as its own layer while the new
scenery comes in from behind the horizon. THAT CHOICE IS STILL OPEN and is the owner's. It is not a
detail: the second option needs the skyline to become two independently positioned layers, and the
first does not.

THE BIOME COUNT WAS ASKED AND IS ANSWERED. The owner named city, forest, mountain and desert and said
"I forget if we have other biomes". There are FIVE. The fifth is TUNDRA, at `road.js:6084`, and today
it is distinguished only by a pale blue-grey grass and a snow chance of 0.62 - the highest of the
five. It has no scenery of its own proposed for it, so it needs one before this work is complete.
- **RULED 2026-08-29T16:15:39-04:00** — THE SWEEP IS BUILT, AND THE MECHANISM IS THE SEGMENT INDEX. This fragment said the work was not a
tuning change because "biome is a single global that stepBiome flips" and "there is no per-segment
notion of place at all". That was right about the problem and wrong about the size of the fix.

`idx` in the road pass is an ABSOLUTE segment number, not a screen position. So a boundary placed at
a fixed index is a fixed place in the world, and it arrives at the camera on its own as `pos`
advances - nothing animates it, nothing tracks it, and no per-segment storage is needed. Three
variables carry the whole feature: `biomeFrom`, `biomeTo`, and `biomeEdge`, the index where the
change is centred. `bioMix(idx)` reads how far into the new place any segment is.

`biome` KEEPS ITS OLD MEANING and that is what made this small. It is where the PLAYER is. Every
existing `bio()` caller - weather, the skyline, the biome-impossible check, the name flash - is
untouched and all of them still fire when the car CROSSES the boundary, which is when you have
arrived. Only the ground painting became per segment.

THE TIMER PLACES, THE DRIVING ARRIVES, and splitting those two apart is the feature. The timer sets
the edge at `here + DRAW`, which is exactly the furthest slice being drawn - the owner's "furthest
point forward". Nothing else changes at that moment. Seconds later the car reaches it and the rest
follows. Firing both together is what made it a cut.

THE BAND IS 18 SEGMENTS. The owner's range was "several to a dozen or a couple dozen"; 18 sits in the
middle. It is a named constant with a committed default rather than a number edited in place.

MEASURED, driving at 0.9 of top speed with the boundary placed 150 segments out:

  car seg 17    edge 167   mix at car 0.000   at horizon 0.500   in FOREST
  car seg 79    edge 167   mix at car 0.000   at horizon 1.000   in FOREST
  car seg 139   edge 167   mix at car 0.000   at horizon 1.000   in FOREST
  car seg 187   edge 167   mix at car 1.000   at horizon 1.000   in MOUNTAIN

THE FAR FIELD ASKED THE WRONG PLACE, and it would have run the transition backwards. `groundBase()`
read `bio()` - where the car is - while filling the band that shows what is at the FAR end of the
road. So the new biome would have reached the horizon LAST. It calls the same `groundTone` the
furthest drawn slice calls now, so the two meet at one colour by construction rather than by two
derivations agreeing.

AND ITS BRIGHTNESS IS SET RATHER THAN HOPED FOR. The owner asked for "the same color but slightly
darker". The haze wash is a mix toward a light grey, so on a DARK biome it made the band BRIGHTER: a
forest verge at 48 came out at 79 with the wash at 0.30. The hue now comes from a much smaller haze
mix and the level is normalised to 0.86 of the foreground's, so a dark place and a bright place are
both slightly darker behind.

THAT FAULT PASSED ITS OWN CHECK ONCE, WHICH IS THE PART WORTH REMEMBERING. The first run of
`biome-test.py` reported the band correctly darker. It had landed in a bright biome, where the haze
does darken. The second run landed in a dark one and failed. A single run of a check whose subject
varies per load is not evidence - which is the same lesson `occlusion-test.py` already carries about
its cull count.

THE CHECK IS NOT VACUOUS. A flip shows the same mix at the car and at the horizon at every instant; a
sweep shows the horizon leading. The harness sets both ends of the blend to one biome - which IS the
old behaviour - and asserts that the car and the horizon then agree, so the distinction the sweep
assertions rest on is shown to be a real one.

NOT VERIFIED HERE. Whether the transition reads as immersive is the owner's call on the device. The
numbers say the colour travels; they cannot say it looks like travelling.

STILL OPEN, AND BOTH ARE THE OWNER'S: which mechanism swaps the skyline while the band passes, and
the blend band's length once it has been seen in motion.
- **RULED 2026-08-29T22:30:16-04:00** — THE SKYLINE FOLLOWS THE HORIZON, NOT THE CAR. Owner, 2026-08-29, correcting what the sweep shipped
with: "The current biome is where the player car is, BUT the horizon and skyline inherit the next
biome per the segments approaching the player car." And then, confirming the model: "you inherit the
new biome AS YOU CROSS INTO THE SEGMENTS THAT REPRESENT IT."

IT WAS INCONSISTENT AND THE OWNER CAUGHT IT. The far-field GROUND already showed the new place the
moment a boundary was placed, because the boundary is placed at the furthest drawn slice. The
skyline was still rebuilt on ARRIVAL. So for the whole transition - the time it takes to drive the
length of the drawn road - the new biome's ground would have sat under the old biome's sky.

The rule is simple once said. The skyline is painted AT the horizon, the horizon is the furthest
segments, and those are already the next place. Only what is about being HERE follows the car.

BOTH SWAP MECHANISMS ARE BUILT, and that was a deliberate choice rather than indecision. The owner
offered an alpha crossfade or moving the old scenery out as its own layer, and asked which would be
better. A crossfade is a DEGENERATE CASE of the layer move - two skylines drawn at once, blended by
opacity instead of by position - so once the layers exist the crossfade costs one branch. Building
both turns an architecture decision the owner would have had to make blind into a switch they can
see both ways. `SKY_SWAP` picks and defaults to 'move'.

WHY 'move' IS THE DEFAULT. It agrees with the ground: the band reaches the horizon first and the
bumper last, so a swap driven by position can be tied to that travel where a dissolve happening at
one moment cannot. And a crossfade is a video transition - it reads as an edit rather than as a place
ending. Driving out of mountains, the mountains do not dissolve; they sink away behind you as the
desert is revealed. The skyline is a silhouette band with no depth to recede through, so that is
faked: the outgoing one sinks and shrinks, the incoming one rises from behind the horizon. Sideways
would read as the world rotating.

THE WEATHER CROSSES TOO, AND OVER A LONGER RUN. Owner: "that should include transitioning weather
effects." Weather the new place cannot produce now thins in proportion to how far into it the car is,
rather than being switched off at a line, and `settleMelt` became a RATE rather than a flag so the
melt comes on with the crossing instead of at full speed the instant a line is passed.

ITS BAND IS FOUR TIMES THE COLOUR BAND, at 72 segments against 18, and the first version did not have
that. Ramping the weather over the colour band is a switch with a ramp painted on it - the car covers
18 segments in well under a second. It is not what happens in the world either: the ground underfoot
changes at a line and the sky above it does not. Measured, driving into a desert with snow falling:

  ground 0.00  weather 0.00   wet 0.900   settle 0.815   in TUNDRA
  ground 0.00  weather 0.35   wet 0.878   settle 0.782   in TUNDRA
  ground 1.00  weather 0.69   wet 0.803   settle 0.474   in DESERT
  ground 1.00  weather 1.00   wet 0.684   settle 0.003   in DESERT

THREE THINGS HAPPEN AT THREE POINTS ALONG THE CROSSING, which is what makes it a transition rather
than a switch: the weather begins thinning as the band reaches the car, the place is renamed at the
halfway mark, and the old place stops existing at the far side.

AND A SECOND HARNESS WAS FOUND TO BE BIOME-DEPENDENT. `weather-test.py`'s verge check went red on
runs that landed in FOREST and green on runs that landed in DESERT, on one unchanged build - a dark
ground leaves the rain darkening least room to show. It is pinned to the darkest biome now, so a pass
is a pass everywhere. That is the third check in two days to be caught varying with something the run
picks at random, after `occlusion-test`'s cull count and `biome-test`'s band brightness.

NOT VERIFIED HERE, AND THE OPEN QUESTION IS NARROWER NOW. Which mechanism reads better is the
owner's, and both are one call apart: `API.skySwap('fade')` or `('move')`.
- **RULED 2026-08-30T07:36:35-04:00** — A RUN STARTED PART-WAY THROUGH A BIOME CHANGE. Owner, 2026-08-30, from the device: "my first test run
started with a raining desert."

THE MECHANISM, and it is a guard that tested the wrong thing. `biomeNext` starts at 0, so the biome
timer fires on the very first frame. The branch that was supposed to handle that read
`if(biomeNext < -1)` - which is only true when the frame that just elapsed was longer than a SECOND.
A real first frame is about sixteen milliseconds. So the first call always took the CHANGE branch
instead: the car began in the declared default of FOREST, a transition to a random biome was placed
at the horizon, and `stepWeather` ran in the same frame with `wetNext` also at 0 and rolled the
weather against FOREST, which rains 42% of the time.

Seconds later the car crossed into whatever had been placed ahead. If that was DESERT, the owner got
a desert with forest weather falling on it - and a desert rains 4% of the time, so it reads as
obviously wrong the moment you see it.

THE FIX IS A FLAG, because "has this run started" is a fact about the run and not something to infer
from how slow a frame was. The first call picks a place outright with no transition, and `stepWeather`
runs after `stepBiome` in the same frame, so the first weather roll is against the place the car is
actually in.

IT WAS WATCHED FAILING, and it reproduced the report exactly. With the old guard restored the check
printed "from=FOREST to=TUNDRA" and went red on the first assertion; with the fix, from and to are the
same place.

THE CHECK IS READ BEFORE ANYTHING ELSE TOUCHES THE STATE, which is the only way this one can be
tested. Every other assertion in `biome-test.py` sets the biome pair or the weather to make its
subject exist; this one is about the state the engine hands you at boot, so it has to run first and
it has to run on whatever the engine chose. It asserts three things: that a run does not begin
mid-transition, that the car is in the place it started in, and that whatever is falling is weather
that place can produce.

WHAT IS NOT CHANGED, and it is worth writing down rather than leaving to be discovered. A second run
in the same session does not re-pick the biome - `biomeStarted` stays set for the life of the page, so
restarting continues wherever the world had got to. That is the behaviour that was there before this
fix as much as after it, and nobody has ruled on whether a fresh run should get a fresh place.
- **RULED 2026-08-30T07:42:04-04:00** — THE DESTINATION SETS THE TAPER RATE. Owner, 2026-08-30: "Part of the weather overhaul should've been
strictly adhering to biome conditional weather. I don't mind whether tapering off into a new biome
instead of just snapping off, but the speed of the taper should be dictated by the new biome. For
example, a snowy forest transitioning to tundra should more than likely not even taper off the snow
at all, a snowy forest transitioning to city could have a chance of tapering it off at a normal rate,
and a snowy forest transitioning to desert should quickly taper off instead of just snapping off."

WHAT WAS THERE ASKED ONE QUESTION AND IT WAS THE WRONG ONE: can the new place produce this AT ALL. If
no, taper at one fixed rate; if yes, do nothing. So a tundra and a city were treated identically to
each other, and a desert identically to both. Snow carried into a tundra ONLY because tundra's chance
is above zero - by accident of the test rather than because the place wants it - and it would have
carried into a forest at 0.06 just as readily.

`support` IS THE NEW PLACE'S OWN ODDS for whatever is falling, and it now sets the whole behaviour
against two named thresholds:

  support >= 0.30      it belongs here too. Nothing tapers. Snow crossing into a tundra simply
                       carries on as if it had always been there.
  0.05 to 0.30         the ordinary crossing - it thins over the full weather band.
  below 0.05           this place refuses it. Gone over a third of the band: quick, and still a
                       taper rather than the snap that was there before.

Measured, snow leaving a forest: TUNDRA support 0.62 span 0.00, CITY 0.10 span 1.00, DESERT 0.00 span
0.30. Those are the owner's three examples and they are three different behaviours.

SETTLED SNOW IS A SEPARATE QUESTION, and only the last case melts it. A city that snows one time in
ten does not melt what is already lying on the ground; a desert does. Splitting that from the falling
weather is what stops a city stripping the ground white the moment you enter it.

THE OWNER'S FIRST SENTENCE IS THE ONE THAT MATTERS MOST: this "should've been part of the weather
overhaul". It should have. The overhaul built accumulation, melt rates, grip, two-layer cover, a
crossing band and a per-biome ground - and left the one question of whether a place WANTS the weather
it is being handed as a boolean nobody looked at twice. Every piece of machinery was there; the rule
was missing.
- **RULED 2026-08-30T19:41:04-04:00** — THE BIOME RAN ON A CLOCK, AND IT SHOULD ALWAYS HAVE RUN ON DISTANCE. Owner, 2026-08-30, from the device: park at the side of the road and eventually the biome changes, without the car having moved.

IT DID. The countdown was biomeNext -= dt, seconds, reset to a random seventy to a hundred and thirty. A place therefore lasted the same wait whether the car was doing two hundred miles an hour or nothing at all. The comment four lines above the declaration has read 'changes biome every few miles' since the day it was written, so the intent was distance from the start and only the arithmetic was time - which is the kind of defect that survives review because the prose next to it is correct.

IT IS SPENT BY THE SAME QUANTITY THE ROAD IS. biomeNext -= spd*dt, which is exactly what pos advances by, so a parked car spends nothing and a fast one spends it quickly. Nothing else had to change.

THE RANGE WAS CONVERTED, NOT REPLACED. Seventy to a hundred and thirty seconds at the speed the interstate is actually driven - drive-test measures a mean of 156 mph over eight runs - is six and a half to twelve miles. So a player at a normal pace feels the same pacing as before, and what changes is that crawling no longer shortens a place and parking no longer ends one.

AND A SECOND RUN WAS INHERITING THE FIRST ONE'S MAP. Neither biomeNext nor biomeStarted was ever cleared at a reset, so only the first run of a page load took the branch that chooses an opening place outright. Every run after it took the CHANGE branch with whatever countdown was left over, which could be a few hundred units - a place that ended before the car had left the line. Both are cleared with the day clock now, on the same reasoning the sky was given one line above: continuity across runs is worth less than a run that starts where it says it does.

MEASURED, AND THE CHECK CATCHES THE OLD ONE. biome-test puts the boundary 600 units ahead - under a tenth of a second at speed, and for ever at a standstill - then parks for two and a half seconds and reads the countdown. Fixed: 600 units left, no change. With the clock put back: 597.5 left after parking, and driving for nine tenths of a second spends nothing and places nothing.

WHAT THAT CHECK CANNOT DO ON ITS OWN: the assertion that the biome did not CHANGE while parked passes on the broken build as well, because six hundred read as six hundred seconds never elapses inside a harness. The discriminating assertion is the one about the countdown running down, and the one about driving spending it. A check that only asked whether the place changed would have agreed with the build the owner was complaining about.
