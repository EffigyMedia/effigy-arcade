### RLG-219 — The van's face is its grille, and the ambulance keeps its own until its turn

**RULED 2026-09-12.** Owner, with a photograph of a GMC Savana: a tall upright grille with
horizontal slats and a badge in the middle of it, headlights either side, **amber turn signals below
the headlights rather than beside them**, a chrome bumper across the bottom, and mirrors on stalks.

This is the second of the three items the thread carried in order. The first was [[RLG-216]].

---

## WHAT WAS THERE

A blank panel. The grille was a letterbox 0.71 of the width and 0.135 of the height, lying along the
bottom of the face; the badge was a four-pixel disc floating alone in the empty white above it; the
headlights and ambers were stacked in the outboard corners, below and beside the grille rather than
flanking it. **The whole middle of the van — which is most of what you see of one — carried nothing.**

## WHAT IT IS NOW

**The grille is the face.** It stands up in the centre at 0.43 wide by 0.375 tall, with a rim so it
reads as a recess rather than a sticker, and five slats across it.

**The badge is on the grille**, which is where a van wears it and the only place on this vehicle
where a badge has anything to sit against. The **middle slat is broken around it** — drawn whole, the
centre bar runs straight through the marque and the badge reads as a lump on a rail. A real grille
parts around its badge.

**The headlights flank the grille at its own height** and are placed off the grille's own edges, so a
grille that is ever retuned takes its lamps with it. **The ambers sit directly below them**, which is
the owner's correction and is what the reference shows.

**The bumper is chrome, and the first build of it did not read.** Flat `#7d838c` with a white
highlight — the pickup's bumper, reused so that the fleet has one answer to what chrome looks like —
ran straight into a white body and the bumper stopped having a top edge. What separates them is a
**dark seam** where it meets the panel, and the bar is a four-stop gradient rather than a fill so it
reads as metal.

**IT STAYS INSIDE 0.055 TO 0.945.** A real bumper stands proud of the body and this one must not: the
widest ink is what `car-ends-test` measures, and a front bumper wider than the tail's is a silhouette
that disagrees with itself ([[RLG-184]]).

**The mirrors are on stalks**, and the stalk is drawn in `rigFurniture` rather than in either painter
**because both call it**. A mirror is outline, not furniture — the note above `carMirrors` says so —
and a stalk drawn on the face alone would give the van two silhouettes. Drawn once, it reads from
behind as well, which is correct: you can see the arms of a van's mirrors from either end.

## AND THE AMBULANCE KEEPS THE OLD FACE, DELIBERATELY

`paintRigFront` remaps the kind `ambulance` to `van` and adds `bar:'medical'`, so **everything written
into the van's face reaches the ambulance unless it is held back**. The thread carried that warning
before this work started and it is the reason the new face is gated on `o.bar`.

The owner's ambulance reference is a different face again — a red light bar across the top of the
box, three amber roof markers, a very tall dark windscreen, mirrors on arms, a grille with red in it,
and **a black bumper with a white plate**. Giving it half of a van's face now and the rest later would
leave it looking like neither. It keeps what it has until its own unit, which is the third of the
three items.

**The black bumper is the case that proves the gate is the right shape.** It is not a temporary
holdback: the two vehicles are *meant* to differ there, so that branch survives the ambulance's own
rebuild.

## AND `fleet-sheet` HAD NOT DRAWN ANY OF THESE VEHICLES IN WEEKS

Its class list ended in `utility`, and no such class exists — the fleet resort made the non-racing
vehicles one class called `traffic`. Every run printed *"utility no vehicles in this class"* and
carried on, so **the VAN, the SEMI, the CAB, the PICKUP and the AMBULANCE have had no sheet at all
since that rename**, and the van's blank face was never in front of anyone.

A name that silently matches nothing is the worst kind of stale: the tool did not fail, it reported
an absence as a fact about the fleet. Fixed here because this work could not be looked at otherwise.

## THE PROOF

`car-ends-test` passes with the van and the ambulance both at 0.0% silhouette error and identical ink
boxes, which is what says the stalks reached both ends and the bumper stayed inside the body.
`lamp-test` passes: 203 lamps across 65 vehicles, every lit lamp inside its own unlit bulb — the
headlights and ambers moved, and the lit drawing moved with them because it is the same declaration
([[RLG-053]]). `ambulance-test` and `smoke-test` are clean.

## WHAT THIS DOES NOT COVER

**How the face reads at the size a van is actually drawn on the road is the owner's verdict on the
device.** The fleet sheet is a desktop render at roughly three times the size a van appears in
traffic. Whether the slats and the badge survive that reduction, or turn to mush, is not something a
green run answers.
