### RLG-087 — The garage car sits in one box, sized to the tallest car
- **RULED 2026-08-30T19:56:13-04:00** — Owner, 2026-08-30: set the car render in the garage to the same static height, using the tallest car as the reference, so that switching cars does not collapse and expand the layout by a small amount. The owner's word for the current behaviour is annoying, and the size of the movement is the point - a large change would read as a transition, and a small one reads as the page failing to hold still.

THE BOX IS RESERVED, NOT THE CAR. The car keeps its own proportions and its own size; what stops moving is the space it is drawn in. A lorry is much taller than a roadster and forcing them to the same drawn height would make the roadster wrong rather than the layout right.

MEASURE THE FLEET RATHER THAN NAMING THE LORRY. The reference is the tallest sprite box any selectable car has, taken from the same table the sprites are built from, so a car added later cannot silently overflow the reservation or leave a gap under it. A hard-coded number here is the same mistake as the pedal box's hard-coded 58, which sat in a different file from the two numbers that decided it.

AND THE CAR SITS ON THE FLOOR OF THAT BOX RATHER THAN IN THE MIDDLE OF IT. Cars stand on the ground. Centring a roadster in a lorry's box leaves it floating with a gap underneath, which trades one wrong layout for another.

RELATED, NOT THE SAME: RLG-086 made the garage answer a tap in about four milliseconds instead of about a quarter of a second. That was work per tap; this is layout per car, and the second is visible now that the first has stopped hiding it.
- **RULED 2026-08-30T20:14:07-04:00** — BUILT, 2026-08-30, AND IT REVERSES A DELIBERATE DECISION. The card height was a constant once and was made per-car on purpose, because a card sized for the tallest vehicle left a band of nothing under a low one and a band of nothing on a phone is a screen's worth of scroll. That reasoning was correct and it is still the cost. What changed is the owner weighing the two against each other with the thing in front of them.

MEASURED BEFORE: the card moved 78, 85, 90 across the three cars a player starts with. Eleven pixels, which is the complaint rather than a defence of it - a large change reads as a transition and a small one reads as the page failing to hold still.

THE RESERVATION IS THE TALLEST CARD ANY BODY PRODUCES, computed by loading each in turn and putting the player's own car back, cached after the first time. A number typed here instead would be right until somebody added a taller car and then wrong silently, which is the fault the pedal box's hard-coded 58 had.

AND THE LORRY IS AN OUTLIER THE OWNER SHOULD KNOW ABOUT. The fleet runs LORRY 150, VAN 115, PICKUP 112, CRUISER 107, down to ROADSTER 78. So the lorry alone raises the reservation by 35 pixels over the next tallest, and a roadster now sits above 72 pixels of air. The capture reads as breathing room above a centred title rather than as a hole, but it is the owner's eye that settles it. THE LEVER IS GARAGE_DEEP: the lorry is the only body that hits that ceiling, so lowering it shrinks the lorry and pulls the whole card down with it.

THE CHECK IS TWO-SIDED. It asserts the reservation covers the tallest car AND that it is exactly that car with nothing spare, because a reservation with slack over every body would satisfy the first half while wasting the difference on all of them. It then switches cars four times and requires the drawn card to measure the same every time; with the per-car height put back it reads 78, 85, 90, 78.
- **RULED 2026-08-30T20:18:50-04:00** — AND THERE ARE TWO SIZES, NOT ONE. Owner, 2026-08-30, on seeing the single card: the lorry and the van are a bummer, that was not considered, so standardize it for the regular cars and expand it only for the extra large vehicles.

ONE RESERVATION MADE EVERY CAR PAY FOR THE LORRY. The measurement above is the argument: LORRY 150, VAN 115, then PICKUP 112 down to ROADSTER 78. A single card sized to the tallest put 72 pixels of air above a roadster to accommodate one vehicle most players will never choose, which is a worse trade than the eleven pixels of movement it was buying out.

TWO TIERS KEEP THE FIX AND DROP THE COST. Ordinary cars reserve 112, the oversized pair reserve 150. A card never changes size between two ordinary cars, which is what the complaint was about; it does change when you scroll onto something the size of a lorry, and that reads as the vehicle being different rather than as the page failing to hold still. Air above a roadster falls from 72 pixels to 34.

 IS DECLARED ON THE BODY, NOT INFERRED FROM A HEIGHT. A rule that said anything over 120 pixels is oversized would silently re-tier a car the day somebody adjusted a sprite, and the tier is a statement about what the vehicle IS rather than about what its picture currently measures. It is one word in the table on VAN and LORRY.

THE CHECK ASKS FOR EXACTLY THE TALLEST OF EACH TIER, and separately that the two tiers differ. A reservation with slack over every car would satisfy a covers-them-all check while wasting the difference on all of them, which is the fault the single tier had; and if the biggest ordinary car were as tall as the lorry there would be nothing to separate and the check would be theatre. Collapsing the two tiers back to one fails both.
