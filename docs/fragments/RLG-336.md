### RLG-336 — A strip of the up face never renders, and it holds a fixed distance
- **RULED 2026-09-24T01:23:46-04:00** — OWNER, 2026-09-24, from the device with two screenshots: "there is a strip not rendering on the upward face. It's at a static distance so it stays there. Like all the strips passing through a certain spot don't get rendered."

VISIBLE IN BOTH SHOTS. A pale vertical strip stands in the face - on the LEFT face in the second shot at roughly a fifth of the way across, and on the RIGHT face in the first. It is the colour of what is BEHIND the face, so it is a band that never painted rather than a band painted wrong.

"AT A STATIC DISTANCE" IS THE WHOLE CLUE AND IT IS A STRONG ONE. The face is painted per slice, and a slice's distance from the car changes every frame as the road moves under it - so a gap belonging to a SLICE would slide toward the camera and vanish. A gap that holds station belongs to the WALK rather than to the road: the same `n` fails every frame, whichever piece of world is at that `n`. The owner says exactly this - "all the strips passing through a certain spot don't get rendered".

SO LOOK AT WHAT IS INDEXED BY `n` AND NOT BY `idx`. The wall is tiled per slice and joined through `wallSeam`, which remembers the last band's far corner so a slice hidden behind a crest does not leave a vertical stripe of missing wall - THE EXACT SYMPTOM HERE, and its own note says so: "without the remembered seam the next one to draw would start at its own far corner and leave a vertical stripe of missing wall". A seam that is reset, or dropped, at one fixed point in the walk would reproduce this precisely.

WHERE TO START: `wallSeam[wk]` and every place it is written or cleared, then whether some `n` reaches the wall block with a seam it should not have. The massif has the same shape of question - it is painted for skipped slices - so check which of the two the strip belongs to first, by turning one off.

- **RULED 2026-09-24T10:57:51-04:00** — FOUND, FIXED AND PROVED, AND THE LEAD IN THIS FRAGMENT WAS WRONG. It is NOT `wallSeam`. The wall painter hands the side over with `if(dB.mass && !massOff) continue`, and MOUNTAIN and CANYON are the only two places in the table that declare `mass:1` - so for both of the places that show the fault, the up face is drawn by `massSlice` and the wall's seam code never runs at all. THE OWNER'S OWN OBSERVATION IS WHAT FOUND IT: "mountain and canyon have the same issue" is the statement that those two places share a mechanism, and `mass` is the only thing they share that no other place has.

THE MECHANISM, AND IT IS ONE LINE. Past `MASS_MERGE` - two fifths of the draw - `massSlice` merges a run of `MASS_RUN` slices into a single quad. It decided to merge from `n > DRAW * MASS_MERGE`, which is THE SLICE'S OWN DISTANCE, while the run is anchored on the slice's ABSOLUTE index so that the rock stands still in the world as the car advances ([[RLG-333]] is why that anchoring exists). The two disagree at the boundary. A slice just past the threshold takes `step = MASS_RUN` and returns, deferring to its run's anchor; the anchor is up to five slices NEARER, so it can sit on the other side of the threshold, where it takes `step = 1` and paints itself alone. Every slice between them is painted by nothing.

MEASURED BEFORE THE CHANGE, on a draw of 300 with MASS_RUN 6 and MASS_MERGE 0.40: the anchors stood at n = 125, 131 and 137 against a threshold of 120, and n = 121 to 124 was covered by no quad at all - the same numbers in CANYON and in MOUNTAIN, which is what a shared mechanism looks like.

AND THAT IS WHY IT HOLDS STATION. The threshold is a fraction of the DRAW, which is a fixed distance ahead of the car, while the world moves through it. The owner said it twice and both halves are literal: "It's at a static distance so it stays there", and "all the strips passing through a certain spot don't get rendered".

THE FIX IS THAT THE RUN DECIDES, NOT THE SLICE. A run wholly past the threshold merges; a run that straddles it paints slice by slice, which is what the near half of the draw does anyway. It costs at most five extra quads a frame, twice over in a canyon, and the quad counts either side of the change are 1,054-1,059 against 1,065-1,101 in a CANYON.

`tools/mass-gap-test.py` IS THE PROOF AND IT IS NOT VACUOUS. The engine now records which slices each quad COVERS - `massTrace.covered`, read through `API.massModel` - so the check asserts completeness without reimplementing the merge decision it is checking, which is [[RLG-065]]'s lesson. WATCHED FAILING with the old line put back: gaps of 1, 2, 4 and 5 slices, always beginning at n=121. THE CAR IS DRIVEN BETWEEN READS ON PURPOSE - the anchor is fixed in the world and the threshold is fixed against the car, so one read is one alignment in six, and one of the eight failing reads showed NO gap because the anchor happened to land on the threshold. A single-sample check here would pass one time in six with the fault present.

SHIPPED IN 0.14.145. NOT YET JUDGED ON A DEVICE - the strip is the owner's to confirm gone, and a harness can only say every slice is covered.
