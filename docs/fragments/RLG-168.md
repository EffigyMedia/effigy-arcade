### RLG-168 — A cursory optimisation analysis, to close the queue

Owner, 2026-09-07, closing the testing queue: "let's finish with a cursory optimisation
analysis."

CURSORY IS THE WORD AND IT IS LOAD-BEARING. This is a survey - where the frames go, what
the worst place costs, what would be worth doing - and not a sweep. RLG-137 is the sweep
and it stays where it is.

WHAT MAKES IT WORTH DOING NOW rather than at the start: this queue adds a great deal to
what the road draws. Scenery on every slice instead of half of them, police in the
traffic, an ambulance with a light bar, roadblocks in the mirror, smoke and fire that
outlives the car, and clouds with a form. Each was measured on its own where it could be;
none has been measured together.

AND THE INSTRUMENT ALREADY EXISTS AND HAS A WARNING ON IT. `fps-test` prints the spread
because a single reading of this engine is worthless - one unchanged build measured a
forest at 48, 54 and 60 fps. Two scenery decisions were made against single readings
inside that spread before anyone noticed, and one reached a shipped changelog entry before
being withdrawn. Any number this produces must carry its spread.

THE KNOWN SUSPECT IS THE FOREST, and the levers named in the record are its scenery rows
and row density. The owner's own hardware question is RLG-149 - an older iPad cannot hold
the rate - and this analysis is the thing that would inform it.
