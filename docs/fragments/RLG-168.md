### RLG-168 — A cursory optimisation analysis, to close the queue

Owner, 2026-09-07, closing the testing queue: "let's finish with a cursory optimisation
analysis."

CURSORY IS THE WORD AND IT IS LOAD-BEARING. This is a survey - where the frames go, what
the worst place costs, what would be worth doing - and not a sweep. RLG-137 is the sweep
and it stays where it is.

WHAT MAKES IT WORTH DOING NOW rather than at the start: this queue adds a great deal to
what the road draws. Scenery on every slice instead of half of them, police in the
traffic, an ambulance with a light bar, roadblocks in the mirror, smoke and fire that
outlives the car, and clouds with a form. Each was measured on its own where it could be;
none has been measured together.

AND THE INSTRUMENT ALREADY EXISTS AND HAS A WARNING ON IT. `fps-test` prints the spread
because a single reading of this engine is worthless - one unchanged build measured a
forest at 48, 54 and 60 fps. Two scenery decisions were made against single readings
inside that spread before anyone noticed, and one reached a shipped changelog entry before
being withdrawn. Any number this produces must carry its spread.

THE KNOWN SUSPECT IS THE FOREST, and the levers named in the record are its scenery rows
and row density. The owner's own hardware question is RLG-149 - an older iPad cannot hold
the rate - and this analysis is the thing that would inform it.
- **RULED 2026-09-08T05:51:04-04:00** — DONE 2026-09-08, against v0.13.37. The survey is `docs/audits/perf_260908_survey.md` and it
changed no code.

THE HEADLINE IS NOT WHAT THE RECORD PREDICTED. The standing suspect was the forest, and the forest
IS the worst place at both hours - but NIGHT is the dominant term. At midday five of the seven
places sit on the 60.4 cap and only the forest is genuinely working; at midnight six of the seven
leave it. The forest loses about 8 fps from day to night, the city about 10 and the swamp about 9.
Night costs more than the forest does.

THE FOREST AT NIGHT IS THE CASE TO TEST ON A DEVICE, at 44.4-52.0 fps. It is the worst combination
the game produces and it is the number [[RLG-149]] - the owner's question about an older iPad -
should be asked against.

THE DESERT IS THE CONTROL AND IT SEPARATES TWO CANDIDATES. It holds 60.4 at midnight while
everything else falls. LIT LAMPS ARE NOT THE COST: counted per frame, the desert lights 415 and
holds the cap while the forest lights 304 and falls to 44 - more lamps, no cost. What is left is
SCENERY VOLUME: the desert has the least of any place and is the only one that does not fall, and
the forest and the swamp have the most and fall furthest at both hours. Night appears to multiply a
per-item cost rather than add a fixed one.

THAT IS AN INFERENCE FROM TWO DATA POINTS AND IT IS WRITTEN AS ONE. It was not proved by turning
the scenery off, because a survey changes no code. The next measurement should be exactly that: a
scratch build with the forest's rows or rowDensity cut, measured against this table. If the
forest's night range moves clear of 44-52, the mechanism is settled.

AND THE VEHICLE SPRITES ARE NOT WHERE THE WORK IS. Drawn totals run from 1 to 14 across every place
at both hours, with almost nothing culled. A dozen billboards is not a frame's work, so a sweep
through the drawing code would be spent where the time is not.

EVERY NUMBER CARRIES ITS SPREAD, five samples a place, and the table records all five. The warning
on this instrument is the reason: one unchanged build has measured a forest at 48, 54 and 60 fps,
and two scenery decisions were once made against single readings inside that spread.

WHAT IT DOES NOT SAY, stated in the report itself: nothing about a phone, since every number is a
headless desktop browser; nothing about where inside a frame the time goes, since this counts frames
rather than milliseconds by function; and nothing about the individual features added by this queue,
since all of them are in these numbers and none was measured against a build without it. The table
is the baseline for that question, not the answer to it.
