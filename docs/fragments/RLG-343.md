### RLG-343 — The horizon parallax follows the road far ahead instead of the car's own heading
- **RULED 2026-09-24T12:19:59-04:00** — THE OWNER HAS DIAGNOSED IT, 2026-09-24: "I've also kind of figured out why the horizon parallax seems weird. It's because you're moving it as the road twists and turns way down the road not where the player's car is if you get what I'm saying."

THAT IS EXACTLY WHAT THE CODE DOES, and the constant says so in its own name. `SKY_BEND_AT = ROAD_FAR` - the WHOLE draw distance, 300 segments of 200 units, sixty thousand units ahead - and the skyline's target is `-bendPx(pos + SKY_BEND_AT) * 0.55`.

AND `bendPx` MAKES IT WORSE THAN IT LOOKS, because of what it subtracts. `bendPx(z)` is `bendCache(z) - bendCache(pos) - slopeCache(pos) * ((z - pos)/BEND_STEP)`: the road's lateral offset at z, measured from the car AND with the car's own heading taken out. So `bendPx(pos)` is identically zero, and what is left at any distance is CURVATURE - how far the road deviates from the direction the car is already pointing.

SO THE SKYLINE SWINGS WHEN THE ROAD BENDS A MILE AHEAD WHILE THE CAR IS STILL GOING STRAIGHT, and does not swing when the car itself turns. That is the wrong way round, and it is why the parallax reads as the world sliding rather than as the viewer turning.

WHAT A DISTANT THING ACTUALLY PARALLAXES AGAINST IS THE VIEWER'S HEADING. A skyline miles away does not care what the road does ahead of you; it sweeps because YOU have rotated. In this engine the camera's heading is `slopeCache(pos)` - the accumulated direction of the road at the car - which is precisely the term `bendPx` SUBTRACTS.

SO THE FIX IS NOT A SMALLER `SKY_BEND_AT`. Shortening the distance would keep reading curvature and simply read less of it, which is a quieter version of the same wrong quantity. The driver has to change from the road's deviation ahead to the car's own heading.

AND THE COEFFICIENT WILL HAVE TO BE RE-TUNED, because the two quantities are in different units - 0.55 was fitted against a deviation in pixels and a heading is not one. It goes in configuration with a committed default, never an edited-in-place constant, so the owner can ask for more or less of it after seeing it move.

THIS IS A FEEL CHANGE AND ONLY THE DEVICE CAN JUDGE IT. A harness can prove the skyline now answers the car's heading rather than the road ahead - park the car on a straight with a bend far ahead and the skyline must NOT move, then turn the car and it must - but whether the amount looks right is the owner's.
- **RULED 2026-09-24T12:59:19-04:00** — BUILT IN 0.14.149, AND THE OWNER'S READING WAS EXACTLY RIGHT.

MEASURED BEFORE THE CHANGE, and one pair of samples carries the whole report. At pos 10,062 and again at pos 39,610 the car's heading was the SAME to five decimal places, -1.13110 - thirty thousand units of dead straight road - while the term the skyline was reading went from 74.58 to 145.62 and swung the horizon thirty-nine pixels. At 105,848 to 139,172 the heading moved 0.203 to 0.210 while the horizon swung forty-two. The horizon was moving while the car was not.

THE DRIVER IS `lookup(slopeCache, pos)` NOW: the direction the road points where the camera sits. `SKY_BEND_AT` is gone from the constants block, and the note there says why rather than leaving a hole.

SKY_SWING IS 60 AND IT IS A TUNABLE, not 0.55 carried across. The old coefficient was fitted against a deviation in pixels and a heading is not one. Measured over the same drive: peaks of 299.9 against 2.62, medians of 88.4 against 0.85, so 60 puts the sweep on the scale it had. `API.skySwing` moves it, because how far a horizon should sweep is taste.

`tools/sky-heading-test.py` ASSERTS AN IDENTITY, NOT A CORRELATION. The target IS the heading times the swing, everywhere - worst departure 0.0003 px over forty samples - and the owner's own case is the second assertion: over six pairs of samples whose heading matched, the horizon moved 0.0059 px while the road ahead differed by up to 223.6. Under the old driver that 223.6 would have swung it about 123 pixels. It needs no scratch build to falsify: put `-bendPx(pos + ROAD_FAR) * 0.55` back and every straight under a distant bend breaks it.

AND IT EXPOSED A WEAKNESS IN `skychase-test`, WHICH IS FIXED RATHER THAN SILENCED. That check pushes the chase 100px off target and compares how far it returns at two frame rates, as a SHARE of the larger residual. The new target is far steadier while the car drives, so both runs now converge to within a pixel or so and the share between two near-zero numbers is noise: it read 0.0641 against 0.4128 and called an 84 per cent gap a frame-counting chase, when both were 99.6 per cent converged. A floor of two pixels was added. IT DOES NOT WEAKEN THE CHECK - a chase that counts frames gets half as far at half the rate, which the original defect showed as a factor of fifteen, and cannot land under that floor.

STILL FOR THE DEVICE: whether the amount is right. A harness can prove the horizon now answers the car; only the owner can say it looks like distance.
- **RULED 2026-09-25T21:43:30-04:00** — STILL WRONG ON THE DEVICE, owner 2026-09-25: "The skyline parallax still seems weird."

WHAT IS PROVED AND WHAT IS NOT. The harness proves the target is now the car's heading times `SKY_SWING` - an identity, worst departure 0.0003px over forty samples - and that a car holding a heading does not swing the horizon, over six pairs, while the road ahead differed by up to 223.6. So the DRIVER is no longer the road far away. What was never proved is that the driver is now the RIGHT one, or that 60 is the right amount.

THREE THINGS IT COULD STILL BE, and they are separable:
1. THE AMOUNT. `SKY_SWING` is 60, chosen to match the old peak sweep. If the horizon now moves too little on a bend it will read as welded on, which is the fault RLG-096 records the city having before. One number, and `API.skySwing` moves it live.
2. THE LAG. The chase is a time constant, and a horizon that answers correctly but LATE reads as sliding. `skychase-test` measures the chase, not whether its speed suits the eye.
3. THE QUANTITY ITSELF. `slopeCache` is the ROAD's direction at the car. The camera also carries `viewShift` and `camX` - where the car sits ACROSS the road - and a driver hugging one rail is pointed slightly differently from the road's own line. If the owner is seeing the horizon shift while cornering steadily, that is candidate 1; if it shifts when they change LANE on a straight, the camera's own yaw is missing from the term.

AND THE BUILD MUST BE CONFIRMED BEFORE ANY OF THAT. The report arrived with two shots that do not show a version, and the fix shipped in 0.14.149. The SETTINGS screen carries the BUILD row; ask for it.

NOT RE-OPENED BLIND. Do not change `SKY_SWING` on a guess - ask which of the three shapes the owner is seeing, because they point at different lines.
