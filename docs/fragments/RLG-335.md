### RLG-335 — The up face reads as a slab close in, where it should stay polygonal
- **RULED 2026-09-24T00:05:40-04:00** — OWNER, 2026-09-24, from the device: "Before the one that went up was polygonal and I feel like you've changed that to be a straight wall. That's not what I wanted. I want to keep the polygon mountain line up."

THE GEOMETRY WAS NOT CHANGED AND THE APPEARANCE WAS. Nothing in this session touched `drawEndWall`, `massSlice`, `wallSides` or the ridge noise that shapes the wall's edge. What changed is [[RLG-331]]'s first cut, which pointed the scenery's ROCK FACES at the ground's tones and took fifteen levels of brightness off the nearest one - and those polygons are what reads as the mountain's shape. Darkened, they sink into the wall behind them and what is left is the wall's own flat surface. 0.14.139 puts that brightness back and the owner has not tested it.

BUT THERE IS A SECOND THING IN THE SAME PICTURE AND IT IS NOT A COLOUR. Up close, the WALL is a flat slab. It is tiled per slice with facets and ridge noise, and that works at distance where a slice is a thin band - but the nearest slices each cover a huge area of screen, so their facets are enormous flat planes and the noise that roughens the edge is far off the top of the frame. The polygonal read belongs to the scenery rocks; the wall alone has no shape close in.

SO THE QUESTION IS WHAT THE NEAR WALL SHOULD BE, and it is a design one rather than a bug. Options nobody has costed: break the near slices' facets smaller so the plane is not one surface; carry the scenery's rock polygons right up to the road edge so they cover the wall close in; or accept a smooth near wall and make sure the polygons always stand in front of it.

NOT STARTED, AND IT SHOULD WAIT FOR THE OWNER'S VERDICT ON 0.14.139 - if the brightness alone restores the read, the rest of this is unnecessary.
- **RULED 2026-09-24T12:20:01-04:00** — STILL PRESENT ON 0.14.147, AND THE OWNER HAS SENT TWO MORE SHOTS: "The same mountain ground clipping bug is still happening and also the mountain polygons are glitching in the distance." Kept at `docs/reference/shots/RLG-335-slab-a.png` and `RLG-335-slab-b.png`.

WHAT THE SHOTS SHOW, and it is two faults rather than one.

THE SLAB. In both, the left of the frame is a single pale grey mass with no facets, no rim and no foot, running from the top of the screen to the bottom and cutting across where the verge and the ground should be. It is not a mountainside seen close up - a mountainside has the form [[RLG-307]] gave it - it is a flat fill. It reaches the bottom of the frame, which is what says it is the NEAR slices of the massif rather than the far ones.

THE GLITCHING RIDGE. In the second shot the distant peaks are a scatter of overlapping triangles that do not join into a ridge line: some stand proud of their neighbours, some are cut off part way down, and the silhouette has holes in it. That is a different symptom from the slab and probably a different cause - it is at the far end of the draw where `MASS_MERGE` takes over and one quad covers a run of six slices, and a merged quad takes its heights from the run's two ENDS. A run whose middle is taller than either end loses the top of it.

NOT THE SAME AS [[RLG-336]], WHICH IS FIXED AND CONFIRMED. That was a whole band of face missing at one fixed distance, and the owner has confirmed the canyon is right now. These two are the rock that IS drawn being drawn wrong.

AND THE MERGE IS NOW A SUSPECT IN BOTH. RLG-336 was a fault in WHICH slices the merge covered; this is a question about WHAT a merged quad looks like. `massHeight(idx, j, dB)` is read at the run's two ends only, so everything between them is a straight line - which is the right saving at the far end of the draw if the run is short enough, and a visible flattening if it is not. MASS_RUN is 6.

TO MEASURE IT: `API.massModel` reports the quads and their tops. Compare the silhouette a merged run draws against the one six single slices draw, on the same road at the same place, and the difference IS the glitch. That is a pixel comparison with a control, not a judgement.
