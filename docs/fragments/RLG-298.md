### RLG-298 — The wheel follows the input, not the forces on the car
- **RULED 2026-09-20T09:20:34-04:00** — OWNER, 2026-09-20: 'the steering wheel rotates based on the movement of the car e.g. all the forces acting upon it, but it should only follow the inputs to the front wheels' - and 'there would be some force feedback based on the friction of the road, coming back up through the tires through the steering column.' So the wheel in the cockpit is driven by the STEERING INPUT, not by the car's lateral movement, with a small feedback term from the road's grip on top - a shiver over a rough or wet surface, a kick when a tyre lets go - rather than the wheel following the slide. Find what the wheel's angle reads today (it is expected to be the car's lateral velocity or camX) and separate the two terms: input, then feedback, each a tunable with a committed default. Not started.
- **RULED 2026-09-20T10:39:25-04:00** — BUILT 2026-09-20, v0.14.101. THE RIM NOW SHOWS THE LOCK STILL OUTSTANDING. `stepWheel` read the car's lateral movement, which is the sum of every force on it - a shunt from behind, a corner running the car wide, another car pushing it out of a pack, and a wet road carrying it past its mark all turned the rim, and none of them is the driver steering. It reads `steerAim - playerX` now: the mark the front wheels are pointed at, against where the car has got to. It winds on the instant a lane is asked for, unwinds as the car arrives, is zero when the car is settled, and is zero pinned against the shoulder with the thumb still dragging, because the aim and the car clamp to the same edge. That last one is the ORIGINAL defect the movement model was introduced to fix, and it stays fixed without reading the car's movement to do it.

WHY `steerAim` AND NOT `targetX`, WHICH IS THE PURER READING OF THIS RULING. The aim is the thumb's mark plus `slideX`, the distance a slippery road will carry the car past it. The slide DOES NOT FADE by design (RLG-048), so against `targetX` the car comes to rest past the mark and the rim would hold that much counter-lock for as long as the driver held the lane - `SLIP.hold` is 1.20 and `WET.floor` leaves 0.66 of slick, which is 0.79 of a lane, PAST FULL LOCK ON A STATIONARY CAR. Against the aim it is zero at rest, and the slide instead shows as extra lock carried for longer on a road that will not hold the car. That is the road at the rim, which is what the second half of the ruling asked for.

THE FEEDBACK IS A SECOND TERM AND IT IS CAPPED. A shiver whose amplitude is how little grip is left - the wet, the verge at `WHEEL.rough`, and `cornerLoad`, which is how hard the corner is already working the tyres (RLG-099) - built from two sines at rates that do not divide into each other so it never settles into a beat, and scaled by speed so a stopped car has a dead wheel. Plus a kick from the rate at which the road is carrying the car past its mark, which is the one number in the lateral block that is grip failing rather than the driver asking. Eight tunables with committed defaults in `WHEEL`, live through `API.wheelModel`.

THE PROOF, AND WHAT MADE IT HARD. Under a first-order lag the car's lateral VELOCITY is about `STEER.snap` times the outstanding ERROR, so the two models agree up to a gain on every ordinary lane change. A harness that steers and watches the rim move scores a pass on either one. THE FIRST VERSION OF THE CHECK PASSED ITS OWN FALSIFY ARM: it shoved the car right and asked whether the rim went left over the following 400ms, and it does under BOTH models, because with the aim still at zero the car is steered back leftwards and the movement model reads that. What separates them is the ONE step on which the car has been moved and has not yet been steered back - the movement model turns right there and the demand model turns left. AND IT HAS TO BE A STEP, NOT A FRAME: `FIXED` is 1/120 against a 60Hz display, so `frameLoop` advances the world twice per animation frame and a reading taken on `requestAnimationFrame` folds the flick into the recovery - measured at -0.014 where the step itself reads +0.117. `API.wheelTrace` fills one row per step for that reason, and `API.steps` publishes the count. tools/wheel-angle-test.py measured -0.117, -0.120, -0.117 across three runs and +0.117, +0.117, +0.112 across three falsified ones - a clean sign flip with nothing else on the sheet changing.

`API.shove` is new and is the affordance the proof needed: it moves the CAR and leaves the aim where it was, which is what a shunt does. Every other force in this engine is applied to `targetX`, so it moves the aim and the car together and cannot tell the two models apart.

WHAT NO HARNESS CAN JUDGE, most likely wrong first: whether the shiver reads as a road surface or as a loose steering rack - it is `WHEEL.grain` at 0.055 and it peaks at 0.036 of lock on snow and 0.086 on the verge; whether `WHEEL.lock` at 0.55 of a lane is the right amount of wheel for a lane change, because it decides the whole gain and the old model's gain was about three times larger; and whether the kick from a tyre letting go is felt at all at `WHEEL.kick` 0.85 capped at 0.35. Each is one constant.
