### RLG-221 — The ambulance is a box on a cab, and the box is the outline at both ends

**RULED 2026-09-12.** Owner, with a box-ambulance reference: a **red light bar across the top of the
box**, **three amber marker lamps on the cab roof**, a **very tall dark windscreen**, **mirrors on
arms**, a **grille with red in it**, and a **black bumper with a white plate**. And the body is a
**box wider than the cab**.

Third and last of the three items the thread carried in order, after [[RLG-216]] and [[RLG-219]].

---

## THE WIDENING IS AN OUTLINE CHANGE AND THE OWNER WAS TOLD SO BEFORE IT WAS BUILT

[[RLG-184]] declares the outline once and both painters trace it, so anything that changes the shape
changes the tail as well. The owner was told that and agreed to plan it that way — **which costs
nothing here, because the box IS the back.** There is no version of this vehicle where the two ends
disagree about how wide it is.

**AND THE BOX ENCLOSES EVERYTHING, WHICH IS WHAT MAKES IT LEGAL.** From the front you see the cab with
the box standing proud above it and to each side; from behind you see the box alone. Both silhouettes
are the same rectangle. The cab is a **detail inside it**, which is exactly what the invariant leaves
free — and it is what the hatchback learned the hard way when a rear-only shape change gave its face
a bus windscreen ([[RLG-216]]).

Measured: **188 × 174 at both ends, 0.0% silhouette error, identical ink boxes.** It was 192 × 166.

## WHERE THE NUMBERS LIVE

`ambBox(h)` owns every dimension the two ends share — the box, the cab, the mirror line. Neither
painter invents one. Written twice they drift, and this file has been caught by that four times.

Each painter is its own block, reached before the van's face rather than as a test inside it. The
first pass put an `isAmb` branch inside the van's grille code and the two vehicles' faces were
interleaved, so every later edit to one had to be reasoned about against the other. **They are two
faces on one rig and they read better as two blocks.**

## THE MIRRORS ARE FRONT-ONLY, AND THAT IS CORRECT RATHER THAN A SHORTCUT

A mirror is outline, not furniture — the note above `carMirrors` says so, and the van's stalks are in
the shared rig for that reason ([[RLG-219]]). **Here the box is wider than the mirrors**, so they fall
inside the outline instead of defining it. That is also what you actually see: from directly behind an
ambulance, the box hides its own mirrors.

**They were on the cab roof in the first build** and read as two marks floating off the corners of the
box. A door mirror sits at eye level, which on this cab is the middle of the glass.

## THE BAR KEEPS THE FLEET'S SPAN, AND THAT IS THE NEAR MISS WORTH RECORDING

The first build drew this bar 0.150 to 0.850, to look proportionate on a box wider than a van. **It
would have been wrong for a reason that is nowhere near the drawing.** `drawCopLights` throws the
bar's WASH onto the road from one hardcoded pair of offsets, and the note beside them says why it
can: *"both cars agree to three decimals, because both bars are drawn from the same 0.24-0.76 span."*
A wider bar here would have put an ambulance's red wash either side of the lamps casting it.

So the bar keeps the fleet's span and only its **height** moves, which is what `barY` on the BODY
record exists to carry.

**`barY` IS MEASURED, NOT DERIVED.** The note that introduced it says the two existing values were
sampled from the built sprites and that "any future force car declares its own" — so this one was
sampled the same way, off the finished sprite: **the bar's red rows are 3 to 8 of 176, mid 0.0313,
against a declared 0.032.** The CRUISER, measured by the same probe, reads 0.125 against its
long-standing 0.122 — which is what says the method agrees with the values already trusted, rather
than only with itself.

## THE TRIM ON THE SCREEN

The windscreen went in at 0.235 of the sprite and came back to **0.215**, because at 0.235 the red
cross had two pixels of air above the grille and a cross touching a grille reads as part of it. A
van's screen is 0.145, so the owner's *"very tall"* survives the trim by half again.

## WHAT THIS DOES NOT COVER

**How it reads at the size an ambulance is actually drawn on the road is the owner's verdict on the
device.** The sheet is a desktop render at roughly three times that.

**The roof has three pixels of headroom.** The bar's top row is 3 of 176. Anything added above the box
clips, and the sprite box would have to grow — which is a shared table, so it would move the van too.

**`ambulance-test` is road-dependent and failed once on this build before passing.** *"An ambulance
turns up in ordinary traffic"* found none in a whole drive, then found one on 113 samples on the next
run, with nothing changed. Traffic spawning is random and that check is a lottery the same way
`occlusion-test` is ([[RLG-062]]) — **do not read one run of it as a result**, in either direction.

**`tools/bar-row.py` does not run.** It waits on `window.__road.fleet`, which the probe does not
expose — the fleet is on `window.__probe.road`. It is the tool whose whole job is measuring `barY`,
so the measurement above had to be taken by a throwaway probe instead. Left as found rather than
fixed inside this unit; it is a separate, small job.
