### RLG-306 — A mountain biome has no face at all on the approach, and wants a low-polygon mountainside
- **RULED 2026-09-21T00:15:45-04:00** — OWNER, 2026-09-21, having tested v0.14.114: 'The mountain face as you approach a mountain biome is completely missing. It just can be a straight sheet face. We need to essentially build a (low) polygonal mountain side for that whole side of the biome. We also need to see it correctly in the mirror as we leave.'

THIS IS THE VERDICT ON [[RLG-303]] AND IT IS NOT A PASS. That ruling took the boundary face OFF the mountain - the owner turned it down on sight as a disconnected curtain - and replaced it with growth: `bioGrow` ramps the wall and the drop from nothing over `GROW_BAND`. The growth is the right half of the answer and it is not the whole of it. Withdrawing the face left the approach with NOTHING to drive at, and the owner is saying so in the first sentence.

AND IT IS NOT A REQUEST TO PUT THE OLD FACE BACK. 'It just can be a straight sheet face' is the same complaint RLG-303 recorded - one quad per column at one z, a billboard with no depth and a flat-bottomed cut across the ground. What is asked for now is larger than either: a LOW-POLYGON MOUNTAINSIDE ALONG THE WHOLE SIDE OF THE BIOME, not an object at the boundary. That is a form, not a facing.

WHAT THAT MEANS FOR THE CODE, READ ONCE AND NOT YET TESTED. The wall beside the road is already a plane with a ridge on its top edge and facets on its surface (RLG-297), tiled per slice. A low-polygon mountainside is that same surface given real breaks across it as well as along it - faces at different angles meeting at edges, rather than one plane shaded by noise. The ridge noise and the facet term are the groundwork; what is missing is that the surface has no geometry across its own width.

THE MIRROR IS PART OF THE ASK AND NOT A FOLLOW-UP. 'We also need to see it correctly in the mirror as we leave.' The glass already draws the wall and the boundary face, and RLG-304 found the mirror reading the wall's height wrong for as long as it had existed. Whatever is built here is built in both views at once, and proved in both.

SEE ALSO [[RLG-312]], which asks for a wall of trees on the same two faces in a wooded place, and [[RLG-307]], the canyon's faces not meeting its walls. The three are the same seam.

Not started.
- **RULED 2026-09-21T20:04:44-04:00** — IN PROGRESS 2026-09-21 under UNT-410, NOT COMMITTED. Recorded now so a context clear does not lose the design.

WHAT THE PICTURE SHOWED ON 0.14.117, driving desert into mountain at 236, 136, 65 and 23 segments before the boundary: nothing, nothing, a thin grey sliver, and then a single dark NEEDLE - the wall is a plane at the road's edge, and growing it out of the ground (RLG-303) means you look along it almost edge-on while it rises. There was never a surface that faces the approaching driver.

THE DESIGN BUILT: A LOW-POLYGON MASS. The wall side of the road is a heightfield - a foot at the road's edge, a cliff, then slopes and peaks out to 40 road half-widths so it reaches the screen edge at the far end of the draw (`MASS_L`, `MASS_H`). Every height is multiplied by `bioGrow`, so arriving is still the land going up (RLG-303 kept), and now the land has a FACE that faces you while it goes up. It is drawn ONE ROAD SLICE AT A TIME in the wall block, outer column first, exactly where the old wall was - which keeps the foot on the road through every bend and keeps crest occlusion right for free. The low-polygon look comes from the HEIGHTS being straight lines across runs of `MASS_RUN` = 6 segments and each run of each column taking ONE flat shade from its facet's normal against `MASS_LIGHT` (`massFacet`, cached per frame). A place opts in with `mass:1`; `massOff` puts the old wall back for comparison.

TWO FAULTS FOUND AND FIXED IN THE PROTOTYPE: the seams between slices showed as vertical STRIPES on the canyon because the first lap pushed the far edge up the screen, and a cliff's seams are vertical - it laps along the road now. And a side 'flip' at the crossing turned out to be a misreading of thumbnails; at full size both sides of the boundary agree.

STILL TO DO: the MIRROR, which the owner asked for in as many words; a proof harness; a frame-rate measurement against the old wall, because RLG-299 is queued and this adds quads; and the owner's look.
- **RULED 2026-09-21T21:37:56-04:00** — BUILT in v0.14.118, and UNSIGNED (RLG-116).

THE MOUNTAIN IS A LOW-POLYGON SURFACE. A heightfield along the wall side of the road, from a foot at the road's edge through a cliff to slopes and peaks out to 40 road half-widths, grown out of the ground by `bioGrow` so RLG-303 stands. Drawn one road slice at a time by `massSlice`, which keeps the foot on the road through every bend; flat facets from heights that are straight lines across 6-segment runs, each run of each column taking one shade from its normal against the light. The front, the side and the ridge are one surface.

AND IT IS THERE OVER A HILL, WHICH THE WALL NEVER WAS. The wall had been drawn inside the crest test, so a slice whose ROAD was hidden drew no wall - and a mountain is tall enough to stand above the hill in front of it. Measured on four raised-hill roads with that behaviour put back: 149, 0, 237 and 172 slices of mountain out of about 237 - one road had no mountain at all. With the mass drawn for hidden slices and for road inverted by under a pixel: 238 to 242 every time.

IN THE MIRROR, AS THE OWNER ASKED. Once a crossing completed, every segment behind the car reported the NEW place, so the glass could never show where you had been. `growLeft` remembers the place left at `growFrom`; `bioBehind` answers the mirror, and `groundTone` gives that place its own ground behind its end. With the old answer put back: 0 mass quads in the glass and desert-coloured ground behind a mountain.

THE COST WAS MEASURED AND CUT BEFORE SHIPPING. In one page, software rendering, the canyon first ran 31.5-34.7 fps against the old wall's 50.7-60.7. Culling quads wholly off the frame and painting one quad per run past 40 per cent of the draw (`MASS_MERGE`) brought it to 42.7-57.8, overlapping the wall. GPU off, so the numbers are relative within one page and say nothing about a phone.

WHAT IS THE OWNER'S: the look - it takes the mountain's own dark ground colour, and a snowy mountain's facets are faint - and whether it reads as a mountain. `MASS_H`, `MASS_L`, `MASS_SHADE` and `MASS_JAG` are the constants. NOT DONE: the valley range across the drop still clips through it on a twisting road (RLG-316).
