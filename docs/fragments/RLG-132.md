### RLG-132 — Wet and snow grip is realistic and less fun - dial it back
- **RULED 2026-08-31T21:04:06-04:00** — OWNER, 2026-08-31, after playing through all the tournaments: "The slipperiness that a wet or snowy
ground creates although realistic, I think makes the game less fun. I don't necessarily want to take
it out of the game, but maybe we just dial it back a bunch I don't know what are your thoughts on
this?"

THE MODEL TODAY, which is one line and is the whole of it:

    wetGrip()  = max(0.34, 1 - wet*(snowy?0.42:0.22) - settle*0.30 - pool*0.26)
    wetBrake() = max(0.32, 1 - wet*(snowy?0.38:0.18) - settle*0.28 - pool*0.24)

WHAT THAT COMES OUT AS, at the cases a player actually meets:

    heavy snow, ground covered   1 - 0.42 - 0.30 = 0.28, clamped to the floor of 0.34
    heavy rain, water lying      1 - 0.22 - 0.26 = 0.52
    ordinary shower, some water  about 0.69
    a tundra with NO weather     0.856, from the settled floor alone
    a mountain with NO weather   0.928, and this is NEW - see below

So a snowy road leaves the car at about a THIRD of its dry steering authority, and it stays there.

THE ANSWER TO THE QUESTION IS YES, AND THE MORE USEFUL ANSWER IS THAT THE SLOPE IS NOT THE PROBLEM.
Three things are wrong with the shape of it, and only the third is about the size of the numbers:

  IT IS A FLAT TAX ON THE CAR RATHER THAN A LIMIT THE DRIVER CAN DRIVE UNDER. `wetGrip()` multiplies
  the steering response directly, so a wet road does not make the car HARDER TO DRIVE WELL - it makes
  it a less capable car, all the time, whatever the player does. That is the difference between
  slipperiness that is fun and slipperiness that is a penalty: the fun version is grip you lose when
  you are greedy, and this version cannot be earned back by driving neatly.

  IT COMPOUNDS THREE WAYS AND TWO OF THEM ARE OUTSIDE THE PLAYER'S CONTROL. Falling weather, settled
  snow and standing water all subtract at once, and `settle` and `pool` both INTEGRATE over time - a
  long fall drives the total to the clamp and holds it there for the rest of the biome. The worst
  case is not a moment, it is a state you sit in.

  AND THE FLOOR IS TOO LOW. 0.34 is the number the snow case actually lands on, so in practice the
  floor IS the snow model rather than a safety net under it.

WHAT TO DO, in the order the effort is worth it:

  RAISE THE FLOOR FIRST, because it is one number and it is what the snow case hits. 0.34 to about
  0.62 halves the worst case at a stroke and changes nothing about rain, which never reaches the
  clamp. This is the cheapest thing to put on a device and the owner can judge it in one run.

  THEN CUT THE ACCUMULATION TERMS rather than the falling-weather ones. `settle*0.30` and `pool*0.26`
  are the parts that persist, that the player cannot drive out of, and that make a long snowfall
  progressively worse for no decision they made. Roughly halving both keeps the moment weather
  arrives sharp while removing the tax that follows it around.

  AND THEN BUILD [[RLG-048]], WHICH IS ALREADY RULED AND UNBUILT. The owner ruled on 2026-08-28 that
  "wet slip should be driven by the steering DELTA not by distance-to-target". That is exactly the
  first problem above stated as a mechanism: grip lost when you SNATCH at the wheel rather than grip
  removed because it is raining. Dialling the numbers back and building RLG-048 are the same fix
  approached from two ends, and together they turn a wet road from less car into a car that will let
  go if you ask too much of it - which is the version worth keeping in the game.

THE RECOMMENDATION IS THEREFORE: DIAL IT BACK, AND KEEP IT. Do not remove it. Take the floor and the
two accumulation terms now, feel it on the device, and treat RLG-048 as the real answer once the
numbers are somewhere comfortable.

AND ONE CONSEQUENCE OF [[RLG-109]] BELONGS IN THIS DECISION, because it landed in the build the owner
has not yet driven. The settled snow floor now DERIVES from a place's temperature instead of being
typed into the tundra alone, so THE MOUNTAIN NOW CARRIES 0.24 OF PERMANENT GROUND SNOW WHERE IT
CARRIED NONE. Through `settle*0.30` that is a standing 7% grip penalty in every mountain, arriving in
the same build as this complaint and not present when the owner formed it. If the mountain feels
greasy in 0.10.38, that is why, and `SNOW_FLOOR_K` is the one number that moves it.

WHAT CANNOT BE SETTLED HERE. Every figure above is arithmetic off one line of code. Whether the
result is FUN is a device judgment and nothing in the harness suite can stand in for it. Note also
that [[RLG-055]] is blocked on a brake and grip measurement that has never been taken, and this work
should not start by inventing numbers that measurement would have supplied - but the floor is a
single constant and does not need it.

NOT STARTED. No number has been changed.
- **RULED 2026-08-31T22:05:19-04:00** — THE DIAL-BACK WAS BUILT AND THEN REVERTED, ON THE OWNER'S CORRECTION, AND THAT IS THE RECORD.

WHAT THIS FRAGMENT RECOMMENDED: raise the floor, halve the two accumulation terms, then build
[[RLG-048]]. The first two were done - floor 0.34 to 0.55, `settle` 0.30 to 0.14, `pool` 0.26 to 0.12
- and measured: heavy snow on settled ground went from 0.34 to 0.60.

THE OWNER'S CORRECTION, 2026-08-31: "The original numbers may not actually necessarily need to be
dialed back. It's just that the implementation we were using before was very unwieldy. Once this new
implementation is in, we might need a dial in new numbers for it to be a hindrance, but still fun and
playable."

THAT IS RIGHT AND THIS FRAGMENT'S DIAGNOSIS WAS HALF WRONG. Three faults were named here and the
first one - that grip is a flat tax on the car rather than something a driver can answer - was the
real one. The other two, that the terms compound and that the floor is too low, are consequences of
the SIZE and the size was never what made it unfun. RLG-048 answers the first, and with it answered
the numbers may be exactly right.

SO THE ORIGINAL NUMBERS ARE BACK, and changing the model and the numbers in one breath would also
have made it impossible to say which of the two did anything.

WHAT SURVIVES FROM THIS FRAGMENT: `WET` is now a struct rather than two lines of literals and is live
through `API.wetModel`, which is what makes dialling it in on a device one call rather than a
rebuild. That is the useful half and it is why the reverted work was not simply thrown away.

AND ONE OBSERVATION STANDS FOR WHOEVER DIALS IT. Heavy snow on settled ground computes 0.28 and the
floor catches it at 0.34, so in that one case THE FLOOR IS THE MODEL rather than a net under it. It
only matters if the owner wants the worst case to differ from the second-worst. slip-test reports it
rather than asserting on it, because it is a tuning judgment and not a defect.
