### RLG-216 — A badge goes on paint, and the hatchback's tailgate glass has none under it

**RULED 2026-09-12.** Owner, looking at the fleet sheet: the hatchback's badge sits on the tailgate
glass and must drop below it. The saloon and the coupe wear theirs on painted metal and the hatch
should too.

---

## WHAT WAS WRONG

The rear painter drew the marque at one height for the whole fleet, `deckY + 0.088`. On every other
body that is the boot lid — painted metal between the lamps, which is where a badge belongs. The
hatchback has no boot lid. [[RLG-213]] gave it a tailgate: one pane carrying on down from the
greenhouse, from `deckY - 0.012` to `deckY + (bot - deckY)*0.30`, which on this body is 0.50h to
0.628h. The badge at 0.588h was inside it, and a marque floating on a rear window reads as a sticker
rather than as a badge.

**The height was right for five bodies and wrong for the sixth, and nothing said so.** No check
looked at what the badge was standing on, because until this body existed there was only one answer.

## WHAT IT IS NOW

The hatch alone wears its badge **level with the lamps** — the first painted panel below the glass,
and where a real hatchback's badge sits. The lamp clusters stop at 0.315 and start again at 0.685 of
the width, so the middle of the car is clear for it.

The height is read from `ly` and `lh`, the lamps' own position and depth, rather than written as a
number. If the lamps ever move the badge follows them, which is the relationship the drawing
actually has. Every other body keeps `deckY + 0.088` untouched.

## AND THE STREAK ON THE TAILGATE IS GONE

A raked quad of flat `rgba(255,255,255,.09)` was drawn across the lower half of the pane to be the
light the sky lays on it. On the fleet sheet it read as a hard-edged triangle cut **out** of the
glass rather than as light lying on it, in all seven rear lamp states.

**Softening it did not fix it.** Narrowing it and fading it to nothing at the foot of the pane still
left a wedge, because the edge was never the fault. The tailgate and the greenhouse are **one pane**
— that is the whole reason [[RLG-213]] draws it from the same three colour stops — and the greenhouse
above carries no streak. A highlight on the lower half alone declares a seam across the middle of a
pane that has none.

So the streak is removed and the glass is continuous. The comment left in its place says that if
light on this pane is ever wanted, it has to run the full height of the greenhouse and the tailgate
together as one drawing, or it will say the same wrong thing again.

## THE PROOF, AND THE TWO VACUOUS ONES BEFORE IT

`tools/badge-test.py` reads pixels out of the built rear sprite. Testing the arithmetic against
itself would have proved nothing: the arithmetic is the fault. Two questions, of the sprite's centre
column, where the badge is:

1. **the glass is unbroken** between its top and its foot — a badge on the glass is a bright island
   in a dark column;
2. **the badge still exists, below the glass** — the centre column differs from the plain paint
   beside it. Without this, question 1 passes by deleting the badge.

`--falsify` serves the engine with the badge back at the boot-lid height. Both questions fail.

**THE FIRST TWO BUILDS OF THIS CHECK WERE VACUOUS AND THE SECOND PASSED ITS OWN FALSIFIER.**

- Taking the **last dark row** of the centre column put the foot of the glass at row 161 of 164: the
  tyre and the shadow under the car are dark as well. The badge was never inside the range examined.
  It reported the fault, and for the wrong reason.
- Taking the **contiguous dark run from the top** fixed that and broke the check. A badge on the
  glass is bright, so the run simply stops where the badge starts and the range is unbroken by
  construction. With the defect served back in it printed *glass unbroken rows 28-92* and passed.

What works is measuring the glass on a column **the badge cannot reach**. The pane is wider than the
badge at every height, so a reference column at 0.42 of the width is inside the glass at every row
and carries nothing drawn on it. Its dark run is the glass whether the badge is misplaced or not,
and the centre column is then judged against a range it did not help to define.

**That is the same shape of error the thread already carried three times from 2026-09-12.** A
falsifier proves the harness *can* report the other answer; it does not prove the harness is looking
at the thing the player meets. Here the harness could report the other answer and was looking at a
range the defect moved.

## WHAT THIS DOES NOT COVER

`car-ends-test`, `lamp-test`, `smoke-test` and `drive-test` all ran. The change is three `drawMarque`
y-arguments and one deleted fill, so nothing physics can read, and `drive-test` fails the same single
Motorsport check — the nitrous settle — **with the change stashed out**: 100.4 per cent of top end at
1.5 seconds unchanged, against 98.1 per cent with the change in. Pre-existing, and not from this
work.

**The fleet sheet is a desktop render.** How the badge reads at the size a car is actually drawn on a
phone is the owner's verdict on the device, not a green run's.
