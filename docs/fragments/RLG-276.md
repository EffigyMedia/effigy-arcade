### RLG-276 — A rival snaps to a new speed as the count reaches zero
- **RULED 2026-09-16T04:00:09-04:00** — FOUND 2026-09-16 AND NOT FIXED. It is NEWLY VISIBLE rather than new: [[RLG-275]] repaired an `API.grid` that two definitions had been fighting over, and the whole rolling-start section of `launch-test` had been unreachable behind a TypeError for as long as that lasted. With the line-up readable again those checks run, and one of them fails:

    the largest speed step at GO itself: 506, against 421 gained over the next second
    FAIL  and no rival snaps to a new speed as the count reaches zero

A RIVAL GAINS MORE SPEED IN THE SINGLE FRAME THE COUNT RELEASES than it gains in the whole second of racing that follows. That is the shape [[RLG-118]] was written to remove - a field that is handed speed at GO rather than accelerating into it - and the check was put there to catch exactly this.

IT IS NOT CAUSED BY RLG-274. That ruling changes where the PLAYER lines up and it only acts inside a tournament from round two on; `launch-test` presses MODE once, which is SINGLE RACE, so `gridSlot()` returns the back of the grid and the geometry is what it has always been. The rivals' positions and their pace spread are unchanged on that path - the same `z` values and the same `base` values, in a different array order. Verified by reading the harness rather than by measuring, which is the weaker of the two and is stated here as such.

WHAT TO MEASURE FIRST. `rollField` holds every rival at `startSpeed()` while the count runs, and `stepRacers` takes over at GO with `aiAccel`. The suspects are that the two do not meet at the same number, or that the first stepped frame is handed a `dt` that covers the whole count. A single 506-unit step against 421 a second is close to one frame of a very large `dt`, which points at the second.

NOT STARTED. The fix belongs with the start line rather than with the grid order, and nothing should be tuned until the mechanism is measured - [[RLG-118]]'s own lesson is that the flying start was arithmetic rather than a tuning fault.
