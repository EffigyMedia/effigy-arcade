### RLG-134 — The centre-screen messages block the road ahead
- **RULED 2026-08-31T21:04:58-04:00** — OWNER, 2026-08-31, after playing through all the tournaments: "the UI elements that pop up in the
dead center of the screen block my view of the upcoming road, so I think we should move that to just
under the other information that's below the rearview mirror."

THIS ANSWERS THE QUESTION [[RLG-082]] WAS WAITING ON. That fragment recorded the owner asking for
"the anchored relative spacing of UI elements" and could not be started, because which elements on
which screen was not known and the note said it must be asked with a capture rather than guessed.
This is the answer, arrived at by playing: it is the transient messages, on the driving screen, and
they are in the wrong PLACE rather than the wrong size.

THERE ARE TWO SEPARATE MECHANISMS PRINTING IN THE MIDDLE OF THE SCREEN, and both have to move or half
the messages will still be over the road.

  THE BANNER, `#warn`, is a DOM element and it is styled at `top:38%` in both `interstate.html` and
  `motorsport.html`. `flashWarn()` writes to it. This is what says CHECKPOINT, ROADBLOCK, BUSTED,
  WRONG WAY and the biome name as you arrive in a new place.

  THE FLOATING LABELS are canvas text in the `fx` array, pushed at `x:W/2` with `y` between `H*0.50`
  and `H*0.62`, and they drift upward. PIT MANOEUVRE, THREADED THE GAP, CRUISER DOWN and the
  checkpoint time award are these. There are four push sites in `road.js`.

WHERE THEY SHOULD GO IS ALREADY A COMPUTED POSITION, which makes this cheaper than it looks. The
information row under the mirror is `.toprow`, and it is placed at

    padding-top: calc(var(--mirror-top,6px) + var(--mirror-h,44px) + 8px * var(--ark-ui,1))

so the shell already publishes where the bottom of the mirror is. The messages should hang off the
same two variables rather than off a new percentage - a percentage is what put them at 38% in the
first place, and it is why they land on the road on one phone and not on another.

FOUR THINGS THAT WILL BITE:

  A MACHINE MUST NEVER DEFINE `--stage-h` OR `--safe-top`. `--mirror-h` and `--mirror-top` are the
  right variables to read and neither of those two may be declared in a cabinet's own `:root`.

  THE CANVAS LABELS DO NOT SEE CSS. `fx` is drawn into the canvas, so it cannot read `--mirror-h`
  directly; the pixel row has to be passed in or computed the same way the mirror's own geometry is.
  The two mechanisms must land in the same band or the move will look like a bug.

  `#warn` IS FULL-WIDTH AND `pointer-events:none`, and the comment above it records why: while a
  warning was on screen it silently swallowed every tap in its band. Whatever it becomes must keep
  that, and moving it into the busier top of the screen makes it matter more, not less.

  MOTORSPORT HIDES IT IN THE PITS - `body.in-pit #warn{display:none!important}`. That must survive.

AND THE READABILITY TRADE SHOULD BE PUT TO THE OWNER RATHER THAN DECIDED HERE. Directly under the
mirror is the busiest part of the screen: the mirror above it, the information row in it, and now
transient messages as well. The messages are currently large - `clamp(20px,6.4vw,30px)` - because
they are alone in the middle of the frame, and they will almost certainly need to be smaller where
they are going. That is a judgment on a device.

`interstate.html` and `motorsport.html` BOTH CARRY THE RULE and both must change. `quietus` and
`hardpoint` have no `#warn`.

NOT STARTED.
- **RULED 2026-08-31T21:43:31-04:00** — BUILT, 0.10.41. Both mechanisms moved and they read one number.

THE BAND IS MEASURED, NOT CALCULATED FROM THE NUMBERS THAT PLACED THE ROW. `.toprow` already anchors
itself under the glass with `--mirror-top` and `--mirror-h`, so its own BOTTOM is the honest answer to
"just under the other information". `publishMsgBand()` reads that rect each frame and publishes
`--msg-top`; the canvas labels ask `msgY()` for the same number. Reconstructing the position from the
mirror height and a guess at the row's height would have been a second copy of a layout that already
exists, and this project has a scar about exactly that - `mirror-shot.py` carried its own copy of the
mirror formula and it went three changes out of date.

THE CANVAS AND THE DOM ARE THE SAME UNIT, which is what makes one number possible. `W` and `H` come
from the canvas's bounding rect in CSS pixels and the backing store is scaled by the device ratio
separately, so a DOM measurement and a canvas coordinate are directly comparable. The check asserts
this rather than trusting it.

THE LABELS ALSO HAD TO STOP CLIMBING. They drifted up at 60 pixels a second over about 1.2 seconds -
some 75 pixels - which was fine from the middle of an empty windscreen and would carry them straight
back into the mirror from the new band. They start 16 pixels into the band and drift 26 a second, so
they stay in it.

AND THE BANNER IS SMALLER. It was `clamp(20px,6.4vw,30px)`, sized to be read across an empty
windscreen. Under the mirror it shares the busiest band on the screen with the glass and the
information row, so it is `clamp(15px,4.6vw,22px)`. THIS IS THE PART MOST LIKELY TO BE WRONG and it is
a device judgment: too small to read at a glance is a different failure from being in the way.

MEASURED at 480x900: the mirror ends at 66, the information row runs to 126, and the band is at 136 in
Interstate and 126 in Motorsport. Both clear the horizon. At 360x780 the mirror ends at 57 and the
band follows to 116.

THE CHECK IS `tools/message-band-test.py` AND ITS FIRST VERSION WAS WEAK. It compared the canvas
labels with the `--msg-top` VARIABLE, which is the engine agreeing with itself: a cabinet whose
stylesheet still read `top:38%` would publish a correct variable, ignore it, and pass. It compares the
labels with the banner's own rect now.

FALSIFIED, and the result is the reason that mattered. With `top:38%` put back in Interstate the
banner sat at 328 while the labels sat at 136 - and EVERY OTHER CHECK STILL PASSED. "Below the row",
"in the top half" and "clears the horizon" are all true of 328 on a 900-tall screen. One check stands
between this and a half-moved interface.

smoke-test 6 of 6, menu-test 4 of 4, drive-test 39 of 39 on two runs of three; the third read 38 and
that is [[RLG-056]]'s known one-in-four speed assertion, which the thread says not to answer by moving
the number.

NOT JUDGED ON A DEVICE. Whether the smaller type is still readable at a glance, and whether three
things stacked under the glass is busier than the owner wants, are the two questions a harness cannot
answer.

AND THIS CLOSES THE QUESTION [[RLG-082]] WAS BLOCKED ON, which was which elements on which screen.
RLG-082 asked for anchored relative spacing in general; what was actually wrong was two transient
mechanisms placed by a percentage. Whether anything ELSE on these screens is still placed that way has
not been swept for.
