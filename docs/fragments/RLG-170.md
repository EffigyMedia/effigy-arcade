### RLG-170 — A pursuit you stay in earns heat by the mile

Owner, 2026-09-08: "We can also increment 2 heat per mile while actively pursued."

**THE OTHER THREE EARNERS ARE EVENTS AND THIS ONE IS A DISTANCE**, which is the whole
point of it. Being seen, running a roadblock and putting a cruiser out are things that
HAPPEN, and a driver good enough to hold a pursuit open without any of them occurring
again was in a chase that could not get worse. This is the floor under that: a chase you
stay in escalates on its own.

`HEAT_PER_MILE = 2`, charged continuously against the road travelled rather than against
the clock — the owner asked for it by the mile, and a distance cannot be farmed by sitting
still. It accrues on the same `chased` test the cooling clock uses (a live cruiser, on you,
inside `LOST_AT`), so a parked trap you have not tripped and a cruiser you have
comprehensively lost both earn nothing. See [[RLG-169]] for the rates it sits beside.

**IT IS SLOW ON PURPOSE AND THE ARITHMETIC SHOULD BE STATED PLAINLY.** At 150mph two
points a mile is one point every twelve seconds, so a star costs fifty miles and about
twenty minutes of unbroken pursuit. Over a chase of a minute or two it adds a few points to
what the events earn. It is pressure, not a source, and it only matters to a player who
keeps one running — which is exactly the player it was asked for. If it reads as too weak
on the device, the number is a single tunable.

**NO `why` IS PASSED WITH THE CHARGE.** `heatWhy` names the last thing that raised the
level for the player to read, and a trickle charged sixty times a second would overwrite a
takedown or a roadblock within a frame of it happening.

**THE MILEPOST IS CARRIED FORWARD WHETHER OR NOT THE PURSUIT CODE RUNS.** `heatMiZ` is
updated outside the `!optEasy && !held` block deliberately: with HOT PURSUIT off, or during
the count-in, nothing inside it executes, and a milepost left behind would bill every mile
driven in the meantime the moment a cruiser found the player again. Forty quiet miles would
have arrived as eighty points in one frame. On a reset the guard `pos > heatMiZ` covers the
same hazard from the other direction, because the position falls rather than rises.

### The check, and the two ways it was wrong before it was right

`heatpoints-test.py` gained a two-armed measurement: the same three miles covered twice, once
with the road empty and once with a cruiser pinned on the player. **The clean arm is what
makes it evidence** — a charge that fired whether or not anyone was chasing would pass a
check that only ever drives with a cruiser behind it.

**IT PASSED WITH THE FEATURE SWITCHED OFF, and that is the finding worth keeping.** The
expected value was computed from the rate the engine reports, so with `HEAT_PER_MILE` set to
0 the check expected nothing, got nothing, and called that agreement. Watched going green on
an engine with the mechanism disabled. The fix is two assertions rather than one: the hot
arm must earn heat **the clean arm did not** — which is the mechanism EXISTING, and cannot
be satisfied by a zero — and only then must the amount match the declared rate, which is
what catches a wrong constant. A check that derives its expectation from the thing under
test can only ever verify self-consistency.

**TWO SOURCES OF CONTAMINATION HAD TO GO FIRST**, and both were found by the numbers rather
than by reading:

- `API.heat(0)` DOES NOT MEAN ZERO. The star setter lands the total in the middle of that
  star's band on purpose, so a check holding a level does not sit on a boundary the decay
  will cross — which makes `heat(0)` fifty points. A rate charged in single points needs a
  known zero, so `API.heatSet(pts)` was added beside it.
- A SPEED TRAP CHARGES 20 POINTS, which is ten miles of the rate under test arriving at
  random. The measurement drives at 180mph past a limit of 80, so the road is now rebuilt
  every four milliseconds around the one pinned cruiser rather than added to, and the
  traffic is parked far up the road. Before that, two traps put 40 points into a
  six-point measurement.
