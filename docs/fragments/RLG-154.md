### RLG-154 — Record an elevation: a real altitude for the water, the skyline and the limits of vertical travel
- **RULED 2026-09-01T23:14:30-04:00** — Owner, 2026-09-01, while [[RLG-143]] was being picked up: "It might be better if we record an
elevation so the relief is more realistic and vertically offsets the skyline. It'll also help
us constrain our up and down travel."

THIS IS THE ANSWER TO THE THING RLG-143 IS STUCK ON, and the owner arrived at it independently.
That fragment says in as many words what is missing: "the rise has to enter the projection as a
world height - the way `BORE.h` does for the tunnel's ceiling - rather than as a screen offset
added afterwards. The accumulators are in screen units, so the rise has to be converted, or a
third accumulator has to carry it in world units." An elevation is that, and it pays for itself
three times over rather than once.

WHAT THE ENGINE HAS TODAY AND WHY IT CANNOT ANSWER. The vertical is integrated in SCREEN space
- `dy += gradeAt(z) * 0.010; y += dy` - and `proj` adds the result as a pixel offset that is
NOT divided by distance. That is the pseudo-3D trick and it is load-bearing: the note in `proj`
records that putting the BEND inside the scale term made every road converge on one vanishing
point and go straight. So the engine holds no altitude at all. It holds a screen displacement.

WHAT AN ELEVATION BUYS, and the first of these is the blocked half of RLG-143:

    THE WATER SITS AT THE LAND'S ALTITUDE. A plane at a stated height projects the way the
    bore's ceiling does - `horizon + scale * (CAM_H - h) * H / 2` - which is a plane at every
    distance rather than a curve pinned to the camera. `WATER_DROP` stops being a constant
    picked by eye and becomes the land's own elevation, correct at the mouths by construction.

    THE SKYLINE MOVES WITH THE GROUND. The owner's second reason. A silhouette drawn at a
    fixed height above the horizon says the land is flat; climbing a mountain pass should
    raise the ground you are on relative to the ridges beyond it, and only an altitude can
    say by how much.

    AND VERTICAL TRAVEL CAN BE BOUNDED. The owner's third. Nothing today stops the rolled
    grade from wandering: it is a random walk with no restoring term, so a long run can drift
    a very long way up or down and nothing notices, because nothing is measuring. A recorded
    elevation is what makes a ceiling and a floor expressible at all.

HOW TO BUILD IT WITHOUT BREAKING THE FEEL, and this is the part to get right. DO NOT convert
the drawn road to world space. The screen-space hill offset is what the owner has tuned every
biome's relief against, and RLG-059's whole `hill` column is calibrated to it.

    SO ELEVATION IS A DERIVED VIEW OF THE SAME ACCUMULATOR, not a second source of truth. One
    committed calibration constant turns the existing integration into world units. Two
    independent integrations would be exactly the fault this file has now been caught by four
    times - a value typed in beside a value derived from it - and they would drift the first
    time anyone tuned a grade.

    THE LAND'S OWN ACCUMULATOR ALREADY EXISTS. RLG-143 built `landCache`, which runs the
    rolled grade straight through an event as though no profile were in force. Read through
    the same constant it gives the LAND's elevation, which is what the water wants.

    AND THE CAMERA IS ON THE ROAD. The reference has to be the road's value at the player's
    own position, not the land's - reading the land through its own camera term is precisely
    the mistake RLG-143 recorded, and it made the sea rise to meet the deck exactly where it
    should have been furthest below.

WHAT NEEDS THE OWNER. The calibration constant is a taste decision wearing a unit: it decides
how many world units of altitude a given screen displacement means, and therefore how deep the
water looks under a bridge and how far the skyline shifts on a climb. It should be set from a
picture, not from arithmetic. And the bounds - how high and how low the road may wander - are
the owner's, because they decide what the game's terrain can be.

WHAT THIS DOES NOT DO. It does not change how the road is drawn, it does not touch the relief
table, and it does not make the engine physically consistent - the hill is still a screen
offset. It adds one honest quantity beside it and uses that for the three things a screen
offset cannot answer.
- **RULED 2026-09-01T23:18:27-04:00** — ATTEMPTED AND MEASURED, 2026-09-01. THE DERIVED PLAN ABOVE IS WRONG AND THE REASON MATTERS,
because it is also the reason [[RLG-143]]'s second accumulator has never worked.

THE PLAN WAS ONE CONSTANT TURNING THE EXISTING ACCUMULATOR INTO WORLD UNITS, so that the
altitude and the picture could never drift apart. That reasoning still holds and it is why two
integrations would be wrong. What it missed is that the existing accumulator is not an
integration of the road at all.

`rebuildBend` REBUILDS EVERY CACHE FROM ZERO, EVERY FRAME. The line is
`let dx = 0, x = 0, dy = 0, y = 0, dyL = 0, yL = 0;`, and the walk starts at `bendZ0`, which is
trimmed to about 40,000 units behind the camera. So neither `hillCache` nor `landCache` holds a
height above anything. Each holds the road's displacement over a sliding 40,000-unit window,
and the difference between them at the player carries only what happened inside that window.

A BRIDGE'S ENTRY RAMP ALONE IS ABOUT 35,000 UNITS. By the middle of the deck most of the climb
has already fallen out of the integration, so the quantity RLG-143 calls "the bridge's height"
is not in the engine even in screen units.

MEASURED RATHER THAN REASONED, which is how this was found. Driving a real bridge and reading
the camera-referenced land line at the player:

    a fourteenth of the way across   -27
    the middle of the deck            +6
    near the far end                 +59
    just past the far mouth            0

A bridge's height above the water is a steady quantity across its deck. That is a window
sliding over a random walk, and no calibration constant rescues it.

SO THE OWNER'S WORD WAS THE OPERATIVE ONE AND I READ IT AS A SYNONYM. "RECORD an elevation" is
not "derive an elevation": it has to be a persistent quantity carried with the car and updated
as it travels, surviving the cache rebuild that throws everything else away. That is a
different and larger thing than this fragment first described, and it is the only thing that
can answer any of the three questions.

WHICH ALSO MEANS THE THREE PAYOFFS ARE NOT EQUALLY CHEAP. Once an altitude is recorded, the
water plane and the skyline offset both fall out of it. But every place that reads the vertical
today reads the sliding window, so the altitude is a NEW quantity beside them and the two can
disagree - which is the exact fault this fragment warned about one paragraph earlier. Keeping
them in step is the real work, and it should be designed before it is started.

AND THE FLOOR IS SEA LEVEL. Owner, 2026-09-01, immediately after: "That also means we can't go
below sea level." So the recorded elevation has a hard floor at zero and the terrain generator
has to respect it - which is the "constrain our up and down travel" half of the original note,
made specific at one end. TWO THINGS THIS LEAVES OPEN AND NEITHER IS MINE TO DECIDE. What
happens when the rolled grade would take the road below the floor - is it clamped, reflected,
or is the grade re-rolled - because each of those makes a different-looking road. And whether a
TUNNEL is exempt: a bore goes down by design, real ones run under rivers and harbours, and the
owner's rule may mean the LAND cannot go below sea level rather than the road.

NOTHING WAS SHIPPED FROM THIS ATTEMPT. `landFromRoadPx` and the `landDrop` probe are kept,
because they are correct as far as they go and they are the instrument that found the problem.
The calibration constant and the world-unit conversion built on top of them were removed rather
than left looking as though they worked.
