### RLG-329 — The mountain needs its sheer cliff face back
- **RULED 2026-09-23T20:47:54-04:00** — OWNER, 2026-09-23, testing 0.14.135 on the device: "The mountain biome needs to have the sheer cliff face back. Because that breaks the illusion."

THIS REVERSES A DECISION THIS ENGINE MADE ON PURPOSE, so the record of why it was taken out has to be read before it is put back. [[RLG-265]] took the rock face out of mid-air beside the guard rail. [[RLG-278]] then chose to paint NOTHING beyond the rim rather than a face: its note says a dark face "reads as a shadow on a field, because the drop is mostly DISTANCE in this projection rather than wall - so the honest move is to stop painting ground there at all and let what lies beyond show."

THE OWNER HAS NOW SEEN BOTH AND WANTS THE FACE. That ruling is the newer one and it is from the device, which is the only instrument that can judge it. The earlier note is not wrong about WHY the first attempt failed - a flat dark band did read as a shadow - so whatever goes back has to solve that, not repeat it.

NOT STARTED.
- **RULED 2026-09-23T21:34:47-04:00** — BUILT 2026-09-23.

OWNER, from the device: "The sheer cliff face is what hangs beyond the road down to the valley. I assume you stopped drawing that to save frames, but that means there is literally nothing and you can see underneath the mountain range." And on the earlier decision: "that is frankly idiotic if that was the decision."

IT WAS NOT A PERFORMANCE DECISION AND IT WAS NOT MADE IN THIS SESSION. [[RLG-278]] took the face out for a LOOK reason, and its note is still at the fill: a dark face "reads as a shadow on a field, because the drop is mostly DISTANCE in this projection rather than wall - so the honest move is to stop painting ground there at all and let what lies beyond show". The diagnosis was right and the conclusion was wrong. Painting nothing does not leave a cliff, it leaves A HOLE: the ground stops at the rim on `y2` and the valley floor starts at `fy2`, and everything between was unpainted, so the sky and the far field showed through UNDER the range that is meant to stand behind the valley.

THE FACE IS ROCK NOW, AND IT IS HAZED. The place's own stone, shaded harder than the floor because a wall under a lip is in shadow, and washed with distance like every other surface in this engine - which is the thing the first attempt lacked. `CLIFF_SHADE` and `CLIFF_HAZE` are its two tunables and `API.cliffModel` reads and sets them, because the numbers are a judgement for a device.

AND IT IS DARKEST UNDER THE LIP, which is what stops it being the flat band the old note described. The top of a slice's band is rock just under the rim, in its own shadow and barely hazed; the bottom is the foot of the fall, as far off as the valley floor and washed with the same air. THE GRADIENT IS ONLY BUILT WHERE IT CAN BE SEEN - a band under 24 pixels takes the flat tone - so this is a handful of gradients a frame rather than one per slice, which matters after [[RLG-299]] measured what a per-frame gradient costs.

THE VALLEY FLOOR TAKES THE MOUNTAINS' COLOUR, which the owner asked for directly: "the valley floor should adopt the same color as the mountains". It already came from the place's own rock, but it hazed toward the biome's `sky` while the range across the valley hazes toward the SKY'S OWN far stop - two different washes over one rock, the same fault as [[RLG-331]] one level down. Both take the same haze now, so the floor and the peaks standing on it go grey together.

WHAT IS STILL OPEN AND NEEDS THE DEVICE. [[RLG-330]], the range's base: the owner reported it still cut off after the colour change, so the straight edge is real and not just a colour seam. It has NOT been touched, because the face and the floor beneath it have just changed what it stands on and the geometry should be judged against the new picture rather than the old one. AND A SECOND REPORT IS UNRECORDED UNTIL IT IS UNDERSTOOD: "a single position of the drawn mountain that rises above you up ahead that doesn't get rendered like a single slice so as we move that slice moves with you in the distance" - a mountain drawn at one place that tracks the camera instead of staying in the world. `drawMassFront` is the suspect: it walks to a place's nearest segment and paints ONE massif there, once a frame.

smoke-test 7/7, cliff-test with no failures.
- **RULED 2026-09-24T00:10:14-04:00** — CORRECTED 2026-09-24, after the owner said to reproduce it properly: "You need to turn towards the valley, but also have the car against the guard rail nearest the valley to catch what I'm talking about."

THAT IS THE CASE, AND IT SHOWED THE FACE WAS THERE AND WRONG rather than missing. Forced with `holdCurve` toward the hazard side and `setLane` pinned to 0.97 of the way to the valley rail, the area beyond the rail came out PALE AND FLAT - it read as more ground, not as a fall, which is how the owner could still say they see into the mountain they are driving on.

THE FAULT WAS HAZING IT BY DEPTH. The first cut washed the face the way the valley FLOOR is washed, because the floor genuinely is far away. Within one slice the band runs from the rim at its top to the floor at its bottom, so hazing by depth makes the BOTTOM of the band the palest part - and the bottom of the band is the part nearest the car, at the bottom of the screen. The near rock came out washed out.

A CLIFF YOU ARE DRIVING ALONG IS NEAR. It is dark rock in its own shadow and it takes barely any air. `CLIFF_HAZE` is 0.16 where it was 0.55, `CLIFF_SHADE` is 0.64 where it was 0.52, and the gradient eases from 0.20 of that haze under the lip to 1.00 at the foot - never toward pale. The face now reads as a wall falling away with the lit rim zigzagging along its top, which is the pairing [[RLG-278]] said makes a cliff read as a lip rather than a shadow.

WHICH MEANS BOTH OF THAT NOTE'S WARNINGS WERE RIGHT AND POINTED AT OPPOSITE FAILURES. Too dark and flat reads as a shadow on a field; too hazed and pale reads as more field. The face has to be dark ROCK with an edge, and the edge is the lit lip that was already there.
- **RULED 2026-09-24T00:42:22-04:00** — SETTLED 2026-09-24 BY THE OWNER, who named the treatment rather than the fault: "The face going down toward that valley used to be the same color as the mountain face going up. It was rendered in strips going down."

THAT ENDED THREE WRONG CUTS, AND ALL THREE FAILED THE SAME WAY. A flat tone, then a haze of its own, then a darker haze - each invented a treatment for this face, when it is not a surface of its own at all. IT IS THE SAME ROCK SEEN OVER THE OTHER EDGE. It takes `WALL_SHADE`, `WALL_HAZE` and `WALL_FACET` and has no numbers of its own to drift from them; `CLIFF_SHADE`, `CLIFF_HAZE` and `API.cliffModel` are gone.

AND IT IS THE MASSIF'S SHADING, NOT THE WALL'S, WHICH TOOK ONE MORE ROUND TO SEE. MOUNTAIN carries `mass`, so the face rising from the roadside is painted by the massif rather than by the wall - and the massif is much the darker of the two, `WALL_SHADE + (1 - lit) * MASS_SHADE` against the wall's `WALL_SHADE` alone. Matched to the wall, the face over the edge came out PALE BESIDE THE ROCK IT IS PART OF, which was visible immediately in the same shot that proved the strips were right.

THE STRIPS ARE THE WALL'S OWN PARTITION. Three of them down the fall, each taking the facet noise fixed to the road so the planes turn as a mountainside's do. The wall's own record says why three and not a gradient: the strips cover the band exactly once rather than painting over each other, which is what makes it affordable - the first facet attempt there re-filled the whole band through a gradient and took a canyon from 50.1-57.3 fps to 31.3-47.4.

HOW IT WAS REPRODUCED MATTERS AND IS WORTH KEEPING. The owner had to say it twice: "You need to turn towards the valley, but also have the car against the guard rail nearest the valley to catch what I'm talking about." Bending alone put the car on the WRONG side. `API.holdCurve` toward the hazard side plus `API.setLane` pinned to 0.97 of the way to the valley rail is the case, and it should be the first thing any later session reaches for on this face.
- **RULED 2026-09-24T01:04:05-04:00** — CORRECTED AGAIN 2026-09-24, and this one was a plain geometry mistake rather than a matter of taste. Owner: "no. Now you've just painted ACROSS the valley level with the road. It should go DOWN over the edge."

THE FACE WAS PAINTED FROM THE RIM OUT TO THE EDGE OF THE SCREEN. That is a band lying flat at the road's own level - the shape the valley FLOOR has, not the shape a cliff has. Every strip ran sideways across the valley instead of down the fall, so getting the colour and the strips right only made a more convincing floor.

A SHEER FACE IS A RIBBON THAT FOLLOWS THE RIM. Its top edge is the rim line, `y1` to `y2`. Its bottom edge is the foot of the fall, `fy1` to `fy2`. Its left and right are the rim's own x at each end of the slice, AND NOTHING MOVES SIDEWAYS - a point directly below another at the same distance projects to the same screen x, because the projection scales x by z alone. Only the y falls. Outboard of the foot is the valley floor, which is painted separately and does run to the frame.

The strips go DOWN the ribbon now, each a band between the rim and the foot, following the fall rather than stacking across it.

FOUR CUTS ON ONE SURFACE, AND THE PATTERN IN THEM IS WORTH MORE THAN ANY OF THEM. Flat tone, own haze, darker haze, the wall's strips - every one of them was about how the face was SHADED, and the fault the whole time was WHERE IT WAS. A surface that is the wrong shape cannot be fixed by colour, and three rounds went into colour before anybody looked at the quad's corners. The lesson is to read the geometry out loud before touching the palette.
