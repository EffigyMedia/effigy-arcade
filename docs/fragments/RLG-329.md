### RLG-329 — The mountain needs its sheer cliff face back
- **RULED 2026-09-23T20:47:54-04:00** — OWNER, 2026-09-23, testing 0.14.135 on the device: "The mountain biome needs to have the sheer cliff face back. Because that breaks the illusion."

THIS REVERSES A DECISION THIS ENGINE MADE ON PURPOSE, so the record of why it was taken out has to be read before it is put back. [[RLG-265]] took the rock face out of mid-air beside the guard rail. [[RLG-278]] then chose to paint NOTHING beyond the rim rather than a face: its note says a dark face "reads as a shadow on a field, because the drop is mostly DISTANCE in this projection rather than wall - so the honest move is to stop painting ground there at all and let what lies beyond show."

THE OWNER HAS NOW SEEN BOTH AND WANTS THE FACE. That ruling is the newer one and it is from the device, which is the only instrument that can judge it. The earlier note is not wrong about WHY the first attempt failed - a flat dark band did read as a shadow - so whatever goes back has to solve that, not repeat it.

NOT STARTED.
- **RULED 2026-09-23T21:34:47-04:00** — BUILT 2026-09-23.

OWNER, from the device: "The sheer cliff face is what hangs beyond the road down to the valley. I assume you stopped drawing that to save frames, but that means there is literally nothing and you can see underneath the mountain range." And on the earlier decision: "that is frankly idiotic if that was the decision."

IT WAS NOT A PERFORMANCE DECISION AND IT WAS NOT MADE IN THIS SESSION. [[RLG-278]] took the face out for a LOOK reason, and its note is still at the fill: a dark face "reads as a shadow on a field, because the drop is mostly DISTANCE in this projection rather than wall - so the honest move is to stop painting ground there at all and let what lies beyond show". The diagnosis was right and the conclusion was wrong. Painting nothing does not leave a cliff, it leaves A HOLE: the ground stops at the rim on `y2` and the valley floor starts at `fy2`, and everything between was unpainted, so the sky and the far field showed through UNDER the range that is meant to stand behind the valley.

THE FACE IS ROCK NOW, AND IT IS HAZED. The place's own stone, shaded harder than the floor because a wall under a lip is in shadow, and washed with distance like every other surface in this engine - which is the thing the first attempt lacked. `CLIFF_SHADE` and `CLIFF_HAZE` are its two tunables and `API.cliffModel` reads and sets them, because the numbers are a judgement for a device.

AND IT IS DARKEST UNDER THE LIP, which is what stops it being the flat band the old note described. The top of a slice's band is rock just under the rim, in its own shadow and barely hazed; the bottom is the foot of the fall, as far off as the valley floor and washed with the same air. THE GRADIENT IS ONLY BUILT WHERE IT CAN BE SEEN - a band under 24 pixels takes the flat tone - so this is a handful of gradients a frame rather than one per slice, which matters after [[RLG-299]] measured what a per-frame gradient costs.

THE VALLEY FLOOR TAKES THE MOUNTAINS' COLOUR, which the owner asked for directly: "the valley floor should adopt the same color as the mountains". It already came from the place's own rock, but it hazed toward the biome's `sky` while the range across the valley hazes toward the SKY'S OWN far stop - two different washes over one rock, the same fault as [[RLG-331]] one level down. Both take the same haze now, so the floor and the peaks standing on it go grey together.

WHAT IS STILL OPEN AND NEEDS THE DEVICE. [[RLG-330]], the range's base: the owner reported it still cut off after the colour change, so the straight edge is real and not just a colour seam. It has NOT been touched, because the face and the floor beneath it have just changed what it stands on and the geometry should be judged against the new picture rather than the old one. AND A SECOND REPORT IS UNRECORDED UNTIL IT IS UNDERSTOOD: "a single position of the drawn mountain that rises above you up ahead that doesn't get rendered like a single slice so as we move that slice moves with you in the distance" - a mountain drawn at one place that tracks the camera instead of staying in the world. `drawMassFront` is the suspect: it walks to a place's nearest segment and paints ONE massif there, once a frame.

smoke-test 7/7, cliff-test with no failures.
