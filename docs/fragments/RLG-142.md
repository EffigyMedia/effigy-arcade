### RLG-142 — Lens droplets outlive the weather that made them, and a hot place can follow a freezing one
- **RULED 2026-09-01T12:55:13-04:00** — RULED 2026-09-01 - Owner, from the device, reporting one thing and raising a second:

"I had snowy mountains transition into swamp (which itself should probably not be allowed to
happen given the immediate current temperature delta), but the snow eventually subsided and
the wet drops on the screen kept going."

THESE ARE TWO ITEMS AND THEY SHOULD NOT BE FIXED AS ONE. The first is a defect - something
outlives the state that produced it. The second is a design question about what the biome
model is allowed to place next to what, and it is a question [[RLG-109]] deliberately left
open when it made every place roll its own temperature.

THE DROPS ARE THE DEFECT AND THEY HAVE A SUSPECT. `wet` is the precipitation amount and
`snowy` says which kind it is; the lens droplets belong to [[RLG-060]]'s weather-on-the-glass
work, and the note in the thread says droplets "fade soon after appearing". The report is
that they did not - the snow ended and the drops continued. So the likely shape is a droplet
whose lifetime is armed when it appears and never re-checked against the weather that armed
it, in the same family as [[RLG-111]]'s thunder outliving its own run: a SCHEDULED effect
that survives the state which scheduled it. That fragment fixed one instance by resetting the
schedule; this is a second instance and the general lesson may be worth stating rather than
fixing twice.

DO NOT DIAGNOSE IT FROM THAT GUESS. [[RLG-041]] cost eleven versions and six fixes, five of
which were real mechanisms that were not the one the owner was seeing, and the lesson written
there is that the DETAIL IN THE REPORT IS THE DIAGNOSIS. Two details are already in this one
and both narrow it: the snow SUBSIDED rather than stopped, so the transition was gradual; and
the drops "kept going" rather than froze, so they are still being stepped. A droplet system
that had simply stalled would look different from one that is running with no source.

AND WHAT ARE THEY DROPS OF, WHICH IS THE OTHER HALF. The place was under SNOW. If the glass
carries rain droplets while snow is falling, the fault may be that the droplets never read
`snowy` at all - in which case they were wrong for the whole of the mountain as well as after
it, and the owner noticed at the transition because that is where it became obvious.

THE SECOND ITEM IS A RULE THAT DOES NOT EXIST YET. Nothing stops a freezing place being
followed by a hot one: `stepBiome` picks the next place with a flat roll over every entry
except a passage, and RLG-109 gave each place an INSTANCE temperature rolled when it opens.
So the model already knows both temperatures and simply does not compare them. A rule is
cheap to state - reject a candidate whose instance temperature is more than some delta from
the current one, and roll again - and it has one real risk: a board where a cold place can
only ever be followed by another cold one is a board that stops travelling. The owner should
say how large a step is too large before it is built, because the answer decides whether this
is a filter or a walk.
- **RULED 2026-09-01T14:14:48-04:00** — THE OWNER ANSWERED THE TEMPERATURE QUESTION, 2026-09-01: "I'd say 10 to 20 degrees, leaning
more towards 10."

AND THE MODEL HAS NO DEGREES IN IT, WHICH HAS TO BE SETTLED BEFORE THE NUMBER CAN BE USED.
[[RLG-109]] states `temp` as 0 coldest to 1 hottest and never says what the ends are. So the
scale has to be defined, and it should be defined ONCE and written down rather than implied by
a conversion buried in a comparison.

PROPOSED, AND THE OWNER SHOULD CONFIRM IT: 0 is -10F and 1 is 120F, a span of 130 degrees, in
Fahrenheit because everything else the player sees in this game is imperial - miles, miles per
hour, and the feet the bridge stands above the water. Against that scale the board reads:

    TUNDRA   0.05  ->   -3F      FOREST   0.45  ->   49F
    MOUNTAIN 0.15  ->   10F      COASTAL  0.55  ->   62F
    CITY     0.45  ->   49F      FARMLAND 0.60  ->   68F
    TUNNEL   0.45  ->   49F      SWAMP    0.80  ->   94F
    CANYON   0.85  ->  101F      DESERT   0.95  ->  114F

Those are plausible for the places they name, which is the only evidence available that the
span is right.

SO 10F IS 0.077 OF THE SCALE AND 20F IS 0.154. And that is a much stronger rule than it
sounds, which the owner should know before it is built: almost no two places on the board are
within 0.077 of each other. A mountain at 0.15 and a swamp at 0.80 are 0.65 apart, which is
85F - the transition the owner reported. Under a 10F cap the board becomes a WALK: you travel
from cold to hot through the places in between, a few degrees at a time, and you cannot jump.

THAT IS PROBABLY WHAT THE OWNER WANTS AND IT IS WORTH SAYING PLAINLY ANYWAY. A run is sixty
seconds plus checkpoints and a place lasts six and a half to twelve miles, so a run sees one
to three places. Under this rule a run stays inside a narrow climate band and the variety
comes from returning, not from one drive. The alternative reading - 10F between the INSTANCES
rather than the recipes - is the same rule with more room, because a place rolls its own
temperature within `vary` and a cold-rolled farmland can meet a warm-rolled forest.

HOW IT SHOULD BE BUILT, AND IT IS NOT A FILTER ON THE PICK. Rejecting candidate places until
one is close enough would bias the board toward whatever happens to be near, and would
deadlock at the ends of the scale where nothing is. The instance is rolled AFTER the place is
chosen, so the constraint belongs there: pick a place, then roll its instance temperature
CLAMPED to within the delta of the current one AND to that place's own range. If the two
ranges do not overlap at all, that place genuinely cannot follow this one - reject it and pick
again. That never deadlocks while any place can be reached, and it keeps every place on the
board reachable eventually.

STILL NOT ANSWERED: what the lens drops were drops OF. The place was under snow, so the glass
may have been carrying rain droplets throughout the mountain and not only after it.
