### RLG-203 — A mode where you are the police and the racers are the target
- **RULED 2026-09-10T03:22:14-04:00** — TRACKED, NOT STARTED, AND IT HAS NO NAME YET. Owner, 2026-09-10: "Add an additional mode
to the game, where you are acting as police trying to stop racers. You have to take out all the
racers before the race finishes I suppose. I'm not sure what to call this mode though."

**IT IS THE GAME'S OWN PURSUIT TURNED AROUND, AND MOST OF IT IS ALREADY BUILT.** Every part this
mode needs exists and is driven by the machine today: cruisers that search, engage, box and ram
([[RLG-158]]), a takedown that is worn down rather than dealt in one hit, a race field of rivals that
is never culled so their positions are known at all times, a wanted ladder that decides how much
force arrives, and a player car that carries a light bar, a siren and a scatter the moment its record
says `force` ([[RLG-181]]). What does not exist is the SCORING and the END CONDITION, which is
exactly what the owner described: take out every racer before the race finishes.

**THE FIVE QUESTIONS THAT DECIDE WHAT IT IS.** None of them can be answered from the code:

    WHAT COUNTS AS TAKEN OUT. The engine's own answer is the takedown it already deals the player -
    damage accumulated to a wreck. A rival that pulls over, or is boxed to a stop, is a different and
    gentler answer, and it changes whether the mode is about driving or about ramming.

    WHAT HAPPENS WHEN THE RACE FINISHES WITH RACERS LEFT. A loss, a partial score, or a shift that
    simply ends. This is the whole shape of the mode's failure state.

    WHETHER THERE ARE OTHER POLICE. The player alone against a full field is a different game from a
    player calling in the units the radio already dispatches.

    WHETHER TRAFFIC AND ITS DAMAGE ARE IN SCOPE. [[RLG-171]] records that a cruiser only takes traffic
    damage while level with the player or ahead, because the police only ever arrive from behind
    ([[RLG-157]]). With the player AS the cruiser, every one of those assumptions is inverted and that
    guard has to be read again.

    WHICH DISCIPLINE IT BELONGS TO. Interstate has the police, the heat ladder and the open road.
    Motorsport is a closed circuit with no police at all and `CFG.circuitOnly` gating them out. A
    race with a finish line is Motorsport's shape and the police are Interstate's, so the mode sits
    across the seam that separates the two games - which is the one design question here that could
    cost real work if it is answered late.

**AND IT NEEDS A NAME.** The owner asked for one. It should sit beside the modes that already exist
rather than describe itself, and it should not read as a driving discipline, because it is a shift
rather than a race.
- **RULED 2026-09-10T03:29:58-04:00** — **THE OWNER PLACED IT, 2026-09-10: it is a mode a CAR unlocks, not a machine or a menu of its

**SO THE FOURTH QUESTION IN THE LIST ABOVE IS ANSWERED.** It sits on Interstate's mode control
beside TEST DRIVE, SINGLE RACE and TOURNAMENT, and it appears only while a CRUISER or a SUPERCRUISER
is selected. That is [[RLG-115]]'s class gate run in reverse: that ruling REMOVES modes a car cannot
enter and greys them with the reason given; this ADDS one a car alone can enter. One control, one
shape, both directions - which also means the mode needs no home of its own and no new screen.

**AND IT SETTLES THE SEAM QUESTION.** A mode that lives on the car's mode control lives where the
car does, and the police are Interstate's. Motorsport gates the police out with `CFG.circuitOnly`
and its garage refuses cars that cannot race; nothing here asks it to change.

**WHAT IS STILL OPEN.** The four remaining questions in the list above - what counts as taken out,
what happens if the race finishes with racers left, whether other police are on the road, and
whether traffic damage still holds when the player is the cruiser ([[RLG-171]] is written from the
opposite side and has to be read again).

**AND THE NAME.** Still none. The owner: "I just don't know what to call that mode." The register is
set by the three it sits beside - TEST DRIVE, SINGLE RACE, TOURNAMENT - so it is one or two plain
words on a button, and HOT PURSUIT is not available because that is already the name of the pursuit
toggle. Suggested and not chosen: INTERCEPT, which names the objective and pairs with the
INTERCEPTOR the ladder pays out; DISPATCH, which is the game's own word for what the radio does to
you today; NIGHT SHIFT; PATROL, which reads as open patrol rather than a race you were sent to break
up. The owner decides.
- **RULED 2026-09-10T08:51:33-04:00** — **THE MODE IS CALLED INTERCEPT.** Owner, 2026-09-10, choosing it from four offered: "I like 'Intercept' let's do that."

**IT NAMES THE OBJECTIVE AND IT PAIRS WITH THE CAR.** The supercar ladder pays the INTERCEPTOR under pursuit, and the mode that car unlocks is INTERCEPT - the same word for the machine and for the job, which is how the CRUISER and the cruiser already read to a player. It also sits in the register of the three it appears beside on the mode control: TEST DRIVE, SINGLE RACE, TOURNAMENT. One word, plain, a job rather than a discipline.

**AND IT DOES NOT COLLIDE WITH ANYTHING THE PLAYER ALREADY READS.** HOT PURSUIT is the pursuit toggle and stays that. PURSUIT is the engine's own word for the state a chase is in, used throughout the code and in `API.pursuit()`. INTERCEPT is unused in the source today, so the label, the mode id and whatever state it needs can all take the name without shadowing an existing one.

**THE NAME IS DECIDED. THE MODE IS NOT BUILT** and the four open questions above are unchanged: what counts as taken out, what happens when the race finishes with racers left, whether other police are on the road, and whether [[RLG-171]]'s traffic-damage guard still holds when the player is the cruiser.
- **RULED 2026-09-10T10:01:26-04:00** — **AND INTERCEPT REPLACES THE RACE MODES RATHER THAN JOINING THEM.** Owner, 2026-09-10: "I feel

**SO A POLICE CAR OFFERS TEST DRIVE AND INTERCEPT, AND NOTHING ELSE.** That is the same control and
the same shape as [[RLG-115]] - a car that cannot enter a mode finds it greyed with the reason
given - except that the police cars also gain one no other car can reach. `RACE_BANNED` is the table
that already does the taking-away, keyed on the unlock class, and `cruiser` and `supercruiser` are
its next two entries.

**AND THE FIELD IS SYMMETRICAL.** Owner: "in a symmetrical fashion, the cruiser intercepts against a
sports class race and the supercruiser intercepts against a super class race." The class is
therefore load-bearing in exactly the way [[RLG-202]] built it - `classOf` reads `raceClass` off the
record, `racerBodies` draws the field from that, and INTERCEPT gets a sports field for a CRUISER and
a supercar field for an INTERCEPTOR with no further branch. That work is done and proved.

**WHICH MAKES THE ORDER OF WORK EXPLICIT.** Taking the race modes away is one entry in a table and
must NOT ship before the mode itself: a police car with TEST DRIVE and nothing else is a prize that
unlocks less than it did the day before. The ban and the mode land together.

**AND THE SIREN IS NOW LOAD-BEARING FOR THE MODE.** The owner: *"they have a secret weapon and that
is their siren because that will move cars out of the way."* While the bar is latched, `scatter(0.90)`
is called every frame on the horn's 0.55s cooldown - a standing request rather than the horn's single
40 per cent one - and it moves TRAFFIC only, never the racers. That is the tool the mode is built
around. **It is also not proved to work**: `tools/ambulance-test.py` asserts that a siren moves
traffic and reports zero cars changing lane, on this build and the one before, recorded in
[[RLG-176]] and never chased. Measure the player's own bar before tuning anything that assumes it.
- **RULED 2026-09-10T10:10:49-04:00** — **THE FOUR QUESTIONS ARE ANSWERED, 2026-09-10, and the mode is specified.**

**1 - A RACER IS STOPPED, NOT DESTROYED.** Owner: *"Just destroying them is kinda boring. I suppose
the only other option IS to box them in. Maybe there should be other friendly cruiser to help, but
its up to YOU! to get in front and slow them down."*

So the takedown is a MANOEUVRE and not damage accumulating to a wreck. The player has to get AHEAD
of a racer and slow it, which is the box the engine already performs against the player -
[[RLG-158]] built it, and the box station is computed from the target's speed and clamped to the
cruiser's own top end. Turned around, that same code is the mode's core verb.

**AND THE PLAYER HAS TO BE THE ONE IN FRONT.** Friendly cruisers help - they pressure, they flank,
they fill the lanes - but the stop is the player's to make. That is the line between a mode you play
and a mode you watch, and the owner drew it with the emphasis on "YOU".

**ONE NUMBER IS STILL UNSTATED**: how slow, and for how long, before a racer counts as stopped. It
is a tunable with a committed default rather than a question to hold the build for, and the value is
a judgement on a device. The pursuit side already has the shape of it - the player is busted when
held near a standstill with cruisers on them.

**2 - THE RACE FINISHING IS THE LOSS.** Owner: *"You lose and the shift ends."* Not a partial score
and not a shift that simply ends: the racers reaching the finish with any of them still running is a
failure, and the run is over. That makes the race distance the mode's clock, which is a clock the
engine already keeps.

**3 - THE WORLD IS AN ORDINARY RACE WITH HOT PURSUIT ON, AND THE PLAYER HAS SWAPPED SIDES.** Owner:
*"Yes. It should play like a standard race with hot pursuit enabled, its just you arent a racer -
you are a cop."*

This is the answer that makes the mode cheap to build and it is worth stating plainly: **nothing new
is spawned and nothing is turned off.** The field, the traffic, the dispatches, the roadblocks, the
traps and the cruisers all run exactly as they do in a race with pursuit enabled. What changes is who
the player is, what the win condition reads, and who the police are hunting.

**4 - EVERYONE TAKES DAMAGE, THE PLAYER INCLUDED.** Owner: *"Yea you need to be able to be damaged
and so do other cops."*

**WHICH MAKES [[RLG-171]] LOAD-BEARING RATHER THAN A CURIOSITY.** That ruling records the guard
`k.z > pz - 900`: a cruiser only takes traffic damage while level with the player or ahead of them.
It was written because the police only ever arrive from behind ([[RLG-157]]), so the guard covered
most of a pursuit and made cruisers effectively immune to the traffic they were weaving through -
and the owner's report that they constantly destroy themselves on traffic could not be reproduced in
a harness because of it. In INTERCEPT the player IS a cruiser and the friendly cars are ahead as
often as behind, so the guard has to be read again. It was left alone before because opening it
makes pursuits materially easier; that trade does not apply to a mode where the cop is the player.

- **RULED 2026-09-10T23:46:49-04:00** — **THE MODE IS PLAYABLE. IT IS NOT FINISHED.** A rival can be stopped, the field can be cleared, the
race finishing loses the shift, and the player never works alone. What is missing is the dial that
decides how much force is out, and [[RLG-171]]'s guard read again from the new side.

**THE STOP IS A MANOEUVRE AND THE CONDITION ASKS ABOUT THE PLAYER ONLY.** A rival under `STOP_SPD` of
the reference top speed, held there for `STOP_HOLD` seconds, with the player AHEAD of it inside
`STOP_NEAR` and within `STOP_WIDE` of its line, is out. It pulls to the verge and comes to rest; it
is not wrecked, which is the owner's own distinction. Friendly cruisers pressure and flank and count
for nothing in the condition, because the owner put the emphasis on YOU. The four numbers are
tunables with committed defaults - 0.20, 2.5 seconds, 3200 units, 0.55 - and the ruling left their
values open, so they are a first answer and a judgement on a device.

**THE PLAYER HAS TO BE IN FRONT, WHICH IS `pz > r.z`.** A rival crawling because it is stuck behind
traffic while you sit behind it is not a car you caught, and the harness asserts that case as
explicitly as it asserts the positive one.

**AND THE MODE'S CORE VERB WAS MISSING FROM THE ENGINE.** `ahead` caps a rival's target speed to
whatever is directly in front of it, and it scanned traffic, the other rivals and the police - not
the player. So a rival would never lift for the car in front of it when that car was the player's:
it would swerve around, or hit it. Without that, "get in front and slow them down" cannot happen at
all.

**THE SAME OMISSION IS ALREADY RECORDED TWENTY LINES BELOW, AND WAS ONLY HALF FIXED.** `laneView`
carries the note "THE PLAYER IS A CAR. Rivals scanned traffic, each other and the police and were
blind to the one car the player is sitting in" - and that fix reached the LANE CHOICE and not the
speed. Two functions, the same blindness, one of them repaired.

**▶ IT IS GATED ON THE SHIFT, AND THAT IS A DELIBERATE REFUSAL TO FIX IT GENERALLY.** `road.js` is
both driving games. Teaching every rival to lift for the player in front would change how a race
feels in Interstate AND Motorsport - a car behind you in your lane would ease off where today it
presses - and no ruling asks for that, no harness can judge it, and the owner cannot test it today.
The general question is real and it is the owner's to answer.

**THE TWO ENDINGS GO THROUGH THE FINISH PATH, NOT THE CRASH PATH.** `wreck()` returns early whenever
the run clock has time left, so an ending routed through it would have done nothing and the shift
would have carried on - which is the same trap the ordinary finish already had to avoid, recorded in
its own comment. `endShift` hands the car to the AI for the same reason a finish does: a player
still steering under the end card can still crash after the thing is decided.

**THE LOSS IS A RIVAL REACHING THE LINE AND NOT THE PLAYER REACHING IT.** The player is policing and
where they are on the road decides nothing, so the ordinary finish is skipped on shift. The harness
asserts both halves, and the first version of that check was WRONG in an instructive way: it parked
the line behind the player, and since rivals start ahead of the player, all eleven of them were past
it and the shift ended as a loss before the question could be asked. The configuration that asks it
is the player past the line with the field still short of it.

**THE WINGMEN ARE A FLOOR AND A CAP WILL NOT DO IT.** The first attempt raised `dispatchCap` and put
NO cars on the road: the radio REINFORCES a pursuit in progress - it requires `onYou`, a cruiser
already reporting the player's position - and on shift nothing is ever on the player, by design. The
cap said two and the road sent none, in the one mode that is entirely about police. The floor is its
own rule now, it fills to strength rather than one car per tick, and it is checked every three to six
seconds rather than every twelve to twenty-two, because a floor checked every twenty seconds is not
a floor.

**THE HUD COUNTS WHAT THE MODE IS ABOUT.** The PLACE cell reads LEFT and shows the field still
running. Same cell, same shape, other side of the board.

**WHAT THE HARNESS PROVES AND WHAT IT CANNOT.** `tools/shift-stop-test.py` stages the CONDITION and
measures the RULE. The manoeuvre itself cannot be flown: getting in front of a rival and holding it
under a fifth of top speed is a driving task, and this project has already been told once what comes
of trusting an autopilot to drive. **So nothing here shows that a player CAN bring a rival to that
speed**, which is the part only a device answers. Every positive check has a negative beside it, and
the hold is sampled half way through so a rule that fired instantly could not pass as one that held.

**TWO STAGING LESSONS WORTH KEEPING.** A rival placed at the player's `x` steers straight back to its
old lane unless `r.lane` moves with it, and the hold breaks part-way through. And a measurement of
one rival has to be a measurement of ONE rival: staging a car ahead of the player and then speeding
up for the next check meant the player overtook it and the rule - correctly - stopped it, so the
tally moved during a check about a different car. `parkRivals` exists for that.

**AND A FLAKE THAT IS NOT THIS UNIT'S, CHECKED RATHER THAN ASSUMED.** `drive-test`'s line "so the car
settles a tenth above its own top end" failed three times under `step.py` and passed three times run
directly. With `road.js` STASHED AT HEAD it failed on one run in two (98.1%), and it failed the same
way on 2026-09-02 under [[UNT-157]]. The check latches the MINIMUM speed it has seen and declares a
settle after five samples that did not fall, so when the engine's frame rate drops relative to the
Python poller it settles on a number the car was still accelerating through. Nothing in this unit is
reachable from that arm - every change here is behind `playerIsPolice()`, which is false in a race.
- **RULED 2026-09-11T11:53:26-04:00** — **THE DIAL IS BUILT, AND RLG-203 NOW HAS ONE THING LEFT.** The field's own lawlessness decides how
much force is on the road, which was the last question the ruling itself had left open.

**WHAT IT COUNTS.** The rivals still running AND still over `SPEED_LIMIT` - the same threshold a
patrol and a speed trap measure every other driver against, so the field is judged by the rule the
world already uses rather than one written for this mode. A stopped rival is parked on the verge and
counts for nothing, which is asserted rather than assumed: a count that included them would keep the
road busy while the player emptied it, which is the exact opposite of the mode's shape.

**THE DENOMINATOR IS THE WHOLE FIELD, NOT THE SURVIVORS**, and that is the part that makes the mode
breathe. Measured against what is left, eleven cars and one car would both read as five stars and the
road would never thin out. Against the whole field, a full grid running flat out is the top of the
ladder and a complement of four, and the last rival is a duel with the two wingmen.

**EVERYTHING THAT READS `heat` IS UNCHANGED**, which is why this was a small change rather than a
wide one - the traps, the roadblocks, the interceptor gate and the cruisers' own skill all go on

**AND THE COMPLEMENT SITS ON TOP OF THE FLOOR RATHER THAN REPLACING IT.** `WINGMEN + dispatchCap()`,

the lawlessness buys.

**TWO INSTRUMENT FAILURES WORTH KEEPING, because both produced confident wrong numbers.**

    A HOLD THAT PINS THE TARGET IS NOT A HOLD. `holdField` first pinned `want` and let the physics
    chase it, so a rival still had to accelerate there and lost it again on the first car it met.
    A field that was supposed to be flat out measured FOUR of eleven over the limit, and the check
    it fed swung between 4, 8 and 11 run to run. The player's own `holdSpd` assigns `spd` outright
    and always has; the field's does now.

    AND A FIELD STAGED IN THE PLAYER'S LINE IS NOT A FIELD. `stageStop` puts one rival in the
    player's lane, which is right for testing a stop and wrong for testing a road: eleven cars
    dropped into one lane queue, lift for each other and drop under the limit every few seconds.
    `stageField` puts them ahead and across the lanes, which is what a field looks like.

**AND A WAIT MEASURED IN WALL SECONDS MEASURED NOTHING.** The level is charged per ENGINE second and
`simTime` runs at a fraction of wall time in this browser, so twenty-four seconds of waiting was
about six seconds of engine and the first run read one star where it expected five. The harness waits
on the engine's clock now, with a wall-clock cap so a frozen engine fails rather than hangs. This
project had already written that lesson down once.

**WHAT IS LEFT IN RLG-203.** [[RLG-171]]'s traffic-damage guard, read again from the side where the
PLAYER is the cruiser. That guard - `k.z > pz - 900`, a cruiser takes traffic damage only while level
with the player or ahead - was written because the police only ever arrive from behind, and in
INTERCEPT the friendly cars are ahead as often as behind and the player is one of them. The owner has
ruled that everyone takes damage including the player, so the guard has to be re-read rather than
inherited.

**AND WHAT NO HARNESS HERE CAN SAY.** Whether a road that thickens with a lawless field and thins to
a duel reads as the mode breathing or as it running out. `FIELD_SWING` is the dial and twenty seconds
is a first answer.
