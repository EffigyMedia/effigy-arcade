### RLG-203 — A mode where you are the police and the racers are the target
- **RULED 2026-09-10T03:22:14-04:00** — TRACKED, NOT STARTED, AND IT HAS NO NAME YET. Owner, 2026-09-10: "Add an additional mode
to the game, where you are acting as police trying to stop racers. You have to take out all the
racers before the race finishes I suppose. I'm not sure what to call this mode though."

**IT IS THE GAME'S OWN PURSUIT TURNED AROUND, AND MOST OF IT IS ALREADY BUILT.** Every part this
mode needs exists and is driven by the machine today: cruisers that search, engage, box and ram
([[RLG-158]]), a takedown that is worn down rather than dealt in one hit, a race field of rivals that
is never culled so their positions are known at all times, a wanted ladder that decides how much
force arrives, and a player car that carries a light bar, a siren and a scatter the moment its record
says `force` ([[RLG-181]]). What does not exist is the SCORING and the END CONDITION, which is
exactly what the owner described: take out every racer before the race finishes.

**THE FIVE QUESTIONS THAT DECIDE WHAT IT IS.** None of them can be answered from the code:

    WHAT COUNTS AS TAKEN OUT. The engine's own answer is the takedown it already deals the player -
    damage accumulated to a wreck. A rival that pulls over, or is boxed to a stop, is a different and
    gentler answer, and it changes whether the mode is about driving or about ramming.

    WHAT HAPPENS WHEN THE RACE FINISHES WITH RACERS LEFT. A loss, a partial score, or a shift that
    simply ends. This is the whole shape of the mode's failure state.

    WHETHER THERE ARE OTHER POLICE. The player alone against a full field is a different game from a
    player calling in the units the radio already dispatches.

    WHETHER TRAFFIC AND ITS DAMAGE ARE IN SCOPE. [[RLG-171]] records that a cruiser only takes traffic
    damage while level with the player or ahead, because the police only ever arrive from behind
    ([[RLG-157]]). With the player AS the cruiser, every one of those assumptions is inverted and that
    guard has to be read again.

    WHICH DISCIPLINE IT BELONGS TO. Interstate has the police, the heat ladder and the open road.
    Motorsport is a closed circuit with no police at all and `CFG.circuitOnly` gating them out. A
    race with a finish line is Motorsport's shape and the police are Interstate's, so the mode sits
    across the seam that separates the two games - which is the one design question here that could
    cost real work if it is answered late.

**AND IT NEEDS A NAME.** The owner asked for one. It should sit beside the modes that already exist
rather than describe itself, and it should not read as a driving discipline, because it is a shift
rather than a race.
