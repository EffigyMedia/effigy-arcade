### RLG-177 — Something like a health bar is drawn on cruisers in the rearview

TRACKED, NOT STARTED. Owner, 2026-09-08: "Why is there something that looks like a health bar
or something only visible on the police cruisers in the rearview mirror? Is this a render
defect? I don't want health bars in either view."

THE RULING IS PLAIN WHATEVER THE CAUSE: no health bar, in the main view or in the mirror. That
part needs no investigation.

WHAT DOES NEED INVESTIGATING IS WHETHER A BAR IS BEING DRAWN AT ALL, and the two possibilities
have nothing in common:

    A BAR IS REALLY THERE. The police system carries a hidden damage stat - `dmg`, and
    `hurtLook` is what the painter reads from it - and a cruiser is worn down over several
    hits rather than dropped by one. If anything renders that stat it is a debug affordance
    that has escaped, and it goes.

    OR IT IS SOMETHING ELSE READ AS A BAR. The mirror is a 44-pixel-tall panel that now
    carries a road, cars, a treeline, a horizon and scenery, and a cruiser has a light bar
    across its roof. A light bar seen at mirror scale is a short coloured horizontal
    rectangle above a car, which is exactly what a health bar looks like. The owner's own
    "is this a render defect?" is the right question and the answer may be that nothing is
    wrong except the scale.

THE CHEAP FIRST STEP IS A CAPTURE, NOT A CODE READ. Take the mirror at its real size, magnify
it, and look. If it is the light bar, the ruling becomes a legibility question rather than a
deletion. If it is a real bar, find what draws it and remove it.

RELATED: [[RLG-053]] converted every rear sprite to declared lamps, and the cruiser's light bar
is one of them - so a change made there is the first thing to check against.
- **RULED 2026-09-08T20:56:23-04:00** — ### 2026-09-08 — it was neither of the two things this record expected

**THE CAPTURE WAS TAKEN FIRST, AND IT SETTLED IT IN ONE PICTURE.** This record named two
candidates — a real bar drawn from the hidden `dmg` stat, or the cruiser's light bar misread at
44-pixel scale — and said the cheap first step was a capture rather than a code read. That was
right, and **both candidates were wrong.**

`tools/cop-mirror-shot.py` parks a cruiser behind the player and captures the glass on its own at
8×, once undamaged and once at 95% damage. The picture shows **three** things stacked: the car,
its light bar on the roof in red and blue, and **a solid blue rectangle floating clear above the
roof.** The damaged capture is pixel-identical in that region, which rules out a health bar
immediately — a bar that does not move with damage is not a bar about damage.

**IT WAS A SECOND LIGHT BAR, AND THE MIRROR DREW IT.** `road.js:23294` filled a rectangle
`sw` wide — **the whole width of the car** — one bar-height ABOVE the sprite, alternating
`#3b6bff` and `#ff2b4a`. The sprite's own bar is 52% of the car's width and sits on the roof. So
the glass drew the bar twice, at two different widths, in two different places, and the copy that
floated above the car is the one the owner saw. A solid, full-width, hard-edged rectangle over a
vehicle is a health bar in every game that has ever drawn one.

**THE COMMENT BESIDE IT EXPLAINS WHY IT SURVIVED.** It read: *"the light bar still goes on top of
a patrol car: it is the one part of a police car that is not in the sprite, because it flashes"*.
That was true when it was written and stopped being true at [[RLG-053]], which converted the
cruiser's front sprite and gave it `bar.fl` and `bar.fr` as declared lamps. **A comment that
states a fact about another part of the system is a fact that can rot**, and this one kept the
code looking correct for as long as anybody only read it locally.

**THIS IS THE THIRD TIME THE SAME MISTAKE HAS BEEN FOUND IN THIS FILE.** `drawCopLights` once
painted the lit bar *"from its own geometry, at its own width and its own height, above a bar the
sprite had already drawn"*, and the title card *"grew its own hand-placed rectangles — two red
bars at guessed coordinates over a sprite that already knew where its lamps were"*. Both are
recorded in the source within a few hundred lines of this one. **The answer has been the same
three times: ask the sprite.** `lampsLit` takes the box and the declaration, and its ten-pixel
floor is correct behaviour rather than a limitation — a bar is not resolvable on a car that
small, and painting one anyway is how this started.

**THE FIRST CHECK WRITTEN FOR THIS PASSED ON THE BROKEN BUILD**, and that failure is worth more
than the working version. The stray bar was filled with two literal colours and one of them
(`#3b6bff`) appears nowhere else in the game, so an exact-match pixel count looked like a perfect
instrument. It read **zero on the broken build**. The bar is painted under the arrival fade, so
`globalAlpha` blends it with whatever is behind and it never lands on its own value. *A check
that reads a colour is not reading the thing* — which is the same lesson as [[RLG-065]], where
reading a gain off a closed audio context reported a healthy 0.13 on a broken build.

**WHAT WORKS IS GEOMETRY, BECAUSE THAT IS WHAT THE RULE ACTUALLY IS.** Not "that colour must not
appear" but **nothing belonging to a police car may be painted above its own sprite**. The engine
now reports where the glass put the cruiser (`API.mirrorCopBox`) and the check reads the band
directly above the roof across the car's own width. On the broken build: **58 saturated pixels
there, in a run 29px wide across a 29px car**. On the fixed build: zero.

**AND THE SATURATION THRESHOLD HAD TO BE MOVED ONCE, FOR A REASON WORTH KEEPING.** The first
predicate asked for blue exceeding red by 45, and **the daylight sky satisfies that** at about
(170,190,215) — so the check reported twelve stray pixels above every cruiser on a build with
nothing wrong. A light bar is saturated and the sky is not. 80 clears the sky by a wide margin
and still catches the palest lens the scheme declares.
