### RLG-278 — The drop beside a mountain road has to read as a fall
- **RULED 2026-09-16T11:47:36-04:00** — OWNER, 2026-09-16: "that sheer cliff face on the mountain biome does not read as a cliff down."

WHAT IS THERE NOW AND WHY IT IS WRONG. [[RLG-265]] part five emptied the mountain's hazard side so no rock face stands in mid-air beside the guard rail. That removed the thing that was wrong and put nothing in its place, so the drop is drawn as ORDINARY GROUND running away to the horizon - the same grass the solid side stands on. The rail says there is a fall beside you and the ground says there is a field, and the ground wins, because it is the larger surface and it is continuous with the verge you can see under your own wheels.

THE MISSING PIECE IS THAT THE GROUND HAS TO STOP. A cliff down is not an absence of scenery; it is an EDGE, with nothing beyond it but air and whatever is far below. The biome's ground is painted across the full width of the screen from the far edge to the bottom - which is what [[RLG-265]] part one relied on to uncover the verge - so on the hazard side that fill has to end at the rail's line and give way to something that reads as depth.

THREE THINGS A DROP COULD BE MADE OF, none of them decided:
- the ground ending at the rail with a dark falling face below it, the way the canyon wall is a face rather than a fill;
- a distant FLOOR far below, hazed, the way the bridge's water sits thirteen camera heights down and is proved to read as depth by being hazed;
- the far skyline showing THROUGH the gap, so the eye reads across the valley rather than down it.

THE BRIDGE ALREADY SOLVED THIS ONCE and its record is the place to start: bridge-test asserts that the water is "far enough off to be hazed, which is what says it is below you", and that the near and far water are different colours. A mountain drop is the same problem with rock instead of water.

NOT MEASURED YET AND NOT STARTED.
- **RULED 2026-09-16T12:07:15-04:00** — BUILT, v0.14.60, AND IT TOOK THREE ATTEMPTS BECAUSE THE FIRST TWO WERE MEASURED AND FOUND WANTING.

THE GROUND NOW ENDS AT THE RIM AND A DARK FALL IS PAINTED BEYOND IT. The biome's ground is cut at the rail's own line on a hazard side - the shoreline's shape, a quad from a line out to the edge of the screen filled to the bottom, painted per slice far to near, which is what builds the rim over crests and round bends with none of it computed. A LIT LIP runs along the cut, because a dark band alone is a shadow and a dark band with a bright top edge is an edge you are looking over.

ATTEMPT ONE THREW AND BLANKED THE FRAME, and the cause is worth recording because both halves are traps. `shade` takes `#rrggbb` ONLY - it regex-matches and returns a grey fallback for anything else - and `groundTone` returns an `rgb(...)` string, so `shade(groundTone(...))` silently painted the fallback. Worse, `hexRGB` calls `.charAt` on its argument, so handing it an array THREW inside the road pass and aborted the rest of the frame: the capture was bare ground with no road, no rail and no car on it. `mixRGB(string, amount, array)` handles both string forms and is the one to use.

ATTEMPT TWO WAS THE BRIDGE'S ANSWER AND IT DOES NOT TRANSFER. [[RLG-112]]'s note says a deck has nothing beside it because "a plane you cannot see the near edge of reads as a plane a long way below", so the ground was clipped at the rim and the far field left to show through. MEASURED, that read 69.0 over the drop against 68.7 over the solid side. THE REASON IS THE COLOUR: the bridge's trick works over WATER, which is a different colour from land, and what shows past a cliff's rim is the same rock in the same light. A cliff needs its own darkness.

AND THE INSTRUMENT WAS WRONG TWICE BEFORE IT WAS RIGHT. Sampling a strip of verge at a fixed row measured the SCENERY: the FARMLAND control, which has no drop at all, swung 19 levels between its own two sides. So the drop is now found the way the rail is - `API.dropOff` paints the ground straight across, the two frames are diffed, and whatever changed IS the drop. MOUNTAIN: 3,588 pixels at x=92.7 with the hazard left, 4,642 at x=388.8 with it right - a swing of 296. FARMLAND: 5 and 11 pixels, which is noise. Both sides are rendered every time, because a mountain that always dropped on the LEFT would pass a check that only ever looked left.

hazard-test, biome-test, farroad-test, occlusion-test, wall-test and smoke-test pass; drive-test is 36/37 on the known environmental settle line.

STILL THE OWNER'S VERDICT, ON A DEVICE. Whether it reads as a FALL rather than as dark ground is a picture, and the owner has already turned down one version of it. What is different this time is that the difference is now measurable at all - the previous build was 0.3 of a brightness level apart from the field beside it.
