### RLG-278 — The drop beside a mountain road has to read as a fall
- **RULED 2026-09-16T11:47:36-04:00** — OWNER, 2026-09-16: "that sheer cliff face on the mountain biome does not read as a cliff down."

WHAT IS THERE NOW AND WHY IT IS WRONG. [[RLG-265]] part five emptied the mountain's hazard side so no rock face stands in mid-air beside the guard rail. That removed the thing that was wrong and put nothing in its place, so the drop is drawn as ORDINARY GROUND running away to the horizon - the same grass the solid side stands on. The rail says there is a fall beside you and the ground says there is a field, and the ground wins, because it is the larger surface and it is continuous with the verge you can see under your own wheels.

THE MISSING PIECE IS THAT THE GROUND HAS TO STOP. A cliff down is not an absence of scenery; it is an EDGE, with nothing beyond it but air and whatever is far below. The biome's ground is painted across the full width of the screen from the far edge to the bottom - which is what [[RLG-265]] part one relied on to uncover the verge - so on the hazard side that fill has to end at the rail's line and give way to something that reads as depth.

THREE THINGS A DROP COULD BE MADE OF, none of them decided:
- the ground ending at the rail with a dark falling face below it, the way the canyon wall is a face rather than a fill;
- a distant FLOOR far below, hazed, the way the bridge's water sits thirteen camera heights down and is proved to read as depth by being hazed;
- the far skyline showing THROUGH the gap, so the eye reads across the valley rather than down it.

THE BRIDGE ALREADY SOLVED THIS ONCE and its record is the place to start: bridge-test asserts that the water is "far enough off to be hazed, which is what says it is below you", and that the near and far water are different colours. A mountain drop is the same problem with rock instead of water.

NOT MEASURED YET AND NOT STARTED.
