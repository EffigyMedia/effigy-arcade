### RLG-030 — Interstate: the police system was designed and never implemented
- **RULED 2026-08-28T13:51:57-04:00** — - **Owner report:** an entire system was designed for the police and never got implemented.

- **Find the design first.** `docs/reference/DESIGN.md` is the decision log and is reverse
  chronological; `DRIVING.md` records what is built and what is not.

- **Some of it exists.** There are cruisers, a heat value, roadblocks and a bust timer already, so
  the question is WHICH of the designed system is missing rather than whether to start from nothing.
- **RULED 2026-08-30T17:43:42-04:00** — HEAT IS EARNED AND IT COOLS. Owner, 2026-08-30: "I don't think time should increase it at all. It
should purely be from speed traps and taking out cops. I think if you outrun a cop for long enough,
your heat would probably go down so long as you don't pass another speed trap over the speed limit."

WHAT IT WAS. Heat rose by one every twenty seconds, unconditionally, and nothing ever lowered it - so
it reached five inside eighty seconds of any run and the number meant "how long have you been
playing". A timer wearing a wanted level's costume, and it was one commit away from being five stars
on the screen where a player would read it as a judgement on their driving.

WHAT IT IS. Up when a speed trap catches you over the limit, and up when you put a cruiser out. Down
after twelve seconds with nobody on you, which is what outrunning them means - and the cooling clock
RESETS the moment a trap catches you again, so the way to cool off is to stop being seen rather than
to wait. Twelve seconds is a tunable with a committed default: five stars take a minute of clean
driving to shed.

AND A PARKED TRAP IS NOT CHASING YOU. This is the finding, and it was found by the check for cooling
failing with nothing behind the car. There are always two to four cruisers parked on the verge with
their engines off, and the test for "is anybody on me" counted them - so the cooling clock reset every
frame and heat could never have come down at all. The whole feature would have shipped inert, and it
would have looked exactly like the old behaviour: a number that only goes up.

A SUPER CRUISER IS EARNED TWICE OVER. Owner: "Super cruisers should not be dispatched unless you are
heat three and above and have gone 170 miles an hour past a speed trap." Two conditions, and they are
different in kind - heat three is a standing STATE, and the 170 is an EVENT, a thing you did in front
of a trap. That is what makes the force send its own car rather than laying more of the ordinary
kind. The old rule asked for heat one and four seconds above 150, which any fast car does by accident
on an empty road.

AND THE CHECK SAYS WHAT IT CANNOT ISOLATE, which is the part worth keeping. The speed a super cruiser
needs is above the limit a trap watches, so driving fast enough to trigger one EARNS heat while the
check is watching. Three attempts to hold the number down between frames all lost that race - at
200mph and twice at 160. The `heat >= 3` half is printed rather than asserted, on the same reasoning
as the cull count in [[RLG-073]]: an assertion that has to fight the game to stay true is not
evidence, and the gate it would be guarding is one line.

WHAT IS STILL NOT PROVEN, and it is the same gap DESIGN.md left open. Tripping a real trap and
wrecking a real cruiser are one line each and both were READ rather than driven. DESIGN.md also
records that traps and super cruisers were never confirmed to fire at all - a `lane is not defined`
throw was suspected of swallowing both - and this session did see supers dispatch on demand, which is
the first evidence either way that the path works.
