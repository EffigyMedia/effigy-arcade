### RLG-180 — Locked cars show as grey silhouettes with the way to win them written underneath

TRACKED, NOT STARTED. Owner, 2026-09-08: *"I want to show all the cars in the garage as grey
silhouettes until you unlock them. This does not include the production and utility vehicles.
Those are secret unlocks."* And, elaborating: *"I figure let me show the lockable cars as
silhouettes — players would never know that they can unlock stuff, especially if when they are
locked, it says underneath them how to unlock them. Add the police cruiser and the super cruiser
as well unless you think those should be secret unlocks too."*

**THE PROBLEM IT SOLVES IS DISCOVERABILITY, NOT DECORATION.** A locked car that is simply absent
from the garage is indistinguishable from a car that does not exist. Everything the tournament
ladder pays out is currently invisible until the moment it is won, so the ladder has prizes
nobody knows they are climbing towards. A silhouette with its condition written underneath turns
the garage into the place where the game explains what it is for.

**THERE ARE SEVEN LOCKS AND THEY ARE ALL ALREADY IN THE CODE**, read rather than assumed, with
the exact conditions — this is what the captions have to say, and none of it needs designing:

| save key | what it opens | how it is won |
|---|---|---|
| `super` | the SUPER class | first overall in a **sports** tournament |
| `formula` | the FORMULA class, all three | first overall in a **super** tournament |
| `iridescent` | the iridescent paints | first overall in a **formula** tournament |
| `cruiser` | the police CRUISER | first in a **sports** tournament **with pursuit on** |
| `supercruiser` | the SUPER CRUISER | first in a **super** tournament **with pursuit on** |
| `utility` | the utility vehicles | 25 miles in one timed run outside a race |
| `production` | the production vehicles | 50 miles in one timed run outside a race |

**THE OWNER'S SPLIT LANDS EXACTLY ON THE SEAM BETWEEN THOSE TWO GROUPS**, which is worth saying
because it is a reason and not a coincidence. The first five are **tournament results**: the
player is already doing the thing, and telling them what it pays costs no mystery. The last two
are **distance milestones on a mode nobody drives for distance** — fifty miles of TEST DRIVE is
not a thing a player stumbles into on purpose, and finding out it paid something is the whole
pleasure of it. So: silhouettes for the ladder, silence for the odometer.

**THE OWNER ASKED WHETHER THE POLICE CARS SHOULD BE SECRET TOO. THEY SHOULD NOT — SHOW THEM.**
Their condition is not merely stateable, it is *instructive*: "win a sports tournament with
pursuit on" tells a player that the pursuit switch changes what a tournament pays, which is a
real rule of this game that nothing currently teaches. Hiding them buys a small surprise and
costs the one place that rule could be learned. They also sit naturally beside the class they
are balanced against — a cruiser against the sports class, a super cruiser against the supers —
so a silhouette in that row reads correctly rather than as an oddity.

**WHAT MUST NOT HAPPEN, and it is the trap in a silhouette feature:** the silhouette must not
leak the car. A grey fill of the real sprite gives away the shape, the width and the class at a
glance, which is most of what a car IS in this game. That is probably fine and may even be the
point — but it is a decision, and it should be made deliberately rather than fallen into by
reusing the painter with a flat colour.

**AND THE SECRET UNLOCKS MUST BE ABSENT, NOT GREYED.** A silhouette that says nothing underneath
it is worse than no silhouette at all: it advertises that something exists and refuses to say
what, which is a puzzle the game never intends to answer. `utility` and `production` leave no
trace in the garage until they are won.

**WHERE THE WORK IS.** The garage is Redline's own screen and the unlock predicate already
exists — `unlocked(key)` at `road.js:633`, reading the per-machine save. The captions are new
copy and the silhouette is a painter change. Nothing here needs a new save field, and nothing
here should touch `road.js`'s driving code.
- **RULED 2026-09-09T08:20:45-04:00** — ### 2026-09-09 — built, and the police question is answered by the code rather than by taste

**THE OWNER ASKED WHETHER THE POLICE CARS SHOULD BE SECRET TOO. THEY ARE SHOWN**, for the reason
this record gave: "win a sports tournament with HOT PURSUIT ON" is the only place in the game that
says the pursuit switch changes what a tournament pays. Hiding it buys a small surprise and costs
the one lesson. Keeping "secret unlock" a two-item category rather than a four-item one is also
what keeps the odometer prizes feeling like a discovery.

**BUT ONLY THE CRUISER CAN BE SHOWN, AND THAT IS NOT A CHOICE — IT IS A DEFECT FOUND WHILE
BUILDING THIS.** `BODY.SUPERCRUISER` carries `npc:true`, so it is not a garage car at all and
never reaches the list. Meanwhile the tournament writes the `supercruiser` unlock flag when a
supercar gold is won under pursuit, `garageBodies` has a debug branch for it, and the reward
screen counts it among the things you have won. **So the game awards a prize that unlocks
nothing.** That is its own ruling and is recorded in the thread; it is not fixed here, because
making the interceptor drivable is a feature rather than a fix and the owner has not asked for it.

**THE GARAGE WENT FROM THREE CARS TO TEN**, measured by walking it with the real arrows: three
owned, seven locked. Production and utility are absent, as the owner split them.

**TWO THINGS WENT WRONG AND BOTH ARE WORTH THE NEXT SESSION'S TIME.**

*The silhouettes were unreachable in the first build.* `enforceCarRules` runs on every garage
render, and pointing it at the playable list meant cycling onto a locked car swapped it straight
back out — the garage cycled between the three cars already owned and looked exactly as it had
before. **Browsing is not driving:** that rule keeps a SAVE honest and must ask the wider
question, while the DRIVE button is what refuses.

*And the first silhouette was a tinted photograph.* A `source-atop` fill at .30 alpha left the
tail lights, the headlights and the paint perfectly readable. It is opaque now — a gradient rather
than a flat grey, so the shape still reads as a solid object standing on a floor. **`source-atop`
is the right technique either way**: it fills only where the sprites already painted, so the
outline is the car's own at its own size, and a hand-drawn second shape would be the duplication
[[RLG-053]] and [[RLG-177]] were both about.

**THE DRIVE BUTTON FOLLOWS [[RLG-115]]'s SHAPE** rather than inventing one: greyed with the reason
given, `disabled` to stop the press, and a second check inside the action, because a hardware
back-press or a stale veil must not walk a car you have not won onto the road.

**VERIFIED BY WALKING THE GARAGE**, not by reading the list: `tools/garage-shot.py` taps the real
arrows and reports each card's name, whether it is locked, what the caption says and whether DRIVE
is disabled. That is what caught both mistakes above.
- **RULED 2026-09-09T08:23:39-04:00** — ### 2026-09-09 — and a locked car gives nothing else away

**THE STATS WERE ALREADY GONE and the name was not.** This record argued for keeping it — "the
shape and the name are the invitation" — and the owner took it further. They are right, and the
reason is worth keeping: a named car with a shape and a condition is a **catalogue entry you
happen to be locked out of**, while `???` is a question. The silhouette still says what CLASS it
is, which is all the invitation needs; the name is the reward for winning it.

**THE CAPTURE TOOL BROKE ON IT TWICE, and both are fixed in the file.** `???` is not a legal
Windows filename, so the screenshot died — the cards are named by position now. And the walk
stopped when a card name repeated, which was fine while every car had its own name and wrong the
moment seven of them were called `???`: the second locked card looked like one already seen and
the walk ended three cards in. It asks the engine how many cars the garage holds and walks exactly
that many.
