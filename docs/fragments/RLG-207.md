### RLG-207 — A merge is decided like a person: both mirrors, both lanes, and a change of mind
- **RULED 2026-09-10T14:17:36-04:00** — TRACKED, NOT STARTED. Owner, 2026-09-10, specifying how a merge is decided and announced.

**THE SPEC, IN THE OWNER'S OWN TERMS.** A vehicle's chance to indicate is decided and assigned
**when it is spawned**. When something tells it to merge, it checks both sides for a legally
available lane; it checks for vehicles approaching from either side **as a human does - left, right,
then back left and back right in the mirrors**; and at the same time it works out which way it is
more able to keep MOVING FORWARD, by looking for blocking vehicles ahead in the left lane and in the
right lane, and picks the better of the two. A vehicle that was assigned no indicator merges
immediately. One that was assigned an indicator starts it and **waits one to three seconds**, and
during that wait it keeps evaluating: if the circumstances change enough that it should not merge,
it **cancels the indicator and stays in its lane**, waits a few seconds, and begins the whole
process again.

---

**MOST OF THE SHAPE IS ALREADY BUILT, and stating which part is the whole value of this record.**
[[RLG-052]] is `built` and it put the announce-then-move sequence in: a car picks a direction, sets
`blinkDir`, sets `mergeWait`, and commits when the wait runs out. **Five things in the spec are not
what the code does.**

**1 - THE HABIT IS DECIDED LATE, NOT AT SPAWN.** `if(c.signals === undefined) c.signals =
Math.random() > 0.22;` sits inside the merge branch, so a car's habit is rolled the first time it
ever wants to change lane rather than when it is built. The comment beside it already says the
intent - *"decided once when the car is built rather than rolled per manoeuvre, because it is a
habit rather than a coin toss"* - and the placement does not match it. A car that never merges never
has one. It is a small move to the spawner and it makes the fleet inspectable: how many indicators
are out there becomes a property of the road rather than of what has happened on it so far.

**2 - THE WAIT IS 1.1 TO 1.8 SECONDS, AND THE OWNER ASKED FOR 1 TO 3.** `rnd(1.1, 1.8)`. It is one
constant and it should be a named tunable with a committed default rather than a literal.

**3 - NOTHING IS RE-EVALUATED DURING THE WAIT.** The car checks the gap ONCE, at the instant the
wait expires. So a car that announced a move into a space which closed a second ago carries on
announcing it, and only discovers the problem at the moment it would have moved. The owner asked for
continuous evaluation across the wait.

**4 - THERE IS NO CANCEL.** When the commit-time check fails, the code sets `mergeCool` and lets the
indicator run down on its own - and it has a comment defending exactly that: *"with its indicator
still running down - which is also what a real one does."* **The owner has now ruled the other way**
for the case where the car decides against the move: *"they will cancel their indicator and remain
in their lane."* Those are not quite the same event - one is a gap closing at the last instant, the
other is a driver changing their mind during the wait - and the new behaviour is the second. Whether
a last-instant failure should also kill the lamp is the owner's to say.

**5 - THE MIRROR HALF OF THE LOOK IS NOT IN THERE.** `laneClear` and `wouldBlock` ask whether the
target lane is OCCUPIED and whether taking it would close the road for somebody else. Neither asks
about a car **approaching from behind in that lane at a closing speed** - the back-left and
back-right of the owner's description. A lane that is empty right now and has a fast car twenty
lengths back is clear by the current test and is not a lane a person would take.

**WHAT IS ALREADY RIGHT, so it is not rebuilt by accident.** Both directions are considered rather
than a preferred side. The forward-progress comparison exists and is the deciding term:
`laneSpeed(c, target) - laneSpeed(c, here)` against `mergeEdge(c)`, which is the "am I more able to
keep going that way" question the owner described, with an OUTLAW holding a much lower bar than
everyone else. And the last-gap guard is [[RLG-037]]'s corridor guarantee, which must survive any
change here - it already fails about one run in three and this ruling must not make that worse.

**AND THE PERSONALITIES ARE NOW IN SCOPE FOR IT.** [[RLG-206]] gave every car a personality with a
declared obedience. The indicator habit is a second axis of the same idea, and the owner has not said
whether the two are linked - whether an OUTLAW is less likely to have been assigned an indicator, or
whether the habit is independent of the personality. **Ask before assuming.** The engine's standing
rule is that the car is capable and the DRIVER decides, so it would be consistent for the habit to
follow the driver; that is an inference and not an instruction.

**WHAT WOULD PROVE IT.** `tools/merge-test.py` already measures traffic's lateral position over time
and is run by hand rather than in a suite, and `signalsStarted` / `signalledMerges` already count
announcements against completions. A cancel is the new event and nothing counts it: a car that
announces, thinks better of it and stays is invisible to both counters, and would read as an
announcement that never completed - which is also what a BUG looks like. Count it separately.
- **RULED 2026-09-10T17:41:13-04:00** — **CORRECTION, before a line of this was built: THE MIRROR CHECK EXISTS.** This record said *"the
mirror half of the look is not in there... Neither asks about a car approaching from behind in that
lane at a closing speed."* That is wrong. `laneClear` carries it, in a line whose comment says
exactly what it is:

    /* closing fast from behind counts as occupied even when it is not yet */
    return dz <= -needB && dz > -6000 && o.spd > c.spd + 900;

So a lane with a fast car coming up behind IS refused today, and it is refused for traffic, for the
police and for the player, because all three lists are swept. **Four deltas, not five.**

**HOW THE CLAIM CAME TO BE MADE.** `laneClear` was read for its FIRST clause - the occupancy window,
`dz > -needB && dz < needF` - and the fallback return underneath it was not read. The fragment then
stated the absence as a fact. This is the same shape as the citation fault recorded in [[RLG-047]]
this morning: reading part of a thing and reporting the whole.

**WHAT IS ACTUALLY WORTH ASKING ABOUT IT is a different question, and it is a tunable rather than a
gap.** The look-back is a flat **6000 units** and the closing threshold a flat **900**. [[RLG-205]]
has just replaced exactly that shape elsewhere - a fixed distance that meant half a second at speed
and eight seconds at a crawl - with a window made of TIME. The same argument applies here: 6000
units is about 0.6 seconds of closing at motorway speed, which is a glance rather than a mirror
check. **Not changed here**, because the owner asked for the merge decision and not for this, and
because widening what counts as "occupied" makes cars merge LESS, which pulls directly against
[[RLG-037]]'s corridor guarantee.
- **RULED 2026-09-10T18:00:34-04:00** — **BUILT IN UNT-260 AND NOT PROVEN. Read the last section before trusting any of it.**

**THE FOUR DELTAS ARE IN.**

**1 - The habit is assigned at spawn, and it follows the driver.** `SIGNAL_ODDS` is 0.95 for a
COMMUTER, 0.75 for a SPEEDER, 0.25 for an OUTLAW and 0 for a RACER - the owner's answer when asked
whether the habit should follow the personality or be independent of it. `signalsFor(mind)` is
called at every place a car is built: the main spawner, the behind-the-cruiser spawner, the
ambulance, `placePatrol` and `parkTraffic`. The old line inside the merge branch survives only as a
fallback for a car staged by something that predates the field, and its comment says so - it is the
habit being MISSING rather than a second roll of it.

**2 - The wait is `MERGE_ANNOUNCE`, 1 to 3 seconds**, a named tunable with a committed default where
it was `rnd(1.1, 1.8)` written inline.

**3 - The car keeps looking while it announces.** `MERGE_RELOOK` is a quarter of a second, which is
four looks inside the shortest possible announcement and avoids sweeping three lists per announcing
car per frame for no gain.

**4 - And it can change its mind.** If the target lane stops being clear during the wait, the car
cancels: `mergeWait` and `mergeWant` cleared, **the indicator switched off**, and `mergeCool` set to
`lookAgain(c, 2, 4)` so it sits for a few seconds before starting the whole process again. The wait
is `lookAgain` rather than a flat roll so it is the DRIVER's, like every other wait in that loop -
an OUTLAW is impatient by declaration and comes back to the idea almost at once.

**THE CANCEL IS A DIFFERENT EVENT FROM THE ONE ALREADY THERE, and the distinction is deliberate.**
The existing commit-time check finds a gap that closed at the last instant and leaves the lamp
running down - which the code defends in a comment, and which is what a real one does. This is a
driver watching the road and thinking better of it, and the lamp goes off. `mergesAborted` counts
them apart from `signalledMerges`, because an announcement that never completes is ALSO exactly what
a defect looks like, and a check that could not tell them apart would call a working change of mind
a bug.

**TWO THINGS FOUND WHILE WRITING IT.** The cancel was first written as `continue`, which would have
skipped the rest of that car's frame - its speed, its brake lights, its blink. And `mergeLook` is
guarded against being undefined even though nothing can leave it so today, because the failure would
be SILENT: `undefined - dt` is NaN, `NaN <= 0` is false, and the car would simply never look again -
which is precisely the behaviour this ruling was written to add.

---

**NOT PROVEN, AND THE REASON IS THE MACHINE.** `tools/merge-mind-test.py` is written and asserts the
whole claim - that every car carries a habit whether it has merged or not, that a RACER never does,
that a COMMUTER carries it more often than an OUTLAW, and that announcements are both completed and
ABANDONED. **It has never produced a result.**

On 2026-09-10 this machine stopped being able to run a browser harness against the two driving
cabinets. `smoke-test` fell to 4 of 6, both driving cabinets timing out at `Page.goto` waiting for
`load`, **and it fails identically with `road.js` stashed at the last commit** - which is what says
the fault is not in this work. A direct probe shows the truth: `window.__road` is an object, the
canvas is there, there are ZERO page errors, and `document.readyState` is stuck at **interactive**.
The engine boots and runs; the page never reaches `complete`, so `load` never fires. The static
server hands over the same 1.5MB of `road.js` in 0.09 seconds.

**THIS IS THE SECOND TIME.** The thread already records it from 2026-09-09: every Playwright harness
timing out at `Page.goto` while curl fetched the same URL instantly, failing identically on the last
known-good commit, cleared by a reboot.

**AND IT EXPOSES A REAL FRAGILITY THAT IS NOT ENVIRONMENTAL: 110 harness call sites wait on `load`.**
Every one of them is unusable in this state, for a page that is running perfectly. A shared boot
helper in `harness.py` that waits for the ENGINE rather than for the document would make the suite
survive it. Filed as its own job rather than done here, because it cannot be tested right now
either.

**SO THE CODE IS COMMITTED AND NOT PUSHED.** It is syntax-checked and read through; no behaviour of
it has been observed. It must not reach the device until `merge-mind-test` has run.
