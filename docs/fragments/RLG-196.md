### RLG-196 — A rear wing seen from the front is the far end of the car
- **RULED 2026-09-09T19:39:41-04:00** — **Owner, 2026-09-09:** *"we need to do the same thing with the rear spoilers from the front view as we
did with the pick up truck since they are further away, they need to be slightly darker from the front
than they are from the back."*

**THIS IS THE THIRD PLACE THE RULE HAS LANDED, WHICH MAKES IT A RULE RATHER THAN THREE FIXES.** The
formula car's two wings ([[RLG-185]]), the pickup's cab ([[RLG-193]]), and now the supercars' wings.
It is worth stating in one line so it does not have to be rediscovered a fourth time:

> **Two ends of a vehicle share one outline ([[RLG-184]]), so what tells the player which end they
> are looking at is the DETAIL inside it - and depth is one of those details. Any part that lives at
> one END of the car is near in one view and far in the other, and should say so.**

A wing is at the back. From behind you are standing at it; from the front it is the furthest thing
away, past the whole length of the body. Both ends drew it in the same three colours, so the face had
no far end - the same fault the pickup's cab had, on a part that is larger and higher and therefore
more obvious.

**`carWing` TAKES A `near` FLAG and the two painters pass opposite values**, which is exactly the shape
`formulaWing` and `formulaLowWing` already use. `shade(..., 0.74)` on the three body values - the same
factor the pickup's cab uses, because it is the same distance being described - so a wing keeps the
car's own colour and simply sits further off.

**AND THE LIFT ALONG THE TOP EDGE DIMS SEPARATELY.** That white line is a HIGHLIGHT rather than paint:
shading it with the body values would have kept it at full strength against a darker blade and made
the far wing read as a brighter object rather than a more distant one. It drops from 16 to 10 per cent
instead.

It changes colour and not shape, so the seventeen silhouettes are unchanged, which the check
confirms.
