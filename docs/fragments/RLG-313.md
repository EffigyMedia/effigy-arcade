### RLG-313 — Nothing in the mirror may pop in or out - it fades
- **RULED 2026-09-21T00:17:36-04:00** — OWNER, 2026-09-21: 'Things shouldn't pop in or out in the mirror. I'd rather they faded in.'

THE WINDSCREEN HAS THIS AND THE GLASS DOES NOT. [[RLG-302]] put the arrival ramp on the gantry, the finish line and the bridge towers, and `EDGE_FADE` is the distance it runs over - now worth about 0.41 seconds since the draw was doubled. The mirror has its own reach, `MIRROR_BACK`, and its own walk, and things entering and leaving it were never given the same treatment.

AND THE MIRROR IS WHERE A POP IS MOST VISIBLE, which is the part that makes this worth doing rather than tidy. A car ahead of you is approached and grows; a car behind is RECEDING, so its last moment on screen is at the far edge of the glass where it is smallest and where an abrupt disappearance is the whole of what you see. [[RLG-133]] is the recorded case - checkpoint boards vanishing from the mirror - and it was found from a device.

WHAT THIS IS NOT. It is not [[RLG-041]]'s flicker, which is a sprite bucket reaching one segment further than the road pass walks and is a geometric fault rather than a fade. Reaching for a fade to hide that would be covering a defect with a dissolve.

WHAT HAS TO BE DECIDED: whether the ramp is the SAME ramp - one expression read by both views, which is this codebase's standing answer to a pair like this - or whether the glass needs its own distance because it looks a third as far. [[RLG-312]]'s mirror survey should list what fades and what does not at the same time as listing what is drawn and what is not; they are one pass over the same list.

Not started.
- **RULED 2026-09-21T23:41:32-04:00** — BUILT in 0.14.121 (UNT-414). THE DECISION THE FRAGMENT ASKED FOR: the glass keeps its OWN distance, not the windscreen's. The windscreen's arrival is EDGE_FADE, the last 6 per cent of its draw; the glass lets go over the last quarter of MIRROR_BACK, which is the ramp the owner already ruled for the glass's roadside in RLG-128. It is now ONE expression for everything in the glass, glassFade(z), so the roadside, the wood's face, the lamps, the portal, every sprite and the faces all let go the same way.

AND THE NEAR END. Anything that spans the road - a board, the finish line, a bridge tower, a roadblock - takes up over GLASS_ARRIVE (600 units, about a tenth of a second at the interstate's usual speed) behind the car, where it used to arrive whole and wider than the pane. A car is deliberately not given this: a car you overtake is the car the windscreen just showed, and a translucent one right behind you is the fault RLG-218 removed. A spawned car keeps its own arrivals ramp.

THE FACES. A canyon's face and a mountainside's closing and opening faces in the glass now carry glassFade at their own distance, so they no longer vanish when the boundary leaves the glass.

PROVED by tools/glass-fade-test.py, which holds a board and the finish line at nine distances behind the car and reads the alpha the glass set for each (API.glassTrace): 0.10 at 260 units, solid from 1,200 to 25,000, then 0.59, 0.24 and 0.06 across the last quarter. The falsify arm draws everything solid and fails exactly the arrival and let-go checks. The trace records the alpha the glass sets for an item on the line before it draws, not a pixel; whether the fade reads as smooth at speed is the owner's call on a device.

NOT DONE: traffic cars far behind are faded by glassFade but were not measured here, and the faces' fade has no harness of its own - canyon-mouth-test only proves they still paint.
- **RULED 2026-09-22T09:22:03-04:00** — REVIEW OF UNT-414, filed here because review.py cannot file a review after its unit is committed. Fresh-eyes review of UNT-414 by a separate agent.
