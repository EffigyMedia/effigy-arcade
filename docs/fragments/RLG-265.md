### RLG-265 — The road edge is the biome's own ground, with guard rails at the limit of travel
- **RULED 2026-09-15T23:31:14-04:00** — OWNER, 2026-09-15: "We also need to remove the white and red road edge and replace them with whatever the natural ground for that biome is. At the furthest extent where the car stops moving we need to put guard rails. On the bridge and in the tunnel, we need to stop the car at the natural physical boundary that they draw."

THREE PARTS, AND THE THIRD IS THE ONE WITH A RULE UNDER IT.
1. The red-and-white kerb strip at the edge of the carriageway goes, and the ground beside the road is whatever the biome paints there - grass, sand, snow, rock.
2. Where the car can go no further sideways there are GUARD RAILS, so the limit of travel is a thing the player can see rather than an invisible wall. Today that limit is `|x| > 1.16`, which is where a cruiser is hurt by "barrier" already.
3. ON A BRIDGE AND IN A TUNNEL the limit is the structure itself: the deck edge and the bore wall. The car stops where the drawn boundary is, not at the open road`s limit.

NOT ASKED YET: whether hitting a rail damages the car or only stops it, and whether the rail is drawn on every biome or only where a real road would carry one.
- **RULED 2026-09-15T23:47:45-04:00** — OWNER, 2026-09-15, on what a rail does: "Hitting a rail shouldn't do much to you but grinding up against it should slow you down hard, harder than simply being off the road."

SO THE RAIL IS A SPEED PENALTY, NOT A CRASH. A glancing hit costs little damage. Sustained contact - the car held against it - sheds speed at a rate above the verge penalty, which RLG-016 set as the cost of running off the road. The numbers are not set yet.

AND THE BIOMES, on the proposal put to the owner and accepted as written:

| biome | at the limit |
|---|---|
| MOUNTAIN, CANYON, COASTAL, SWAMP, FOREST, JUNGLE | steel guard rail |
| CITY | concrete barrier |
| BRIDGE | the parapet, which is the deck edge |
| TUNNEL | the bore wall |
| FARMLAND | no rail: open verge |
| DESERT | no rail: sand berm |
| TUNDRA | no rail: snow bank with marker poles |

Where there is no rail the limit of travel does not move; the ground and a low berm show the edge instead of a barrier.
- **RULED 2026-09-15T23:58:54-04:00** — OWNER, 2026-09-15, redesigning the biomes around the rail rather than bolting a rail onto them: "In mountain we have big mountainous scenery on both sides of the road. I feel like we should pick one side of the road, and the other side is a cliff with a guard rail. The side with the mountainous stuff does not have a guard rail and hitting the mountainous stuff is the physical stuff you can bang up against just like coastal and farmland. Mountain will spawn the cliff on one side or the other with the mountain scenery on the opposing side. Tundra I feel could be like a snowy version of the desert - instead of mountainous we can still keep mountains as its skyline."

THE PRINCIPLE THAT CAME OUT OF IT, put to the owner and settled: each side of the road is either a HAZARD side - a drop or water, where the rail goes - or a SOLID side, which is rock, trees or a bank the car bangs against and which needs no rail. A rail in front of something already solid is the furniture RLG-264 just deleted.

THE TABLE, as settled (it replaces the one above where they differ):

TWO OF THESE CORRECT WHAT WAS PROPOSED FIRST. Forest and jungle were to be railed, and the owner chose the tree line. The canyon was proposed as a rim road with a drop; the owner: "I envisioned the road at the bottom of a canyon with the canyon walls on either side, no rails."

AND TUNDRA IS RESHAPED: a snowy desert rather than a second mountain, with the mountains kept as skyline only.
- **RULED 2026-09-16T08:26:13-04:00** — PART ONE IS BUILT, v0.14.52. The red-and-white kerb is gone and the tarmac meets the biome's own ground.

NOTHING REPLACES IT, AND THAT IS THE WHOLE CHANGE. The ground is already painted across the full width of the screen in the same function - `ctx.fillRect(0, y2, W, H - y2)`, far edge to the bottom - and the road is laid on top of it. So the kerb was covering ground that was there the whole time, and not drawing it uncovers exactly what the owner asked for in every biome at once. No new colour table, and nothing to keep in step with the twelve `grassLo`/`grassHi` pairs that already exist.

THE ROAD EDGE IS STILL MARKED. The white shoulder line inside the tarmac at 0.965 is untouched. The red-and-white strip was never the thing that said "the tarmac ends here" - it said "this is an ordinary roadway", which is the thing the owner is removing.

A DECK KEEPS ITS KERB, for the reason [[RLG-112]] gave it one: a bridge's railing needs something to stand up from, and that kerb is concrete which is actually there rather than road furniture. It is the `deckB.truss` branch that already existed; only the ordinary-road branch is dropped.

A TUNNEL NEEDED NO EXCEPTION, and that was the risk worth checking rather than assuming. `drawBore` runs AFTER `drawRoad` in the frame, so the bore paints its own walls and floor over the road - a screenshot of the tunnel shows the walls meeting the tarmac with no ground visible between them.

MEASURED THE ONLY WAY A PICTURE CAN BE. One frame per biome, all twelve, captured at dusk with the weather off. FARMLAND reads as tarmac straight onto grass with the shoulder line intact; CITY's ground is already a concrete tone so the road runs onto grey; TUNNEL is clean; BRIDGE keeps its concrete kerb with the red railing standing up from it. bridge-test, farroad-test, wall-test, occlusion-test and smoke-test all pass.

WHAT IS NOT BUILT, and it is most of the ruling: the guard rails at the limit of travel, the hazard-side/solid-side table, mountain choosing its cliff side per stretch, the canyon floor, tundra as a snowy desert, and stopping the car at the deck edge and the bore wall rather than at the open road's limit. The map for that work: the player's lateral clamp is 1.18 and is written out at SEVEN steering sites rather than as one named constant, `edgeX()` at 22148 is the only place that already narrows it (for a bore), `TRUSS.out` is 1.13 and `boreWallX()` is about 1.25, and `sideRoll` is already a stable per-stretch side coin - but it is a single global that the sea and the crops both read, so a hazard side that must differ from the water's would need its own roll beside it.

THE TWO NUMBERS THE RULING LEAVES OPEN ARE STILL UNASKED: what a glancing rail hit costs in damage, and the rate sustained contact sheds speed at, which the owner ruled must be above the verge penalty. `OFF_SPD` is 4200 and the off-road block scrubs at `1 - dt*1.9`; those are the numbers a rail has to beat.
- **RULED 2026-09-16T09:51:44-04:00** — A CIRCUIT KEEPS ITS RED AND WHITE KERB, v0.14.53. Owner, 2026-09-16, after part one shipped: "We need to keep the red and white kerb for Motorsport."

PART ONE WAS RIGHT AND ITS REACH WAS WRONG. `road.js` is both driving games, so dropping the ordinary-road branch of the kerb dropped it from the circuit as well. That is the carry-over rule biting exactly where [[RLG-002]] said it would: Motorsport is parked, and a change made for Interstate which the circuit would have to undo later is the thing that rule exists to stop.

THE STRIP MEANS THE OPPOSITE ON A CIRCUIT. On an interstate a red-and-white kerb says ORDINARY ROADWAY, which is the thing the owner removed. On a circuit it says APEX: a racing circuit has red and white kerbs at every apex and every exit, and it is the one part of the track that tells a driver where the limit of the tarmac is before they arrive at it. The same pixels, the opposite meaning, decided by which discipline is running.

THE GATE IS THE SEAM AND NOTHING ELSE. The kerb quads are drawn when `deckB.truss || CFG.circuitOnly`, and the colour expression is the original ternary restored unchanged - concrete `#8e8a80`/`#6f6b62` on a deck, red and white `#c9c3b4`/`#8c3346` otherwise, alternating on the segment's own `dark` parity. `CFG.circuitOnly` is set in one place, `games/sw/motorsport.html`, so the engine still never names the game.

MEASURED BY LOOKING, WHICH IS THE ONLY WAY A KERB CAN BE. One midday frame from each cabinet with the road cleared: Interstate's tarmac runs straight onto the city ground with the shoulder line intact and no kerb, and Motorsport's canyon circuit carries the red-and-white strip down both edges. circuit-test, bridge-test and smoke-test all pass, which says the seam still holds and a deck still gets concrete rather than red.

WHAT THIS DOES NOT TOUCH: part one stands for Interstate, and the rest of the ruling - the guard rails, the hazard-side table, the canyon floor, tundra as a snowy desert, and the deck edge and bore wall as limits - is still unbuilt. The two open numbers are still unasked.
- **RULED 2026-09-16T09:59:21-04:00** — THE LIMIT OF TRAVEL IS ONE NAMED CONSTANT NOW, v0.14.54. `EDGE_X`, declared beside `LANE_X` and `LANE_W` where the other lateral units live. Nothing about the game changes; this is the groundwork the rail cannot be built without.

IT WAS WRITTEN OUT SEVEN TIMES. The literal `1.18` sat at every site that clamps a lateral position: the player's own steering, the drift, the two scripted-steer paths, the shunt a collision applies, the autopilot's target, and `edgeX()` - which is the only one that ever narrows it, for a bore. Seven copies of a number are harmless while nothing else reads them. A GUARD RAIL READS THEM. A rail drawn from one copy would disagree with the car at the other six, and a rail the car stops short of, or drives through, is worse than no rail at all.

`edgeX()` IS STILL THE FUNCTION TO ASK. It returns `EDGE_X` on the open road and `Math.min(EDGE_X, boreWallX() - playerW()/2 - BORE.skin)` inside a bore, so the tunnel still narrows the limit off the drawn wall. The constant is the open road's value that it starts from; it is not a replacement for the question.

A GREEN SUITE COULD NOT TELL ME THIS WORKED, and that is the shape this project keeps getting caught by. Every harness passes whether the sites read `EDGE_X` or still read `1.18`, because the two are the same number today. So `tools/edge-proof.py` NARROWS the constant to 0.90 and asserts the car follows: `tunnel-wall-test`'s own measurement of how far out the car actually reaches went 1.149 to 0.900 and back to 1.149. It reported the other answer, which is what makes the passing run mean something, and it refuses to finish if `road.js` is not restored.

WHAT THE PROOF COVERS AND WHAT IT DOES NOT, stated rather than blurred. Driving the car proves the STEERING clamp and `edgeX()`. It does not exercise the collision shunt, the two scripted-steer paths or the autopilot target - nothing in that harness scripts a steer or takes a hit. Those four rest on the static half: no clamp site anywhere in the engine still writes the literal. That is the weaker claim and the proof prints it as one.

tunnel-wall-test, wall-test and drive-test pass; `pack.sh --check` validates. drive-test is 36 of 37, and the one failure is the nitrous settle line already recorded as environmental - it has failed with `road.js` stashed at HEAD, and it reported 97.8% here against the 97.9% and 98.1% on record.
