### RLG-265 — The road edge is the biome's own ground, with guard rails at the limit of travel
- **RULED 2026-09-15T23:31:14-04:00** — OWNER, 2026-09-15: "We also need to remove the white and red road edge and replace them with whatever the natural ground for that biome is. At the furthest extent where the car stops moving we need to put guard rails. On the bridge and in the tunnel, we need to stop the car at the natural physical boundary that they draw."

THREE PARTS, AND THE THIRD IS THE ONE WITH A RULE UNDER IT.
1. The red-and-white kerb strip at the edge of the carriageway goes, and the ground beside the road is whatever the biome paints there - grass, sand, snow, rock.
2. Where the car can go no further sideways there are GUARD RAILS, so the limit of travel is a thing the player can see rather than an invisible wall. Today that limit is `|x| > 1.16`, which is where a cruiser is hurt by "barrier" already.
3. ON A BRIDGE AND IN A TUNNEL the limit is the structure itself: the deck edge and the bore wall. The car stops where the drawn boundary is, not at the open road`s limit.

NOT ASKED YET: whether hitting a rail damages the car or only stops it, and whether the rail is drawn on every biome or only where a real road would carry one.
- **RULED 2026-09-15T23:47:45-04:00** — OWNER, 2026-09-15, on what a rail does: "Hitting a rail shouldn't do much to you but grinding up against it should slow you down hard, harder than simply being off the road."

SO THE RAIL IS A SPEED PENALTY, NOT A CRASH. A glancing hit costs little damage. Sustained contact - the car held against it - sheds speed at a rate above the verge penalty, which RLG-016 set as the cost of running off the road. The numbers are not set yet.

- **RULED 2026-09-16T08:26:13-04:00** — PART ONE IS BUILT, v0.14.52. The red-and-white kerb is gone and the tarmac meets the biome's own ground.

NOTHING REPLACES IT, AND THAT IS THE WHOLE CHANGE. The ground is already painted across the full width of the screen in the same function - `ctx.fillRect(0, y2, W, H - y2)`, far edge to the bottom - and the road is laid on top of it. So the kerb was covering ground that was there the whole time, and not drawing it uncovers exactly what the owner asked for in every biome at once. No new colour table, and nothing to keep in step with the twelve `grassLo`/`grassHi` pairs that already exist.

THE ROAD EDGE IS STILL MARKED. The white shoulder line inside the tarmac at 0.965 is untouched. The red-and-white strip was never the thing that said "the tarmac ends here" - it said "this is an ordinary roadway", which is the thing the owner is removing.

A DECK KEEPS ITS KERB, for the reason [[RLG-112]] gave it one: a bridge's railing needs something to stand up from, and that kerb is concrete which is actually there rather than road furniture. It is the `deckB.truss` branch that already existed; only the ordinary-road branch is dropped.

A TUNNEL NEEDED NO EXCEPTION, and that was the risk worth checking rather than assuming. `drawBore` runs AFTER `drawRoad` in the frame, so the bore paints its own walls and floor over the road - a screenshot of the tunnel shows the walls meeting the tarmac with no ground visible between them.

MEASURED THE ONLY WAY A PICTURE CAN BE. One frame per biome, all twelve, captured at dusk with the weather off. FARMLAND reads as tarmac straight onto grass with the shoulder line intact; CITY's ground is already a concrete tone so the road runs onto grey; TUNNEL is clean; BRIDGE keeps its concrete kerb with the red railing standing up from it. bridge-test, farroad-test, wall-test, occlusion-test and smoke-test all pass.

WHAT IS NOT BUILT, and it is most of the ruling: the guard rails at the limit of travel, the hazard-side/solid-side table, mountain choosing its cliff side per stretch, the canyon floor, tundra as a snowy desert, and stopping the car at the deck edge and the bore wall rather than at the open road's limit. The map for that work: the player's lateral clamp is 1.18 and is written out at SEVEN steering sites rather than as one named constant, `edgeX()` at 22148 is the only place that already narrows it (for a bore), `TRUSS.out` is 1.13 and `boreWallX()` is about 1.25, and `sideRoll` is already a stable per-stretch side coin - but it is a single global that the sea and the crops both read, so a hazard side that must differ from the water's would need its own roll beside it.

THE TWO NUMBERS THE RULING LEAVES OPEN ARE STILL UNASKED: what a glancing rail hit costs in damage, and the rate sustained contact sheds speed at, which the owner ruled must be above the verge penalty. `OFF_SPD` is 4200 and the off-road block scrubs at `1 - dt*1.9`; those are the numbers a rail has to beat.
- **RULED 2026-09-16T09:51:44-04:00** — A CIRCUIT KEEPS ITS RED AND WHITE KERB, v0.14.53. Owner, 2026-09-16, after part one shipped: "We need to keep the red and white kerb for Motorsport."

PART ONE WAS RIGHT AND ITS REACH WAS WRONG. `road.js` is both driving games, so dropping the ordinary-road branch of the kerb dropped it from the circuit as well. That is the carry-over rule biting exactly where [[RLG-002]] said it would: Motorsport is parked, and a change made for Interstate which the circuit would have to undo later is the thing that rule exists to stop.

THE STRIP MEANS THE OPPOSITE ON A CIRCUIT. On an interstate a red-and-white kerb says ORDINARY ROADWAY, which is the thing the owner removed. On a circuit it says APEX: a racing circuit has red and white kerbs at every apex and every exit, and it is the one part of the track that tells a driver where the limit of the tarmac is before they arrive at it. The same pixels, the opposite meaning, decided by which discipline is running.

THE GATE IS THE SEAM AND NOTHING ELSE. The kerb quads are drawn when `deckB.truss || CFG.circuitOnly`, and the colour expression is the original ternary restored unchanged - concrete `#8e8a80`/`#6f6b62` on a deck, red and white `#c9c3b4`/`#8c3346` otherwise, alternating on the segment's own `dark` parity. `CFG.circuitOnly` is set in one place, `games/sw/motorsport.html`, so the engine still never names the game.

MEASURED BY LOOKING, WHICH IS THE ONLY WAY A KERB CAN BE. One midday frame from each cabinet with the road cleared: Interstate's tarmac runs straight onto the city ground with the shoulder line intact and no kerb, and Motorsport's canyon circuit carries the red-and-white strip down both edges. circuit-test, bridge-test and smoke-test all pass, which says the seam still holds and a deck still gets concrete rather than red.

WHAT THIS DOES NOT TOUCH: part one stands for Interstate, and the rest of the ruling - the guard rails, the hazard-side table, the canyon floor, tundra as a snowy desert, and the deck edge and bore wall as limits - is still unbuilt. The two open numbers are still unasked.
- **RULED 2026-09-16T09:59:21-04:00** — THE LIMIT OF TRAVEL IS ONE NAMED CONSTANT NOW, v0.14.54. `EDGE_X`, declared beside `LANE_X` and `LANE_W` where the other lateral units live. Nothing about the game changes; this is the groundwork the rail cannot be built without.

IT WAS WRITTEN OUT SEVEN TIMES. The literal `1.18` sat at every site that clamps a lateral position: the player's own steering, the drift, the two scripted-steer paths, the shunt a collision applies, the autopilot's target, and `edgeX()` - which is the only one that ever narrows it, for a bore. Seven copies of a number are harmless while nothing else reads them. A GUARD RAIL READS THEM. A rail drawn from one copy would disagree with the car at the other six, and a rail the car stops short of, or drives through, is worse than no rail at all.

`edgeX()` IS STILL THE FUNCTION TO ASK. It returns `EDGE_X` on the open road and `Math.min(EDGE_X, boreWallX() - playerW()/2 - BORE.skin)` inside a bore, so the tunnel still narrows the limit off the drawn wall. The constant is the open road's value that it starts from; it is not a replacement for the question.

A GREEN SUITE COULD NOT TELL ME THIS WORKED, and that is the shape this project keeps getting caught by. Every harness passes whether the sites read `EDGE_X` or still read `1.18`, because the two are the same number today. So `tools/edge-proof.py` NARROWS the constant to 0.90 and asserts the car follows: `tunnel-wall-test`'s own measurement of how far out the car actually reaches went 1.149 to 0.900 and back to 1.149. It reported the other answer, which is what makes the passing run mean something, and it refuses to finish if `road.js` is not restored.

WHAT THE PROOF COVERS AND WHAT IT DOES NOT, stated rather than blurred. Driving the car proves the STEERING clamp and `edgeX()`. It does not exercise the collision shunt, the two scripted-steer paths or the autopilot target - nothing in that harness scripts a steer or takes a hit. Those four rest on the static half: no clamp site anywhere in the engine still writes the literal. That is the weaker claim and the proof prints it as one.

tunnel-wall-test, wall-test and drive-test pass; `pack.sh --check` validates. drive-test is 36 of 37, and the one failure is the nitrous settle line already recorded as environmental - it has failed with `road.js` stashed at HEAD, and it reported 97.8% here against the 97.9% and 98.1% on record.
- **RULED 2026-09-16T10:07:48-04:00** — PART TWO IS THE TABLE, v0.14.55. Each side of the road is now declared a HAZARD or a SOLID, and nothing is drawn yet - this is the data the rail is drawn from.

THE RULE IN ONE SENTENCE: a rail goes where nothing is. A hazard side is a drop or open water, so a rail is the only thing that can stop the car there and it earns its place; a solid side is rock, trees, a fence line or a bank, and a rail in front of one is the road furniture [[RLG-264]] deleted.

FOUR PLACES DECLARE A LIMIT AND EIGHT DO NOT. COASTAL and SWAMP carry `hazard:'water'`, MOUNTAIN carries `hazard:'roll'`, CITY carries `edge:'barrier'`. FARMLAND, DESERT, TUNDRA, FOREST, JUNGLE, CANYON, BRIDGE and TUNNEL declare nothing and are solid on both sides.

THE SECOND COIN IS THE PART THAT NEEDED THOUGHT. Where the hazard IS the water, the rail must agree with `sideRoll`, because the sea is already drawn on that side - a coast railed on the landward side would fence the player off from the dunes and leave the water open. A MOUNTAIN HAS NO WATER TO AGREE WITH. Reading `sideRoll` for its cliff would not merely be untidy: it would weld a cliff to a coin other places turn for other reasons, so the day a mountain grows a second thing with a side, neither could move. So `hazardRoll` is turned beside `sideRoll` in the same `rollSide()`, on the same event, and `hazardSide()` is the single place that decides which coin a place reads.

`edgeAt(B, side)` answers `'rail'`, `'barrier'` or null. The CAR STILL STOPS AT `EDGE_X` either way - a side with no rail is not a side you can drive off.

WHY THE CHECK IS A NUMBER AND NOT A PICTURE. `tools/hazard-test.py` reads the table off `API.edgeOf` rather than looking at a frame, because a rail drawn on the LANDWARD side of a coast is a rail correctly drawn as far as any screenshot is concerned - the right object, the right colour, in the right place on the screen. Only the side is wrong, and the side is the whole ruling.

AND IT ASSERTS THE COIN IS NOT STUCK, over 200 rolls of the game's own `rollSide()` rather than a harness's imitation of it. The cliff landed 100 left and 100 right, and the two coins agreed on 105 of 200 - a single coin read twice agrees 200 of 200. `rollSide`'s own note records that exact fault being missed once before, 40 out of 40 on one side while the game was rolling correctly.

NOT VACUOUS, WATCHED FAILING WITH TWO DEFECTS PUT BACK: railing a solid side (FOREST given `hazard:'roll'`) was caught on the FOREST line, and welding `hazardRoll = sideRoll` was caught at 100 per cent agreement. Both were restored.

biome-test and smoke-test pass. NOT BUILT YET: any rail is drawn, the physics of touching one, and the deck edge and bore wall as limits.
- **RULED 2026-09-16T10:48:40-04:00** — PART THREE IS THE RAIL ITSELF, v0.14.57. It stands at `EDGE_X`, on the side the table names, and a place with no hazard draws none.

IT STANDS AT `EDGE_X` AND THAT IS THE WHOLE POINT. The car's clamp and the rail's line are the same constant, so the rail is WHERE the car stops rather than near it. A rail drawn at its own number would be a rail you stop short of, or drive through, and the ruling would be worse than not building it. This is what the constant in [[UNT-340]] was for.

DRAWN THE WAY THE BRIDGE RAILING IS, because a bridge railing is what it is: a beam at a height in WORLD units above the road, per slice, in the road pass where a crest occludes it for nothing. `trussTop` does the projection and `postIn` spaces the posts in the world rather than on the screen - spacing them by pixels crowds them at the horizon and stretches them at the bumper, a distinction that cost the tunnel three builds and was not re-learnt here.

A STEEL RAIL IS A W-BEAM AND A CONCRETE ONE IS A WALL, which is why they are one function with a kind rather than two functions. The steel has a gap under it, a lit top lip, a dark under-edge and posts you see between; the concrete is solid to the ground, lower, capped, and has no posts. Getting that wrong makes a motorway of a city street.

THE HEIGHTS ARE FROM THE REAL THING, on this engine's own anchor - a car is 380 units and about four and a half metres, so a metre is close to 84 units. A W-beam stands about 0.75 m, which is `RAIL.h` 63; a concrete barrier about 1.05 m, which is `RAIL.wallH` 88. `TRUSS.h` is 260 beside them, and a bridge parapet really is three times the height of a crash barrier.

A DECK IS EXEMPT BY CONSTRUCTION rather than by a branch. The rail block runs only when `!deckB.truss`, and a bridge declares no hazard side anyway, so `edgeAt` answers null for it - a crossing can never be given a second railing inside its own.

HOW THE DRAWING IS CHECKED, AND THE FIRST TWO WAYS WERE WRONG. Hunting for the rail's own colour is guessing: steel grey has to be told from a white shoulder line, from wet tarmac and from a city's concrete-toned ground, and every guess is a way to report a rail that is not there. So `API.railsOff` renders the SAME frame without rails and the difference IS the rail, whatever colour it is - the same shape as `seaStraight`, a switch whose only job is to produce the other picture.

THEN THE SIDE, WHICH ABSOLUTE POSITION CANNOT SETTLE. A rail converges on the vanishing point, so its far end crosses the middle of the screen whichever side of the road it stands on - and a left-or-right split at the screen's centre reported 101 pixels of phantom rail on a coast's landward side. The place is rendered TWICE, with the hazard forced to each side, and the rail's mean position must MOVE. Every other term - the road, the light, the scenery, the vanishing point's own offset - is shared and cancels.

  COASTAL    749 px at x=197 with the hazard left, 799 px at x=279 with it right
  MOUNTAIN   569 px at x=206 with the hazard left, 727 px at x=285 with it right
  CITY      1297 px at x=247.0 and 1294 px at x=247.8 - it does NOT move, because a barrier has no side
  FARMLAND  0 to 14 px either way - no rail at all

THREE THINGS HAD TO BE HELD STILL BEFORE ANY OF THAT MEANT ANYTHING, and each was found by the check reporting a number that could not be true. The car: the first run read 8,211 changed pixels in FARMLAND, which draws no rail - the two frames were a third of a second apart at sixty miles an hour and what it measured was the crops going past. The position: `setSpd(0)` is a value the engine then moves on from, so `pos` crept by part of a segment and the road's own light/dark banding shifted right across the carriageway - `holdSpd(0)` pins it. And the curve, because a bend carries the far rail across the midline; `holdCurve(0)` pins that.

THE BAR BETWEEN "NO RAIL" AND "A RAIL" IS MEASURED RATHER THAN CHOSEN. A place with no rail has been seen at 0, 2, 6, 8, 14, 15 and 41 changed pixels; a place with one reports 233 to 1,401. `BARE` is 120, in the middle of an empty band of more than five to one. A first version at 40 failed one run in three on a farmland reading 41, which is a threshold drawn on the noise.

NOT VACUOUS, WATCHED FAILING WITH THE RAIL PUT ON THE WRONG SIDE: the two means inverted - COASTAL x=284 with the hazard left against x=184 with it right - and both hazard places failed while CITY and FARMLAND correctly did not, because neither has a side to get wrong.

IT COSTS NOTHING TO DRAW. fps-test reads 54.8 to 55.2 in every place on the board, railed and unrailed alike, with no range separated from any other.

hazard-test, smoke-test and bridge-test pass; drive-test is 36/37 on the known environmental settle line. Screenshots of COASTAL, MOUNTAIN, CITY, SWAMP and FARMLAND at midday: the coast is railed on the sea side with the dunes open, the mountain on the cliff side with the trees open, the city walled both sides, the farmland bare.

STILL NOT BUILT: the physics of touching a rail, which is where the ruling's two open numbers bite; the deck edge and the bore wall as the limit rather than the open road's; and the biome reshapes - the canyon floor, tundra as a snowy desert, and the mountain's rock face standing opposite its cliff.
- **RULED 2026-09-16T11:23:05-04:00** — THE TWO OPEN NUMBERS ARE ANSWERED, AND NEITHER CHANGES ANY CODE (owner, 2026-09-16).

A RAIL HIT COSTS NO DAMAGE. The earlier ruling stands: the verge costs TIME, NOT HEALTH, and damage is reserved for hitting something. That note sits in the engine beside the barrier scrub and records that scraping the barrier used to take 9 health, which made the edge of the road as dangerous as a car - "a wall you are sliding along is not something you hit; it is somewhere you should not be." Drawing the rail did not turn it into a car. So "shouldn't do much to you" resolves to nothing at all, and hitting a rail and sliding along one stay the same event.

THE GRIND KEEPS THE RATE IT HAS. The ruling asked for sustained contact to shed speed above the verge penalty, and it already does by a wide margin: running wide caps the car at `OFF_SPD` 4200, about 55 mph, and pressing INTO the edge sheds roughly 85 per cent of speed a second on top of that - `spd *= 1 - dt*1.9`, every frame you press in rather than slide along. Both numbers predate the rail being visible, and the owner chose to feel them now that it is drawn rather than move them blind. They are one constant each if the device says otherwise.

SO PART THREE OF THE RULING NEEDED NO BUILD. What it needed was the rail to be VISIBLE, which parts one to three did: the behaviour the owner described was already there and was being met by an invisible wall. That is worth stating plainly, because a ruling closed with no diff looks like a ruling that was dropped.
- **RULED 2026-09-16T11:36:51-04:00** — PART FOUR, v0.14.58: A DECK STOPS THE CAR AT ITS PARAPET, and the rail moved to clear the car.

THE TUNNEL ALREADY DID AND THE BRIDGE DID NOT. `TRUSS.out` is 1.13 and the open road's limit is 1.18, so the car ran five hundredths of a road half-width PAST the railing it can see. `edgeX()` now takes a `Math.min` against the deck's own line as well as the bore's - the same sum in both cases, the structure's line less half the car less a skin, read off the drawn geometry rather than written down. Both branches can only ever bring the car IN; neither can widen the limit.

`onDeckNow()` ASKS THE SEGMENT UNDER THE CAR, not the one under the camera. The camera trails by `PLAYER_Z`, so asking it would widen the limit a car's length before the deck ended and narrow it a car's length after it began.

AND THE RAIL I DREW YESTERDAY RAN THROUGH THE CAR. Drawn at `EDGE_X`, it was in the wrong place, and the arithmetic says why: the car's centre rests at about 1.145 and the widest body is 0.2994 across, so the FLANK reaches 1.295 - eleven hundredths past a rail at 1.18. A screenshot of the car hard over on a beach shows the posts crossing the bodywork. The ruling says the rail goes where the car stops, and the thing that stops is the flank.

SO THE RAIL'S LINE IS COMPUTED FROM THE SAME TERMS THE CAR IS: `railX()` is `EDGE_X - 0.03 + widestHalf() + RAIL.skin`. The WIDEST body is what it clears, because a rail a supercar passes through but a hatchback does not would move with the garage - the shape [[RLG-058]] spent a unit taking out of the collider. `widestHalf` walks the painter's own table, so a new body cannot silently start clipping it.

THE ALTERNATIVE WAS TO MOVE THE CAR AND IT WAS REJECTED. Narrowing the drivable road to meet a rail already drawn would change how much lateral room the player has, in four biomes out of twelve, which is a gameplay change the owner did not ask for. Moving the rail changes a picture; moving the limit changes the game.

MEASURED. On a deck the car now pins at 0.9950 against a parapet at 1.13, a gap of 0.135 - which is half the widest car plus the skin, so the car is inside the railing rather than short of it. On the open road it still pins at 1.1300, unchanged. `wall-test` carries both, and the second assertion is the one that stops a build that narrowed EVERY road from passing: the deck must not be so far inside that it reads as a single lane.

NOT VACUOUS, watched failing with the deck branch removed: the car pinned at 1.1321, outside its own parapet, which is what shipped before this unit.

ONE OBSERVED FLAKE, RECORDED RATHER THAN HIDDEN. On one run of `tunnel-wall-test` the right-hand wall reported the car 4 pixels INSIDE it where every other run reports a 27-pixel gap. Three consecutive re-runs were clean on both sides. Nothing in this unit touches the bore's own sum, and it was not reproduced - so it is an observation, not a finding. If it returns, the thing to suspect is the frame the car arrives at the mouth on.

wall-test, tunnel-wall-test, hazard-test, bridge-test and smoke-test pass. drive-test is 36/37 on the known environmental settle line.
