### RLG-300 — The rock sprites in canyon and mountain are replaced by the sheer faces
- **RULED 2026-09-20T15:34:08-04:00** — OWNER, 2026-09-20, from the device: 'The original rock pieces and rock faces and cliff faces whatever from the canyon in the mountain don't need to be rendered anymore since we're using sheer faces.' RLG-297 put a continuous plane on the canyon's two sides and the mountain's uphill side, and the loose rock that used to stand for those walls is now drawn IN FRONT OF a solid face - it is doing no work and it is being rasterised. Take it out of the places that have a wall, on the sides that have one, in the windscreen and in the glass. IT IS ALSO PART OF RLG-299: the two places that pay most for the doubled draw are the two with a per-slice surface, and their roadside is the densest on the board - the canyon is 0.98, which the scenery table calls the densest thing on it. Watch scenery-test, occlusion-test and biome-shot: the drop side of a mountain must keep whatever it has, and the strip of sky a canyon leaves between its walls must not change shape. Not started.
- **RULED 2026-09-20T15:52:50-04:00** — BUILT 2026-09-20, v0.14.109. THE ROCK IS OFF THE WALLED SIDES, in the windscreen and in the glass. `wallSides(B)` is the same coin the wall itself reads, so the two can never disagree about which side has a face on it, and it is the same shape as the rolled hazard's own skip one line above.

THE SPEC ITSELF STAYS AND THAT IS NOT TIDINESS. `skyRiseOf` derives the skyline's height CAP from this place's own scenery height (RLG-104) - the whole point of that ruling being that a horizon may not stand taller than the furthest thing the road pass draws. Deleting the row would drop a canyon's horizon to the default and put the band back BEHIND the wall it was capped to, which is the picture RLG-104 was raised to remove. What changed is which sides it is drawn on.

IT IS FASTER, AND MEASURED AGAINST ITSELF. `wall-cost.py` now compares the sheer face against the rock it replaced, because the same switch that takes the wall away puts the rock back - CANYON 53.3-60.9 fps with the faces against 45.9-49.4 with the rock, two ranges that do not overlap, which is fps-test's own rule for a real change. MOUNTAIN is 40.2-60.9 against 35.1-60.5 and overlaps, which is what you would expect: it is walled on ONE side and the canyon on two. This is a real contribution to RLG-299 and it came from taking something out.

AND IT FOUND A CHECK THAT HAD QUIETLY BECOME A MEASURE OF SOMETHING ELSE. scenery-test asserts the glass carries 'a comparable roadside rather than a token one', as the mirror's object count over the WINDSCREEN'S - which measured 27 per cent for as long as the road was drawn 30,000 units and the mirror looked 34,000 back. RLG-295 doubled the draw and left `MIRROR_BACK` alone, deliberately, so the windscreen now draws about twice the objects for the same roadside and the share halved to 12 with the glass unchanged. The ratio had become a measure of the two DRAW distances. It is scaled by the reaches it compares now, and asks what it always asked. NOT CAUSED BY THIS WORK - scenery-test drives a FOREST, which has no wall - but found by it.

canyon-test's three failures are the same ones it has at HEAD. smoke-test 7/7.
