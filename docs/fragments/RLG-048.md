### RLG-048 — Slip on rain and snow wiggles; it should carry the way you turned
- **RULED 2026-08-28T17:41:46-04:00** — **Owner, 2026-08-28:** "slip because of rain or snow needs to feel a bit better. Currently the
steering just wiggles. What it needs to do is continue moving in the direction you're turning, with
a magnitude based on the speed at which you've done that - so a delta. Obviously snow has a higher
multiplier of this effect than rain. Because the slippage is based on your delta, manoeuvring
gingerly minimises the slippage."

**The wiggle is not a bug in a coefficient. It is the shape of the equation**, and this is worth
writing down because the temptation will be to retune the numbers. From road.js, in the lateral
block:

    const carry = Math.min(0.86, slick * (snowy > 0.5 ? 2.6 : 1.5));
    const want2 = clamp((targetX - playerX) * grip, -4.2*dt, 4.2*dt);
    slideX = slideX * carry + want2;

`want2` is proportional to the DISTANCE still to travel, and `slideX` carries the previous frame's
lateral velocity at up to 0.86. A proportional term plus a carried velocity term with no damping
proportional to that velocity is an underdamped oscillator: it overshoots the mark, is pulled back,
overshoots the other way. **That is the wiggle, and it is what the equation is for.** Raising or
lowering `carry` changes how long it wiggles, not whether it does.

**What the owner has ruled instead:** slip is driven by the DELTA of the steering input - how fast
the wheel was moved - and not by how far the car still is from its mark.

- a sharp input injects a lateral impulse in the direction that was turned, and the car keeps going
  that way until it bleeds off
- a gentle input injects almost nothing, so driving carefully is the counter-play. **This is the
  point of the whole change:** it turns weather from an affliction into something the player has an
  answer to
- snow multiplies the impulse harder than rain, which keeps the existing intent that snow is a
  different curve rather than a stronger rain

**One thing to watch when building it.** `slideX` is currently zeroed against a wall
(`if(playerX < -1.18 || playerX > 1.18) slideX = 0`), and the drive-test autopilot steers by pressing
arrow keys with a proportional centre-seeker - it saws at the wheel. **Under a delta-driven model a
sawing driver generates maximum slip**, so the harness will report the weather as far worse than a
person experiences it. The project already has this exact lesson recorded from tyre wear: a harness
once said Raceway's tyres died in twenty seconds and the autopilot was the cause. Measure this one
with a steady driver, or the numbers will be about the harness.

**This is a feel change and a harness cannot sign it off.** The owner's verdict on a real device is
the acceptance test.
- **RULED 2026-08-31T22:05:18-04:00** — BUILT, 0.10.42, AND THE MODEL IS NOT THE ONE THIS FRAGMENT ORIGINALLY DESCRIBED. The owner corrected
it twice during the build, and both corrections made it simpler.

WHAT THE FIRST BUILD DID, AND WHY IT WAS WRONG. It took the 2026-08-28 ruling literally: a sharp
input injects a lateral VELOCITY, which then bleeds off while the steering fights it. That works on
paper and could not be seen. Measured: `slideX` peaked at 0.73 lane units a second on snow - a real
slide by any reading - and produced TWO PER CENT of a lane of overshoot, because the restoring term
recovers about a fifth of the remaining error every frame and simply absorbed it. The build then
weakened the steering on a slick road to let the slide show, which is where the owner stopped it:
"Why even restore? It's more that when you turn the vehicle, it moves further than you intend based
off of the Delta that you've moved."

THAT IS THE MODEL AND IT IS ONE LINE. The slip is not a body fighting the steering and it is not a
reduction in how fast the car answers. IT IS AN OVERSHOOT OF THE MARK:

    slideX += dTarget * slick * (snowy ? SLIP.snow : SLIP.rain)
    aim     = targetX + slideX

You ask for a lane and you get a lane and a bit. How much extra is how big the movement was, times
how slippery the road is. Nothing detects that the player was careful, because CAREFULNESS IS A
SMALLER DELTA - the counter-play falls out of the arithmetic rather than being arranged.

THE SECOND CORRECTION IS WHAT MAKES IT A FEATURE RATHER THAN A PENALTY. Owner: "with practice, you
can understeer to get the amount of steering you need given the environmental state of the ground.
Instead of it being a pure hindrance, it's something you can learn to work with." That property is
DESTROYED BY A TIMER, and the build before it had one - a bleed of 0.8 a second. If the overshoot
washes off, aiming short lands you short: the road gives the extra and then takes it back, and there
is nothing to learn. So the offset does not decay. It is bounded by the SURFACE instead - it cannot
exceed what the road can produce, and on a dry road that is zero, so driving out of the snow takes it
with you without a rule saying so.

AND IT NEEDS NO OTHER WAY BACK, which is the part that makes the no-decay version safe. Steering back
is itself a movement and carries its own slip in the opposite direction, so an out-and-back cancels
exactly. The offset cannot ratchet.

THE LEARNABLE PROPERTY IS ASSERTED RATHER THAN DESCRIBED, and it is the check this whole file exists
for. On snow at slick 0.66, aiming at 0.5596 to arrive at 0.700 landed at 0.7000. If the slip ever
gains noise, a decay, or a fight with the steering, that check fails.

MEASURED, all with the car parked and the road cleared:

    a movement of 0.700 on snow    overshoots 0.1978, and is still there three seconds later
    a movement of 0.175 on snow    overshoots 0.0440 - a quarter of the movement, a quarter of the slip
    the same movement in rain      overshoots 0.0612 against a predicted 0.060
    the same movement dry          overshoots 0.0000

BRAKING IS DELIBERATELY NOT THIS. Owner: "it also directly affects braking, but that should be
modelled more simply such that your braking grip is just reduced based on the wetness/iciness." It
already was `wetBrake()`, a plain multiplier, and it stays one. The check asserts it is simple rather
than asserting a number.

TWO MEASUREMENT FAULTS WERE FOUND AND ARE RECORDED RATHER THAN PAPERED OVER, because both would have
produced a confident wrong answer. THE FIRST WAS MEASURING COLLISIONS: the check drove at speed and
read an overshoot of 0.1494 of a lane on a DRY road, where the model injects nothing at all - a
collision shoves the car sideways by up to 0.30 and lands in exactly that reading. The dry control
caught it, which is what a control is for. THE SECOND WAS THE BARRIER: asking for a full lane on snow
put the aim past the edge of the road, and hitting the barrier zeroes the slip, so the reading was
0.9894 for a movement that should have overshot - the engine behaving correctly and the experiment
asking the wrong question. THE THIRD WAS A RESIDUE: because the offset does not decay, a car steered
on snow still carries that offset when the road turns to rain, and rain measured 0.1525 against a
predicted 0.060 until the residue was accounted for.

NOT JUDGED ON A DEVICE, AND THE NUMBERS ARE EXPECTED TO MOVE. `API.slipModel` and `API.wetModel` are
both live for exactly that. What a harness cannot say: whether 0.20 of a movement is the right amount
of hindrance, whether an offset that never decays is unsettling over a long run rather than
learnable, and whether the understeer trick is discoverable in play or needs teaching the first time
it matters.

ONE THING THE MODEL DOES NOT DO, stated so it is a decision rather than an oversight: the slip does
not scale with road speed. It comes off the wheel's movement alone, so a wheel snatched at a crawl
slips as much as one snatched at 180mph. The old model did not scale with speed either.
- **RULED 2026-08-31T22:32:11-04:00** — AND IT PLAYS INTO A BEND, WHICH THE OWNER ASKED TO HAVE CONFIRMED RATHER THAN ASSUMED. Owner, 2026-08-31: "I want to make sure that it still plays into roadway turns, e.g. You are more greatly pulled to the outside curve due to slippage."

IT DOES, AND BY TWO MECHANISMS THAT COMPOUND - neither of which was written for the other, so this was a question rather than a certainty. The cornering force already divides by grip, so a slick road pushes harder toward the outside on its own. That push is applied to `targetX`, which is the wheel - so the slip model reads it as a movement and adds its own overshoot on top of it.

MEASURED, at a pinned curvature and a fixed speed, over one second: a DRY road runs the car to -0.038 and a SNOWY one to -0.089. Two and a third times as wide, to the same side, with the slip offset itself carrying a measurable part of it rather than the cornering force being the whole story.

THE FIRST ATTEMPT MEASURED NOTHING AND SAID SO. A hard bend held for two and a half seconds put BOTH arms against the edge of the road at -1.08, where the difference cannot exist - the check read dry -1.0886 against snow -1.0817 and failed. A gentle bend held briefly leaves both free to differ. That is the same shape of fault as the collision reading earlier in this unit: an experiment run past the point where its subject can vary.

`API.holdCurve` is new and pins the curvature the cornering force reads. Finding a bend of a stated severity by driving is a search, and it would measure the road generator rather than the physics.
