### RLG-056 — drive-test's 150mph assertion is marginal now the rivals hold their lanes
- **RULED 2026-08-28T21:32:20-04:00** — **Found while shipping [[RLG-033]] part 2, and NOT fixed by lowering the threshold.**

Rivals now hold a lane instead of wandering out of one, so Interstate is a genuinely busier road for
anything trying to drive fast down it. `drive-test`'s `speed rises above 150mph` check now fails
about one run in four, at 146-148mph. It is marginal, not broken.

**Two faults were tangled here and only the first is fixed.** The autopilot's `aim()` read
`R.traffic` alone and was blind to every racer on the road - it survived only because the old rivals
were a lateral pressure that drifted out of the way by accident. That is fixed: it reads
`traffic.concat(racers)` now, and the wrecks it was causing are gone. **The remaining failure is not
a blind driver, it is a slow one.**

**The threshold was deliberately NOT lowered.** Moving an assertion to fit the change that broke it
is how a suite stops meaning anything, and this project has the scar: a green `pack.sh` once shipped
a syntax error, a missing file and two black screens. If 150 is the wrong number now, that is a
decision to take on its own evidence, not a side effect of a feature commit.

**Three ways it could go, and they are not equal:**

1. **a better driver.** The autopilot is a proportional centre-seeker that re-picks a lane five times
   a second and only avoids cars SLOWER than itself. It does not plan an overtake, and on a busier
   road that is what costs it the last few mph. Most work, and it makes every future measurement
   better rather than only this one.
2. **a threshold that reflects the road**, argued from measured peaks across many runs rather than
   from the number that used to pass.
3. **measure peak speed on a clear road**, the way `stat-test` does - it clears traffic and police
   deliberately, and it measures every car's true top speed without trouble. The speed assertion and
   the congestion assertion are arguably two different tests wearing one name.

**Recommended: 3, then 1.** `stat-test` already proves a clear-road measurement is easy and exact;
what `drive-test` should assert on a busy road is that the car makes progress, which it has its own
checks for.
- **RULED 2026-08-28T22:13:20-04:00** — **IT GOT WORSE IN v0.9.10, and the cause is known rather than suspected.** [[RLG-040]]'s traffic side
ended the yield: a car that gives way used to be left parked on the shoulder at 48mph for the rest
of its life, and it now rejoins the traffic at its own pace. That is what the owner asked for, and it
means the lanes are genuinely fuller than they were.

| `drive-test`, Interstate peak speed | runs | range | mean | under 150mph |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| before RLG-040's traffic side | 4 | 150-188 | 175 | 0 |
| after | 8 | 126-182 | 156 | 2 |

**The threshold was not moved, for the second time.** Moving an assertion to fit the change that
broke it is how a suite stops meaning anything, and this fragment exists because that was already
declined once.

**What is NOT established:** eight runs against four, with ranges that overlap heavily, is a
difference worth reporting and not a number worth quoting. Anyone who picks this up should measure
both arms properly before deciding whether the assertion, the traffic density, or the player's car is
the thing to change. The owner ruled on 2026-08-28 to commit and revisit.
