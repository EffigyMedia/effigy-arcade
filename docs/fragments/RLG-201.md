### RLG-201 — Crates stand on both shoulders, one mile apart, except where a checkpoint falls
- **RULED 2026-09-10T03:22:13-04:00** — TRACKED, NOT STARTED. Owner, 2026-09-10: "the pickup crates should spawn on both sides of
the road every mile not including the miles where checkpoints are."

**THIS REPLACES A TIMER WITH A RULER, which is the substance of it.** Crates are laid by a clock
today - `nextCrateT = rnd(20, 34)` seconds, one crate, on a side chosen at random. So how many
crates a mile of road carries depends on how fast you drove it: a slow car meets more of them per
mile than a fast one, which is exactly backwards, because the slow car is the one that needs the
ten seconds less urgently than the car that is out-running its clock.

**FOUR THINGS CHANGE TOGETHER:**

    the trigger becomes DISTANCE rather than elapsed time - a crate at every mile marker;

    both shoulders carry one, where a crate is a single `side * rnd(0.86, 1.02)` today;

    a mile that holds a checkpoint holds no crates, which needs the checkpoint plan to be readable
    at the moment the crate is placed - `nextCP` and `cpGantries` are the state that knows;

    and the count per mile doubles at the same time as the spacing becomes fixed, so the amount of
    clock the road pays out per mile changes twice over. `CRATE_SECS` is ten today.

**IT INTERACTS WITH A RULING THAT IS OPEN AND WAS WRONGLY BELIEVED CLOSED.** [[RLG-100]] says three
cars cannot reach the second checkpoint at full throttle, and the ROADSTER runs out of clock BEFORE
THE FIRST - so it can never reach the crate that was supposed to close the margin. A crate on every
mile except the checkpoint miles is a different economy entirely, and `tools/verify-097-100.py`
should be re-run against it rather than reasoned about.

**AND CRATES ARE ALREADY GATED ON `roadFurniture`**, which the circuit turns off. That gate stays:
Motorsport has no crates and this does not give it any.
