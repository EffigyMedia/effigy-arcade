### RLG-281 — The road to the horizon is the wrong colour for its place
- **RULED 2026-09-16T12:54:47-04:00** — OWNER, 2026-09-16: "the simulated roadway that goes off into the horizon, needs to match the color of the road per biome."

WHAT THIS IS. [[RLG-101]] carries the tarmac past where the road pass stops painting, on its own coarse walk to the vanishing point - `FAR_ROAD_STEPS` is 30 - so the road reaches the horizon instead of ending in a band under the skyline. That far ribbon is painted with its own colour, and the near road's colour is built by `tarmacTone`, which takes the segment's light/dark parity, the distance fade and whether it is a deck. The two do not agree, and a place that tints its tarmac shows the join.

THE FAR SEA ALREADY SOLVED THE SAME PROBLEM and its record is the place to start. `SEA_HAZE` was MEASURED rather than chosen - six values read from the same band at the same hour, because the road is generated per load and the day is always turning, so two runs cannot be compared. The winning value sat at a clean minimum of the step at the join with the curve rising on both sides of it. A colour match at a join is exactly that kind of measurement.

AND farroad-test ALREADY PRINTS THE NUMBER, without asserting on it: "the tone step reads 33.5 here and has swung 34, 84 and 39 across runs of one build - the join is the owner's to judge." So the instrument exists, the fault is already visible to it, and the reason it was left unasserted is that nobody had ruled on what the step should be. The owner has now.

NOT STARTED.
- **RULED 2026-09-16T13:29:32-04:00** — BUILT, v0.14.64, AND IT WAS A ONE-LINE TYPE ERROR THAT NOTHING REPORTED.

THE BAND WAS FILLED WITH `rgb(NaN,NaN,NaN)`. `mixRGB` takes a colour STRING and an RGB ARRAY - it reads `T[0]`, `T[1]` and `T[2]` off the third argument - and the far road handed it a second STRING. `'rgb(35,34,49)'[0]` is the character `r`, so `c[0] + ('r' - 35) * 0.5` is NaN and every channel came out NaN.

AND A CANVAS SILENTLY IGNORES AN INVALID `fillStyle`, KEEPING THE ONE BEFORE IT. That is the whole defect and it is why the owner's report says "per biome": the fill in force at that moment is the GROUND or the SKYLINE of the place being drawn, so the road to the horizon took on whatever the place happened to be painted in. There is no error, no warning and nothing in any log.

MEASURED BOTH WAYS, which is what settles it. In a FARMLAND at midday with the weather off:

| | near road | far ribbon | ground beside it |
|---|---|---|---|
| broken | [96,112,54] | **[119,133,96]** | [114,128,82] |
| fixed | [61,51,51] | **[33,32,46]** | [115,128,84] |

Broken, the ribbon sits 15.7 from the grass and 52.3 from the road it continues - it IS the field. Fixed, it is 131.8 from the grass and 34.2 from the road. Sampled across four places, the broken ribbon read [115,128,84] in farmland - PIXEL-IDENTICAL to the grass - [127,113,95] in a desert against sand at [122,104,79], and [73,95,86] in a forest; fixed it is [33,32,46] in every one of them, because tarmac has no biome tint.

THE DECK IS ASKED FOR NOW TOO. A crossing that runs past the draw distance has a deck surface out there rather than asphalt, and the old call passed `false` unconditionally.

THE EXISTING INSTRUMENT ALREADY SAW THIS AND DECLINED TO SAY SO. `farroad-test` printed "road against ground at the half-way row reads 11.2" as a diagnostic, with the reason given: the ribbon is two or three pixels wide up there and a sample placed by interpolation misses it too often. 11.2 is the road being the same colour as the field, printed on every run, unasserted.

SO THE CHECK IS PINNED NOW. The free-driving median swung 23.9, 36.6 and 82.2 across three runs of ONE build, purely on which biome each run spent its time in - a forest's ground is nearly tarmac-coloured and a farmland's is not, so the bands overlap and nothing can be asserted across them. Pinned to FARMLAND, the brightest ground on the board, the number is about 150 when right and effectively zero when broken. AND A MISSED SAMPLE CAN ONLY FAIL IT, NEVER PASS IT: a miss reads toward the ground's colour, which is the failing direction, so the harness's original worry is answered rather than ignored.

NOT VACUOUS, watched failing with the defect put back: 15.7 against the ground where it wants more than 60, and 52.3 from the near road where it wants under 40.

THIS IS THE THIRD COLOUR-HELPER TRAP TODAY and they fail in three different ways. `shade` returns a grey fallback for anything that is not `#rrggbb`; `hexRGB` THROWS on an array, which aborts the road pass and blanks the frame; `mixRGB` produces NaN and the canvas keeps the previous fill. Not one of them says anything.

farroad-test and smoke-test pass. occlusion-test's lamp line - "a lamp coming over a brow is CUT OFF rather than popped in", clipped=0 - is PRE-EXISTING, confirmed by running it at HEAD with this work stashed, and it reads 0 with the flatten reverted as well. It is a new observation rather than a new fault; the likely cause is that lamps are only lit at night and the run does not always land there.
- **RULED 2026-09-16T16:51:38-04:00** — THE OWNER ASKED WHETHER IT TRACKS SNOW, AND THE ANSWER FOUND ONE MORE FAULT. v0.14.65.

FIRST, THE PREMISE IS NOT QUITE RIGHT AND IT MATTERS. The road is NOT a different colour per biome. `tarmacTone(dark, fade, onDeck)` takes a parity, a fade and a deck flag and nothing else - there is no biome term anywhere in it, and the asphalt fill passes no biome either. The tarmac is the same in every place. WHAT DIFFERS BETWEEN PLACES IS THE WEATHER AND THE LIGHT they produce: snow cover whitens it, rain darkens it and lays a sheen on it, night and golden hour move the white it mixes toward. So the question is really whether the far band picks up those globals, and it does, because it calls the same function.

SNOW IS TRACKED EXACTLY: dry the band computes [33,32,46] and under nine tenths of cover it computes [134,134,141], which is 172 apart - and the road it joins moves with it, to within 0.7.

RAIN WAS NOT. `tarmacTone`'s last term is the sky coming back off wet tarmac at a grazing angle, and it is scaled by `1 - fade` so that it is absent at your feet and strongest at the far end of the draw - WHICH IS WHERE THIS BAND BEGINS. Painted at fade 0 it took the full sheen while the slice it joins, at the far end of the draw, had lost all of it: [56,66,87] against [21,24,36], a gap of 72 where dry and snow-covered both read under 1. It takes fade 1 now - this band is further away than anything the road pass draws, so if the sheen has gone by the last slice it has gone here too - and the gap falls to 0.5. Dry and snow are untouched, because the term is multiplied by `rainDark` and that is zero unless it is wet.

AND THE OWNER IS RIGHT ABOUT HOW THIS SHOULD HAVE BEEN ASKED. They said: "Why would you measure it that way instead of just looking up what you have applied the color as code?" Reading the code is what FOUND the original defect - three lines in node proved `mixRGB` returns `rgb(NaN,NaN,NaN)` with no browser at all - and then I reached for pixel sampling out of habit for the follow-up question, which is a worse instrument for it and cost two wrong readings: the same place on the same build sampled [33,32,46] and [73,80,64] depending on where the bend put a ribbon one or two pixels wide.

BUT ONE PIXEL CHECK DOES EARN ITS PLACE, and stating why is the useful part. The original defect was that the canvas SILENTLY IGNORED the colour and kept the previous fill. The code tells you what colour was COMPUTED; it cannot tell you whether the canvas ACCEPTED it. That is the one question a picture has to answer, and it is exactly the question this bug was. So the division is: the pinned FARMLAND pixel check proves the paint took, and everything about what the colour should BE is read from the values the engine computes.

NOT VACUOUS, watched failing with fade 0 put back: the rain line reported 72.1 against a bar of 8, while dry and snow-covered stayed green - which is the defect's own signature.

AND THE SNOW CHECK IS GUARDED AGAINST BEING TWO CONSTANTS. A band that ignored the weather as completely as a road that also ignored it would match perfectly, so the check also asserts that snow MOVES the far band - 172 apart between dry and covered.

farroad-test and smoke-test pass.
- **RULED 2026-09-16T17:49:28-04:00** — THE OWNER WAS RIGHT AND I WAS WRONG. v0.14.66. Owner, 2026-09-16: "No, you're wrong. The bridge is concrete. So please check again."

I HAD CLAIMED THE ROAD HAS NO BIOME COLOUR AT ALL. `tarmacTone(dark, fade, onDeck)` takes a parity, a fade and a DECK FLAG - and `onDeck` comes from `bioAt(idx).overWater`, which is a property of the place. A crossing is concrete and every other road is asphalt, so the road DOES differ by biome. I read the signature, saw no argument called `biome`, and stopped one term short.

AND THE DECK WAS BROKEN IN THE SAME WAY THE FAR BAND HAD BEEN. `DECK_FACE` was `'#4a4a52'`, the only mix target in the engine written as a hex STRING - `WET_DARK` and `WET_SHEEN` beside it are both arrays - and `mixRGB` reads `T[0]`, `T[1]`, `T[2]` off it. On that string those are `'#'`, `'4'` and `'a'`, so a deck's base colour computed as `rgb(NaN,15,NaN)`: two dead channels and a middle one that is an accident of `'4' - 29`. A canvas silently ignores an invalid `fillStyle` and keeps the previous one, so EVERY running surface on EVERY crossing - the windscreen's and the mirror's, both of which mix toward this - has been painted in whatever colour happened to be in force. It looked plausible because what precedes it is usually dark.

IT IS AN ARRAY NOW AND THE COLOUR IS UNCHANGED: 0x4a4a52 is [74,74,82]. A deck computes [57,57,67] against ordinary asphalt at [30,29,42] - 27 levels paler - and the far band on a crossing computes [58,58,68] against the deck it joins at [57,57,67]/[59,59,69]. Snow and rain track on a deck as they do on a road.

THE INSTRUMENT TOOK FOUR TRIES AND THE FIRST THREE WERE THE WRONG KIND. The owner had already said it: "Why would you measure it that way instead of just looking up what you have applied the color as code?" Reading the code is what found both defects. But one question genuinely needs a run, and stating it exactly is what finally produced a good check: THE CODE SAYS WHAT COLOUR WAS COMPUTED; IT CANNOT SAY WHETHER THE CANVAS ACCEPTED IT, and silent rejection is precisely what both bugs were.

SAMPLING A PIXEL IS THE WRONG WAY TO ASK THAT. Four placements were tried and every one was flaky, because the ribbon is one or two pixels wide and the scene moves between the frame's bookkeeping and the read: readings of [115,128,84], [113,127,81], [112,127,76] and [185,166,72] - grass, grass, grass and a CORNFIELD - all came off builds whose colours were exactly right. Holding the car still helped and did not fix it.

A FILLSTYLE ROUND TRIP ASKS IT DIRECTLY AND DETERMINISTICALLY. Set a canvas to black, set it to the tone, read it back: it returns the normalised colour if it took it and BLACK if it refused. `farroad-test` now offers it all 18 tones the road can have - both parities and the far band, over dry, snow-covered and wet, on an ordinary road AND on a deck - and asserts every one is taken. No sampling, no flakiness, and it names the exact tone that fails.

NOT VACUOUS, WATCHED FAILING AGAINST BOTH DEFECTS SEPARATELY. With `DECK_FACE` back to a string it named `a bridge deck, dry, far: rgb(NaN,15,NaN)` and three more. With the far band's own mix back to two strings it named `an ordinary road, dry, far: rgb(NaN,NaN,NaN)` and three more. Each reported exactly which of the eighteen was broken.

`bridge-test` also asserts the deck is CONCRETE - measurably paler than asphalt - and that every channel of it is a real value, asked in that order because a brightness comparison against a NaN is meaningless. Its first version cast straight to int and died with a traceback on the very defect it was written for, which is not a failure anyone can read.

THIS IS THE FOURTH COLOUR-HELPER TRAP OF THE DAY and the fourth distinct way of failing. The owner found it by knowing what a bridge is made of, which no check was asking.
