### RLG-091 — Night becomes day by degrees, not in one frame
- **RULED 2026-08-30T20:42:36-04:00** — Owner, 2026-08-30: the transition from night to day, and from day to night, is a gigantic snap. It should lerp.

THE DAY IS 240 SECONDS AND THE LIGHT IS READ FROM A PHASE, so something in that chain is reading a BRANCH rather than a blend - a test for whether it is night, used where a proportion belongs. clockLamps already returns a fraction and ramps across dawn, which is why the street lamps and the headlights fade correctly; whatever snaps is reading the hour some other way. Find the branch before writing any easing, because easing a value that is already smooth would leave the snap exactly where it is.

THE GROUND TONE IS THE FIRST PLACE TO LOOK. groundTone takes a `dark` argument that is a BOOLEAN, and a boolean cannot be halfway. The biome work has a note about the same function taking both an hour and an amount, so the shape for a blended answer may already be there.

AND IT IS TWO TRANSITIONS, NOT ONE. Dusk and dawn are separate crossings and only one of them may be broken; the owner named both, so check both rather than fixing the one that is easier to reach. A capture across the phase - the same instrument the beam and biome work used - will say where the step is to the frame.
- **RULED 2026-08-30T22:41:27-04:00** — BUILT, 2026-08-30, AND THE BRANCH WAS FOUND BEFORE ANY EASING WAS WRITTEN - which the fragment asked for and which turned out to matter, because the thing that needed easing was already smooth.

ONLY THE GROUND WAS SNAPPING. nightFall and goldenHour have always returned continuous 0 to 1 ramps, and the SKY reads them properly: every sky stop is a mix against those fractions. Three places took the same smooth numbers and thresholded them - if nAmt > 0.5 the night tone, else if gAmt > 0.25 the golden tone, else the day tone - so the ground flipped between three fixed looks in one frame while the sky above it crossfaded. A smooth sky over a ground that jumps is worse than either being wrong alone, and it is exactly why the owner described the whole world jumping rather than a colour being off.

THREE PLACES, NOT ONE: the ground tone, the sea, and the snow colour in the mirror. The fragment's instruction to check both crossings was right for a different reason than expected - the crossings were fine, and it was the surfaces that were not.

THE FRACTIONS ARE NOW USED AS FRACTIONS, with night applied after gold so that at full night the result is the night tone whatever gold says. That reproduces what the old ordering did at its extremes, which is why dusk still reads as gold giving way to dark rather than the two fighting each other.

MEASURED ACROSS A WHOLE DAY IN 360 STEPS: the worst single frame carried 100 per cent of the ground's colour travel, at phase 0.128 - which is where nightFall crosses 0.5. After, 3 per cent, at no particular place. The verge is the same figures.

THE CHECK COMPARES THE STEP WITH THE DAY'S OWN RANGE rather than with a number. A threshold in brightness levels would need re-tuning the day anybody changed a biome palette; a share of the day's own travel does not. And sampling four times a day - which is what a screenshot pass would have done - would have found three plausible colours and no snap at all, because the fault existed only between the samples.
