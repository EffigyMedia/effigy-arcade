### RLG-309 — The mountain's parallax scenery moves too fast
- **RULED 2026-09-21T00:16:32-04:00** — OWNER, 2026-09-21, from the device: 'The parallaxing mountain scenery looks weird. They are parallaxing too fast or something.'

WHAT IS PARALLAXING AT A MOUNTAIN, AND THERE ARE THREE THINGS, WHICH IS THE FIRST JOB. The skyline band slides with the road's bend (`ox`, and `SKY_BEND_AT` through `skySmooth`). The valley RANGE across the drop has its own rate, `RANGE_DRIFT`, stated in pixels per road unit. And the roadside scenery is not parallax at all - it is objects at real distances, which move because they are passed. The report has to be attached to one of the three before a number is touched, and 'mountain scenery' most naturally names the range, which is the newest of them (RLG-297) and the only one with a hand-set rate.

`RANGE_DRIFT` IS 0.0042 PIXELS PER ROAD UNIT AND NOTHING DERIVED IT. Every other depth cue in this engine falls out of the projection; that one is typed. A drift that does not match the distance the range is supposed to be at will read as the wrong speed however it is tuned, which is the honest suspicion to test first: derive it from `RANGE_AT` and the projection rather than set it.

AND THE SKYLINE HAS A RECORDED HISTORY OF READING AS THE WRONG MOTION. [[RLG-094]] is the case where the horizon appeared to pop and the cause was not motion at all - it was being rebuilt as a different horizon ten times a minute. [[RLG-096]] is the case where its chase rate was measured in FRAMES, so the device decided the speed. Both are reasons to identify the object before believing a rate is wrong.

A SCREEN CAPTURE OR A SHORT VIDEO FROM THE DEVICE WOULD SETTLE WHICH OBJECT IT IS, and is worth asking for before any measuring.

Not started.
- **RULED 2026-09-21T00:33:58-04:00** — OWNER, 2026-09-21, asked which of the three things parallax at a mountain: 'I'm talking about the valley range.' So this is `drawValleyRange` and nothing else. The skyline band and the roadside scenery are out of scope.

AND THE FIRST SUSPECT IS NOT THE DRIFT. The range's offset is three terms added together:

    o2 = (-camX * W * 0.030) + skySmooth * 1.55 - pos * RANGE_DRIFT

Only the last of those is the forward travel this ruling was opened against. The middle one is the BEND, and it is 1.55 times what the skyline band takes for the same corner - the skyline uses `skyShift` at 1.0 and the same lane term at 0.018 rather than 0.030.

THAT MULTIPLIER IS THE WRONG WAY ROUND. `skySmooth` chases `bendPx(pos + SKY_BEND_AT)`, which is the road's sideways accumulation at the FAR end of the world - the distance the skyline sits at. The range sits at `RANGE_AT`, 0.78 of the draw, which is NEARER. Bend accumulates with distance, so the road's offset at the range's distance is LESS than at the horizon's, and the range should swing LESS with a corner than the horizon behind it. It swings half as much again. In a canyon or a mountain, which are the two places on the board at 0.90 and 1.00 of bend, that is the largest lateral motion in the frame attached to the object that should have the least.

THE LANE TERM IS PROBABLY RIGHT AND IS STATED SO IT IS NOT SWEPT UP. Moving a lane shifts a nearer object more than a farther one, so 0.030 against the skyline's 0.018 is the correct direction. Nothing about it is derived either, but it is not the reported fault.

SO THE FIX IS THE ONE RLG-304 USED: the range reads the bend at ITS OWN DISTANCE - `bendPx(pos + RANGE_AT * DRAW * SEG)` - instead of borrowing the horizon's and multiplying it by a typed constant. Then a corner moves it by the amount the road actually turns where it stands, and `RANGE_DRIFT` can be judged on its own once the term that is drowning it is correct.

THAT IS A CODE READ AND NOT A MEASUREMENT. The three terms are being instrumented before anything is changed, because this record already carries the lesson that a rate which looks wrong may not be the term that is moving.
- **RULED 2026-09-21T00:55:34-04:00** — BUILT in v0.14.115, and UNSIGNED - the feel is the owner's on a device (RLG-116).

MY FIRST READING WAS WRONG TWICE AND THE MEASUREMENT CORRECTED IT BOTH TIMES. I read the code and said the corner term was the fault, swinging 2.3 times too much. Measured over six seconds of mountain road, the three terms are: DRIFT 296.2 px, BEND 109.7 px, LANE 11.5 px. The slide is nearly three times the corner swing and it is what the owner is seeing. Then I said the corner term was 2.3 times too big; held-corner readings put `skySmooth * 1.55` within a few per cent of the range's own bend - 0.74, 1.04, 1.05, 1.10, 0.99, 0.93 of it across six corners. The borrowed term was accidentally about right, because the chase's own 0.55 times the typed 1.55 lands near the turn at the range's distance.

THE RATE WAS THE ONLY THING IN THIS MODEL NOTHING DERIVED, AND THAT IS THE FAULT. A point at lateral `L` and distance `dz` sits at `CAM_D * L * W/2 / dz` from the vanishing point and sweeps outward at that over `dz` again, so a slide rate IS a standoff read backwards. `RANGE_DRIFT` was 0.0042, which puts the range 45,700 units out while it stands 46,800 ahead - forty-four degrees off the road. That is land BESIDE you, and it moved like it.

`RANGE_OUT` STATES THE STANDOFF AND THE RATE FALLS OUT OF THE PROJECTION. Twelve multiples of `ROAD`, which is the unit every other roadside distance is written in - about 27,600 units, a little over twice the valley's own depth. The rate it produces is 0.00254 px per unit. MEASURED ON ONE ROAD, ALTERNATING INSIDE ONE PAGE: 50.0 and 50.2 px a second on the old rate against 30.4 and 30.4 on the derived one, and on a second road 37.8 against 21.8. The slide is about 40 per cent slower.

AND THE CORNER TERM CARRIED AN UNSTATED SIGN, WHICH IS THE PART THAT NEARLY SHIPPED WRONG. `skyWant` is `-bendPx(pos + SKY_BEND_AT) * 0.55`, so taking `skySmooth * 1.55` inherited a negation nobody had written down. Replacing it with `bendPx` at the range's own distance dropped that negation and INVERTED the swing - the range turned the opposite way to the horizon behind it. It is `-bendPx(pos + rangeZ)` now, and the check that caught it is the first thing in the proof.

THE HORIZON'S OWN 0.55 IS DELIBERATELY NOT COPIED. That band stands for things at no particular distance and its fraction is a feel. This one is at a stated distance and takes the whole of the turn there.

PROVED BY `tools/range-drift-proof.py`, WHICH IS NEW, AND BOTH HALVES WERE WATCHED FAILING. Inverting the sign takes the agreement from 100 per cent to 11. Typing the rate back takes the doubling ratio from 2.00 to 1.003. The arithmetic is DELIBERATELY NOT RE-STATED in the check: recomputing the same formula from the same constants and finding it equal proves that multiplication works, so what is asserted is that MOVING the standoff moves the picture.

AND THE CHECK ITSELF WAS WRONG FIRST, WHICH IS WORTH KEEPING. The sign was originally asserted on HELD CORNERS, and the run failed on a correct engine at 4 of 5: a horizon reading of 8.2 px against a range of -16.9, both sitting on a zero crossing. Two reasons, both measured. `skySmooth` is a LAGGING chase, so the two terms cross zero at different moments and disagree for a second every time the road straightens. And `holdCurve` never settles - it only shapes road that has not been generated yet, so the bend at the far end keeps moving for as long as the car drives into road that was already made. The check counts many frames of ordinary driving now, ignores everything under 20 px, and asks what fraction agree. This is the standing lesson 'when a check fails, suspect the check' paying for itself on my own instrument.

WHAT IS STILL THE OWNER'S: whether 40 per cent slower is slow enough, and whether the range now reads as land across a valley. `RANGE_OUT` is the one constant and it is now a distance rather than a rate, so it can be reasoned about.

SUITE: smoke-test 7/7, wall-plane-test all pass, drive-test 36/37 with the nitrous settle line that is recorded as environmental.
