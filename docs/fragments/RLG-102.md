### RLG-102 — A farmland biome, and what else a place could be
- **RULED 2026-08-31T09:05:10-04:00** — Owner, 2026-08-31: "I want to add another biome farmland. This one would have one side with a
cornfield and the other side flat with houses and barns. Just like the ocean, the sides would be
randomly decided at generation. The skyline would just be open sky just like ocean." And a question
with it: are there other biomes we could come up with that do not overlap too much with what we
already have? To be taken up at the end of the current queue.

FARMLAND IS A SIDED PLACE, AND THAT MACHINERY NOW EXISTS. `rollSeaSide` picks which side the water is
on per place, and the coast reads it everywhere - the drawn slices, the far band and the mirror. A
cornfield on one side and yards on the other is the same idea with two kinds of land instead of land
and water, so the sided mechanism should be GENERALISED rather than copied. A second function that
rolls a side is a second thing to keep in step, and this project has the receipts on that.

THE OPEN SKY IS ALREADY A FIELD. `cover` was added with the coastal rename and defaults to 1, so
farmland states a low number and gets the same open sky the coast has, with no new code at all. That
is the test of whether the coastal work was built as a tendency or as a special case, and it passes.

WHAT IS GENUINELY NEW IN IT. A cornfield is not a scatter of objects the way a forest is - it is a
TEXTURE with rows in it, and rows have a direction that the perspective has to respect or it reads as
carpet. That is the one part of this with no precedent in the engine, and it is where the effort will
go. Barns and houses are the beach houses again with different paint.

THE SIX PLACES THAT EXIST, so a seventh does not repeat one: CITY towers and lit windows, DESERT mesas
and sand, FOREST treeline and the heaviest scenery, MOUNTAIN peaks with a snow line, TUNDRA mountain
painted white, COASTAL a sided sea, SWAMP half water with a broken treeline. What separates them in
the record is not their art but their four numbers - rain, snow, hill, bend - plus the ground, the
skyline and the scenery. A new place earns its keep by being somewhere on those axes that nothing else
is.

WHERE THE GAPS ACTUALLY ARE, stated as axes rather than as themes, because a theme with the same
numbers as an existing place is a reskin:

  NOTHING IS BOTH FLAT AND TIGHT. Hill and bend move together across the table except for FOREST and
  COASTAL. A place that barely climbs but turns constantly is unlike anything on the board.

  NOTHING IS ENCLOSED. Every place has a horizon. A CANYON or a cutting - rock walls close on both
  sides, the skyline is the wall rather than a silhouette, and the sky is a strip - would be the first
  place where the skyline is not a distant band, which is a structural difference rather than a new
  palette. It is also the natural home for the vertical curvature the owner keeps asking not to be
  flat.

  NOTHING IS DARK BY DAY. Weather and the hour drive the light, and every place is lit the same way at
  the same hour. A TUNNEL, or a place under permanent overcast, would put `lampsOn` and the headlight
  work to use in daylight - and the headlights are already built and currently only earn their keep at
  night.

  NOTHING IS INDUSTRIAL. CITY is towers. Refineries, gantries, flare stacks and freight - low, wide,
  lit at night, and a skyline of horizontal masses rather than vertical ones. It is the visual
  opposite of CITY using the same generator.

  AND FARMLAND ITSELF FILLS THE FLATTEST GAP. Almost no hill, gentle bends, an enormous sky. Nothing
  on the board is open in that way; COASTAL is the closest and it is defined by having a hard edge
  down one side.

NOT A DECISION. This is a list to put to the owner, and the question they asked is the one to answer
first: which of these is different enough to be worth building. Nothing here should be started before
that answer.

- **RULED 2026-09-01T00:10:47-04:00** — BUILT, 0.11.1. Farmland is on the board and the sided mechanism generalised as this fragment said it
should.

THE SIDE ROLL IS ONE FUNCTION, NOT TWO. `seaSide` and `rollSeaSide` are `sideRoll` and `rollSide`,
renamed across all twenty-two sites that read them. A place says what it does with the side it rolled:
COASTAL says `sea` and draws NOTHING there because it is water; FARMLAND says `crop` and draws a
different spec there. Both read the one roll, so a place can never disagree with itself about which
side it is - which is exactly what a second roll would eventually produce, and the whole reason this
fragment asked for a generalisation rather than a copy. `API.seaSide` is kept as a name because
harnesses use it, alongside the general `API.sideRoll`.

TWO SPECS IS HONEST HERE AND WAS NOT FOR THE COAST, and the distinction is worth keeping. The coastal
record carries a note against a second, landward-only spec: with the seaward side empty, that left the
first spec with no side to draw on - dead code dressed as generality. Farmland uses BOTH sides, so
both specs are live.

THE CORNFIELD IS THE PART WITH NO PRECEDENT, and it turned out to be two lines rather than a second
drawing system. Every roadside in this engine is a SCATTER: a hash decides whether an object stands at
this segment, a second hash jitters where in the band it stands, and the eye reads woodland or desert
precisely BECAUSE it is irregular. A planted field is the opposite - straight rows, evenly spaced,
running away from the road. `lattice` is that difference: the density roll is skipped so every rank at
every segment is planted, and the jitter is dropped so the ranks line up across segments. What the
perspective then does with a regular grid is what it does with a real field.

MEASURED, and this is the property no screenshot of a three-pixel sprite could settle: THE CROP USES
ONE DISTINCT DISTANCE FROM THE ROAD PER RANK, and the yards on the other side use SIXTEEN. The
regularity is the crop rather than the engine.

AND BOTH VIEWS AGREE ABOUT WHICH SIDE IS WHICH: 4,810 placements on the rolled side out of the
windscreen against 250 on the other, and 2,758 against 68 in the mirror. A second side-roll would have
put the field on the left out of the front and the right in the glass.

THE OPEN SKY COST NOTHING, which was the stated test of whether the coastal work was built as a
tendency or as a special case. Farmland states the coast's own 0.45 and needed no new code. It passes.

RELIEF 0.10 IS THE LOWEST ON THE BOARD, which is the gap this place was argued to fill: nothing else
is open in that way, and the coast comes closest while being defined by a hard edge down one side.
Still not zero, because the owner has said twice that nothing is completely flat.

AND IT SNOWS DESPITE A STATED SNOW CHANCE OF ZERO, which is the case the owner raised the whole
two-temperature model with. Measured at about four per cent of what falls on it - occasional and
light, which is what a temperate January is. That measurement also corrected a loose sentence in
[[RLG-109]]: it claimed three precipitation events in ten, which is the fraction of MOMENTS cold
enough to snow at all rather than the fraction of falls that come down as snow.

NOT JUDGED ON A DEVICE. Whether the corn reads as corn at speed, whether a red barn is too loud
against the palette, and whether a lattice of six ranks is dense enough to be a field or so dense it
is a wall, are all feel.
