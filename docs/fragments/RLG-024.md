### RLG-024 — Interstate: widen the road so the lanes are bigger
- **RULED 2026-08-28T13:51:40-04:00** — - **Owner report:** the road needs to be a bit wider to make the lanes bigger.

- **What it touches.** `ROAD` is the half-width and `LANE_X` places four lanes inside it. Widening
  changes the projection of every sprite, the overlap tests for collisions, the traffic spawn
  geometry, the roadblock gap, and the verge/barrier limits at 1.15. **Do not widen `ROAD` alone** -
  check each of those, because several are written in lane units and several in world units.
- **RULED 2026-08-29T23:59:11-04:00** — THE ROADSIDE IS NOW INDEPENDENT OF THE ROAD'S WIDTH, before the road is widened rather than after.
Owner, 2026-08-29: finish the scenery "in a way that widening the road soon won't negatively effect".

WHAT WOULD HAVE HAPPENED. Everything beside the road was written in multiples of `ROAD`: the lamps at
1.15 of it, the trees at 1.35, and every object SIZED by it as well. Widening `ROAD` scales all of
that together, so a wider road would have arrived with a proportionally wider verge and bigger trees.
The forest would have thinned at exactly the moment the tarmac got closer to it, and the lamps would
have walked away from the kerb.

THAT IS THE DANGEROUS KIND OF REGRESSION - nothing breaks, no check goes red, the picture just
quietly stops being the picture. It would have been found, if at all, as "the widening looks wrong"
with no obvious cause.

WHAT IT IS INSTEAD. `SCENE_UNIT` is the unit the roadside is sized and spaced in, and the lateral
position is measured from the road EDGE - `p.w + scale * SCENE_UNIT * W/2 * out` - rather than from
its centre in road widths. Widen `ROAD` and the tarmac gets wider while the verge beside it stays the
width it was.

IT STARTS EQUAL TO `ROAD` ON PURPOSE, and the conversion was done arithmetically so today's picture
is unchanged: `2 * p.w * 1.35` and `p.w + p.w * 1.70` are the same number. A decoupling that also
retunes is a decoupling nobody can verify. Confirmed by capture as well as by arithmetic.

MEASURED, at 12,000 units out:

  ROAD     road edge   lamp gap   tree gap
  1900        31.89      41.45      54.21
  2600        43.63      41.45      54.21
  3400        57.06      41.45      54.21

`ROAD` IS A TUNABLE NOW RATHER THAN A CONSTANT, which this ruling needs anyway. `LANE_X` is in
normalised units so the lanes are fractions of it and widen with it automatically, and everything
that moves sideways is already written in lane units. Making it settable turns the widening into a
runtime experiment rather than a rebuild - and it is what makes the independence above testable at
all, because the only honest proof is to widen the road and look at what moved.

STILL NOT DONE, and this fragment remains open: the widening itself. This unit only makes it safe to
do.
- **RULED 2026-08-30T01:59:47-04:00** — THE ROAD IS WIDER. `ROAD` goes from 1900 to 2300, a fifth more. Owner's original report: "the road
needs to be a bit wider to make the lanes bigger."

THE NUMBER WAS CHOSEN BY LOOKING, at 1900, 2300 and 2700. At 2700 the near tarmac swallows the bottom
of the frame, the car looks small on it, and the verge is pushed out of the near view entirely - it
stops reading as a road and starts reading as an apron. 2300 gives visibly bigger lanes and keeps the
roadside in shot. "A bit wider" is a judgement and a picture is the only way to make it.

THE LANES CAME FOR NOTHING, which is what this fragment predicted. `LANE_X` is in normalised units so
four lanes inside a wider road are four wider lanes, and every lateral rate, threshold and bound is
written in lane units for the same reason - so traffic merging, giving way and the corridor guarantee
all move with it without a number being touched.

THIS FRAGMENT'S OWN WARNING WAS THE USEFUL PART: "Do not widen `ROAD` alone - check each of those,
because several are written in lane units and several in world units." The list was projection,
collisions, traffic spawn, the roadblock gap, and the verge limits. Checked:

  the projection            scales, by construction
  the player's bounds       +/-1.18 in NORMALISED units, so they widen with the road
  steering                  the input divides by screen pixels into normalised units, so the same
                            thumb travel crosses the same number of LANES - the feel is preserved
                            in the terms that matter rather than in world distance
  traffic and merging       lane units throughout; merge-test measured 0.6% parked between lanes,
                            inside its usual range, and traffic-test saw 31 merges
  the corridor guarantee    traffic-test green, nothing spawning inside the drawn road
  the circuit               circuit-test green, still empty of civilian traffic
  the roadside              independent since UNT-036, and the captures confirm the trees stayed at
                            the kerb while the tarmac grew

AND ONE THING ON THAT LIST WAS STILL WRONG. `drawSign` placed its chevron boards at 1.34 ROAD WIDTHS
from the centre and sized them by the road's width. Widening would have walked them away from the
kerb and grown them - exactly the fault UNT-036 removed from the lamps and the scenery, in the one
roadside object nobody had looked at. It is measured from the tarmac edge in scenery units now.

AND IT HAD NO OCCLUSION EITHER. Its guard was `overBrow`, the same dead call the lamps were found
behind: it returns false on its first line and has since `crestY` was re-enabled. So a sign drew
straight through a hill. That is the THIRD thing found behind that one dead function, after the lamps
and after nothing else was checked at the time. Anything else that calls `overBrow` should be assumed
broken until looked at.

NOT VERIFIED HERE. Whether the road now FEELS right at speed - whether the lanes are big enough to
place a car in, and whether the wider carriageway makes traffic easier or harder to read - is the
owner's call on the device. The harnesses can say the geometry holds; they cannot say a road is the
right width.
- **RULED 2026-08-30T07:26:03-04:00** — THE WIDENING SCALED EVERYTHING, WHICH IS NOT WIDENING. Owner, 2026-08-30, on the device: "the road
was supposed to get wider while everything else stayed the same size. It feels like all you did was
just scale everything up so that defeats the purpose." And then, exactly: "I want the cars to be the
same size they used to be just the road widening so cars at their original dimensions fit in the
lanes better."

THAT IS PRECISELY WHAT HAPPENED. Every vehicle's width is stored as a fraction of `ROAD` - the player
at 0.265 of it, each traffic type and rival at its own - and `drawSprite` computes
`w = scale * worldW * ROAD`. Widening the road by a fifth widened every car by a fifth. The lanes got
bigger and the cars got bigger with them, so nothing fit any better and the whole change was a zoom.

I HAD ALREADY FIXED THIS FAULT ONCE, FOR THE ROADSIDE, AND DID NOT LOOK FOR IT ANYWHERE ELSE. UNT-036
decoupled the scenery and the lamps from `ROAD` and recorded why: "everything beside the road was
written in multiples of ROAD... widening would have pushed the whole roadside outward and grown it."
The same sentence is true of every car on it, and the unit that widened the road went through this
fragment's warning list - projection, collisions, spawns, verge limits - and did not think to ask
what else was measured in road widths. The captures at three widths were taken and compared, and the
cars growing in all three of them was not noticed, because everything grew together and the picture
still looked like a road.

WHAT IT IS NOW. `CAR_UNIT` is the road half-width as it was BEFORE the widening - the size the cars
have always been - and every vehicle's width reads against that. POSITIONS still use `ROAD`, and that
is the whole point: a car's lateral place is a lane, lanes are fractions of the road, so the cars sit
where they always sat inside lanes that are now wider than they are.

AND THE COLLISIONS HAD TO GO WITH THEM, which is the part that would have been worse than the
original fault. Overlap is tested in road-normalised units - `Math.abs(c.x - playerX) < (c.w+0.26)/2`
- so leaving those alone while the sprites shrank would mean a car collides WIDER than it looks. You
would clip something you can see you have missed. `carW()` is the one place that converts a width in
car units into the units positions are compared in, and every overlap test goes through it.

MEASURED, and the check is in `biome-test.py` so it cannot come back: the player is 230.17 pixels
wide at ROAD 1900, 2300 and 3000, while the road edge at the same distance goes 31.89, 38.60, 50.35.
The second assertion is there because the first alone would pass if the road stopped widening too.

THE LESSON IS NOT ABOUT UNITS. It is that a fault found and fixed in one place is a fault to go
looking for in every other place with the same shape, in the same session - not a fix to be pleased
with. The roadside decoupling was recorded as a success, complete with a measurement table, on the
same day the identical fault sat untouched in every vehicle in the game.
