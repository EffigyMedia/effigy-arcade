### RLG-328 — What inside the scenery costs a frame
- **RULED 2026-09-23T14:44:10-04:00** — MEASURED 2026-09-23. The owner chose the scenery as the next cost after the three fills, and asked for the measurement before any of it is changed: it is the largest single number in RLG-299's table and the only one with NO known remedy.

FIRST, A CORRECTION TO RLG-299'S OWN TABLE. That table put the scenery at 18.0 fps in a CITY and 13.9 in a COASTAL, with 3.4 in a FOREST. IT IS THE OTHER WAY ROUND. Two separate three-road runs agree: FOREST 16.3 and 16.8, SWAMP 18.0 and 15.9, and CITY, COASTAL, DESERT, TUNDRA and MOUNTAIN all within noise of zero. The object counts are what exposed it - a COASTAL paints 87 scenery objects a frame and a MOUNTAIN paints NONE, and you cannot spend fourteen frames a second painting 87 sprites. The old figures passed their own sign test on 8 of 9 and 7 of 9 pairs with spreads of 9.1 to 18.3 and -0.0 to 17.8, which is a spread that should have disqualified them.

THE COST IS ENTIRELY THE RASTER AND NONE OF IT IS THE WALK. `tools/scenery-cost.py` runs three arms on one road: everything, then the whole walk with neither `drawImage`, then nothing at all. Across every place and every road the WALK measured between -0.9 and +0.6 fps. So the hashes, the projections, the crest gates, the saves and the clips are free, and deciding sooner or walking less buys NOTHING. Whatever is done here has to paint fewer pixels.

AND THE PIXELS ARE IN THE FEW LARGE NEAR SPRITES, NOT THE MANY SMALL FAR ONES. Measured by how wide each object is drawn, per frame in a FOREST: 876 objects under 8 pixels carry 10 PER CENT of the painted area, while 244 objects over 16 pixels carry 70 PER CENT. A SWAMP is the same shape and a CITY is more extreme at 88 per cent.

THAT KILLS THE OBVIOUS REMEDY. Raising the minimum size and dropping the distant smudges - which this engine already does in the glass, where a sub-pixel tree costs a full drawImage - would buy about a tenth of the scenery's cost while thinning the woods at the horizon, which is exactly where the depth reads from. The wrong trade.

WHAT THE ROWS COST, which is the lever that is left. A dense place draws several ROWS of objects walking outward from the road edge. Capping them, against the uncapped frame, two roads and three pairs each:

  FOREST   6 rows  1730 objects   control
           4 rows  1395 objects   +3.8 fps
           3 rows  1002 objects  +12.1 fps
           2 rows   682 objects  +13.3 fps
           1 row    361 objects  +19.1 fps

  SWAMP    all     1252 objects   control
           4 rows  1246 objects   +1.7 fps
           3 rows   912 objects   +3.6 fps
           2 rows   596 objects   +9.6 fps
           1 row    344 objects  +12.4 fps

THREE ROWS INSTEAD OF SIX BUYS 12.1 OF A FOREST'S 16.3 FPS - three quarters of the cost for half the trees.

AND IT RUNS STRAIGHT INTO AN OWNER RULING, WHICH IS WHY IT IS A QUESTION AND NOT A FIX. [[RLG-059]] records the owner asking for exactly these rows: "heavily populated biome scenery should be replaced from the road edge to out beyond the edge of the screen like forests and mountains tundra", and the engine's own note says why - "A forest is not a row of trees, it is trees all the way out until you cannot see any more of them." Cutting rows is cutting that.

THERE IS A THIRD OPTION AND THE MEASUREMENT POINTS AT IT. The area is in the NEAR objects; the impression of "all the way out" is carried by the FAR ones, which are cheap. So the rows want TAPERING BY DISTANCE - every row far away, where they cost a tenth of the area and do all the work of the ruling, and fewer rows close to the camera, where six rows of canopy overlap into a wall you cannot see past anyway. That keeps the ruling and takes most of the saving, and it has not been built or measured.

NOTHING IS CHANGED. `API.sceneryRaster`, `API.sceneryRows` and `API.sceneryCount` are debug switches and readers, off unless a harness turns them on.
