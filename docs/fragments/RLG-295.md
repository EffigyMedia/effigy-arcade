### RLG-295 — Double the draw distance forward
- **RULED 2026-09-20T09:12:48-04:00** — OWNER, 2026-09-20, from the device: 'I think we may need to double the draw distance forward.' DRAW is 150 segments (30,000 units) and the note on it records that it was raised from 95 because the road stopped short of the horizon. Doubling it is not one constant: the road pass walks every slice each frame, the sprite buckets and the hill clip are built over it, the far road band begins where it ends, and the skyline's growth is measured against it. MEASURE THE FRAME RATE BEFORE AND AFTER with fps-test, on the phone's own terms, and read what else reads DRAW before changing it. Not started.
- **RULED 2026-09-20T12:44:41-04:00** — MEASURED 2026-09-20, NOT CHANGED. This ruling's own instruction is to measure the frame rate before and after and to read what else reads DRAW before changing it. Both are done and the answer needs the owner, because doubling the draw is not free and it is not one constant.

THE FRAME COST, measured with tools/draw-cost.py (EVD-1143). `DRAW` is a `const` read in over a hundred places and cannot be toggled at runtime, so the engine is SERVED with the one line rewritten and the two builds are ALTERNATED in one page, reloading between arms - two sequential fps-test runs cannot be compared on this machine, which was demonstrated twice today. At 55 per cent of top speed, midday, no weather:

  MOUNTAIN   150 (30,000 units) 60.7-60.8    300 (60,000 units) 38.4-53.7
  CANYON     150 (30,000 units) 60.6-60.7    300 (60,000 units) 46.8-58.6
  FOREST     150 (30,000 units) 60.7-60.7    300 (60,000 units) 51.3-60.7

A second run agreed: MOUNTAIN 60.6-60.7 against 36.5-60.6, CANYON 51.4-60.7 against 38.2-59.5, FOREST 60.7 against 60.6-60.8. The two ranges do not overlap for the mountain or the canyon, which is fps-test's own rule for a real change. THIS IS A DESKTOP, and RLG-002 makes this a phone game.

THE PLACES THAT COST THE MOST ARE THE ONES WITH A SURFACE PAINTED PER SLICE - the mountain's drop and the canyon's walls, both of which are a quad a slice a side. The forest, which has neither, pays least. So the cost is not the road pass alone, and it will grow with every surface added beside the road.

WHAT ELSE READS DRAW, which is the other half of the instruction. It is genuinely 'the far end of the draw' nearly everywhere, and four of those are behaviour rather than drawing:
- `OUT_OF_SIGHT` is `DRAW*SEG + 5000` and is where deer, traffic, crates, police and road furniture are SPAWNED. It must follow the draw or a car spawns in view and pops in, so this coupling is correct and unavoidable - but every spawn distance doubles with it.
- `RADAR_RANGE` IS `OUT_OF_SIGHT`, so the radar detector's warning distance doubles too. That is a FEEL number the owner has already been asked about once - 'whether a quarter of a mile is enough warning' - and doubling it is a gameplay change nobody asked for. It can be pinned instead.
- The PLACE PLANNER plants the next biome at `pos/SEG + DRAW`, so a place is chosen and its edge is set twice as far out.
- `skyRiseOf` derives the skyline's height cap from `CAM_D / (DRAW*SEG)`, so doubling the draw HALVES the cap. That is correct by RLG-104's own reasoning - the furthest thing the road pass draws is smaller on screen, so the horizon may not be taller - but it visibly shortens the canyon's skyline band.
- The ARRIVAL RAMP is `1 - n/DRAW` and everything reads it (RLG-218), so the fade band doubles in world units and stays the same fraction. That one is right.
- `SIREN_LOOK_MAX` is `DRAW*SEG`, so a siren's cap doubles.

SO THE DECISION IS THE OWNER'S AND IT IS THREE QUESTIONS: whether to pay 60 to about 40 fps in the mountain and the canyon for twice the road; whether the radar detector's range should double with it or be pinned where it is; and whether 300 is the number or something between. Nothing was changed.
