### RLG-043 — Lower the production and sports class stats, and traffic normalized so the lorry is not made worse
- **RULED 2026-08-28T17:33:45-04:00** — **Owner, 2026-08-28:** "let's lower the stats a bit for the production/sports class vehicles. Do the
same with the traffic but slightly normalized so we don't end up making the Lorry any worse."

**Blocked on [[RLG-042]].** Lowering numbers that three different physics models read in three
different ways would change the player's cars, leave the rivals untouched, and not reach the traffic
on the road at all - because traffic never reads `BODY`. The verification comes first so that a
reduction means one thing.

**The production and sports class** is read as ROADSTER, TUNER and MUSCLE - the three the code calls
the sports league, "a triangle, not a ladder" at road.js:1458. Not the supercars, and not CRUISER,
which is a police body. **Put to the owner if the reduction is meant to reach CRUISER as well.**

**"Slightly normalized so we don't end up making the Lorry any worse"** has a concrete reading, and
this is the one to confirm: pull each traffic body DOWN TOWARD the lorry rather than by a flat
percentage, so the fastest traffic drops most and the lorry does not move at all.

    new_vmax = LORRY_vmax + (old_vmax - LORRY_vmax) * (1 - k)

LORRY `vmax` is 0.40, which is 80mph. With k = 0.05 the lorry stays at 80, a coupe moves 160 to 156,
a van 120 to 118. A flat 5% would take the lorry to 76, which is what the owner ruled out.

**"A bit" is not a number, and the number is the owner's.** The proposal above uses 5% as a
placeholder so the shape can be seen. The measurement from RLG-042 has to land first: if the cars do
not currently reach their claimed figures, then lowering the claim and lowering the behaviour are
two different changes, and only the owner can say which was meant.

**Interacts with [[RLG-045]].** The owner has separately ruled that traffic keeps to a 70-80mph
limit most of the time and that the limitation is the DRIVER rather than the car. If a traffic car's
speed comes from its driver keeping a limit, its `vmax` stops being what you see on the road - so
these two must be designed together or the reduction will be invisible.
- **RULED 2026-08-28T17:41:45-04:00** — **Owner's answers, 2026-08-28, to the two questions this fragment parked.**

**1. The reduction covers ROADSTER, TUNER, MUSCLE and CRUISER.** The owner chose the sports league
plus the police car, and then gave the reason as a design rule rather than as a one-off:

> "The cruiser is designed or should be designed to be comparable to the sports class, while the
> super cruiser is designed to be comparable to the super class."

**That is a standing rule and it outlives this reduction.** CRUISER tracks the sports class;
SUPERCRUISER tracks the supercars. Both move whenever their class moves, and neither is tuned on its
own again.

**The table does not currently obey that rule, in both directions.** From `BODY`, with `vmax` read
as a fraction of 200mph:

| | vmax | mph |
|---|---|---|
| MUSCLE | 0.94 | 188 |
| ROADSTER | 0.88 | 176 |
| TUNER | 0.82 | 164 |
| **CRUISER** | **0.95** | **190** |
| SUPERCRUISER | 0.92 | 184 |
| MATADOR | 0.97 | 194 |
| CREST | 1.00 | 200 |
| STALLION | 1.03 | 206 |
| FORMULA | 1.09 | 218 |

**CRUISER at 190 is quicker than every car in the class it is supposed to be comparable to** - two
miles an hour faster than the quickest, and twenty-six clear of the slowest. **SUPERCRUISER at 184
is slower than every car in ITS class**, by ten miles an hour against the slowest supercar. So the
two police cars are each on the wrong side of their own rung, and the reduction is not just a
lowering - it has to seat both of them inside the class the owner has now named for them.

**The SUPERCRUISER comment is also wrong about its own car.** road.js:1495 says the equipment "costs
4mph of top end" against the MATADOR it is built from. MATADOR is 194 and SUPERCRUISER is 184: the
cost is 10mph. The comment describes a car that is not in the table.

**2. Nothing here is acted on before [[RLG-042]].** These are the numbers the table declares. Whether
they are the numbers the cars ACHIEVE is exactly what RLG-042 measures, and a police car that
out-runs the sports class on paper may or may not do so on the road.
- **RULED 2026-08-28T19:40:08-04:00** — **BLOCKED IN ONE HALF, CLEARED IN THE OTHER, by the measurement in [[RLG-042]].**

**The traffic half cannot be done at all yet.** Traffic never reads `BODY`: its `cruise` is drawn
from a random range keyed off a type string, and the measured ratio of road speed to declared `vmax`
not only sits between 0.29 and 0.66 but MOVES between sessions for the same vehicle type. So the
'slightly normalized so we don't make the Lorry any worse' scheme - pulling each traffic body down
toward the lorry - would edit numbers that nothing on the road consults. **It is blocked on the
traffic actually reading the table**, which is the owner's own ruling in RLG-042 that traffic cars
have real stats with the driver as the limitation, and which [[RLG-045]] must land alongside.

**The sports and police half is clear to proceed.** Every driveable body reaches exactly `vmax * 200`,
measured, so lowering ROADSTER, TUNER, MUSCLE and CRUISER will do precisely what it says.

**Two corrections to what this fragment recorded earlier**, now that there is a measurement rather
than a code read:

- The table's `vmax` figures are **correct** and the comments around them are the wrong ones. The
  earlier entry set 'MUSCLE 188 by the table' against 'MUSCLE 184 by the comment' without saying
  which was true. 188 is true.
- The seating problem is confirmed rather than merely declared: CRUISER measured **190mph**, above
  every car in the sports class it is meant to match, and SUPERCRUISER **184mph**, below every
  supercar. Both are on the wrong side of their own rung, by measurement.

**A third thing to decide, which the measurement surfaced.** `zeroSixty()` - the number on the garage
card - is optimistic by +0.1s on a supercar and +1.3s on a van, because it approximates the torque
curve instead of calling `gearFactor()`. Lowering `vmax` will move those printed figures and they
will still be wrong by the same growing margin. **Ask the owner whether the card should be made
honest as part of this**, or tracked separately: it is a different change from a stat reduction, and
doing both at once would make it impossible to tell which one moved a number.

**Still unmeasured: `brake` and `grip`.** RLG-042 covered straight-line acceleration and top speed
only. If 'lower the stats a bit' is meant to include braking and cornering, those need the same
treatment first.
- **RULED 2026-08-28T19:53:01-04:00** — **Owner, 2026-08-28, giving this fragment the rule it was missing:** "I want the stats to be mostly
realistic but biased towards fun."

**That is the tie-breaker.** This fragment has been carrying "a bit" as its only guidance and three
parked questions. Now there is a principle a proposal can be argued against, which means the numbers
can be drafted for the owner to review rather than asked for one at a time.

**How it will be read, so the reading is on the record and can be corrected before any work:**

- **realistic sets the SHAPE** - the ordering of the classes, roughly plausible power-to-weight,
  a lorry that is slow because a lorry is slow, brakes and grip that track what a vehicle is
- **fun breaks the tie whenever realism would make the game worse** - a supercar that reaches its
  top speed inside a stretch of road the player can actually see, a lorry that is a hazard rather
  than a wall, differences between cars that are FELT rather than merely true
- when the two conflict and it is not obvious which should win, **the owner decides**, and the
  proposal will say where those points are rather than quietly choosing

**Still true from the measurement:** the sports and police half can proceed, the traffic half cannot
until traffic reads the table. And `brake` and `grip` remain unmeasured - if "the stats" includes
them, they need the [[RLG-042]] treatment first, because a number lowered without knowing whether it
was honest is a change nobody can interpret afterwards.
