### RLG-052 — A traffic car signals before it merges, and sometimes does not
- **RULED 2026-08-28T18:03:02-04:00** — **Owner, 2026-08-28:** "Before a traffic car merges, it turns on its turn signal for a couple
seconds. This gives players a broadcast that someone is about to merge. We should have a small
percentage chance of merging without using a turn signal, maybe 25% or 20%."

**What makes this good is the second sentence.** A signal on every car is a HUD element painted on
the world - it removes the reading rather than rewarding it. One car in four or five saying nothing
keeps the player watching the traffic instead of watching for indicators, and it makes the signal
information rather than a guarantee.

**It cannot be built before [[RLG-040]], and that is a design fact rather than a scheduling one.** A
signal has to come on BEFORE the move, for a couple of seconds. Today a lane change is a per-frame
lateral pressure that starts the instant something is in front and stops when it goes away - there
is no moment at which the car has decided to move and has not yet moved. There is nothing to signal.
RLG-040 introduces exactly that: a target lane, committed to, and finished. The indicator hangs off
that decision. Build it before, and the signal can only come on at the same time as the movement,
which broadcasts nothing.

**It needs [[RLG-053]] for the drawing**, which is the owner's ruling that a lit lamp is the unlit
bulb drawn again rather than its own rectangle.

**Open, and the owner should answer it:** does the player's own car have indicators, and are they
driven by steering or by a control? The ruling names traffic only. A player signal is a different
feature - it is an input, not a tell - and inventing one is not implied by this.

**One thing to check rather than assume.** The turn signal is a repeating flash, and the project has
a note about periodicity being audible and visible where it is not wanted. At distance and at 190mph
a blinking two-pixel lamp may read as the rendering pop the owner has separately reported in
[[RLG-041]]. Whatever else happens, this must not be built while that report is open and unexplained,
or the two will be confused for each other.
- **RULED 2026-08-28T18:07:02-04:00** — **Owner's answer, 2026-08-28, to the question this fragment parked.**

> "The player's car should have unlit turn signals, because every car should have turn signals, but
> the player doesn't really have a need to use them, so there shouldn't really be controls to engage
> them."

**So: the bulbs exist on the player's car and they never light. No control, no binding, no automatic
signalling from steering.**

**This is a ruling about the MODEL, not about the feature**, and it lands more neatly than a
compromise would have. Under [[RLG-053]] a lit lamp is the unlit bulb drawn again, so the two halves
are already separate things: the painter DECLARES a bulb, and a lit overlay CONSUMES it. The player's
car declares its indicators like every other vehicle and nothing ever consumes them. There is no
special case in the painter and no `if(isPlayer)` anywhere - the overlay simply never fires.

**It also settles what the feature is for.** An indicator here is a TELL, not an input. It exists so
the player can read what another car is about to do. Giving the player a control would have made it
an input as well, and a signalling mechanic nobody needs is a button that has to be taught, bound on
a touch device that has no room for it, and then ignored. [[RLG-002]] rules this game MOBILE ONLY,
touch is the input, and screen space is the scarcest thing there is.

**Consequence worth stating plainly:** the player's indicators are geometry that is drawn and never
illuminated. A later session finding unlit bulbs with no code path to light them should read this
ruling before concluding something is unfinished. **They are not a stub.**
- **RULED 2026-08-28T18:09:24-04:00** — **Owner, 2026-08-28, refining the ruling above. This CORRECTS the previous entry's wording.**

> "Racers also don't have a need to engage their turn signals, but the vehicles should still have
> them. They should be wired to engage their turn signals, but racers shouldn't really have a reason
> to do so nor should the player."

and, on what "wired" means:

> "By wired, I mean the car's turn signals should be wired to function."

**So the mechanism is REAL AND WORKING on every vehicle in the game.** Racers get indicators, the
player gets indicators, traffic gets indicators, and on all of them the signals actually function.
What differs is only whether anything asks them to come on.

| | bulbs | wired to function | anything triggers them |
|---|---|---|---|
| traffic | yes | yes | **yes** - before a merge, 75-80% of the time |
| racers | yes | yes | no reason to |
| the player | yes | yes | no reason to, and no control |

**CORRECTION.** The previous entry said the player's indicators are "geometry that is drawn and
never illuminated" with "no code path to light them", and called them "not a stub". The first half
is now wrong and is withdrawn: there IS a working path on the player's car, the same one every other
vehicle has. What is true is that nothing currently triggers it. "Not a stub" was right for the
wrong reason - they are not unfinished work because they are FINISHED and unused, rather than because
they are decoration.

**This is the same shape as the owner's ruling in [[RLG-042]]**, and the consistency is worth naming
because it is now a pattern rather than a one-off: *traffic cars all have real stats as well, except
their drivers are the limitation.* The car is fully capable; the DRIVER decides. Indicators are that
rule applied to a lamp instead of to an engine. A design that made indicators a traffic-only feature
would be the same mistake as giving traffic fake stats - a capability faked at the level of the
class instead of decided at the level of the driver.

**What it rules out, concretely:** no `if(isTraffic)` around the indicator logic, and no separate
traffic-only lamp code. One mechanism, on every vehicle, driven by whoever is driving.

**It also makes a later feature nearly free, which is the test of whether the seam is right.**
[[RLG-047]] has a non-racer pulling over for the police. A car pulling onto the verge signals first,
because that is what pulling over IS - and under this ruling there is nothing to build for it. If
that turns out to need new lamp code, the seam was drawn in the wrong place.
- **RULED 2026-08-29T07:39:25-04:00** — **THE LAMPS EXIST ON EVERY VEHICLE NOW (v0.9.23), and nothing asks for them yet.**

[[RLG-053]] converted the whole fleet: 84 lamps across 26 vehicles, each a single drawing run unlit
into the sprite and again lit on top. Every vehicle except the FORMULA declares `turn.l` and
`turn.r`, wired and working, and `Arcade`'s blink phase runs at about 1.5Hz whether or not anything
is signalling - so a lamp that comes on is already in step with every other one on the road.

**So this fragment is no longer blocked.** Both of its blockers are gone: [[RLG-041]] is closed on the
device, and the lamp technique is built. What remains is exactly the ruling it was written for - a
traffic car turns its indicator on for a couple of seconds BEFORE it merges, and one in four or five
does not.

**The hook already exists and is not wired to the lamp.** `c.blink` is set to 1.2 seconds when a
traffic car decides to merge, and it is read by nothing since the lamp did not exist. Wiring it is
most of the work; the percentage that stays silent, and whether it is a per-car trait or a per-move
roll, is the part that needs the ruling's judgment rather than its code.

**Owner's ruling of 2026-08-29, recorded here because it belongs to this fragment as much as to
RLG-053: the FORMULA car does not need turn indicators.** A single-seater has none. It is the one
vehicle in the fleet where "every vehicle's lamps are wired" does not apply, because the lamp itself
does not exist.
- **RULED 2026-08-29T13:20:59-04:00** — BUILT. A traffic car announces a lane change, waits, and then makes it - and something finally draws
the lamp.

THE SECOND HALF IS THE ONE THAT MATTERED. `c.blink` has been set on every merge decision since the
merge logic was written, and NO RENDERER HAS EVER READ IT. The feature was complete in the engine and
invisible on the screen for months, which is why nobody could tell it was not working: there was
nothing to see either way.

WHAT A DRIVER DOES NOW. On deciding to move it sets the direction, blinks for between 1.1 and 1.8
seconds, and only then starts the lane change. The gap is re-checked at the moment of committing
rather than trusted from a second earlier - a car that announced a move into a space somebody has
since taken gives up and looks again, with its indicator still running down, which is also what a
real one does.

ONE DRIVER IN FIVE DOES NOT BOTHER. `c.signals` is decided once when the car is built rather than
rolled per manoeuvre, because it is a HABIT rather than a coin toss - the same rule the rest of the
engine follows, that the car is capable and the driver decides. Measured over 26 seconds: 7 drivers
with a settled habit, 2 of whom never signal.

A CAR GETTING OUT OF THE WAY OF A SIREN indicates as it goes rather than announcing first. There is
no polite version of that manoeuvre.

MEASURED, AND ON THE SCREEN RATHER THAN IN THE ENGINE. `traffic-test` forces every car to blink and
counts amber pixels against a frame with none: 11 pixels with every blink off, 57 with them on. A
check that only read `signalling()` would have passed the entire time the feature was invisible,
which is precisely the trap this fragment existed to describe. Over a normal run, something is
indicating in 62% of samples and merges still happen - 41 over the run, against 33 before the wait
was added, so announcing first has not stopped anybody moving.
- **RULED 2026-09-09T22:08:49-04:00** — **THE CHECK THAT WATCHES THE SCREEN WAS WATCHING A FLOOR THAT MOVED, 2026-09-09.** `amber_off` - the
count of amber pixels with every blink forced off - read 0, 10, 17 and 196 across four runs of one
build, and the line asserted a margin of eight pixels above it. Eight above a floor that moves by two
hundred is not a margin.

**MEASURED ACROSS FOUR PLACES AND EIGHT HOURS.** The floor is 0 in the desert and the tundra at every
hour, 42 in the city at midday and 183 in the forest. That is sunlit ground and foliage inside the
filter, and sodium lamps after dark. **Pinning the light is only half of it, and the smaller half.**
The SIGNAL moves as well: with the blinks on it ran from 6 pixels to 1,050 over the same sweep,
because it depends on how many cars are in shot and how far away they are, and in one condition it
came out LOWER with the indicators on than off. Staging four cars close to the camera then pushed the
floor to 370 in bright daylight - traffic PAINT - against a lamp signal of 18 to 38.

**WHAT THE MEASUREMENT IS NOW.** The place, the hour and the weather are pinned; the whole road is
brought to a stop; four cars are placed abreast 1,400 units ahead; and the count is of pixels that
BECOME amber between a dark frame and a lit one. Anything already amber is subtracted, which removes
the paint, the ground, the sky and the dials in one step.

**FIVE THINGS HAD TO BE HELD THAT WERE NOT, and every one of them was found by watching the control
fail rather than by reading the code.** The control is a second DARK frame: any pixel that turns
amber between two frames with no indicator lit turned amber for a reason that is not an indicator.

| What was moving | How it showed | What holds it |
|---|---|---|
| the day clock and the weather | a floor of 528 where the same scene reads 0 | both re-pinned on every pass, not once ([[RLG-055]]) |
| the police | a drifting patch at the frame edge | `cops()` stopped as well as `traffic` |
| a DIFFERENT four cars each pass | the drift was car-shaped, because it was a car | the traffic array is re-sorted by `z` every frame, so the four are tagged once and found by the tag |
| `cruise` set to zero | a car kept arriving from the right-hand edge | the traffic step winds `cruise` back up to `cruiseFloor` every frame, so both are held |
| the scene settling on WALL time | brake lamps still fading, the needle still falling, the mirror still emptying | settled against `simTime`, which runs at a fraction of wall time in this browser |

**AND THE FLASH IS SAMPLED ON THE ENGINE'S CLOCK.** `blinkPhase` advances at 9.4 radians a second and
the lamp is lit for the half cycle where its sine is positive - a third of a second on, a third off.
A fixed number of 55ms waits can land entirely in the dark half, which is what produced 17 newly lit
pixels on one run and 95 on the next. The shot now runs until a full cycle of simulated time has
passed, and says how much it got.

**MEASURED IN PLACE.** Drift 0 in four runs of five and 0 or 1 in the others; the signal 28 to 95
newly lit pixels. With the indicator painter unwired it is 0. `tools/amber-falsify.py` is the proof
and it refuses to claim one unless the staging and the stillness lines both passed - it caught its
own vacuous run that way, on a frame where the scene had not held.

**THE FIFTH RUN IN FIVE IS RETAKEN RATHER THAN TOLERATED.** The shot is attempted up to three times
and the bar on stillness is not moved. A tolerance widened until the bad frames fit is a control that
no longer says anything.
