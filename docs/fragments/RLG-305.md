### RLG-305 — Traffic stops dead when a race ends, instead of driving on
- **RULED 2026-09-21T00:15:28-04:00** — OWNER, 2026-09-21, from the device: 'When the race is over all the traffic comes to a dead stop they should just keep moving just like the player.'

THE PLAYER IS ALREADY HANDLED AND THE WORLD IS NOT. Crossing the line sets `finished` and `coasting`, and the note on that block says why the car is handed to the AI: it lifts, holds its lane and coasts down, so you cannot crash after winning. The road around it was never given the same treatment.

FIRST SUSPECT, AND IT IS ONE LINE. `step` runs `if(mode === 'race' && !finished){ ... if(!held) stepRacers(dt); }`, so every RIVAL stops being stepped the instant the line is crossed. Whether the civilian traffic freezes by the same gate or a different one is not established and must be read rather than assumed - the owner's report says 'all the traffic', which is the wider claim.

AND THERE IS A RECORDED LESSON ABOUT EXACTLY THIS SHAPE. The early `return` in `step` was removed once before for the same reason, and its own comment records it: 'THIS USED TO return... the world weather, the traffic, the police, and stepRacers' all stopped for two seconds. The fix then was to let everything below run and hold only the CAR. This is that fix, not yet applied to the finish.

WHAT IT MUST NOT DO: the rivals must not keep RACING. A race is over; the cars on the road are traffic now. Whether a rival that crosses the line becomes an ordinary driver or simply coasts in its lane is a question this has to answer, and the owner's sentence - 'just like the player' - points at coasting rather than at racing on.

Not started.
- **RULED 2026-09-21T08:19:56-04:00** — BUILT in v0.14.116, and UNSIGNED - whether a living road around a finished car reads better than a frozen one is the owner's (RLG-116).

IT WAS ONE ASSIGNMENT, AND NOTHING READS THE VALUE IT SET. Crossing the line ran `state = 'wrecked'`. No code anywhere tests for that string - its only effect is to send `frameLoop` down the branch that does not call `step()` at all. So the traffic, the rivals, the police, the weather and the biome all stopped dead, while the player's own car rolled on through them under the frozen branch's `spd *= 0.985`. MEASURED: the traffic's mean position advanced 69,492 to 72,178 in the three samples before the line and then sat at 76,619 for the whole roll-out, while the car travelled another seven thousand units past it. After the fix, on the same kind of run, 73,962 to 85,978.

MY FIRST SUSPECT WAS WRONG AND THE MEASUREMENT FOUND THE REAL ONE. The record's first reading was `step`'s own race branch - `if(mode === 'race' && !finished)` around `stepRacers` - which does freeze the rivals and is not the reported fault. It would have explained the RIVALS stopping and not the traffic, and the owner said 'all the traffic'. The gate that matters is one level up, in the frame loop, and it stops everything.

AND IT MADE THE FINISH'S OWN PROMISE DEAD CODE. The note at the finish says the car is handed to the AI, which 'lifts, holds its lane, and coasts down'. That AI is `if(coasting && state === 'driving')` in `step`, so it had NEVER ONCE RUN after a finish since it was written. What actually stopped the car was the frozen branch's crude decay, which does not centre it and does not keep it off the barriers. Measured: the wheel held at 0.505 through the whole roll-out with the freeze in, and unwinds 0.630 to 0.028 without it.

THE RUN IS OVER, NOT THE WORLD, AND THE CAR IS HELD BY NAME. This is the shape RLG-121 settled for the count-in and RLG-110 for the penalty, both of which replaced a `return` with a set of named holds. Four things hold the car now and each is findable by its own name: `coasting` holds the input and the engine voice, which it already did; `finished` holds the clock, which it already did, and now the ODOMETER, because a run's distance is what it was when it ended; a refreshed `iframe` holds the collision, which is the affordance `serving` already uses for a car being put back on the road; and `finished` holds the BUST.

THE BUST IS THE ONE THAT WOULD HAVE BITTEN AND IT WAS NOT IN THE REPORT. It sits behind `if(state === 'driving' && wreckWait <= 0)`, so it was unreachable after a finish BY ACCIDENT, for as long as the freeze existed. With the world running, a player who crosses the line with heat on coasts to a stop in front of cruisers that are still chasing, and the bust reads neither heat nor worth - only a cruiser standing near a stopped car. Three seconds of that would have replaced the finish card with BUSTED. The guard is on the outer gate so the count cannot accumulate either.

PROVED BY `tools/finish-world-proof.py`, WHICH IS NEW, AND EVERY CHECK WAS WATCHED FAILING WITH ITS OWN DEFECT PUT BACK. Restoring `state = 'wrecked'` takes the traffic's advance to 0 units a second and leaves the wheel at 0.505. Removing the odometer's guard takes the distance from 0.224 to 0.312 across the line. Removing the `iframe` refresh takes it to -0.01. The traffic is measured by its MEAN position rather than one car, because the array is re-sorted by z every frame and a car picked by index is a different car a frame later.

WHAT IS DELIBERATELY NOT CHANGED, AND BOTH ARE SEPARATE QUESTIONS. `wreck()` and the clock running out still freeze the world, because a crash is not a finish and the owner did not ask about either. And `endShift`, which ends an INTERCEPT shift, still sets `state = 'wrecked'` the old way: it is the same shape and the same fix would apply, but that mode is unsigned and untested on a device, so changing how it ends is more risk than value today.

WHAT IS STILL THE OWNER'S: the world now runs for as long as the end card is up, not just through the roll-out, because the card does not hide the road - that is what 'just like the player' reads as, since the player's own car keeps rolling under it. If the card should freeze the road once it lands, that is one line in `openVeil`.

SUITE: smoke-test 7/7, bust-test passes, drive-test 36/37 with the nitrous settle line that is recorded as environmental.
