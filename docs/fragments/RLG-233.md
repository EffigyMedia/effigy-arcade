### RLG-233 — A checkpoint pays by class: production 30, sports 20, super 10

**RULED 2026-09-12.** Owner: *"I'd like to change how many seconds are rewarded for the checkpoint
based on class: production class is awarded 30 seconds per checkpoint. Sports class is awarded 20
seconds per checkpoint. Super class is awarded 10 seconds per checkpoint."*

**BUILT, v0.14.9.**

---

## WHY IT MATTERS MORE THAN THREE NUMBERS

A supercar covers the two miles to the next gantry in a fraction of the time a saloon needs, so a
flat twenty seconds was worth far more to the fast car than to the slow one. **The run got easier as
the ladder went up.** Ten seconds in a super and thirty in a production turns that the right way
round.

## WHAT CHANGED

`CP_SECONDS` is a table keyed on class, beside `CLOCK_BONUS`, which stays and is **the default
rather than a fourth number**. A class with no row is paid the twenty it has always been paid, which
makes this a change to three classes rather than a rewrite of the rule for every vehicle.

The award and the number on the flash are read **once, together**. A gantry that pays ten and says
twenty is worse than either being wrong on its own.

## TWO CLASSES ARE NOT IN THE TABLE, AND THE OWNER HAS NOT RULED ON THEM

**FORMULA** is a novelty with no league of its own ([[RLG-213]]) and is quicker than a super.
**UTILITY** — and the two police cars — only ever go on TEST DRIVE, which is exactly the mode a
checkpoint clock belongs to. Both hold the default twenty. **Guessing a number for either is
inventing a balance decision**, so both are asked about rather than assumed.

## THE POLICE ARE DERIVED, NOT LISTED — AND THE FIRST BUILD GOT THIS WRONG

A CRUISER declares `raceClass: 'sports'` and a SUPERCRUISER declares `'super'`, so they take 20 and
10 **through the record**. A third force car would need no edit, and two hand-written rows could not
drift from the declaration.

**THE FIRST BUILD ASKED `classOf` AND WOULD HAVE PAID A VAN TEN SECONDS.** That function falls
through to `super` for every body outside its three lists, and the utility vehicles are in none of
them — so the slowest thing in the game would have been handed the supercar's reward. Caught by
writing the test's expectations down before running it. **That fall-through is one of the things
[[RLG-227]] exists to remove.**

## THE PROOF

`tools/checkpoint-pay-test.py`, new. It measures **the clock**, not the table: reading `CP_SECONDS`
back and asserting it holds 30, 20 and 10 would test the change against a copy of itself. Each car
is driven to a real gantry and the seconds that land on the run clock are read.

    ok  SALOON        pays 30 sec   (production)
    ok  COUPE         pays 30 sec   (production)
    ok  TUNER         pays 20 sec   (sports)
    ok  STALLION      pays 10 sec   (super)
    ok  CRUISER       pays 20 sec   (a sports saloon underneath - declared, not listed)
    ok  SUPERCRUISER  pays 10 sec   (a MATADOR underneath - declared, not listed)
    ok  VECTOR        pays 20 sec   (formula: NOT RULED, so it keeps the old twenty)
    ok  VAN           pays 20 sec   (utility: NOT RULED, so it keeps the old twenty)

`--falsify` empties the table so every car is paid the flat twenty. **The four whose award changed
fail and the four that should read twenty either way pass** — which is what tells the three groups
apart rather than failing everything at once.

## THE HARNESS TOOK THREE GOES, AND THE REASONS ARE WORTH KEEPING

1. **A board is only laid within 90,000 units of the player**, and a mile is about 128,700 — so the
   first one sits at 257,400 and does not exist until the car passes 167,400. Jumping to 120,000
   found nothing and reported "no gantry ahead" for all eight cars.
2. **`nextCP` ONLY EVER MOVES FORWARD.** Once one car had been jumped a long way up the road,
   jumping the next one BACK laid no boards at all — the engine believes it has already placed them.
   A harness that stepped its own base position went blind partway down the list, **and did it to a
   different car on each run**, which is what a flaky check looks like from the outside.
3. **A gain of zero is the instrument missing, not the engine paying nothing.** STALLION read 0.0 on
   one run and 10.0 on the next on an unchanged build. Reading zero as an award prints "SALOON paid
   0" and blames the product.

The probe walks forward and asks the engine after each step, rather than working out where a board
ought to be. Run twice on the same build, all eight pass both times.

## AND RLG-100 COULD NOT BE RE-MEASURED

[[RLG-100]] — whether a car can hold its clock at full throttle — is the ruling this arithmetic
belongs to, and it was unresolved before this. `tools/verify-097-100.py` was run afterwards, as it
should have been. **It reported three green checks against three cars that never moved**: 0.00
miles, 0 mph, and a clock that never left 60.0. See [[RLG-234]]. **Nothing here says what this change
did to RLG-100**, and the ruling stays open.
