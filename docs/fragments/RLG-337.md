### RLG-337 — The ground is see-through where the road falls away after a crest
- **RULED 2026-09-24T01:23:47-04:00** — OWNER, 2026-09-24, from the device: "When the road falls away after a crest, the ground (not the road) is see through."

AND IT MAY BE MINE. [[RLG-299]] tiled the ground in 0.14.130 - every slice used to fill from its own far edge to the BOTTOM OF THE SCREEN, which hid every gap in the walk, and each slice now paints only its own band. `groundBot` was written for exactly this hazard: the chain of bands BREAKS at a crest, where an inverted slice is skipped and then every slice behind the crest is skipped by `groundMax`, so a band can start below where the last one ended. A band starts at its own top OR at the bottom of the last one, whichever is higher, and that was proved to close it.

BUT THE PROOF ONLY COVERED WHAT IT COULD SEE. `tools/fill-tile-proof.py --fill ground` compares the tiled ground against the old fill on three roads at seven places and found them pixel-identical, and it was watched failing at 17.5 per cent with holes opened deliberately. A crest severe enough to break the chain the other way - where the road FALLS AWAY and the next band's top is above the last band's bottom rather than below it - may simply not have occurred on those roads.

SETTLE IT IN ONE STEP BEFORE READING ANY CODE. `API.groundFull(true)` puts the old fill-to-the-bottom back at runtime, with no reload. Drive to a crest that shows the fault and toggle it: IF THE HOLE CLOSES, IT IS THE TILING AND IT IS MINE; if it persists, it is the older fault this engine already has a record of - the notes at the far field's fill say you can see through the ground where the road crests above the skyline, and that predates all of this.

DO THAT FIRST. It is one call and it decides which of two very different fixes is needed.
- **RULED 2026-09-24T03:28:49-04:00** — SETTLED, AND THE ANSWER IS THE CLIFF RATHER THAN THE FLAT GROUND. `API.groundFull(true)` was run against the tiled ground at seventy stops on fourteen roads, seven places, with the hill factor forced to 1.00 so the road folds hard, and with a crest hiding slices at sixty-five of the seventy. `tools/crest-hole-test.py` is the instrument and it reads three frames at every stop - the tiled ground, the old fill-to-the-bottom, and the same frame with the ground layer taken away - so a pixel is only called BARE when it matches the frame that has no ground in it. The control pair, the same setting twice, moved ZERO pixels at every stop.

THE FLAT BAND CHAIN IS CLEAN. Zero bare pixels at all seventy stops, in FOREST, CITY, TUNDRA, DESERT, SWAMP and COASTAL as well as MOUNTAIN, and `API.groundBands` - added for this - reports NO GAP anywhere in the chain on any frame read. `groundBot` is doing its job.

EVERY BARE PIXEL IS ON THE CLIFF SIDE, AND `API.dropOff(true)` TAKES ALL OF THEM AWAY. MOUNTAIN, and only MOUNTAIN, came out at 5,429, 1,855, 439 and 3 bare pixels with the rim in, and 0 in the same frame with the ground painted straight across. The picture says the same thing: a strip of ground beside the rim, running down the fall in steps, that the old fill painted and the tiled one does not.

WHICH MAY BE THE CHANGE THE OWNER ALREADY APPROVED. [[RLG-299]]'s note at that fill says tiling the ground beside a drop MOVES THE RIM, that it is the one part of the work that changes the picture on purpose, and that it was owner-decided on 2026-09-23 and measured at up to 1.3 per cent of the frame. 5,429 pixels of the band read here is about 2.2 per cent, which is the same order. So the choice is between two readings and only the owner can make it: either the rim now lands where the rim really is and the valley showing through is correct, or there is a defect sitting on top of that change. THAT IS THE QUESTION TO PUT, and it is a question about a picture.

THREE THINGS TRIED AND DISPROVED, so they are not tried again:
- A HAIRLINE WHERE THE CLOSING BAND MEETS THE LAST ONE. `gTop` was changed to `Math.min(y2, groundBot - GROUND_LAP)` on the reasoning that a shared edge is overlapped and never met on. Seventy stops before and seventy after: the same events at the same size. Reverted, with a note at the line.
- THE FAR FIELD'S JUNCTION. The rows nearest the horizon that read as see-through are the skyline's own gaps, not the ground's, and the bands there overlap cleanly.
- A GAP IN THE CHAIN. There is none. At the row that looked worst the coverage composited from the band list came out at 1.000 and the three arms read tiled 44,47,54 / full 52,57,65 / bare 51,54,62 - the ground is THERE and eight levels dark. A tolerance of eight levels was calling that bare. The bare test is two levels now, and that one change turned every flat-ground reading to zero.

AND ONE THING SEEN IN PASSING, NOT CHASED: COASTAL road 2 stop 4 differed between the two arms over 246,240 pixels with NOTHING bare - a shade across most of the frame rather than a hole. It is the same effect as the eight levels above, at scale, and it is what [[RLG-299]]'s own proof allows at a quarter of one per cent. Worth a look if the owner ever reports the coast's ground as the wrong colour.
- **RULED 2026-09-24T18:11:53-04:00** — OWNER RULES IT A DEFECT, 2026-09-24: "It's def a defect... I didn't approve have clipping through the roadway on the mountain biome."

THAT ANSWERS THE ONE QUESTION THIS FRAGMENT WAS WAITING ON. [[RLG-299]] moved the cliff rim on purpose and the owner approved THAT; they did not approve ground missing beside it. So the block on the verge is to be closed, and the rim keeps its new position.

AND ONE WORD IN THE RULING IS NEW EVIDENCE: "through the ROADWAY". Every measurement so far was taken with the road layer OFF, so what was proved absent is ground on the verge. If the owner is seeing it through the tarmac as well, that is either a second region or the same hole seen where the road is thin at distance - and the fix has to be checked with the road drawn, not only on the ground layer.
- **RULED 2026-09-26T09:26:12-04:00** — THE OWNER RESTATED IT ON 2026-09-26 AND THE NEW WORDING IS SHARPER THAN THE FIRST: "when the player crests a hill the ground is invisible beyond a certain y cording on screen." Invisible below a ROW of the picture, rather than see-through in patches.

THE INSTRUMENT HAD A HOLE OF ITS OWN AND IT IS NOW CLOSED. `tools/crest-hole-test.py` takes three frames at each stop - tiled, old fill, and no ground at all - with every other layer switched off so that nothing stands between the camera and the ground. Its list of layers included the WALL and left the MASSIF standing, and the massif IS the wall in this place: the wall painter hands the side over with `if(dB.mass && !massOff) continue`, and the engine's own note there says "the mass is the wall now". `layerOff` cannot reach the massif at all - it has its own switch, `massModel({off:1})` - so a mountainside stood in front of the ground in all three arms and anything missing behind it cancelled out of every comparison. Four rounds of this hunt ran that way. The check now takes the massif out with the rest, and `--massif` puts it back.

WITH THE MASSIF OUT, THE HEADLINE CHECKS STILL PASS and the numbers underneath moved a lot: bare pixels went from 0-76 to 12-13,938 across six stops. THE BIG ONE IS NOT A FIND. It is attributed to the cliff rim by the check's own second arm, which repeats each stop with `dropOff` and reports zero every time - and it has a plainer cause than that: the drop's FACE is one of the layers this check switches off, so in the ground-only arm nothing paints beyond the rim and the ground is absent there by arrangement rather than by fault.

AND THE BAND CHAIN IS NOT WHERE THE HOLE IS. `groundBands` was walked far to near over thirty reads on a MOUNTAIN at the hilliest shape the game ships, WITH THE CAR MOVING rather than parked, including reads with 33 to 88 slices hidden behind a crest: no band ever started below the row the chain had reached, and the chain reached the last row of the canvas every time. BE CAREFUL WITH HALF OF THAT RESULT - on the flat side `gTop` is `Math.min(y2, groundBot)` by construction, so a check that compares it against the running bottom can only agree with itself. The DROP side is the half that can fail, because its main quad starts at `y2` and not at `gTop`, and that half found nothing here.

THE INVERTED SLICE IS STILL NOT THE CREST, MEASURED AGAIN. Over 140 inverted slices in a single draw, the worst inversion was 0.12 pixels, which is the road flattening toward the horizon.

WHAT IS NOT YET RULED OUT, AND IT IS THE NEXT THING TO TEST: a hole shows the frame BEFORE it, so a parked car hides one completely - the frame before is the same frame. Every round of this hunt has stopped the car to hold the world still, for good reasons about tone and settling, and that is exactly the condition under which this fault cannot appear. `RLG-335-slab-c.png` is a crest shot taken at 68mph and it is a DOUBLE EXPOSURE: the clouds, the moon and the skyline peaks all show through the ground and the road, and the tarmac is washed toward the sky's own dusk colour. That is what a frame looks like when something did not cover what it was meant to cover and the canvas kept what was under it. A check that wants to see it has to read a MOVING frame.
