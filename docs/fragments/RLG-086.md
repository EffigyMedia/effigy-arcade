### RLG-086 — The garage answers a tap at once, not half a second later
- **RULED 2026-08-30T19:35:39-04:00** — Owner, 2026-08-30, from the device: on the title menu, choosing a new colour takes about half a second before the selection changes, and toggling is the same. The owner's own reading: the stripes were changing the vehicle, so it is probably having to rebuild the rendered car.

THE READING IS ALMOST CERTAINLY RIGHT AND IT SHOULD STILL BE MEASURED. setBody already calls buildSprites, which rebuilds every sprite in the game rather than the one car whose paint changed, and a colour or a stripe is exactly the kind of change that would be routed through it. That is a hypothesis with an obvious shape and this project has been wrong about obvious shapes before - the traffic stat table read TRUE while three comments around it were false. Time the handler before changing it.

HALF A SECOND IS NOT A RENDER PROBLEM, IT IS A WORK PROBLEM. Nothing in a menu should take that long to paint. Either the rebuild is doing far more than the one car needs, or it is being done on the tap rather than being deferred, and those have different fixes: build one car's sprites instead of all of them, or paint the new colour immediately and rebuild behind it. The first is better if it is fast enough, because the second leaves a frame where the car and the swatch disagree.

AND IT IS A TOUCH TARGET, WHICH RAISES THE BAR. A control that answers half a second after a thumb lifts reads as a control that did not register the tap, so the player taps again. That is the actual cost of this, rather than the half second itself.
- **RULED 2026-08-30T19:50:36-04:00** — BUILT, 2026-08-30, AND THE OWNER'S READING WAS RIGHT. Timed before anything was changed: the click handler ran 215 to 335 ms on a desktop and the sprite build alone accounted for 219 to 240 ms of it. Everything else on that path, the menu redraw included, was noise. On a phone that is the reported half second.

AND ALMOST NONE OF THAT WORK WAS THE CAR WHOSE COLOUR CHANGED. One call to buildSprites painted the two player sprites, then the entire rival cache - every rival body in every paint - and then every traffic sprite, the patrol car and the super cruiser. Not one of those reads optPaint, optBody or optStripes. They were rebuilt on every tap because they happened to live in the same function.

SPLIT AT A SEAM THAT WAS ALREADY THERE. buildPlayer is what a garage choice changes; buildFleet is everything the road brings with it, built at boot and left alone. buildSprites still calls both, so a caller that genuinely wants everything reads as it always did. Nothing was rewritten and no painter was touched - the change is which of the two halves a tap runs.

MEASURED AFTER: a colour tap 3.9 to 6.2 ms against 215 to 335, and the stripes toggle 3.8 to 4.2 against 209 to 405. The full build is unchanged at about 215 ms, which is the control: it confirms the cost was in the half that no longer runs.

THE CHECK IS NOT A STOPWATCH. A time is a number the machine decides, so a check written against one agrees with whatever machine ran it. A sprite is a canvas, so the exact question - was this rebuilt - is a question about IDENTITY. title-test writes a mark on the lorry's sprite and on the player's, taps a colour, and asserts the lorry still carries its mark and the player does not. Put the full rebuild back and the lorry's mark is gone, which is the check failing on the build the owner was complaining about.

WHAT WAS NOT CHANGED: the other callers. The boot, the fleet sheet and API.setBody still run the full build. Only the three paths a thumb can reach in the garage - the swatches, the stripes toggle and choosing a car - were narrowed.
