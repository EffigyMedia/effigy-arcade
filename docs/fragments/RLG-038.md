### RLG-038 — Interstate: the tow gets a ceiling of its own, so a rival that falls behind can recover
- **RULED 2026-08-28T17:10:13-04:00** — **Owner's call, 2026-08-28**, put to them as a decision because the tow could only start working if
something gave: either rivals get slower all the time so the tow has room under the existing
ceiling, or the ceiling rises while a rival is being towed. The owner chose the ceiling.

**Shipped in v0.9.5.** One line in `stepRacers` became two:

    const ceiling = AI_TOP * (1 + Math.max(0, band));
    want = Math.min(want, ceiling);
    r.spd += aiAccel(r.spd, want, ceiling, dt);

`Math.max(0, band)` is what keeps this from being a rebalance. A negative band - the governor -
leaves the ceiling at `AI_TOP` exactly as before, so only the tow half of the band changes.

**Measured, before and after, on the same instrument** (`tools/band-test.py`, three paired runs
against a band-off control, evidence `[[EVD-004]]` before and `[[EVD-005]]` after):

| rival held at | before | after | governor claim |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2.00 miles behind | +0.17% | **+13.21%** | 14%, so the tow now lands nearly all of it |
| 2.00 miles ahead | -7.78% | **-7.80%** | unchanged, which is the point |

A rival held two miles behind now runs 201.6mph against 178.1mph with the band off. The `over 180`
column reads 0% in every band-off row and 93% for the towed rival with the band on, which is the
old flat ceiling and the new one side by side.

**Why the ceiling had to go to `aiAccel` as well, and not only to `want`.** `aiGearFactor` clamps
`v / top` to 1, so a car above `top` is treated as sitting on the limiter and accelerates on 6% of
torque. Raising the target without raising the drivetrain would have lifted the tow and left it
toothless - the rival would crawl toward a speed it never reached, and the measurement would have
shown a small positive that looked like a partial fix. Both arguments take the same `ceiling`.

**What this costs.** A rival at full tow may reach 205mph, above the 200 the player can do. The
stance in `buildField` that the player is the quickest thing on the road is therefore no longer
true in the strict sense. It is still true of everything the player can SEE: the tow only saturates
past 1.27 miles of separation, which is far off the back of the screen, and a rival closing from
there is what a tow is for.

**Regression, because `road.js` is both driving games.** drive-test 23/23 across Interstate and
Motorsport, circuit-test holds at a standstill, smoke-test 6/6. Motorsport uses the same
`stepRacers`, so the raised ceiling reaches its field too - which is wanted, and is the same
behaviour rather than a second rule.

**The report was corrected in the same unit.** `band-test.py` had printed an `at cap` column
measured against a flat `AI_TOP` and a closing paragraph explaining that the cap is applied after
the band and therefore eats the tow. Both were true when written and both went false with this
change, so the harness would have kept printing a confident explanation of a mechanism that no
longer existed. The columns are now `over 180` and `base/180` - readings rather than
interpretations - and the closing text says what was seen rather than why.

**Not done here:** `aiGearFactor` still clamps at `top`, so a towed rival's torque curve is
computed against a moving ceiling. It works and it measures correctly, but it means the gear model
sees a slightly different car while the tow is on. Nobody has asked whether that is audible or
visible, and it is not tuned on a guess. [[RLG-029]] holds the measurement that started this.
- **RULED 2026-09-06T13:27:37-04:00** — A RIVAL HOLDS A GEAR NOW, AND YOU HEAR IT SHIFT. Built at 0.12.9 on the owner's request,
2026-09-06: "the AI racers should have shift events all the same no matter what... I should hear them
hit the red line and shift up or shift down if they slam on the brakes next to me." Clarified
immediately after, and it is the clarification that shaped the build: "I don't mean I actually hear
the shifter. I just hear what their engine does as an effect of them shifting up or down."

SO NOTHING NEW IS PLAYED. No shift sample, no clunk, no new voice. The rival's revs simply stop being
a smooth function of its speed. Against raw speed the note climbs in one unbroken sweep from a
standstill to the car's top end, which is the sound of a car with no gearbox; against revs-in-gear it
climbs to the redline, the gear changes, and it drops into the new band. The fall IS the shift.

WHAT THIS RULING CONFIRMS. RLG-038 already enforced one physics for every car and this did not
change that - a rival always used that car's ratio table, that car's redline and the SAME `torqueAt`
curve as the player. What it lacked was a HELD gear. The band was recomputed from speed every frame,
so a rival was always in exactly the right gear, instantly, and there was no shift event to hear or
to pay for.

`stepAiGearbox` gives it one: a `gear` it keeps, an upshift when speed passes the top of the band, a
downshift when it falls below the floor, and `shiftT` running an 0.11s interruption during which the
car makes no torque. THE BANDS ALREADY OVERLAPPED - `gearTableFor` widens each by `lap` so that "a
shift has somewhere to happen" - so the hysteresis this needs was built long before anything needed
it, and the shift cannot chatter.

AND THE SHIFT COSTS SOMETHING, WHICH IS WHAT MAKES IT AN EVENT. Measured over a window rather than
sampled: mean rival speed 6694 before against 6612 after, two trials each on one seed, so rivals are
about 1.2% slower. That is the price of gearchanges they never used to pay, and it is the right
direction.

THE FIRST MEASUREMENT SAID THE OPPOSITE AND WAS WRONG. racer-test's `mph` column reports `s.r.spd` -
a SNAPSHOT of one rival's speed at the instant the run ends, not a mean - and it swung 89 to 95 and
106 to 122, which read as rivals getting FASTER from a change that can only slow them. Averaging the
same quantity over a 200-sample window on the same seed gave the small, consistent slowdown above. A
snapshot of a noisy quantity is not a measurement of it.

A SECOND DEFECT WAS FOUND ON THE WAY AND FIXED. The traffic mixer resolved a body through
`snd.rigBody()[c.type]`, which maps a TRAFFIC TYPE - a rig name - to a body. A racer has no `type`
at all; it carries `body`, a BODY key. So every rival fell through to a null body and was voiced at
pitch 1.0 against a shared 0.9 ceiling: eleven cars of three different models all sounding like the
same engine, and none of them sounding like the car it actually is. `c.body` is preferred now.

tools/shift-test.py IS NEW and checks the consequence rather than only the mechanism: that a rival's
revs BREAK while the car is still accelerating, which a gearbox-less car can never do. It also has to
WAIT FOR THE FLAG - during the count-in the grid is held at one identical speed with no gear picked,
and sampling then reports "0 of 11 cars hold a gear", which is true and meaningless. The first run
did exactly that and the fix was to wait for the release rather than to relax the check.

drive-test READ 37 THEN 38 OF 39 ON THE IDENTICAL BUILD, and both failures were the recorded nitrous
intermittent of EVD-267 - the second line is its documented cascade, "nothing below is about the
bottle". Two different results from one build is what an intermittent IS, and the nitrous button is
the player's own touch control, which a rival's gearbox cannot reach.

WHAT IS NOT PROVEN AND NEEDS THE DEVICE. Whether the shift is actually AUDIBLE beside you at speed,
whether 0.11s reads as a gearchange rather than a stumble, and whether eleven cars shifting at once
is texture or noise. A harness can say the revs break; it cannot say you can hear it.
