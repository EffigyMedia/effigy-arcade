### RLG-068 — Vehicles close to the player are clipped by the road slices
- **RULED 2026-08-29T09:40:27-04:00** — The owner reports, from the device: a vehicle close to the player has the very bottom of its sprite
clipped by a slice of road. Not game-breaking, and recorded rather than fixed - the owner asked for it
to be written down and left.

What is known without measuring anything. A car is drawn as a billboard at a projected point, and the
road is drawn as a run of horizontal slices from far to near. A slice painted AFTER the sprite covers
whatever of the sprite falls below its own top edge, so a car whose base sits a pixel or two low
loses that strip. It shows up CLOSE because a slice is deepest there: at the bottom of the screen one
slice is many pixels tall, so the strip it takes is wide enough to see.

Do not fix this by lifting the sprite. The base of a car is where its shadow and its tyres are, and
raising it makes the car float - a worse fault, and one that is much harder to see in a screenshot
than the clipped strip is. The two candidates worth measuring are drawing the near slices before the
sprites that stand in that band, and rounding the sprite base and the slice edge to the same pixel.

RLG-041's lesson applies. Five mechanisms there were real and none was the reported one until the
owner's own wording named it. Measure the clipped strip at a known speed and distance first, and
watch the measurement change, before believing any fix.
- **RULED 2026-09-19T09:16:10-04:00** — MEASURED AND FIXED, v0.14.85. The road walk paints far to near and emitted a segment's cars right after that segment's own slice, so the NEXT, nearer slice was painted across the car's bottom edge. Measured with a sedan parked on a still, straight road, diffing the frame with and without it: in the old order the car lost its bottom 15 rows (7,148 px visible against 8,684) at 700 units ahead and 13 rows at 900, and about one row from 1,200 out - which is why it showed only close to the player. A capture shows the bumper, number plate, tyres and shadow missing.

THE FIX IS PAINT ORDER, NOT POSITION: a segment's cars are now painted one slice later, after the ground in front of them (`EMIT_LAG` = 1), and the nearest buckets after the walk. The car was not lifted, which the ruling forbade. Occlusion is unchanged because `crestGate` alone hides a car behind terrain: occlusion-test still cuts cars off at a brow (clipped=52, culled=0); its lamp line fails exactly as it did before this work.

PROVEN by `tools/clip-test.py`, which measures both orders in one run - `emitLag(0)` is the defect put back - and asserts the new order shows at least as much of the car at every distance and the rows the old order lost.
