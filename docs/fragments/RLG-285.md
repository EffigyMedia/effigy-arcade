### RLG-285 — A road rager that wins goes back to its business
- **RULED 2026-09-16T23:56:57-04:00** — TRACKED, NOT STARTED. Owner, 2026-09-16: "The road rager should reset and go about their business if they successfully brake check you to a stop. Otherwise if they get a few good hits on you they do the same."

THE RAGER NEEDS A WAY TO BE SATISFIED. [[RLG-241]] built the anger - a driver you wronged comes after you - and gave it no ending except the player driving away. A grudge that never resolves stops being a reaction to what you did and becomes a car that hates you, which is a different and worse thing: the player learns nothing from it because nothing they do ends it.

TWO WAYS TO SETTLE IT, AND THEY ARE THE OWNER'S OWN TWO. A brake check that brings the player to a STOP is one, and a few solid hits is the other. Either one satisfies the rager, and then it resets - the anger clears and the car goes back to being traffic.

WHAT HAS TO BE DECIDED WHEN IT IS BUILT, and none of it is answered here: what counts as "a stop" (the engine already has STOP_SPD, a fifth of top speed, and reusing it is the obvious first answer rather than inventing a second threshold); how many hits is "a few"; and whether a satisfied rager can ever be angered again by the same player, or is done for the run.

THE SHAPE THE ENGINE ALREADY HAS FOR THIS is RLG-203's stop rule: a condition held for a duration rather than an instant reading. A brake check that satisfies on a single frame under the threshold would fire on a pothole.
- **RULED 2026-09-17T07:48:02-04:00** — BUILT, v0.14.76. A raging driver now has two endings the player can cause, beside the two it had (cooling off and being outrun).

THE BRAKE CHECK. It settles when the rager is ahead, within `RAGE_NEAR`, and within `RAGE_LINE` (0.30) of the player across the road, and the player is under `RAGE_STOP_SPD` of top speed - Intercept's `STOP_SPD`, 0.20 - for `RAGE_STOP_HOLD`, 1.2 seconds. The first build also required the move to be BLOCK, and that never fired. A brake check that works brings the player up to the rager's bumper, and the move-picker calls anything within 260 units "alongside", so the move turned to SWIPE at the moment of the stop. The rule reads the positions now, not the label.

THE HITS. `rageHit` counts a contact only when its severity is at least `RAGE_HIT_SEV`, 0.15 on `impactWith`'s 0-1 scale, and the third such hit (`RAGE_HITS`) settles the driver. The contact event asks `rageHit` before `anger`, so a hit that settles a driver does not also re-arm it.

AFTER IT SETTLES: rage is cleared, the driver returns to its own cruise, and for `RAGE_CALM`, 10 seconds, `anger` refuses it. That window exists because the settling hit is itself a hit. The owner has not ruled on whether a calmed driver can be angered again later; after the window it can. All five values are tunables with committed defaults.

PROVEN by rage-test, eight new checks. A brake check with the player held at 0.45 of top settles nothing, which is the control. At 0.08 of top it settles within the hold. Five weak contacts count nothing. Two good hits leave the driver angry, and the third settles it. A fourth hit straight after does not re-anger it. Falsified with three defects at once - the stop never counts, weak hits count, and the calm window is ignored - and six checks failed.

STAGED, NOT DRIVEN. The player's speed is pinned, and the hits go through `API.rageHit`, which calls the same `rageHit` as the contact event, because RLG-279 records that the collider does not yet reliably land a contact on a staged car. Whether a sedan can actually brake a player to a stop, and whether three hits feel like "a few", is for the device.
