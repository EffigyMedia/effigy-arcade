### RLG-282 — Flatten the board - only a mountain is vertical
- **RULED 2026-09-16T13:05:50-04:00** — OWNER, 2026-09-16, after driving a city: "it went up and down way too much. I think we need to flatten it... a lot of it across-the-board, only really mountain should have extreme verticality. Most places are pretty flat." They chose the harder of three flattens offered: everything but a mountain at 0.30 or under, with 0.10 held as the floor.

`hill` IS A PLAIN MULTIPLIER ON THE CLIMB RATE. `makeHill` rolls a gradient of 0, or 1.2 to 2.8, or 3.4 to 5.6, and scales it by the place's own `hill` - so halving the figure halves the climb with nothing else in the chain. THERE IS NO GLOBAL TERM, which is why a flatten is twelve numbers rather than one, and why one was worth asking about rather than guessing.

| biome | was | now |
|---|---|---|
| MOUNTAIN | 1.00 | **1.00** |
| TUNDRA | 0.80 | 0.18 |
| FOREST | 0.70 | 0.30 |
| JUNGLE | 0.55 | 0.30 |
| DESERT | 0.45 | 0.20 |
| CANYON | 0.40 | 0.15 |
| CITY | 0.30 | 0.12 |
| SWAMP | 0.15 | 0.10 |
| COASTAL, FARMLAND, TUNNEL, BRIDGE | 0.10 | 0.10 |

THE CITY IS THE REPORT AND IT WAS ALREADY THIRD LOWEST, which is the thing worth keeping out of this. At 0.30 it sat below nine of the twelve places and the owner still called it too much - so the fault was the SCALE rather than that one entry, and a proportional trim would not have fixed it. Mountain is now more than three times the next place on the board, which is the ruling stated as a number.

AND TUNDRA'S 0.80 WAS SIMPLY STALE. It was set when the place was mountain's rock in a white palette; [[RLG-265]] made it a snowy desert the same day this was measured, and a snowy desert climbing harder than a mountain range is a figure nobody had revisited. It is below the desert's 0.20 now, which is what a snowier, flatter desert should be.

`bend` IS UNTOUCHED. Sinuosity is not what was reported and the two are separate axes - a canyon floor turns constantly and climbs little, which is the pair its own note is about.

THE CHECK ASSERTS THE RULE OVER THE WHOLE BOARD rather than over a few names: nothing but a mountain climbs harder than 0.30, a mountain stands at least two and a half times clear of the next place, and nothing is below the floor of 0.10. A new place added at 0.7 breaks it; a check on four names would not have noticed. The floor is asserted BECAUSE the cheap way to satisfy a ceiling is to push everything to zero, and the owner has twice said never completely flat.

AND TWO EXISTING CHECKS WERE SNAPSHOTS DRESSED AS RULINGS. biome-test asserted a FIXED chain - MOUNTAIN, TUNDRA, FOREST, DESERT, CITY - for both the declared factor and the generated road, and the flatten broke both LEGITIMATELY, because tundra's place in that chain came from art it no longer has. They now assert what the owner has actually ruled: that a mountain is the most vertical place there is, that a desert climbs more than a city (the pair the owner named), and that the generated road FOLLOWS each place's declared factor whatever the table says. That last one tests the mechanism and survives any future retune.

NOT VACUOUS, watched failing with tundra put back to 0.80: the ceiling check named it and the clearance check reported 1.00 against 0.80.

biome-test, smoke-test pass; drive-test 36/37 on the known environmental settle line.

WHAT A HARNESS CANNOT JUDGE: whether the board now reads as FLAT rather than as calm. Every one of these is a single number and `API.setBiomeShape(key, hill, bend)` moves any of them live, so a device can sweep one without a rebuild.
