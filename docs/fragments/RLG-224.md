### RLG-224 — From behind, the body stands in front of the mirror

**RULED 2026-09-12.** Owner: *"The rearview mirrors from the rear view are being drawn on top of the
body, which suggests they are not being obscured by the body."*

---

## WHAT WAS WRONG

They were painted **last**, after the greenhouse, the body, the arches and everything else, so a
mirror sat *on* the shoulder like a sticker rather than standing out from behind it.

A door mirror is mounted halfway down the car. From the back, **the bodywork is between you and
everything of it that lies inboard of the flank** — only the part that clears the body should be
visible at all.

## WHAT IT IS NOW

The rear painters draw the mirrors **first** and let the body cover them. Two places: the saloon
family in `paintRig`, where the call moved above the greenhouse, and the supercars in `paintCar`,
where it moved above the lower-body gradient.

**THE FRONT STILL PAINTS THEM LAST, AND THAT IS NOT AN INCONSISTENCY.** From the front you are
looking at the face of the mirror with nothing in the way, and it is the nearest thing on that end of
the car. **Near is on top, far is behind** — the same rule as [[RLG-222]]'s shading, applied to draw
order instead of colour. The two changes say one thing about depth in two different ways.

**THE VAN ALREADY DID IT THIS WAY**, which is what gave the answer away: its rear branch has always
called `rigFurniture` before the slab, and its mirrors have always looked right.

## THE PROOF, AND THE THING IT HAD TO RULE OUT

`car-ends-test` passes. **The risk was specific and worth naming**: if a mirror had been the widest
ink at the rear, hiding part of it behind the body would have shrunk that end and left the two
disagreeing — an outline change made by accident, through draw order rather than geometry
([[RLG-184]]). Every car reports **dW = 0**, so no mirror was carrying its car's width.

The four supercars still show their long-standing 1-pixel height difference, unchanged by this and
present before it.

## WHAT THIS DOES NOT COVER

**The pickup and the truck were checked and left alone.** The pickup's mirrors stand outboard of its
cab at a height where no bodywork is behind them, so there is nothing to occlude them with; the
truck's furniture is an underrun bar and mud flaps, which sit at both ends and are not mirrors.

**How much of the mirror should clear the body is a judgement made on the device.** On the sheet the
saloon's now reads as a nub at the shoulder and the supercars' as a sliver at the flank. Whether that
is too little is the owner's call.
