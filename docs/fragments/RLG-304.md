### RLG-304 — A canyon's skyline is capped to the loose rock it no longer has, not to its walls
- **RULED 2026-09-20T19:54:28-04:00** — OWNER, 2026-09-20: 'as for the skyline, in the canyon, we should rise it up to meet the height of the now three D canyon walls.'

AND THE CAP IS NOW DERIVED FROM SOMETHING THAT IS NOT DRAWN. RLG-104 is the ruling that set it, and its reasoning is exactly right: 'nothing at the horizon may be taller on screen than the last thing the road pass draws, or the eye is told that the furthest object is the nearest one'. It was six times too tall because the height was TYPED, so `skyWall` made the place ASK for a cap instead, derived through `skyRiseOf` from the same expression `drawScenery` sizes a wall with - the tallest rock sprite the road pass can put at the far end of the draw.

THAT SPRITE IS NO LONGER THERE. RLG-297 replaced the loose rock with a continuous plane and RLG-300 stopped drawing the rock on the walled sides altogether - the SCENERY spec was kept only so this cap would not fall back to the default. So the cap is now derived from a thing the place does not draw, and the wall it is supposed to stand no taller than is a different, much taller object. The ruling has not changed; the thing it points at has.

THE FIX IS TO POINT IT AT THE WALL. The wall's height at the far end of the draw is the same expression the wall pass uses: `CAM_H * H / 2 * wallRiseAt(seg, B)` against `proj` at `DRAW * SEG`, which is a constant for a given draw and does not breathe frame to frame - the same property `skyRiseOf` already relies on. A walled place should read that; a place with no wall keeps the scenery derivation it has. ONE EXPRESSION, READ BY BOTH, so a change to `WALL_RISE` moves the horizon with it and the two cannot drift - which is the whole of RLG-104 restated for the object that replaced the one it was written about.

WHAT TO WATCH:
- `skyRiseOf` IS CALLED FROM `drawSky` PER FRAME and its result scales the band's tile, so it must stay cheap and must stay CONSTANT for a given place. `wallRiseAt` carries the ridge noise, which varies per segment - the cap must take the PLAIN rise, not a sampled one, or the skyline will breathe as the road goes by.
- `skyRiseOf` IS ALSO WHAT `RANGE_SCALE` MULTIPLIES (RLG-297): the range across a valley is the place's own skyline art at 2.1 times the horizon's scale. Raising the cap raises the range with it, on a MOUNTAIN as well as a canyon, and that may be too much - the range is already the largest thing in the drop.
- `canyon-test` asserts three things about a continuous horizon and already fails all three at HEAD for unrelated reasons; it also holds the claim that a canyon's skyline is capped to its walls, which is the claim this changes the meaning of.
- AND THE STRIP OF SKY IS THE PICTURE. A canyon reads as a canyon because of the ribbon of sky its two walls leave; a taller skyline band fills more of that ribbon. Whether the place still reads as a slot is the owner's on a device, and it is the one thing the arithmetic cannot answer.

Not started. It belongs with RLG-303 - a mountain rising out of the ground moves the same cap, for the same reason, on the same expression.
