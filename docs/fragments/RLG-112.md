### RLG-112 — A bridge, where the water is under the road rather than beside it
- **RULED 2026-08-31T11:03:39-04:00** — Owner, 2026-08-31: "if we're doing tunnel, we could also do a bridge biome that would be a Golden Gate
Bridge analog over water. I suppose the quirk with that one is that the water wouldn't be level with
the bridge - it would be drawn as a complete surface underneath the bridge. Thoughts on that one?" And
immediately after: "The bridge would start with a vertical ramp up, be flat the entire way and then
ramp down at the end transitioning into the next biome."

THE OWNER HAS ALREADY NAMED THE HARD PART, AND IT IS HARDER THAN IT SOUNDS. Every surface this engine
draws sits on ONE ground plane. The road's altitude and the ground's altitude are the same number:
hills move the road, and the land goes with it. The coast looks like an exception and is not - the sea
is beside the road, at the road's own level, which is why it could be built as a fill on one side. A
bridge needs a surface at a DIFFERENT ALTITUDE from the road, spanning the full width, with the road
suspended above it. Nothing in the projection does that today.

SO THE FIRST QUESTION IS WHETHER THE WATER IS A SECOND GROUND PLANE OR A CHEAT. Both are real answers.
A second plane is honest, reuses `proj` with a different camera height, and would also serve a valley,
a viaduct and a cliff road later. A cheat - the water drawn as a fill from the horizon down to a line
that follows the bridge's own edge - is much less work and may be indistinguishable at this speed and
this screen size. THE CHEAT SHOULD BE TRIED FIRST, and the reason is in this project's own record: the
far sea was built twice, and the version that measured better was the one that made no estimate at
all. Build the cheap one, capture it, and let the picture say whether the plane is needed.

THE RAMP-FLAT-RAMP PROFILE IS THE SECOND STRUCTURAL NOVELTY AND THE MORE IMPORTANT ONE. Every place on
the board generates its terrain RANDOMLY from `hill` and `bend`. A bridge does not have terrain: it
has a shape, the same shape every time, with a defined length. That is an authored profile, and the
engine has no notion of one.

AND THE TUNNEL WANTS EXACTLY THE SAME THING, which is the observation worth acting on. A tunnel has a
mouth, a length and an exit; a bridge has a ramp, a span and a ramp. Neither is a "place that lasts
6.5 to 12 miles at random", which is what every biome is today. They are EVENTS with a fixed length
and an authored profile. Building that mechanism once, for both, is a much better trade than building
two special cases - and it would also give the checkpoint-to-checkpoint work in [[RLG-100]] something
to hang a known distance on.

SO THE ORDER CHANGES. [[RLG-105]] (tunnel) and this one should be designed together even if they are
built apart, because the shared piece is the fixed-length authored place. Whichever is built first
should build that mechanism properly rather than scripting one road.

THE TOWERS ARE NOT A SKYLINE PROBLEM, WHICH IS GOOD NEWS. A suspension tower stands ON the road and
passes OVER the camera. That is what a checkpoint gantry already does - `cpGantries` places them, they
are drawn in the world, and they pass overhead. So the towers and the cables can reuse machinery that
exists and is understood, rather than needing the skyline generator to learn a new shape the way
[[RLG-104]]'s canyon wall does. The cables between towers are the one genuinely new drawing, and they
are a curve between two known points.

AND IT IS A COAST WITHOUT A SHORELINE, WHICH SIMPLIFIES THE WATER. `seaTone`, the distance recession
added in [[RLG-093]], and the far band all work already. What a bridge does NOT need is `seaSide`,
`roadsideAt`, a beach, or a shore edge - the water is on both sides and reaches the frame edges. Every
one of the coast's hard parts is absent.

WHAT TO WATCH. The far band's sea and the drawn sea have to agree at the join here too, and the fix
for that is already in - the recession is a tunable measured at the join. And the bridge's road surface
should not be the same tarmac as the land: a span reads as a bridge because of its deck, its
expansion joints and the rail down each side, and those are the parts a player sees at speed rather
than the towers.
- **RULED 2026-09-01T09:24:38-04:00** — THE MECHANISM IS BUILT, 2026-09-01, v0.11.7, and the bridge is the first place to use it.
The water, the towers and the deck are not built and follow this.

THE FRAGMENT SAID TO BUILD THE FIXED-LENGTH AUTHORED PLACE ONCE, FOR BOTH, AND THAT IS WHAT
WAS DONE. It named the ramp-flat-ramp profile as the second structural novelty and the more
important one, observed that a tunnel wants exactly the same thing, and warned that whichever
was built first should build the mechanism properly rather than scripting one road. The
tunnel was built first and did not, so the debt fell here.

TWO FIELDS, AND KEEPING THEM SEPARATE IS THE TEST OF WHETHER THIS IS A MECHANISM. `span` is
how long a place lasts and `profile` is what shape it has. THE TUNNEL TAKES THE FIRST AND NOT
THE SECOND, because the owner has already ruled on a bore's form - both axes capped at 0.10 -
and one combined field would have overruled that ruling in order to get a bridge.

AND THE TUNNEL'S LENGTH WAS A REAL DEFECT, FOUND BY GIVING IT THE FIELD. A bore ran the
ordinary six and a half to twelve miles, which at the speed the interstate is actually driven
is two and a half to four and a half MINUTES underground, against a run clock that starts at
sixty seconds. A tunnel could outlast the run it appeared in. It is 1.4 miles now, about
eleven seconds.

AN AUTHORED PROFILE STATES A SLOPE AND NOT A GRADE, WHICH IS THE WHOLE DESIGN AND WAS NOT
OBVIOUS. The integration is `dy += grade; y += dy`, so what the biome table calls relief is
the RATE OF CHANGE of the slope. Ask for a flat deck by stating a grade of zero and the road
holds whatever slope it arrived with: measured, a constant +0.017 continuing to the horizon.
So the profile is read inside the integration, where `dy` lives, and it REPLACES `dy`. Outside
an event nothing changes at all.

THAT ALSO MAKES THE SHAPE INDEPENDENT OF WHERE THE INTEGRATION STARTS. `dy` is reset by the
authored section, so the slope after an event owes nothing to the terrain before it, and the
deck is flat whether the integration window begins before the bridge or on it. A profile that
ADDED to `dy` would have had neither property.

THE VERTICAL SHAPE NEEDED A MECHANISM AND THE HORIZONTAL ONE DID NOT. Nothing authors
curvature. A suspension bridge is straight because it is built straight, so it says so with a
low sinuosity the ordinary generator already reads - and that asymmetry is worth stating,
because the obvious move would have been to author both.

AND THE BRIDGE BECAME A PASSAGE BY STATING ONE FIELD. [[RLG-140]] chose a property over a
name the picker knows, on the argument that a second event place should need no second edit.
One version later a second event place arrived and needed none. `tools/tunnel-test.py` asserts
BOTH places rather than being loosened to let the new one through.

THE CHECK READS THE ROAD RATHER THAN THE TABLE, and that distinction is where a false green
was available. The rolled segments are still generated inside an event and the integration
overrules them, so `roadRoughness` - which reads the segment list - is unchanged and would be
green on a build where the profile does nothing. `tools/event-test.py` reads the INTEGRATED
cache instead. Both ways of getting it wrong were put back and watched going red: with the
override removed the deck read +0.076, and with the profile added to `dy` rather than
replacing it the deck read a constant +0.017 - a road climbing for ever, which is the exact
fault the slope-versus-grade note predicts.

WHAT IS NOT BUILT, in the fragment's own order. The water under the deck, which it says to
try as a CHEAT first - a fill from the horizon down to a line following the bridge's own edge
- before reaching for a second ground plane. The towers and cables, which it says are NOT a
skyline problem: a suspension tower stands ON the road and passes OVER the camera, which is
what a checkpoint gantry already does. And the deck itself: a span reads as a bridge because
of its surface, its expansion joints and the rail down each side, which are the parts a player
sees at speed rather than the towers.

AND [[RLG-113]] MIS-CITES THIS FRAGMENT ON ONE POINT. It says the bridge towers are a third
request for a continuous skyline, after the canyon wall and this. They are not: this fragment
says in as many words that the towers are gantry work and explicitly contrasts them with the
canyon's need. The continuous form built in [[RLG-104]] has two customers, not three - the
canyon and the jungle canopy.
- **RULED 2026-09-01T09:41:25-04:00** — THE WATER IS BUILT, 2026-09-01, v0.11.8, and it is the cheat the fragment said to try first.
The towers, the cables and the deck are still not built, so this is a causeway.

THE CHEAT IS THAT THE PLACE'S GROUND IS WATER. One line in the road pass and one in
`groundBase`: the whole width of every slice, both sides, out to the frame edges, with the
road painted over it exactly as it is painted over grass anywhere else. Nothing knows the
deck is above the water, and at this speed and this size nothing needs to.

EVERY HARD PART OF THE COAST IS ABSENT, which is what the fragment predicted and what makes
it cheap. No side to roll, no beach to converge, no shoreline, no join, and no crease for
[[RLG-093]] to have been written about. What is KEPT is the distance recession, because
water at one flat colour from the bumper to the skyline reads as a painted floor.

A FAR-FIELD RECTANGLE WRITTEN FOR IT WAS DEAD CODE, AND THE HARNESS PROVED IT. The band
between the skyline and the furthest drawn slice is where the coast used to run out of
water, so a rectangle was written to fill it. It was already filled: `groundBase` paints the
whole frame below the horizon and it now answers with the sea, so the band was being painted
twice in two slightly different tones. Removing the rectangle changed the row under the
skyline by nothing at all - 480 of 480 pixels of water either way. That is the
one-function-two-callers rule paying off rather than luck: `groundBase` serves the far field
ahead AND the band under the mirror's own horizon, so answering there covers both and a
bridge needs no far-field case of its own.

AND THE MOST USEFUL THING FOUND HERE WAS A FLAW IN THE CHECK, NOT IN THE CODE. The bridge's
`grassLo`/`grassHi` were written as a second water colour, on the reasoning that a table
entry with no ground colour reads as an omission. That made every pixel check
unfalsifiable: with the water treatment removed the ground was still blue, so the harness
still found sea-coloured pixels everywhere and still passed. A FALLBACK THAT LOOKS LIKE
SUCCESS HIDES THE FAULT. The pair is the headland the bridge springs from now, and on a
build with the water taken out all four water checks read 0 of 480.

ONE CONTROL IS PRINTED RATHER THAN ASSERTED ON, and that was measured. The coast's row under
the skyline came back 238, 183 and 340 of 480 on three runs of one unchanged build: its
shore converges on the road's own vanishing point, which the bend moves across the frame, so
on a hard corner a coast can legitimately fill most of that row. An assertion on it would
fail at random, which is the failure mode `occlusion-test.py` was written against. The
stable discriminator is the row a quarter of the way down, where a bridge reads 25 and 26
either side and a coast reads 0 and 0.

WHETHER THE CHEAT IS ENOUGH IS NOT SETTLED HERE. The fragment says to build the cheap one,
capture it, and let the picture say whether the second ground plane is needed. The capture is
taken and the water reads as one body running to the horizon; whether it holds up in motion,
and whether the deck wants to be visibly ABOVE it once the towers are there, is the owner's
call on a device.
- **RULED 2026-09-01T12:57:09-04:00** — THE IRONWORK IS BUILT, 2026-09-01, v0.11.10. Owner, while the canyon was being fixed: "the
bridge needs its red truss, like Golden Gate Bridge." And immediately after: all of it "needs
to be properly visible in the mirror."

INTERNATIONAL ORANGE, WHICH IS NOT PILLAR-BOX RED. It is what the Golden Gate is painted, and
it was chosen because it holds against fog and water - the same reason it works here, over a
sea that is grey-blue at every hour.

THE FRAGMENT WAS RIGHT THAT THE DECK IS WHAT A PLAYER SEES, and building it bore that out.
What runs past for the length of the crossing is the RAILING, with the suspender ropes rising
off it and the cable sweeping between them; a tower passes occasionally. So the rail, the
ropes and the cable are drawn per slice IN THE ROAD PASS, where they inherit the crest
occlusion and the far-to-near ordering for nothing - a pass of their own would have had to
build both again. The cable needed no curve code either: each slice paints the chord between
its own two ends and the shape falls out of the sampling.

AND THE TOWERS ARE THE GANTRY, EXACTLY AS THE FRAGMENT SAID. A suspension tower stands on the
road and passes over the camera, which is what `cpGantries` already does. They are emitted
into the same depth-sorted list as the cars, so a vehicle between you and a tower is drawn in
front of it.

THREE FAULTS FOUND BY CAPTURE AND ONE BY PROBE, and the last is the one worth keeping.

  THE KERB. The red-and-white rumble strip is the single strongest thing in the frame saying
  ORDINARY ROADWAY and it runs the whole crossing, so the ironwork could not read while it
  was there. Painting the kerb the ironwork's own colour was worse: the two merged into a
  solid orange ribbon lying on the deck, because a railing needs something to stand UP from.
  Grey concrete, which is also what is there.

  THE LADDER. Every tower on the crossing was painted at once - a tower is 13,000 units tall
  and clears the horizon from far past where the road stops being drawn - and they stacked at
  the vanishing point into what read as a ladder standing in the sea. Culled and faded at the
  draw distance now, by [[RLG-073]]'s own rule.

  THE GATE. Five evenly spaced braces from just above the deck came out as a gate across the
  road. A suspension tower has a LARGE OPEN PORTAL at deck level and two or three braces well
  above it, and the opening you drive through is most of what says the thing is a tower.

  AND THE TOWER COUNT IS GEOMETRIC RATHER THAN A MATTER OF TASTE, WHICH ONLY A PROBE COULD
  SHOW. The cable rises to tower height wherever a tower stands, and a tower is culled at the
  draw. So towers spaced further apart than the draw leave the cable climbing to a peak with
  NOTHING AT THE TOP OF IT. At the Golden Gate's own proportions - and at four towers - a
  probe of the live game found no tower inside the draw at all: one 1,600 units behind and the
  next 42,000 ahead, against a draw of 30,000. Seven towers put the bays at 27,349. The
  literal count loses to the geometry, and an argument about the capture would never have
  settled it: two people can look at that picture and disagree about whether it is wrong.

THE MIRROR TAKES THE SAME PAINTER, NOT A SECOND ONE. It passes its own vertical scale as an
argument, so the glass gets the identical railing, cable, ropes and towers from one
definition - which is what the note at the top of the mirror pass exists to insist on. The
posts and ropes are spaced by WORLD POSITION rather than by a segment index, because the
mirror walks in 900-unit steps against the road's 200 and an index cycle would have put the
rail on a different pitch in each view.

FOUR THINGS THE HARNESS FOUND OUT ABOUT ITSELF, and they are the useful part of this record.

  A CHECK FOR ORANGE CAUGHT THE RUMBLE STRIP. A coast, which has no ironwork anywhere, came
  back with 250 orange pixels. Sampling them settled it in one run: rgb(146,86,85), the
  antialiased edge between the rumble's red stripe and its pale one. That blend sits at
  R = 1.7 x G and the ironwork sits at 2.4 to 3.6, so the RATIO is the discriminator.
  Guessing a threshold would not have found it; looking at the pixels did.

  MY OWN NEW FEATURE BROKE AN EARLIER CHECK'S PREMISE. "The row under the skyline is water
  edge to edge" read 480 of 480 before the ironwork existed and 432 after, because a cable
  and a tower cross the skyline exactly where the water is. It asserts NO LAND in that row
  now, which is the sharp form of the same claim and is why the bridge's unused ground colour
  was made a headland green in the first place.

  A THRESHOLD PICKED FROM ONE READING FAILED ON THE NEXT FEATURE. "More than 20 water pixels
  a side" was set when the reading was 26 and 26; the kerb and the rail then took a few and a
  correct build came back 21 and 20. It is a SYMMETRY now - both sides, in comparable amounts
  - because that is what the claim was always about.

  AND THE MIRROR CHECK DOES NOT SEPARATE THE TWO THINGS IN THE GLASS, measured rather than
  assumed: 319 orange pixels with the railing and the towers, 253 with the towers alone, 72
  with the railing alone, 0 with neither. It proves ironwork reaches the mirror and would not
  catch one of the two going missing. Recorded rather than papered over.

WHAT IS STILL NOT BUILT: the deck surface itself. RLG-112 says a span reads as a bridge
because of its surface, its expansion joints and the rail down each side - the rail is done
and the other two are not, so the deck is still ordinary tarmac.

draw "only the tarmac and the bridge structure and leaving the water to be just the far edge

THE FAULT WAS A PERSPECTIVE ONE AND THE FIRST CUT'S OWN NOTE MISSED IT. Every slice painted
water from the ROAD'S OWN LINE downward, so the sea met the kerb and converged at exactly the
rate the tarmac did. That is what a surface AT the road's level does. The picture said the sea
was lapping at the verge, and no amount of colour work would have changed it.

BOTH OF THE OWNER'S OPTIONS TURN OUT TO BE ONE FIX, AND IT IS ABOUT DISTANCE. A second ground
plane cannot be drawn as geometry - every surface here sits on one plane, which is the hard
part RLG-112 named from the start. But a plane far below the eye is a plane a long way AWAY,
and "a long way away" is something this engine paints all day through the recession. Water
thirteen camera heights under the deck reads 0.34 of the draw distant at the foot of the
frame, against 0.02 for the tarmac at the same screen row. Seventeen times further. So the
suspension is drawn as haze, and a surface that never comes near has no near edge for the eye
to sit the road on.

AND THE BOATS ARE WHAT GIVE IT SCALE. Owner, immediately after: "can we make little boats (the
same as intended to be used on coastal but smaller since they are 'further away') out in the
water scattered down below?" THE SMALLNESS IS NOT A SETTING - it falls out. A boat sits on the
water plane, so the nearest one that can appear at the foot of the frame is already about
10,000 units away. The owner's own reasoning, made into geometry.

A flat colour has no scale in it: water at one tone is a distance you have to be TOLD about. A
boat is a known size, so a small one is a statement about how far down the surface is, and the
eye takes it without any of the rest of this being explained.

THESE BOATS ARE NOT BLOCKED BY WHAT BLOCKS THE COASTAL ONES. [[RLG-059]] deferred those
because they stand outboard of the lamp posts, so [[RLG-073]]'s culling rule governs them and
that rule is undecided. A boat on the water below a bridge stands on no ground plane at all
and is placed by its own distance, so nothing RLG-073 decides can reach it. The coast's boats
remain deferred and this is not a precedent for building them.

TWO WRONG SCATTERS CAME FIRST AND THE SECOND IS THE ONE WORTH RECORDING. A fixed lateral
spread of 120,000 units put all but one boat off the sides, because the offset that lands
inside the frame runs from about 14,000 units at the foot of the frame to 357,000 at the
skyline and one number cannot serve both. AN ANGULAR SPREAD FIXED THAT AND WAS WORSE: making
the offset proportional to the distance cancels the perspective out, so boats scatter evenly
at every depth - and a boat's position in the WORLD then depends on where the CAR is. It swims
sideways to stay on screen. Both versions looked entirely fine in a still picture.

THAT IS THE FOURTH TIME THIS ENGINE HAS BEEN CAUGHT BY THE SAME SHAPE. `hillClip` indexed by
the count of segments ahead of the player; the mirror walked a distance behind the car instead
of the world; the crest table flipped once per segment crossing. ANYTHING WHOSE WORLD POSITION
IS DERIVED FROM `pos` IS NOT IN THE WORLD. It is worth stating as a rule rather than finding a
fifth time.

SO THE HARNESS CHECKS THE FAULT THAT WAS ACTUALLY MADE, which is the only kind worth adding
after a mistake: it reads each boat's world place, drives on, and reads again, and nothing may
have moved. On the angular build ten of ten moved.

AND THE MIRROR WALKS THE WATER BEHIND YOU. The first cut passed the glass the same forward
range, which would have shown the boats in FRONT of the car in the rear-view - and looked
perfectly plausible doing it. `back` is one argument and it is the whole of RLG-079's point.

NOT SETTLED: THE DECK STOPS SHORT OF THE HORIZON. Owner, immediately after seeing this build:
"the end of the rendered bridge ends under the horizon, which wouldn't happen." It is the same
gap [[RLG-101]] is about - the road is drawn for DRAW segments and simply stops - but on land
the far field fills it with more ground and nobody reads it as an end. On a bridge the
STRUCTURE stops, in mid-water, which is a thing that cannot happen. Its own unit follows.
- **RULED 2026-09-01T14:05:59-04:00** — THE DECK RUNS TO THE SKYLINE, 2026-09-01, v0.11.12. Owner, on seeing the previous build: "the
end of the rendered bridge ends under the horizon, which wouldn't happen."

IT IS THE SAME GAP [[RLG-101]] IS ABOUT AND IT HAS ALWAYS BEEN THERE. The road is drawn for
`DRAW` segments and stops before the vanishing point - 12.6 pixels short on this screen. On
land nobody reads it as an end, because the far field fills the gap with more ground and
ground is what is supposed to be there. On a bridge the STRUCTURE stops, in the middle of a
stretch of water, and that is a thing which cannot happen. The same limit, made visible by the
one place that has no ground to hide it.

THE TECHNIQUE IS [[RLG-093]]'S AND IT IS NOT AN EXTRAPOLATION. That note records an
estimated tangent measuring WORSE: at the far end of the draw two neighbouring slices are a
fraction of a pixel apart vertically, so the slope is a small number over a smaller one, and
it put the crease at thirteen times the edge's own curvature where a straight line had been at
two. So the deck's walk asks `proj` the same questions the drawn slices ask, in the same
steps, and a point the projection will not give is simply not added. The failure mode is a
shorter deck, never a wrong one.

WHAT IS CARRIED AND WHAT IS NOT. The tarmac, the kerb and both rails. NOT the lane markings,
and RLG-101 already gives the reason: a dashed line is a strobing pattern that has to keep its
phase, and past a certain distance a marking is under a pixel wide anyway.

AND THE TARMAC'S COLOUR IS ONE FUNCTION NOW, because the join between a far band and the
slices below it is exactly where two derivations that agree today stop agreeing. That is this
file's own repeated lesson and it cost nothing to obey here.

THE CHECK READS THE WALK RATHER THAN THE PIXELS, AND THAT WAS DECIDED AFTER TRYING THE OTHER
WAY. Two things sit exactly where the deck's tip is and cannot be told from it by colour: the
skyline's own headlands, which are dark silhouettes on the horizon line, and the ironwork's
stanchions. Scanning the road's VANISHING COLUMN is worse still - near the horizon the road is
at a finite distance and the bend puts it well off that column, so one unchanged build read
353, 356, 358 and 397 on four runs. A measurement that swings by 44 pixels on a still world is
not measuring the world.

IT CLOSES 8.4 PIXELS OF THE 12.6 BY SAMPLING AND THE REST BY CONSTRUCTION. The polygon ends on
the vanishing point itself, which is a value rather than a trend. A road converging on a
vanishing point is a POINT there, so its last pixels are under a pixel wide and asking the
picture to show them is asking perspective for something it does not offer. The check bounds
how much is SAMPLED and says so.

SCOPED TO THE BRIDGE, DELIBERATELY. Every place has this gap; only this one has nothing to
hide it. RLG-101 is the general work and remains open - this is a down payment on it, and the
walk is the shape that work should take.
