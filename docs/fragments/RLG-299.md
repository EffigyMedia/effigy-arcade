### RLG-299 — Optimise the road pass, now that the draw is doubled
- **RULED 2026-09-20T13:18:50-04:00** — OWNER, 2026-09-20, taking the doubled draw with the cost shown to them: 'Let's do 300 and I'm gonna have to optimize like we were planning on two anyways. Hopefully we can find other ways to optimize so we don't sacrifice what we're doing.' So this is the work that follows RLG-295 and it is REQUESTED, not started.

WHAT IT HAS TO BEAT, measured at v0.14.104 by fps-test at midday, 55 per cent of top speed, against the same harness at DRAW 150 earlier the same day:

  CITY      58.0-60.4  ->  38.8-41.6
  FOREST    59.6-60.0  ->  50.4-52.8
  MOUNTAIN  55.2-60.0  ->  33.2-56.4
  COASTAL   57.6-60.0  ->  28.4-60.0
  SWAMP     60.0-60.0  ->  49.2-60.0
  DESERT    59.6-60.4  ->  60.0-60.4
  TUNDRA    58.8-60.0  ->  60.0-60.0

THE DESERT AND THE TUNDRA DID NOT MOVE, which is the most useful line in the table: the cost is not the road pass walking twice as many slices, or every place would have paid. It is what is drawn BESIDE the road. RLG-168's survey already said so from the other end - the DESERT lights 415 lamps a frame and holds the cap while the FOREST lights 304 and falls - and RLG-297 measured the same shape again: a canyon's two per-slice wall quads cost 60 down to 44 before they were tiled, and nothing at all after.

SO THE FIRST SUSPECT IS OVERDRAW, not slice count. Every surface beside the road is a quad a slice a side, and doubling the draw doubled how many of them there are. tools/wall-cost.py and tools/draw-cost.py are both instruments for this: each alternates its two arms inside one page, because two sequential fps runs on this machine cannot be compared and that was demonstrated twice on 2026-09-20.

AND THE STANDING NOTE ABOUT NIGHT STILL APPLIES: RLG-168 measured six of seven places leaving the cap at midnight against two at midday. Every number above is a MIDDAY number. Not started.

- **RULED 2026-09-22T20:45:26-04:00** — MEASURED 2026-09-22, and this is the table RLG-299 asked for. `tools/layer-cost.py` is the new instrument: it takes ONE layer away and puts it back inside one page, alternating, and reports what the frame recovered. `API.layerOff` in the engine is the switch it uses - one call sets the whole skip state across 30 layers, and it drives the four switches that already existed (the wall, the rail, the range, the end face) so a caller has one list to read and not five.

THREE THINGS HAD TO BE FIXED IN THE METHOD BEFORE ANY NUMBER WAS TRUSTWORTHY, and each one produced a wrong answer first.

ONE - UNPAIRED SAMPLES CANNOT SEE PAST THIS MACHINE'S NOISE. The first cut compared a range of samples with a layer against a range without it, which is fps-test's own rule. CITY's baseline swung 18.9 to 60.6 fps inside one run, and the END FACE - a layer that cannot possibly make a frame faster - came back at MINUS 3.4. Every sample is now taken back to back with its own control and what is reported is the spread of the DIFFERENCES.

TWO - THE ROAD IS GENERATED FRESH AT EVERY LOAD, so a MOUNTAIN with a cliff beside it and a MOUNTAIN without one are two different measurements of the same place name. One road put the cliff floor at 16.0 fps and the next put it at 1.1. Neither was wrong. The harness now drives three roads and prints the road-to-road spread - the same treatment `occlusion-test.py` was given after its cull count swung 0, 40 and 147 on one unchanged build. EVD-1294 CARRIES THE WRONG CLAIM IN ITS NAME for exactly this reason: it was recorded before the road effect was understood, it says the cliff floor costs sixteen frames a second, and the run inside it shows 1.1.

THREE - a harness bug re-indented the layer loop out of the place loop, so a sixteen-minute run measured only the last place and threw the rest away. Fixed and watched reporting every place before the definitive run.

THE OVERDRAW SUSPICION IS CONFIRMED, AND IT IS ONE FAULT WITH THREE NAMES. The ground, the sea and the cliff floor are the same piece of geometry written three times: a quad from the slice's own line out to the edge of the frame and DOWN TO THE BOTTOM OF THE SCREEN, painted once per slice, far to near, so each nearer slice paints over the last. That is about 130 slices of full-height fill to produce one band of ground. The ground is the only layer that is real in all four places. The sea is the largest single number in COASTAL at 14.2, and the cliff floor is worth 5.9 on a MOUNTAIN that has a cliff.

AND THE REMEDY IS ALREADY PROVEN IN THIS ENGINE. [[RLG-297]] tiled the canyon wall for this exact reason - each slice paints only the band it contributes rather than running to the edge - and the wall went from costing 60 fps down to 44, to costing nothing at all. The three surfaces here are the same shape and want the same treatment.

THE SECOND FINDING WAS NOT PREDICTED AND IT IS THE MORE INTERESTING ONE. The player car costs NOTHING undamaged and 6.8 fps at 95 damage, measured directly at four damage levels on COASTAL: 0 damage gives -0.8 with 2 of 5 pairs agreeing, 30 gives 1.9, 60 gives 3.4 with 5 of 5, and 95 gives 6.8 with 5 of 5. It is `damageFx` - the smoke, the fire and the embers, each an arc with its own radial gradient. THE FRAME GETS SLOWER EXACTLY AS THE PLAYER GETS INTO TROUBLE, which is the worst possible time for it, and it is why the player column reads 4.7 and 4.3 in the two places measured late in a run and 0.1 and -1.2 in the two measured early. It also settles [[RLG-326]]: those effects are absent from the glass, and they want to be cheaper BEFORE they are drawn a second time.

THE SKY AND THE GLASS ARE REAL EVERYWHERE AND NEITHER IS LARGE. The sky costs 3.5 to 5.9 and is repainted in full every frame though it changes slowly. The glass costs 2.8 to 6.9 and is a second render of the world.

NOTHING IS FIXED YET. The owner asked on 2026-09-22 for each cost to be proposed and fixed one at a time with approval, so this entry is the proposal list and nothing below the measurement has been touched.
