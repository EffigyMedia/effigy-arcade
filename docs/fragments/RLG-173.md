### RLG-173 — A trap leaves to chase its speeder, and the police ignore racers

TRACKED, NOT STARTED. Owner, 2026-09-08, answering the question left open by [[RLG-172]]: "Yes it
should leave to chase them, but there should also be a system that has cops ignore racers so that
one will exist for you to pass by. I wonder how the old Need for Speed hot pursuit games handled
that. The alternative is simply a speed trap car at the side of the road just increments, erases,
heat level or a heat level and spawns a car, but that's not really diegetic."

THE FIRST HALF IS DECIDED: a trap leaves its post to chase the speeder it catches. That is already
what the engine does and it stays.

THE SECOND HALF IS THE REAL DESIGN PROBLEM AND THE OWNER HAS NAMED IT EXACTLY. If the police
enforce the law against everybody, the field ahead of the player consumes the traps: every rival
running at race pace trips one, and by the time the player arrives there is no parked cruiser left
to meet. The fiction eats its own set dressing.

WHERE THE ENGINE ALREADY STANDS, read rather than assumed - and it is HALF of what the owner asked
for, which is worth knowing before anything is built:

    A TRAP IS ONLY EVER TRIPPED BY TRAFFIC. The sweep in `trapWatch` iterates `traffic`, so a
    RACER at any speed goes past a parked trap and nothing happens. The traps already survive the
    field.

    BUT AN ACTIVE CRUISER DOES CONSIDER RACERS. The retarget search sweeps `racers` as well as
    `traffic`, so a cruiser already on the road can take a rival as its target. That is the half
    that does not match the ask.

SO THE CHANGE IS SMALLER THAN IT SOUNDS: drop `racers` from the retarget sweep, and the police stop
policing the race while continuing to police the road. It also removes a thing that reads as a bug
in a race - a cruiser abandoning a pursuit to chase the leader.

WHAT IT COSTS, AND IT IS THE ARGUMENT AGAINST: a rival tearing past a police car at 180 and being
ignored is its own kind of wrong. The honest answer may be that a racer IS policed but a trap is
not spent on one - the trap logs it and stays put - which keeps both the fiction and the set
dressing. That is a third option and it is the owner's to choose between.

AND THE OWNER'S OWN ALTERNATIVE IS REJECTED BY THEM IN THE SAME BREATH, correctly: a trap that
merely increments heat and spawns a car is the arcade answer, and it is not diegetic. Worth
recording that it was considered and why it was not taken.

ON THE NEED FOR SPEED QUESTION, and this is inference from how those games BEHAVE rather than
knowledge of how they were built: they did not simulate policing a whole field. The police existed
for the player. A patrol sitting in a lay-by was placed scenery that activated when the PLAYER went
past it, and the rivals were never subject to it - which is exactly the owner's "cops ignore racers"
arrived at from the other direction, and is why the problem never came up there. This project has
gone further than those games did by policing the traffic at all ([[RLG-046]]), so it has a problem
they did not have.

### 2026-09-08 — the owner replaced the question with a stronger rule, and the diagnosis was wrong

**FIRST, THE READING ABOVE WAS PUT TO THE OWNER AND THE PREMISE WAS REJECTED.** Asked to choose
between exempting racers from policing, policing them but never spending a trap on one, and a
middle course, the owner answered: *"I don't think I ever said racers were exempt from
policing."* Their original words were "a system that has cops ignore racers so that one will
exist for you to pass by", and the PURPOSE in that sentence is the second half. What had to be
protected was the TRAP. The exemption was this record's invention.

**THEN THEY REPLACED THE WHOLE APPROACH.** Owner, same day: *"I think the simplest solution is
to have many speed traps and whenever a cop engages a target it continues to engage that target
until that target either pulls over voluntarily, is forced to stop due to entrapment, or fails
to keep up with its target and it disengages and pulls back over to make a new trap. Having it
retarget something else because it is slightly faster while cool I think will just break this
illusion. So that means one engagement to one target permanently."*

**THAT DISSOLVES THIS RULING'S PROBLEM RATHER THAN SOLVING IT.** The worry was that a field of
rivals would consume the traps and leave none for the player to meet. Under a cruiser that gives
up and parks again, the trap population is CONSERVED — a trap that leaves comes back as a trap
somewhere else — so no amount of anybody's driving can empty the road of them. Nothing had to be
exempted from anything.

**AND THE DIAGNOSIS ABOVE WAS WRONG ON THE PART IT WAS MOST CONFIDENT ABOUT.** This record says
`trapWatch` sweeps `traffic` only, so "the traps already survive the field". The first half is
true and the conclusion does not follow. **A trap is a cop in the `cops` array**, and the main
cruiser update had no `k.trap` guard at all: an armed trap ran the same retarget sweep as a
cruiser every 1.4 seconds, took whatever speeder was within 9,000 units, and drove off. It did
not even need a target — the floor under every cruiser's speed is 2,000 units, about 26mph, so
a trap crept off its post on an empty road.

**THE OWNER SAW IT FROM THE DEVICE INDEPENDENTLY**, in the same conversation and without
connecting it to this ruling: *"The speed trap cruisers shouldn't be driving on the side of the
road. They should be parked on the side of the road."* Measured at **93mph while still flagged
as a parked trap**. It had joined the BOX as well, because `onPlayer` is undefined on a car that
has never chosen anybody and the box test asks whether it is *not* the player — undefined passes
that test. The speed clamp hid half of it, which is why it looked like nothing worse than a
stationary car reporting a speed.

**THE LESSON, AND THIS PROJECT HAS PAID FOR IT BEFORE.** The wrong conclusion came from reading
the function whose NAME matches the behaviour. `trapWatch` is what trips a trap; it is not what
a trap DOES for the rest of its life. Reading it and stopping produced a confident, written,
wrong finding. *Test for the effect, not for your own idea of where the effect lives.*

**WHAT WAS BUILT, in v0.13.42:**

    A PARKED TRAP DECIDES NOTHING AND MOVES NOWHERE. It is excluded from the retarget
    sweep, from the box, from steering and from the speed floor. `trapWatch` trips it and
    nothing else does. The collision work still runs, because a trap on the verge is a
    solid object you can hit — and being knocked back 500 units by a car that hit it is
    not the same as leaving its post.

    A CRUISER SEARCHES ONCE. `k.engaged` is the commitment; the sweep runs only for a car
    that has not made one. `COP_PASS_BY` and `COP_SWITCH_MPH` are deleted, and the
    comments that described the switching rule are corrected in place rather than removed,
    because they were believed for a day.

    IT REMEMBERS THE CAR, NOT THE POSITION. The commitment turned a snapshot into a bug:
    while the sweep re-ran every 1.4 seconds, keeping the target's coordinates was enough.
    A cruiser that never searches again would have driven at a point the car left seconds
    ago. `k.tgt` is the object; the position is read fresh every frame.

    IT GIVES UP AFTER 20,000 UNITS HELD FOR 5 SECONDS, and parks. The threshold is
    deliberately larger than `LOST_AT` (12,000) so that a cruiser stops holding your heat
    up before it stops chasing, and it is a SUSTAINED loss rather than a distance,
    because a crest or a corner opens twenty thousand units on a road that draws thirty.

    TRAPS ARE LAID THICKLY: `TRAPS_BASE` 4 and `TRAPS_MAX` 8, against the old 2 and 4, and
    laid every 5–10 seconds rather than every 9–16, or the cap is a number the road never
    reaches.

**TWO JUDGEMENTS WERE MADE THAT THE OWNER DID NOT STATE, AND BOTH ARE THEIRS TO OVERTURN.**

*A dispatched car takes the player without searching.* A radio car and a super cruiser exist
because of the player's heat. While a cruiser re-picked every 1.4 seconds it did not matter
which car it opened on; once the first choice is the only choice, one that opens on a passing
NPC is spent and a wanted level buys nothing. **Measured: heat five with the 170-past-a-trap
condition earned put FOUR interceptors on the road before this change and NONE after.**

*A pursuit of a driver who is still wanted is never given up on.* Taken literally the rule
applies to the player too, and measuring it showed the cost: at heat five and 160mph the
interceptors reached 13,700 of their 14,566 ceiling while close, then fell into the traffic
behind — where the cruiser's own traffic-following rule caps them near 7,700 — and gave up in
waves. Parking one there achieves nothing either, since it is already past 20,000 units back and
is culled at 34,000. So a car on a wanted driver is dropped the old way, left behind and culled,
which is the mechanism the owner already chose for losing the police on 2026-09-07.

**PROVED BY `tools/commit-test.py`**, which took four attempts to become a check rather than a
coincidence, and each failure is worth more than the passing version:

    counting frames where the cruiser was on the player called THREE RULES WORKING IN
    SEQUENCE a failure — it had lost its car, given up, parked, and caught a player doing
    196;

    watching for the trap state in between still failed once, because giving up and being
    tripped again both fit inside one 60ms sample;

    reading `cops[0]` was not an identity at all: a cruiser left behind is culled and the
    slot quietly becomes a different car, whose FIRST commitment reads as the tagged one
    switching.

    What works is asking the car how many times it has chosen — `k.commits` — and following
    a tagged object rather than an index.

**AND TWO CHECKS IN `patrol-test.py` WERE STAGING STATES THE ENGINE CAN NO LONGER PRODUCE.** One
built a cruiser holding a target without the commitment, so it measured a FIRST choice and called
it a switch. The other tagged every cruiser it saw with its current position as its birthplace,
so a car already standing ahead of the player at the first sample was recorded as having been
born ahead — it reported "born +891 from trap" on an engine whose arrivals were all correct, and
it had passed until now only because the road happened to be quiet at that moment. Denser traps
made the luck run out.

**WHAT A HARNESS CANNOT JUDGE, most likely wrong first.** Whether eight traps is too many — every
one passed at speed is a sighting, and five sightings is a star, so the heat economy of
[[RLG-169]] is being asked a new question. Whether a cruiser giving up reads as the police
losing interest or as the police being beaten. And whether twenty thousand units over five
seconds is the right size for "beaten" at every speed, since the same gap means different things
at 90 and at 190.
