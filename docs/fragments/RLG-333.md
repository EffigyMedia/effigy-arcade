### RLG-333 — A single mountain ahead tracks the camera instead of staying in the world
- **RULED 2026-09-23T21:35:48-04:00** — OWNER, 2026-09-23, from the device: "There's also a single position of the drawn mountain that rises above you up ahead that doesn't get rendered like a single slice so as we move that slice moves with you in the distance."

READ AS: one mountain ahead is drawn at ONE place rather than as part of the terrain, and it TRAVELS WITH THE CAMERA instead of staying where it is in the world. That is the classic tell of a billboard pinned to a distance rather than to a position, and it breaks the illusion harder than a missing surface does, because the eye reads relative motion before it reads shape.

`drawMassFront` IS THE SUSPECT AND IT MATCHES THE DESCRIPTION. It is called once a frame, it walks BACKWARD from the current slice to the place's true nearest segment - `while(e > 0 && bioAt(e - 1) === F.B) e--` - and paints one massif there out of `MASS_L` bands. Two things about that walk are worth checking against the report: the segment it lands on is the boundary of the PLACE, which does stay put in the world, but `massFront` is set during the slice walk and consumed on the next biome change, so which frame paints it and where depends on what the walk saw. If the massif is instead being placed relative to the DRAW distance rather than to that boundary, it would sit at a fixed distance ahead and slide along with the car, which is exactly what was reported.

NOT DIAGNOSED AND NOT STARTED. THE FIRST JOB IS TO WATCH IT MOVE, not to read it: drive a MOUNTAIN, record where the massif lands on screen against the car's own position over a few hundred units, and see whether its world position is constant. A thing that is fixed in the world moves DOWN and OUT as you approach it; a thing pinned to the camera does not move at all. That is a measurement a harness can make and it settles the question before a line is changed.

AND IT IS NOT THE VALLEY RANGE, which is a different painter: `rangePeak` is drawn every `RANGE_STEP` segments at `idx`, so its peaks are fixed to the road and were not what the owner described.
- **RULED 2026-09-24T01:19:57-04:00** — INVESTIGATED 2026-09-24. NOT REPRODUCED, AND WHAT WAS LEARNED NARROWS IT SHARPLY.

`API.massAt()` is added: it reports the segment the massif last stood on, the distance that was AHEAD of the car, and where it landed on screen. That is the instrument the question needs - a thing fixed in the world keeps its SEGMENT while `pos` advances and its distance ahead shrinks; a thing riding the camera keeps its distance ahead constant. One reading of each settles it without any judgement.

IT NEVER FIRED IN EITHER TEST, and that is the useful part. `drawMassFront` is only reached when `massFront` is set, and `massFront` is only set inside `massSlice` - WHICH THE ROAD PASS CALLS ONLY FOR SLICES IT SKIPS. It is called in the inverted-slice branch and in the branch for a slice hidden BEHIND A CREST. So the massif is a mountain painted for geometry the road pass has decided not to draw, and `drawMassFront` then fires once when the place changes across those skipped slices.

THAT FITS THE REPORT BETTER THAN ANYTHING ELSE IN THE FILE: "a single position of the drawn mountain that rises above you up ahead... as we move that slice moves with you in the distance". A mountain drawn for whatever is currently hidden behind the crest ahead would be re-placed every time the crest moves, which is every frame - and the crest ahead is always about the same distance ahead, so what is painted for it would hold station with the car.

TO REPRODUCE IT, DRIVE A ROAD WITH CRESTS. Neither test road had a crest at the moment the place changed: a flat MOUNTAIN never skips a slice, and forcing a change on a flat road changes nothing. The next attempt should pin the vertical curvature high - MOUNTAIN takes `hill:1.00`, the maximum on the board - drive until `API.massAt()` answers at all, and only then read whether its segment holds or moves.

DO NOT CHANGE THE MASSIF BEFORE THAT READING. Its per-slice painter is what draws the face rising from the roadside, which the owner has said twice he likes.
