### RLG-297 — Canyon and mountain walls built like the cliff face, going up
- **RULED 2026-09-20T09:20:34-04:00** — OWNER, 2026-09-20, from the device: 'The canyon walls might look better if they are built like the cliff face of the mountain biome, but going up off screen instead. Maybe the same for the upwards wall of mountain.' The mountain's drop was rebuilt on 2026-09-19 as a real plane far below the road (RLG-278, DROP_DEPTH twelve camera heights, painted per slice from the rim out to the screen edge, filled to the bottom, hazed with distance, with a lit lip at the rim). The same construction turned over: a plane far ABOVE the road on the wall side, painted from the rim UP off the top of the screen, which would give a canyon wall and the mountain's uphill side the same scale cue the drop has. READ RLG-278's entry first - the traps are there, including that a slice the road pass skips must not leave a hole, and that a plane at the road's own level reads as ground however it is coloured. Not started.
- **RULED 2026-09-20T12:13:03-04:00** — BUILT 2026-09-20, v0.14.102. A CANYON WALL IS THE DROP'S OWN PLANE WITH THE SIGN TURNED OVER. A surface `WALL_RISE` (6) camera heights ABOVE the road, projected by the road's own rule with that much height taken off the eye: `y - scale*CAM_H*H/2*WALL_RISE`. It is painted per slice, far to near, from the rim out along the wall's own line. A canyon is walled on BOTH sides, which is what a canyon is; a mountain is walled on the side its drop is NOT on, and the two can never land together because both read `hazardRoll`. One flag - `wall:1` - in a place's own recipe, the way a hazard is declared. The MIRROR carries it, put in with the windscreen's rather than after a report: the drop had to be added to the glass twice, the second time because the owner reported a mountain still showing 'rocks on both sides'.

WHAT THE OWNER ASKED FOR - the top edge leaving the frame - FALLS OUT OF THE PROJECTION rather than being drawn. A plane that high lands above the top of the screen for every slice near the car and comes down to meet the horizon at the far end of the draw. Measured: the top edge is at 0 within six slices of the bumper and at 264 pixels across the furthest fifth.

THREE THINGS WERE WRONG BEFORE IT LOOKED RIGHT, AND EACH WAS FOUND BY LOOKING.
(1) A HORIZONTAL TOP EDGE per slice turned the strip of sky a canyon leaves between its walls into a hard staircase, one step per segment, beside a road drawn as a smooth quad. That is the COAST'S OWN FAULT word for word (RLG-059) and it has the coast's answer: the wall's top is not horizontal on screen, it is the plane's line at the rim, and the rim converges.
(2) DRAWN FLAT PAST THE NEAR CORNER it left a horizontal ledge wherever the nearer slice's rim had moved outboard - invisible on a canyon floor, which barely climbs, and a flight of steps up the side of a MOUNTAIN, which climbs more than anywhere on the board.
(3) THE WALL'S FOOT TOOK `rimX`, WHICH CARRIES THE CLIFF'S JAG. The jag is rock that has BROKEN AWAY AND FALLEN (RLG-284); the foot of a wall is where rock meets the ground it stands in and nothing has fallen off it. It also broke `cliff-test`, which renders a place twice with the jag changed and asks which side of the frame moved: a wall carrying the same jag on the OPPOSITE side moved the answer to the middle, 246 of 480 against the drop's own 85. `wallFootX` has no jag and cliff-test reads 87.2 and 403.1 again.

AND THE FIRST TWO BUILDS WERE A FRAME RATE. Every slice's quad was painted from its own rim OUT TO THE EDGE OF THE SCREEN, the way the drop's floor is painted to the bottom of it - a hundred and fifty overlapping quads, each a large fraction of the frame, of which all but a sliver is painted over by the slice in front. A wall does not need it: the floor is the surface FURTHEST from the camera in its direction and has to reach the bottom or a skipped slice shows sky, while a wall is the surface NEAREST and the slice in front paints over its foot. It tiles now - each slice paints only the band from the last seam to its own rim - and `wallSeam` is what makes the tiling survive a SKIP, because a slice hidden behind a crest paints nothing and the next one to draw would otherwise start at its own far corner and leave a vertical stripe of missing wall.

THE COST, AND HOW IT HAD TO BE MEASURED. TWO fps-test RUNS CANNOT BE COMPARED ON THIS MACHINE and the attempt produced a wrong answer that was nearly acted on: one run put MOUNTAIN at 52.0-59.2 and the next at 40.4-50.0, and COASTAL, which has no wall at all, moved with it - a second harness was running beside it. `tools/wall-cost.py` alternates the two arms INSIDE ONE PAGE on ONE ROAD through `API.wallOff`, so the place, the hour, the terrain, the browser and the machine's load are shared and cancel. Painting to the edge: CANYON 43.6-51.8 fps with the wall against 60.6-60.7 without. Tiled: 57.5-60.6 against 60.6-60.7 on one run and 54.8-60.7 against 60.6-60.9 on another, which overlap.

PROVEN by tools/wall-plane-test.py (EVD-1130, EVD-1135): a canyon is walled on both sides (4,619 and 3,033 pixels) and a farmland on neither (0); a mountain's wall moves from x=395.0 to x=59.6 when the coin turns, opposite the drop, which is the rail's differential and is the only thing that can settle a side; the trace's top edge is 0 at the bumper and 264 at the far end, which a skyline band cannot do because it stands at one height at every distance; and the glass carries 25,901 pixels of it. --falsify strikes `wall:1` out of both recipes and SEVEN checks fall while BOTH CONTROLS HOLD (EVD-1131) - the noise floor and the farmland line say what the instrument can see, and a falsify arm that took them down would prove only that the harness had stopped working.

AND THE INSTRUMENT WAS WRONG TWICE FIRST, WHICH IS THIS PROJECT'S RECURRING FAULT. (a) The frames were read with `getImageData` on the canvas, which catches a HALF-DRAWN frame: 94,116 changed pixels between two renders with nothing altered, on a place with no wall. A screenshot waits for a settled frame. (b) A CANYON TAKES LONGER TO GO STILL THAN ANYWHERE ELSE ON THE BOARD - at hazard-test's twelve settle holds it still moved 7,349 pixels between two identical renders, the same size as the wall being measured; at forty it is ZERO, and at eighty still zero. The place has the tallest skyline band and the densest roadside and both are CHASED frame to frame. The noise floor is a check now, so it cannot rot back.

NOT CAUSED BY THIS WORK, MEASURED: hazard-test's 'past the rim lies a floor far below' line is STOCHASTIC and fails about one run in eight at HEAD as well - the floor slice count swings 53 to 104 with the terrain, because a crest hides slices. Held at ONE standstill with the wall toggled on and off, the count is 103/104 either way and the floor found is slice 150 in all four reads, which is the controlled experiment. canyon-test's three failures are identical at HEAD and on this build.

WHAT NO HARNESS CAN JUDGE, most likely wrong first: whether `WALL_RISE` at 6 leaves the right strip of sky - it is the one number that decides how narrow a canyon feels, and the walls close in fast; whether the rock reads as rock at speed, since a mountain's wall is a large flat field of the place's own ground colour with only the roadside sprites in front of it; and whether `WALL_SHADE` at 0.16 against the drop's 0.30 is right - darkening a canyon as hard as a valley floor turned it into a tunnel, and this may still be too dark at night. Each is one constant, live through `API.wallModel`.
- **RULED 2026-09-20T14:23:17-04:00** — OWNER, 2026-09-20, from the device: 'The mountain needs to read as a mountain face going up and off screen not a flat wall', and then 'The mountain face can't be see through and it should go up then angle back but not uniformly.' Built in v0.14.106.

IT WAS SEE-THROUGH AND THE CAUSE IS THE TILING I ADDED FOR THE FRAME RATE. The bands abut edge to edge, and the canvas antialiases each side of a shared edge independently - neither covers the shared column completely, so the sky shows between them. One hairline a band, three hundred bands, and it reads exactly like a transparent wall. I HAD BEEN READING THOSE STREAKS AS ROCK TEXTURE in my own captures for three iterations. Every band now starts a little behind the seam so the pair overlaps.

AND THE FLAT SLAB IS NOW A FACE, in four parts, none of which adds a painted pixel:
- A RIDGE. The height is modulated per segment by two scales of noise fixed to the road, so the crest has shoulders and saddles. The silhouette is the only part of a face you see against the sky and it does most of the work.
- FACETS. The shade varies band to band on its own slower noise, because rock is planes at different angles taking different light.
- A CAP THAT LEANS AWAY, which is the owner's 'go up then angle back': the top of the face takes more of the place's sky, which is what distance does to everything else here.
- A SHADOW AT THE FOOT.
- AND THE HEIGHTS THE CAP AND THE FOOT START AT WANDER along the face on their own noise, which is the 'not uniformly'.

THE BAND IS PARTITIONED, NOT OVERPAINTED, AND THAT IS WHAT MAKES IT AFFORDABLE. Three strips that between them cover the band exactly once, so the area rasterised is what the single flat fill cost. THE FIRST ATTEMPT AT THE FALLOFF re-filled the whole band through a vertical gradient and MEASURED a canyon from 50.1-57.3 fps down to 31.3-47.4 - every pixel of the face painted twice. The picture was worth having and the price was not.

THE MOUNTAIN STANDS AT 2.2 TIMES THE CANYON'S HEIGHT, and `wall` is a MULTIPLE rather than a boolean for that reason. At the canyon's height the crest sat low enough on screen to see over, which is an embankment; at 2.2 the face leaves the top of the frame well before it reaches the car. `ridge` is the place's own too - a slot canyon is cut by water and its walls really are near-uniform, so it takes a quarter of the form and keeps the picture the owner accepted.

AND THE RIDGE FLATTENS WHERE IT CANNOT BE DRAWN. Past about seven eighths of the draw a band is a fraction of a pixel wide, and a height that changes every few thousand units changes faster than the screen can resolve: what came out was a COMB of thin vertical spikes along the crest, one per band, worse the taller the face was made. Lengthening the runs did not fix it - no run is long enough at the far end of a 60,000-unit draw - so the ridge is damped to nothing over the last eighth, which is the same thing the haze does to colour and the arrival ramp does to opacity.

STILL THE OWNER'S, ON A DEVICE: whether the face reads as rock, and whether `WALL_CAP` and `WALL_CAP_H` lean it back by the right amount. Every part of it is live through `API.wallModel`.
