### RLG-179 — The finish line gantry draws in front of the cars that pass under it

TRACKED, NOT STARTED. Owner, 2026-09-08: "the finish line renders before vehicles. The part of
the finish line it's on the ground should render after vehicles, but the gantry up above it up
and across the road should render after vehicles vehicles drive through it."

THE REPORT IS DICTATED AND THE MIDDLE OF IT CONTRADICTS ITSELF - both halves say "after
vehicles" - so what follows is the reading that matches the physical world, and the owner
should confirm it before anything is built:

    THE PAINT ON THE ROAD IS UNDER THE CARS. A line on the tarmac is drawn BEFORE the
    vehicles, so a car passing over it covers it.

    THE GANTRY OVERHEAD IS IN FRONT OF THEM. A structure spanning the road above head
    height is drawn AFTER the vehicles, so a car passing beneath it goes behind its legs
    and under its span rather than in front of the whole thing.

WHAT THE OWNER IS SEEING is a finish line drawn as ONE object at ONE depth, so whichever
choice that object makes is wrong for half of it. The fix is to split the thing in two and
give each half its own place in the draw order, rather than to move the whole line.

AND THE TWO HALVES ARE NOT THE SAME KIND OF OBJECT, which is why this is worth doing properly:
the ground paint belongs with the road surface and the gantry belongs with the scenery that
stands beside and over the road. Sorting them into the passes that already exist is likely to
be cheaper than adding a rule for the finish line.

CONFIRM THE READING FIRST. If the owner meant something else by the ground half, the split is
still right but the order is not.
- **RULED 2026-09-08T22:16:43-04:00** — ### 2026-09-08 — looked first, and the reading in this record was beside the point

**THE CONTRADICTION IN THE REPORT DID NOT MATTER, BECAUSE THE DEFECT WAS BIGGER THAN EITHER
HALF.** This record worked out which way round the two halves should go and asked the owner to
confirm. `tools/finish-shot.py` parks a car on the line and captures the frame, and the answer was
plainer than the question: **the whole finish line was drawn over every car on the road, at any
depth.** A patrol car half a car short of the line — *nearer to the camera than the line* — was
**completely hidden** by the gantry's board.

**`drawFinish` WAS OUTSIDE THE SCENE.** It was called from the frame loop next to `drawMirror`
and the HUD, which is after `draw()`, which is after everything. So it was not a ground-versus-
gantry ordering question at all. Neither half was in the scene's depth order, and the owner's
"renders before vehicles" is exactly right about the effect however the sentence came out.

**THE CHECKPOINT BOARDS NEVER HAD THIS COMPLAINT, AND THAT IS THE FIX.** `cpGantries` are pushed
into `items` as `kind:'c'` and painted by the bucketed pass that walks far to near, so a nearer
slice paints over anything further away. The finish is the same kind of object and is now the same
kind of item — `kind:'f'` — so it needs no rule of its own. **The split this record proposed is
unnecessary:** the tarmac chequer sits in the same bucket as the road slice it is painted on, so
cars in nearer buckets cover it, and the gantry is above the road where nothing overlaps it until
you are under it. One item, and the pass does the rest.

**NO AUTOMATED CHECK EXISTS FOR THIS, AND THAT IS RECORDED RATHER THAN PAPERED OVER.** Four were
built and every one failed, each for a reason worth keeping:

    A WHOLE-BAND FRAME DIFF, with and without a car — the frame is never still, because a
    scanline overlay and a moving day clock see to it, and the band drifted by more than a
    distant car changed it. The signal was 1.35x the noise.

    AN EXACT-COLOUR COUNT of the chequer's two literal fills — **read zero on a build drawing
    the line perfectly.** The scanline overlay modulates every pixel, so neither colour ever
    reaches the screen intact. That is the same trap as the stray mirror bar in [[RLG-177]] on
    the same day, where the arrival fade blended a colour away from its own value.

    THE TARMAC STRIP ALONE — the strip is a thin rectangle BELOW the projected point, and a car
    standing at the same z has its base there and its body above, so the two never overlap and
    the check measured nothing at all.

    THE WHOLE STANDING BAND, board top down to the road, against the frame's own drift — a
    strong 31x signal, and it **PASSED ON THE BROKEN BUILD TOO** at 20x. Even with the board
    over it, enough of the car shows below to move the band. "The band changed" is true either
    way; only *how much* differs, by about 1.5x, and that depends on the biome.

**SO THE FIX IS VERIFIED BY CAPTURE AND ONLY BY CAPTURE.** Two matched full-frame shots of the
same staging, one per build: **before, the traffic car is nowhere in the frame; after, it is
there.** That is decisive and it is not automated. A check that would work has to ask whether the
car's own pixels appear inside the board's rectangle, which needs a description of the car — and
the cars here are painted every colour there is.

**AND THE CAPTURE TOOL COST TWO ATTEMPTS OF ITS OWN**, both recorded in the file: hand-written
traffic objects are enough to be in the array and not enough to be PAINTED, so the first captures
showed a finish line with no cars anywhere near it; and `pz` read once while the player was still
moving left the cars drifting behind the camera. `placePatrol` is the road's own builder and is
what the file uses now.
