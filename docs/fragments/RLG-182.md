### RLG-182 — A car's front and back are not the same height in the garage, and no tyres show

Owner, 2026-09-09, on seeing a locked car's silhouette: *"That silhouette raises a concern that I
hadn't caught before. The height of the vehicles aren't the same. Every vehicle needs to have the
same height, and we need to show a little bit of the tires on the bottom. Run this check against
every vehicle."* And, correcting a misreading before it could be made: *"When I say every vehicle
needs to be the same height, I mean its front relative to its back, not different vehicles
relative to each other."*

**THE SILHOUETTE IS WHAT MADE IT VISIBLE.** The fault is not new — it has been in the garage since
the card grew two ends — but a car painted in its own colours hides it, and a flat grey shape
against a dark card does not. That is worth recording on its own: [[RLG-180]] was built for
discoverability and its first effect was to expose a rendering fault nobody had caught.

## The measurement, against every vehicle, as asked

`tools/car-ends-test.py` walks all seventeen garage bodies. **Ten agreed exactly and seven did
not, and every one of the seven is a winged car:**

| car | back | front | out by |
|---|---|---|---|
| STALLION | 69.0 | 86.0 | **17.0** |
| MATADOR | 61.9 | 76.1 | **14.2** |
| APEX / COMET / VECTOR | 61.9 | 71.2 | **9.3** |
| CREST | 79.4 | 83.8 | 4.4 |
| LORRY | 134.4 | 137.2 | 2.8 |

AMBULANCE, CAB, COUPE, CRUISER, MUSCLE, PICKUP, ROADSTER, SALOON, TUNER and VAN were all 0.0 —
which is why this could sit unnoticed: **the three cars a fresh save can drive are three of the
ten that agree.**

## Why

`garageFit` measured the ink of both ends and picked ONE scale from the larger of them. Two
drawings with different amounts of ink then came out at two different heights. And `drawGarageCar`
placed each end's content **TOP** on the ceiling line, so the two started together and finished
apart — the rear of a STALLION stopped seventeen pixels above its own front.

**THE CODE ALREADY CLAIMED OTHERWISE, IN WRITING.** The comment in `drawGarageCar`, written for
[[RLG-087]], says: *"Both are then drawn at ONE scale... with their content bottoms on the same
line. A car is the same size from both ends and stands on the same floor, which is the whole of
what the owner asked for."* The scale half was true. **The floor half was never what the code
did**, and the comment kept it looking correct to anybody who read it instead of measuring it.
That is the third time in two days that a comment stating a fact about the drawing has been found
to be out of date — see [[RLG-177]] and [[RLG-179]].

## What was built

Each end now gets **its own scale**, chosen so both reach the same drawn height, and the height is
still the one the larger end would have taken — so nothing grows and neither end leaves its half of
the card. And the **bottom** is what is placed, so they stand on one line. Re-measured: all
seventeen at 0.0.

## The tyres are NOT done, and here is what is known

**The silhouette answers the question directly: there is no wheel in the sprite below the body.**
The grey shape is the sprite's own ink, and its bottom edge is flat across the whole width on both
ends of every car. So this is not a cropping or an alignment problem that the layout can fix —
**nothing is being cut off, because there is nothing there.**

Showing "a little bit of the tires" therefore needs the garage view to draw wheels that the body
sprite does not carry. That is a painter change across the fleet rather than a layout one, it
touches the same declaration machinery [[RLG-053]] set up, and it should be its own unit. **Do not
attempt it by lowering the floor or extending the box** — that would move a car that has no wheels
down the card and leave a gap.

> **THE THREE PARAGRAPHS ABOVE ARE WRONG, AND THEY ARE KEPT SO THE MISTAKE IS VISIBLE.** They were
> written from the SILHOUETTE, which is the one drawing in the game with its detail deliberately
> removed — it is a flat grey shape, so of course it has no wheels in it. **The sprite was never
> measured.** The section below measures it.

## BUILT 2026-09-12, v0.14.7 — AND THE WHEELS WERE THERE ALL ALONG

**Measured off the front sprite**, per row, ink count and mean luminance:

| rows | what | ink | luminance |
|---|---|---|---|
| 149–151 | the bumper | 180 of 180 | 90–96 |
| **152–154** | **two wheels, 36 px each** | **72** | **16–22** |
| 156–162 | `groundShadow`, full width | 178 | 0–5 |

A VAN's wheels are rows 161–166 at luminance 12–21, under the same black bar. **So the bottom of
every car in this card was a near-black tyre, above a black shadow, on a near-black card — three
dark things that could not be told from one another.** Nothing was missing. Nothing was visible.

**THE CAR IS ALSO ANCHORED ON ITS SHADOW.** `spriteInk` counts the shadow as ink and `put` stands
the ink bottom on `FLOOR`, so every car floats above the floor line by the height of a shadow nobody
can see.

## SO THE ROOM IS LIT, AND NO PAINTER IS TOUCHED

Two reasons a painter change would have been wrong, and both bite:

1. **Lightening a tyre lightens it on the road**, where it is correct.
2. **[[RLG-184]] declares the outline once and both painters trace it**, so anything added under the
   body at one end is a change to both ends by construction.

The fault exists only in this card, so the fix is only in this card: a pool of light at the floor
line, with the shadow falling on it. The tyres become the darkest thing in a lit area instead of one
dark thing among three, and `groundShadow` starts doing the job it was drawn for — on the road it
lands on tarmac, and this is the tarmac it never had in the garage. **It is also the floor
[[RLG-210]] already asked for**: *"anchor all the vehicles at the bottom"* was built as an alignment
rule with nothing drawn at the line.

## AND IT IS CSS, WHICH IS NOT A STYLE CHOICE

It was drawn on the card canvas for one build, and that **broke `garage-card-test`**: the harness
asks how wide the car is by counting non-transparent pixels, and a floor laid across the card is
non-transparent across the card. Three cars went from a 224-to-266-pixel answer to a flat **287** —
the width of the floor.

**A check that cannot tell the car from the scenery is the check being right.** The canvas is the
car; the floor is the room it stands in. So the floor is a `.gwrap::before` in each cabinet's
stylesheet and `drawGarageCar` only writes `--gfloor`, the line's position for the car in it. Every
existing measurement of the car's own pixels still means what it meant, and the fleet sheet — which
draws its own card from these sprites — is untouched.

**Both cabinets got the rule**, not only Interstate. That is the lesson [[RLG-226]] had learned four
hours earlier: the engine cannot know which game it is running, so a style the engine asks for has to
exist in both, or the two cabinets silently disagree.

## THE PROOF, AND ITS FIRST VERSION PASSED ITS OWN FALSIFIER

`garage-card-test.py` gained two checks and a `--falsify floor`, which deletes the rule out of the
live stylesheet. **Because the floor is CSS it is not on the canvas**, so these cannot read `#gcar`
the way every other check in that file does — they photograph the composed card and measure what the
eye gets.

    ok  the card has a lit floor under the car
    ok  the tyres read against the floor

**The first version of the floor check passed with the floor deleted.** Its control read the card's
outer edges, which are dark either way. Measuring the floor's own columns 70 pixels higher gave the
real numbers: **71.9 against 11.6 with the floor, 20.2 against 11.6 without it** — the card's own
background gradient is worth 8.6 and had been clearing a threshold of 6. The bar is 30 now, measured
rather than chosen, with room on both sides.

## AND THE HARNESS HAD BEEN RED FOR A REASON THAT WAS NOT THE PRODUCT

Found while running it: `garage-card-test` walked **TUNER, MUSCLE and ROADSTER**, and [[RLG-213]]
locked the sports class on a fresh save. So every card it measured was a **locked** one. The width
checks passed anyway — a silhouette is fitted exactly like a car — and three button checks failed on
every run because [[RLG-210]]'s amendment correctly takes the flip button off a locked card.

**The button works and the harness was wrong**, which is this project's own standing rule: when a
check fails identically everywhere, suspect the check. It walks SALOON, COUPE and HATCH now, and four
checks that had never once been reached — the flip turning the car round, the paint not flipping it
back, the reset on reopening — run for the first time. All pass.
