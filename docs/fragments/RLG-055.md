### RLG-055 — The full fleet stat table, rewritten and signed off by the owner
- **RULED 2026-08-28T20:21:02-04:00** — **Owner, 2026-08-28:** "we need to also rewrite and sign off on the full stat table of the whole
fleet", and "the stats do need to include brake and grip".

**This supersedes [[RLG-043]].** That fragment asked for the production and sports class to come down
a bit, with traffic normalized so the lorry was not made worse. The job is now larger in three ways
and a fragment whose title says "lower the production and sports class" would send a later reader to
the wrong scope:

1. **the whole fleet**, not one class - every entry in `BODY`, supercars, sports, police and traffic
2. **`brake` and `grip` as well as `vmax` and `pull`** - the owner has ruled them in
3. **owner sign-off** on the finished table, which makes this a proposal to be reviewed rather than
   a change to be made and reported

RLG-043's content is not discarded: its measured findings, its class rule for the police cars, and
its normalization scheme for the traffic all carry forward and are named below.

**IT IS BLOCKED ON A MEASUREMENT THAT HAS NOT BEEN DONE.** [[RLG-042]] measured straight-line
acceleration and top speed, and found the table truthful about `vmax` and the garage card optimistic
about 0-60. **Nothing has measured `brake` or `grip`.** Lowering a number without knowing whether it
was honest produces a change nobody can interpret afterwards - which is the whole lesson of RLG-042,
where the top-speed column turned out to be true and three comments around it false. So
`stat-test.py` needs a fourth experiment first: real braking distance from a fixed speed, and real
cornering, per body, against what `brake` and `grip` declare.

**What carries forward from RLG-043, unchanged:**

- **the class rule the owner gave as a standing rule:** CRUISER is comparable to the sports class,
  SUPERCRUISER to the supercars. Measured, both are on the wrong side of their rung - CRUISER at
  190mph above every sports car, SUPERCRUISER at 184 below every supercar - so seating them is part
  of the rewrite rather than a consequence of it.
- **the traffic normalization:** pull each traffic body down toward the lorry rather than by a flat
  percentage, so the fastest traffic drops most and the lorry does not move. `new = LORRY + (old -
  LORRY) * (1 - k)`.
- **the traffic half stays blocked** until traffic actually reads `BODY` ([[RLG-042]]), because a
  number nothing consults cannot be signed off as anything.

**The rule for the numbers is [[RLG-043]]'s:** mostly realistic, biased towards fun. Realism sets the
shape and the ordering; fun breaks the tie wherever realism would make the game worse; where the two
conflict and it is not obvious, the proposal names the point rather than choosing quietly.

**Three comments are wrong and are fixed HERE rather than earlier**, which is why RLG-042 left them:
the rung comment claiming MUSCLE 184 and TUNER 172, the supercar comment claiming the fastest car
sits at 200 and that anything above 1.0 is clamped, and the SUPERCRUISER note claiming its equipment
costs 4mph when the gap is 10. They describe numbers this unit changes. Rewriting them any earlier
means writing them twice.

**Sign-off means the table is proposed, not applied.** The deliverable is the full fleet in one
readable table - every body, every stat, old against new, with the reasoning for each move and the
conflicts named - for the owner to approve, amend or reject before a line of `BODY` is edited.
- **RULED 2026-08-28T20:32:57-04:00** — **A decision has arrived for this table from [[RLG-042]] part 2, and it should be taken here rather
than separately.**

Giving the rivals their own gearbox and torque replaced a flat `2850` with the player's own
`1000 * gearFactor * pull`. Measured, a rival's recovery from 40% to 90% of pace went from **3.82s to
13.5s** - because rivals had been pulling about three and a half times harder than any car the
player can buy, with no `pull` term at all. The old number was the wrong one, but the race was tuned
around it, and the rubber band's measured effect fell with it: the governor from -7.80% to -3.21%,
and a towed rival no longer reaches the ceiling the tow raises for it.

**So the fleet table now has a job it did not have when the owner asked for it:** `pull` is the stat
that decides how a race feels, and it is currently set for cars that were never using it. The
proposal must set `pull` deliberately for every rival-capable body and say what it does to the race,
not only what it does to the garage card.

**This is the right home for that decision** rather than a constant in `aiAccel`. `pull` is
published - it is on the unlock card, it is what `zeroSixty()` reads, and it is a promise to the
player. A rival that pulls harder should be a car that pulls harder. The alternative, an AI-only
multiplier, would put "AI cars are special" back into the engine under a better name, three commits
after it was measured out of it.

**It also means the sign-off is not only about numbers being plausible.** The owner is signing off
how hard the field races, and the proposal should say so plainly and give the before-and-after from
`band-test` and the `stat-test` gearbox experiment for whatever it proposes.

**1. `pull` is replaced by an ACCELERATION stat.** The table gets `accel`; `pull` goes.

Worth noting what is already true, so the change is not overstated: `pull` is **not on any card
today**. The unlock card prints TOP SPEED, GEARBOX, REDLINE and 0-60, and road.js:4455 already
carries a heading reading `0 TO 60, NOT "PULL"`. So the front-facing half of the ruling is already
satisfied, and what remains is the internal name and the concept behind it - a torque multiplier
called `pull` becomes a stat that means acceleration, with the 0-60 figure as its public face.

**The rename is folded INTO this table rewrite rather than done first.** `BODY` is about to be
rewritten line by line under the owner's signature; renaming a column in it beforehand means editing
every entry twice and reviewing a diff that mixes a rename with a retune. Same reason the three wrong
comments were left for this unit.

**2. NPCs DO NOT GET BETTER CARS. This closes [[RLG-042]] part 2's open decision.**

That unit found rivals had been accelerating about three and a half times harder than any car the
player can buy, fixed it, and put three options to the owner: accept the slower field, raise `pull`
for rival-capable bodies here, or add an AI-only multiplier. **The owner has rejected the second and
third and taken the first.**

So this table does **not** get a set of numbers tuned to make the field competitive. Every car gets
the stat it should have on its own merits, and a rival is competitive because it DRIVES well.

**That moves the load onto [[RLG-033]], and it should be said plainly: RLG-033 is now load-bearing
rather than polish.** Its measurement is done; parts 2, 3 and 4 - racing lines and committed
overtakes, defending, and mistakes and personality - are what a rival now has instead of a faster
car. The field got slower in v0.9.8 and the answer is that it must get better.

**It is also the more honest design and the record should say why.** A rival that beats the player
because its car pulls harder is a car the player cannot buy doing something the player cannot do. A
rival that beats the player because it picked a better line is something the player can learn from,
and then beat. The first is difficulty; the second is a game.

**One consequence for the sign-off:** the proposal can now be argued purely on "is this what this
vehicle should be", without a second column of "and does this make the race close enough". That is a
much easier table to review and a much easier one to get right.

BRAKING. From 140mph to 40mph, brake held, no throttle, in cleared air, best of two:

    VECTOR 250m   APEX 254   COMET 264   CREST 367   STALLION 374   MATADOR 377
    ROADSTER 433  TUNER 473  CRUISER 483  COUPE 550  MUSCLE 565     SALOON 605
    CAB 599       PICKUP 661 VAN 683      LORRY 997

The order is the declared order, and the spread is four to one from the formula cars to the lorry.
`brake` is honest.

CORNERING, AND NOBODY TOUCHES THE WHEEL. The engine pushes a car to the outside of a bend at
`pushK * v^2 * dt * cornerG()`, and `cornerG()` is `0.42 / grip`. So the car is driven into one bend
at a fixed 120mph with no steering input at all, the push is integrated exactly as the engine applies
it, and the drift is divided by it. That gives `cornerG` as measured. Multiplied by the declared grip
it should read 0.42 for every car, and it does:

    mean 0.420, spread 0.406 to 0.432, standard deviation 0.006, across all sixteen bodies.

`grip` is honest too.

WHAT IT TOOK TO GET A NUMBER, because two earlier versions returned a clean-looking nothing:

1. THE AUTOPILOT WAS DRIVING. The first run reported no drift at all for all sixteen cars, which
   looked like a result. `__probe.drive()` is a centre-seeker holding the arrow keys, and it was
   steering the push out as fast as the corner made it. A grip measurement with a driver in it
   measures the driver. The driver is stopped for the whole experiment now.

2. PINNING THE CAR IN THE BEND MEASURED NOTHING EITHER. Holding one world position with `jumpTo` on
   a 16ms timer rebuilds the bend cache sixty times a second, so nothing accumulates - it is a car
   being teleported to the start of a corner, not a car in one. The car drives into the bend now.

3. A RATE IS NOT COMPARABLE BETWEEN TWO CARS unless they were pushed by the same amount, and they
   never are: the entry point differs and `pushK` lags the road by CORNER_LAG. Integrating the push
   and dividing by it removes both.

4. A CAR STARTS WHERE THE LAST ONE STOPPED. The verge snaps `targetX = playerX*0.7` when a car is
   against the barrier - a third of a lane, arriving inside the measurement as though a corner had
   caused it. Two bodies read 2.0 instead of 0.42 from exactly that. `setLane(0)` between cars.

THE CHECK WAS WATCHED FAILING. With `grip` removed from `cornerG` and `brake` removed from the brake
rate, the same run reports every car stopping in 483m and `x grip` spreading from 0.175 to 0.871
(sd 0.212 against 0.006). Both halves detect their own defect, so neither column is vacuous.

THREE NEW INSTRUMENTS, all read-only except the two that a harness cannot do without: `setBrake`
(the same call the brake button makes, not a shortcut past it), `pushK`, `targetX`, `curvatureAt`,
and `setLane`.

The table proposal can now be written against measured behaviour rather than against declarations.

THE CORRECTION ON BRAKING, and it matters because it decides whether a term goes in or stays out.
IN IDEAL BRAKING, MASS CANCELS. A tyre-limited stop decelerates at the coefficient of friction times
gravity - `a = mu * g` - and the mass appears on both sides of the equation and falls out. A heavy car
needs more force to stop, and its weight presses the tyres down proportionally harder to provide it.
That is why a loaded lorry and an empty one stop in similar distances, and why a heavy saloon can
out-brake a light one on the same tyres.

What DOES make a heavy vehicle stop badly is not its mass directly: it is brake capacity relative to
mass, tyre load sensitivity - a tyre loses grip per kilogram as the load rises - and, in a lorry's
case, the fact that its tyres are hard compounds built for wear.
