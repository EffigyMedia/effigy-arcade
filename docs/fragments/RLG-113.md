### RLG-113 — A jungle, which is a climate the swamp already has and a form it does not
- **RULED 2026-08-31T11:14:21-04:00** — Owner, 2026-08-31, immediately after settling the temperature-and-precipitation model: "with that
being said, let's add a jungle biome."

AND THE FIRST THING TO SAY IS THAT ITS CLIMATE IS ALREADY TAKEN. On the two axes just agreed, a jungle
is hot and very wet. So is the swamp: temp 0.80, precip 0.64. A jungle at temp 0.88 and precip 0.78
sits almost on top of it, and the owner's own instruction when the biome question was first asked was
not to overlap too much with what already exists. That has to be answered before this is built, not
after.

IT IS ANSWERABLE, AND THE ANSWER IS THAT CLIMATE IS NOT WHAT SEPARATES THEM. Two places can share a
climate and be nothing alike - which is worth knowing, because the temperature model just adopted
could otherwise be read as saying a place IS its two numbers. It is not. The four things that make a
place are its climate, its TERRAIN, its scenery and its light, and jungle differs from swamp on three
of the four:

    the swamp is FLAT and half water     hill 0.15, standing water at the roadside, a broken
                                         treeline with holes in it because a swamp is half water
    the jungle is HILLY and closed       hill around 0.55, no standing water, and a canopy that
                                         closes over the road rather than a treeline beside it

That difference in `hill` is larger than the difference between most pairs on the board, and it is
felt through the wheel rather than seen - which makes it a better distinction than a palette.

THE CANOPY IS THE REAL FEATURE, AND IT CONNECTS THIS TO THE TUNNEL. A jungle road is a cut through
vegetation with the light coming down in patches. That is a PARTIAL tunnel: not dark, but darker by
day than any place currently is, with the sky mostly hidden. [[RLG-105]] is about teaching `lampsOn()`
to read the PLACE and not only the hour, so a tunnel can be dark at midday. If that lands first, a
jungle asks the same function for a smaller number and gets its gloom for nothing. Built the other way
round, the jungle would grow its own darkening and the tunnel would arrive to find two answers to one
question.

SO THE ORDER IS TUNNEL FIRST, JUNGLE SECOND, and the jungle is cheap once the tunnel exists.

WHERE IT SITS IN THE TABLE:

    place    temp  vary  precip |  rain  snow  floor  cover  |  hill  bend
    JUNGLE   0.88  0.06   0.78  |  0.78  0.00   0.00   1.00  |  0.55  0.80
    SWAMP    0.80  0.08   0.64  |  0.64  0.00   0.00   1.00  |  0.15  0.70

That makes the jungle the wettest place on the board, which is correct and is the one climate claim
worth making: it should rain there more than anywhere else.

AND IT NEEDS A SKYLINE THAT IS NOT A TREELINE. `buildSkyline` draws SWAMP and FOREST with the same
`tree` kind - narrow trunks with holes punched in the line. A jungle horizon is a solid mass with no
gaps and no individual trees readable in it, which is closer to the canyon wall than to a forest. That
is the third place now asking the skyline generator for a continuous form rather than a row of
objects, after [[RLG-104]] and [[RLG-112]] - three requests for the same missing capability is an
argument for building it once, deliberately.

NOT STARTED. Raised as the temperature model was being settled, and it is the first test of whether
that model can express a place rather than only a table row.
